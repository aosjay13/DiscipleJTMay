Clean and Unclean Animals

A Teaching on the Distinction God Set · West Virginia Reference

✦

The Heart of It

From the beginning, God drew a line between what He set apart for food and what He did not. This was never about earning His favor, for our standing before Him rests on grace through faith in the Lord Jesus Christ. Honoring this distinction is fruit that grows from a heart that loves Him and trusts that He knows His creation better than we do. He calls His people to be set apart, and He gave us a plain table of what nourishes the body He gave us.

What follows is a teaching on how the Scriptures mark the difference, followed by a practical reference for the animals most people in West Virginia will actually encounter, whether in the field, on the water, or at the table.

Part One

The Distinction Came Before Sinai

Long before the commandments were given at the mountain, the difference between clean and unclean was already known. When God told Noah to bring the animals into the ark, He sent the clean ones in by sevens and the unclean ones by twos. Noah understood the categories without being handed a list, which tells us the knowledge was already settled in the earth.

Scriptural Reference

Genesis 7:2 Of every clean beast thou shalt take to thee by sevens, the male and his female: and of beasts that are not clean by two, the male and his female.

When the categories were later written down for the nation, they were not a new invention. They were a clear record of a line God had drawn into the order of His creation.

Part Two

Land Animals: Two Signs Required

For an animal of the land to be clean, it must carry both signs at once. It must have a divided, cloven hoof, and it must chew the cud. One sign by itself is not enough. An animal that meets only one of the two remains unclean.

The Two Signs for Clean Land Animals

  • The hoof is parted and clovenfooted
  • The animal chews the cud

Scriptural References

Leviticus 11:3 Whatsoever parteth the hoof, and is clovenfooted, and cheweth the cud, among the beasts, that shall ye eat.
Leviticus 11:4 Nevertheless these shall ye not eat of them that chew the cud, or of them that divide the hoof: as the camel, because he cheweth the cud, but divideth not the hoof; he is unclean unto you.
Leviticus 11:7 And the swine, though he divide the hoof, and be clovenfooted, yet he cheweth not the cud; he is unclean to you.
Why the swine fails the test: the pig parts the hoof but does not chew the cud, so it carries only one sign and stays unclean. This is why pork, ham, bacon, and the wild boar all fall on the unclean side.
Part Three

Creatures of the Water: Fins and Scales

The rule in the water is just as simple. A water creature is clean only if it has both fins and scales. If it lacks either one, it is unclean. This single test sorts nearly everything that swims, and it removes a great deal of guesswork.

The Two Signs for Clean Water Creatures

  • It has fins
  • It has scales

Scriptural References

Leviticus 11:9 These shall ye eat of all that are in the waters: whatsoever hath fins and scales in the waters, in the seas, and in the rivers, them shall ye eat.
Leviticus 11:10 And all that have not fins and scales in the seas, and in the rivers, of all that move in the waters, and of any living thing which is in the waters, they shall be an abomination unto you.
A common surprise: the catfish has fins but no true scales, so it is unclean. The same goes for shellfish such as crawfish, shrimp, crab, lobster, clams, and oysters, none of which have fins and scales.
Part Four

Birds: Named by Kind

For the birds, God gave a list of those that are unclean rather than a pair of signs. The pattern across that list is plain. The birds of prey and the scavengers, the ones that hunt with talons or feed on flesh and carrion, are the unclean ones. The eagle, the hawk, the owl, the vulture, and the raven all belong to that group. The birds that are clean are the ground feeders and grain eaters that have long been kept and hunted for the table, such as the chicken, turkey, dove, quail, and goose.

Scriptural References

Leviticus 11:13 And these are they which ye shall have in abomination among the fowls; they shall not be eaten, they are an abomination: the eagle, and the ossifrage, and the ospray.
Deuteronomy 14:11 Of all clean birds ye shall eat.
Part Five

Insects and Creeping Things

Most flying insects are an abomination, but there is a narrow group that is clean. The flying insects that have jointed legs above their feet for leaping, namely the locust, the grasshopper, and the cricket after their kinds, may be eaten. As for the things that creep on the ground, those that go upon the belly, that go on all fours, or that have many feet, they are unclean. This sweeps in the weasel, the mouse, the lizard, the snail, the mole, and the like.

Scriptural References

Leviticus 11:21–22 Yet these may ye eat of every flying creeping thing that goeth upon all four, which have legs above their feet, to leap withal upon the earth; Even these of them ye may eat; the locust after his kind, and the bald locust after his kind, and the beetle after his kind, and the grasshopper after his kind.
Leviticus 11:42 Whatsoever goeth upon the belly, and whatsoever goeth upon all four, or whatsoever hath more feet among all things that creep upon the earth, them ye shall not eat; for they are an abomination.
Part Six

Set Apart Because He Is Holy

The reason behind all of it is given plainly. God ties the distinction to His own holiness and calls His people to reflect it. To honor what He set apart for food is one small, daily way of remembering whose we are. We do not keep it to be saved; we keep it because we are His, and because we trust the wisdom of the One who made every creature.

Scriptural Reference

Leviticus 11:44 For I am the LORD your God: ye shall therefore sanctify yourselves, and ye shall be holy; for I am holy.
Part Seven

A West Virginia Reference

The charts below apply the signs above to the animals most common to West Virginia fields, forests, waters, and farms. When in doubt about any creature not listed here, return to the signs themselves. For land animals, look for the cloven hoof and the chewing of the cud. For anything from the water, look for both fins and scales.

Land Mammals (Game & Farm)

Clean
  • White-tailed deer
  • Elk
  • Bison
  • Cattle (beef)
  • Sheep (lamb, mutton)
  • Goat
Unclean
  • Wild boar / feral hog swine
  • Domestic pig pork, ham, bacon
  • Black bear
  • Squirrel
  • Rabbit / cottontail
  • Raccoon
  • Opossum
  • Groundhog
  • Beaver
  • Coyote, fox, bobcat
  • Horse

Birds & Fowl

Clean
  • Wild turkey
  • Ruffed grouse
  • Bobwhite quail
  • Ring-necked pheasant
  • Mourning dove
  • Wild duck (mallard, wood duck)
  • Canada goose
  • Chicken
Unclean
  • Hawk
  • Eagle
  • Owl
  • Vulture / buzzard
  • Crow / raven
  • Heron

Fish & Water Creatures

Clean (fins & scales)
  • Trout (brook, rainbow, brown)
  • Bass (largemouth, smallmouth)
  • Bluegill / sunfish
  • Crappie
  • Walleye
  • Northern pike
  • Muskellunge (muskie)
  • Carp
Unclean
  • Catfish no scales
  • American eel no scales
  • Crawfish / crayfish shellfish
  • Frog amphibian
  • Snapping turtle
  • Shrimp, crab, lobster, clam, oyster

Insects & Small Creatures

Clean
  • Grasshopper
  • Locust
  • Cricket
Unclean
  • Most flying insects
  • Mouse / rat
  • Weasel
  • Lizard, salamander
  • Snail, slug
  • Mole
  • Snake
A note on the deer family: deer and elk both carry the two required signs, the cloven hoof and the cud, which is why venison sits firmly on the clean side and has long been a staple of the West Virginia table.
