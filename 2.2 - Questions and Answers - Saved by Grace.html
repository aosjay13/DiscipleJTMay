Discipleship Class · Level 1 · Lesson 2.2

Questions & Answers

Salvation by Grace

✦

Part One

Discipleship Questions

✦ ✦ ✦

Question 1

Read Luke 18:9

And he spake this parable unto certain which trusted in themselves that they were righteous, and despised others:

To whom did Jesus direct this parable? What was their attitude toward other people?

Question 2

Read Luke 18:11–12

The Pharisee stood and prayed thus with himself, God, I thank thee, that I am not as other men are, extortioners, unjust, adulterers, or even as this publican. I fast twice in the week, I give tithes of all that I possess.

Note that fasting and tithing are themselves righteous acts. What then was the Pharisee's specific error? What was he trusting in that kept him from being justified before God?

Question 3

Read Luke 18:13 and 2 Peter 2:21

And the publican, standing afar off, would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but smote upon his breast, saying, God be merciful to me a sinner.

For it had been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them.

What did the publican's prayer show about his heart? Is a single prayer of repentance sufficient on its own, or does genuine repentance require something more ongoing? What does 2 Peter 2:21 teach us about the danger of turning back?

Question 4

Read Romans 3:20 and Luke 18:14

Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.

I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other: for every one that exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted.

According to Romans, why couldn't the Pharisee be justified by his good deeds? Why was the tax collector declared righteous (justified) instead?

Question 5

Read Ephesians 2:8–10

For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

Are we saved by our own works? Once we are saved by grace, what has God ordained that we should walk in? Is this obedience passive or active?

Question 6

Read Romans 6:1–2, James 2:20, and 1 John 5:3

What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein?

But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?

For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.

Does grace give us freedom to continue breaking God's Law? According to 1 John 5:3, what is the love of God? What does James 2:20 say about faith that produces no obedience?

Question 7 · Personal Reflection

Are there areas of your life where you have acted like the Pharisee, pointing to your own religious acts as the basis of your standing before God? How does knowing that you are saved by grace change your motivation for keeping His commandments? And how does the truth that the love of God is the keeping of His commandments (1 John 5:3) shape the way you walk out that obedience daily?

Part Two

Answer Key

✦ ✦ ✦

This answer key is designed to help teachers and group leaders evaluate student responses for Lesson 2, ensuring students grasp not only the free gift of grace but also the active, continuous nature of the obedient life that saving faith produces.

Answer 1 · Luke 18:9

To whom did Jesus direct this parable? What was their attitude toward other people?

Jesus directed this parable to people who "trusted in themselves that they were righteous." Because they believed they were acceptable to God on account of their own behavior, their attitude toward others was arrogant, and they despised those who did not perform as well as they did.

Answer 2 · Luke 18:11–12

Note that fasting and tithing are righteous acts. What then was the Pharisee's specific error?

The Pharisee's error was not that he tithed or fasted. Tithing predates the Mosaic Law, going back to Abraham and Melchizedek (Genesis 14:20), and it is a righteous mandate that continues under the eternal priesthood of Christ. His error was that he used these righteous acts as the grounds for his justification before God, replacing humble dependence on God's mercy with confidence in his own religious record. The act was right; the trust in the act for salvation was the deadly trap.

Answer 3 · Luke 18:13 and 2 Peter 2:21

What did the publican's prayer show? Is a single prayer sufficient, or does genuine repentance require more? What does 2 Peter 2:21 warn against?

The publican's prayer shows the correct posture for approaching God: brokenness, humility, and complete dependence on His mercy, not on one's own merit. However, a single prayer is the beginning of repentance, not the whole of it. Because sin is the transgression of God's Law (1 John 3:4), genuine repentance requires a sustained turning away from lawlessness and a continuous walking in His commandments. Second Peter 2:21 warns that it would have been better never to have known the way of righteousness than to know it and then turn back from it. Students should understand that the publican's humble posture must continue as an ongoing way of life.

Answer 4 · Romans 3:20 and Luke 18:14

Why couldn't the Pharisee be justified by his good deeds? Why was the tax collector declared righteous instead?

The Pharisee could not be justified by his deeds because the purpose of the Law is to give us the knowledge of sin, showing us our need for a Savior, not to serve as a record of achievement that earns us favor with God. No flesh can be justified by the deeds of the law. The tax collector was justified because he humbled himself, recognized his own sinfulness, and cast himself entirely on the mercy and grace of God.

Answer 5 · Ephesians 2:8–10

Are we saved by our own works? What has God ordained that we should walk in? Is this obedience passive or active?

No, we are not saved by our own works. Salvation is the free gift of God received through faith. However, once we are saved by grace, we become His workmanship, and He has ordained that we should walk in good works, that is, in obedience to His commandments. This obedience is active and ongoing. James 1:22 commands us to be doers of the Word, not merely hearers. Students should understand that empowerment is the means, not the excuse. Grace empowers active, sustained obedience.

Answer 6 · Romans 6:1–2, James 2:20, and 1 John 5:3

Does grace give freedom to continue in sin? What is the love of God? What does James say about faith without obedience?

"God forbid!" Grace is absolutely not a license to continue breaking God's Law. The love of God, according to 1 John 5:3, is the keeping of His commandments. It is not merely a feeling that may eventually produce obedience; it is obedience itself. James 2:20 states plainly that faith without works is dead. Students should see that grace, love, and faith are all inseparable from active obedience to God's commandments.

Answer 7 · Personal Reflection

Pointing to religious acts as the basis of standing; motivation changed by grace; love of God as the keeping of His commandments shaping daily life.

This is a personal, open-ended response. Look for the student to distinguish between pointing to their works for justification (the Pharisee's error) and performing righteous works out of love as genuine fruit of faith. Their motivation should shift from pride and self-reliance to gratitude and love. A strong answer will also connect 1 John 5:3 to their daily practice, recognizing that the love they feel toward God is expressed concretely in walking in His commandments, not as a burden but as the natural overflow of a heart that has received His grace.
