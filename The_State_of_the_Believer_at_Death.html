The State of the Believer at Death

Sleep of the Body, Presence With Christ

✦

Preamble

What happens to a believer the moment he closes his eyes in death? Scripture gives two sets of language on this question. In one place death is called sleep, and the dead are said to know nothing. In another place death is called departing to be with Christ, and the dead are described as conscious and alive unto God. Both sets of language are inspired, and both are true. This study lays them side by side, lets each one speak plainly, and then shows how they fit together into a single, whole picture of what happens to a child of God at the hour of death.

I.

Two Descriptions, One Man

A man is not one simple thing. He is a body formed of dust and a spirit breathed into him by God, joined together as one living soul. Genesis tells us plainly how this union was made, and it is this same union that comes apart at death. The body returns to the ground it came from. The spirit returns to God who gave it. Keeping this two-part picture in view is what makes sense of every verse that follows, because the sleep language and the presence language are not arguing with each other. They are describing two different halves of the same man.

Scriptural References

Genesis 2:7 And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.
Ecclesiastes 12:7 Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it.
II.

The Body Sleeps

Scripture speaks of death again and again as sleep. This is not a soft way of avoiding the word death. It is a description of the body, laid in the ground, still and resting, waiting for a day when it will be raised. Jesus used this very word for Lazarus, and had to correct the disciples' confusion by telling them plainly that Lazarus was dead. Paul comforts grieving believers by pointing them to the resurrection of those who sleep, not by pointing them somewhere else entirely. The whole hope tied to this word is bodily and future. Nobody is described as waking from this sleep until the trumpet sounds and the dead are raised.

Scriptural References

John 11:11, 14 Our friend Lazarus sleepeth; but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep... Then said Jesus unto them plainly, Lazarus is dead.
1 Thessalonians 4:13–16 I would not have you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning them which are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others which have no hope... the dead in Christ shall rise first.
1 Corinthians 15:51–52 We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed, in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible.
Daniel 12:2 And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt.
Ecclesiastes 9:5, 10 For the living know that they shall die: but the dead know not any thing... there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave, whither thou goest.
Teacher's Note Ecclesiastes is written from the vantage point of a man watching life "under the sun." Read in that light, chapter 9 is describing what the living can observe of the dead, not making a claim about where the spirit has gone. The same book tells us plainly in its final chapter that the spirit returns to God at death, which is not a description of silence or nothingness.
III.

The Spirit Departs Into His Presence

Alongside the sleep language stands a second set of statements that are just as plain, and they speak directly to what happens the moment a believer dies. Jesus promised the dying thief a place with Him that very day. Paul weighed staying alive against departing, and called departing to be with Christ the better of the two, not a blank pause between them. He told the Corinthians outright that to be absent from the body is to be present with the Lord, with nothing between those two states. And Jesus Himself, answering the Sadducees, declared that the patriarchs who had died centuries before were still living unto God. These are not poetic guesses. They are direct teaching, some of it from the mouth of Christ.

Scriptural References

Luke 23:43 And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise.
Philippians 1:21, 23 For me to live is Christ, and to die is gain... for I am in a strait betwixt two, having a desire to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better.
2 Corinthians 5:6, 8 Therefore we are always confident, knowing that, whilst we are at home in the body, we are absent from the Lord... we are confident, I say, and willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord.
Luke 20:37–38 Now that the dead are raised, even Moses shewed at the bush, when he calleth the Lord the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob. For he is not a God of the dead, but of the living: for all live unto him.
Matthew 17:2–3 And was transfigured before them... And, behold, there appeared unto them Moses and Elias talking with him.
Revelation 6:9–11 I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the testimony which they held: and they cried with a loud voice, saying, How long, O Lord... and it was said unto them, that they should rest yet for a little season.
Teacher's Note Some have tried to move the comma in Luke 23:43 so that Jesus is only saying "I tell you this today" rather than promising the thief presence with Him that day. This is not the natural reading of the sentence, and it should not be leaned on to answer the plain sense of the verse.
IV.

One Man, Two Halves, No Contradiction

Put together, the two sets of verses are not opposing witnesses. They are describing the two parts of a man that Genesis and Ecclesiastes already told us come apart at death. The body is laid down, and scripture rightly calls that sleep, because the body will rest exactly where it was laid until the day it is raised incorruptible at the last trump. Nothing in scripture ever describes a body as conscious or aware in the grave, and nothing needs to, because it is the spirit that carries the man's life and awareness. The spirit does not go into the ground with the body. It returns to God who gave it, and for the believer in Christ, that return is a return into blessed and conscious presence with the Lord, exactly as Jesus promised the thief and exactly as Paul described his own longing to depart.

This is why a believer can be told at the very same time that he sleeps and that he is present with the Lord, without any contradiction at all. The sleeping is the body's, held in the ground in patient hope of the resurrection. The presence is the spirit's, already at rest with Christ. And the story is not finished until the two are brought back together. At the resurrection, the body is raised and glorified, and reunited with the spirit that has been with the Lord all along. That reunion, not the waiting in between, is the full and final hope scripture is pointing every believer toward.

✦ Bonus

Paul's own words in 2 Corinthians 12:2–4 are worth noticing here. He describes being caught up into paradise, and says plainly that he did not know whether this happened in the body or out of the body. That a man inspired of God could even speak of being "out of the body" as a real and conscious experience tells us that such a state is not a foreign idea to scripture. It is the same paradise Jesus promised the thief, and the same presence Paul later called far better than remaining in this life.

V.

Raised Incorruptible, Caught Up Together

First Corinthians 15 tells us exactly what happens to the body that was sown in the ground. Paul pictures it as a seed. It is sown in weakness, in dishonor, in corruption, a natural body, the very same body that returned to dust. It is raised in power, in glory, incorruptible, a spiritual body fit for eternity. This is not a different body replacing the old one. It is the same body, sown one way and raised another, changed at the sound of the last trump.

This is the moment the two halves of the man come back together. The spirit, already with Christ, is reunited with the body, now raised and glorified out of the ground. Paul describes this same gathering in his letter to the Thessalonians, where the dead in Christ rise first, and then those still living are caught up together with them to meet the Lord in the air. The rising of the body and the meeting in the air belong to one and the same event. The grave gives up what it was holding, the spirit returns with it, and together they are caught up to meet the Lord.

Scriptural References

1 Corinthians 15:42–44 So also is the resurrection of the dead. It is sown in corruption; it is raised in incorruption: It is sown in dishonour; it is raised in glory: it is sown in weakness; it is raised in power: It is sown a natural body; it is raised a spiritual body.
1 Corinthians 15:53–54 For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality... then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory.
1 Thessalonians 4:16–17 For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord.
Teacher's Note First Corinthians 15 and First Thessalonians 4 are two parts of one picture. Corinthians tells us what happens to the body sown in the ground. Thessalonians tells us where that rising leads, a meeting with the Lord in the air. Read together, they show a body raised incorruptible and a spirit brought with it, joined once more into a whole man fit for glory.
