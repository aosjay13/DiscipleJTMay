A Core Pronomian Foundation

Christ the Fulfillment of the Law

How Jesus completed the sacrificial and priestly shadows once for all, while the moral standard He upheld remains binding on the believer

✦

The Word That Confuses So Many

Of all the words Jesus spoke about the law, one has been twisted more than any other. He said He came to fulfil the law. Many hear that word and assume it means He came to finish the law off, to bring it to an end, to clear it away so that nothing of it remains. But Jesus said the opposite in the very same breath. He said He did not come to destroy the law, and then He said not one jot or tittle would pass from it. So whatever fulfil means, it cannot mean abolish, because the One who spoke it ruled out abolishing in the same sentence.

The confusion comes from treating the law as one undivided block. If the whole law is one thing, then either all of it stays or all of it goes, and the word fulfil seems to make it all go. But we have already seen that the law is not one undivided block. There is the sacrificial and priestly system that pointed forward to Christ, and there is the moral standard that flows from the unchanging character of God. Christ fulfilled these in two very different ways, and seeing the difference unlocks the whole question.

This teaching shows what fulfillment actually means and how Christ fulfilled each part of the law according to its purpose. He fulfilled the sacrifices by becoming the one true sacrifice, ending them forever. He fulfilled the priesthood by becoming our great High Priest. And He fulfilled the moral law not by ending it but by keeping it perfectly, embodying it, and writing it on the hearts of His people. The shadows are completed in Him. The standard is upheld by Him. Both are the work of the same Lord.

Part One

What Fulfill Actually Means

We must start with the word itself, because everything turns on it. To fulfil something is to fill it full, to bring it to its intended completeness, to make it what it was always meant to be. A promise is fulfilled when the thing promised arrives. A prophecy is fulfilled when the event it foretold comes to pass. In neither case does fulfillment mean the promise was bad or the prophecy is now to be ignored. Fulfillment means the purpose has been reached.

Matthew 5:17–18 Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.

Read the words of Jesus with care and the meaning is plain. He sets destroy and fulfil against each other as opposites. He is not come to destroy, He is come to fulfil. If fulfil meant abolish, He would be saying He came not to abolish but to abolish, which is nonsense. Then He guards the law even more strongly. Not one jot, the smallest letter, not one tittle, the smallest stroke of a letter, will pass away. The law stands firm until all is brought to its appointed completion.

Matthew 5:19 Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven: but whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.

Notice what Jesus does in the very next verse. Far from clearing the commandments away, He warns against breaking even the least of them and teaching others to do the same. He honors those who do and teach the commandments. This is not the speech of a man abolishing the law. This is the speech of a man establishing it, lifting it up, and setting it in its proper place under His own authority. So when we read that Christ fulfilled the law, we must let Him define the word, and He has defined it as the opposite of destruction.

Fulfill Is Not Abolish

To fulfil is to fill full, to bring to intended completeness, to make a thing what it was always meant to be. Jesus sets fulfil against destroy as its opposite, and in the same breath protects the smallest letter of the law. Whatever fulfillment means, it cannot mean the law is cleared away, for the One who spoke it forbade that very reading.

Part Two

Christ Fulfilled the Sacrifices by Becoming the Sacrifice

Now we come to the first kind of fulfillment, and it is the one that does bring a part of the law to its end. The sacrifices of the old order were never the real thing. They were pictures, drawn in the blood of animals, of a sacrifice yet to come. Every lamb laid on the altar pointed forward to the Lamb of God. When the true Lamb came and was offered, the pictures had served their purpose, and the offering of animals was finished forever.

John 1:29 The next day John seeth Jesus coming unto him, and saith, Behold the Lamb of God, which taketh away the sin of the world.

From the very beginning of His ministry, Christ is named the Lamb of God. The whole sacrificial system had been crying out for a lamb that could actually take away sin, because the blood of bulls and goats never could. When John points to Jesus and calls Him the Lamb of God, he is announcing that the substance has arrived. The thousands of lambs offered through the centuries were all looking ahead to this one.

Hebrews 10:11–14 And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God; From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool. For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.

Here the matter is settled with finality. The old priests stood daily, offering the same sacrifices over and over, and those sacrifices could never take away sin. But Christ offered one sacrifice for sins forever, and then He sat down. The sitting down is the proof that the work is done. A priest never sat in the old order, for his work was never finished. Christ sat because His one offering perfected forever those it sanctifies. There is nothing left to offer. To bring back the altar would be to deny that the Lamb has come.

Hebrews 9:25–26 Nor yet that he should offer himself often, as the high priest entereth into the holy place every year with blood of others; For then must he often have suffered since the foundation of the world: but now once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself.

The Spirit guards us against any thought of repeating the sacrifice. Christ did not offer Himself again and again. He appeared once, at the climax of the ages, to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself. This is fulfillment in the fullest sense. The sacrifices reached their purpose, poured all their meaning into the cross, and ceased. This part of the law is completed and closed, not because it failed, but because it succeeded perfectly in pointing to Christ.

The Sacrifices Are Finished

The animal sacrifices were shadows of the Lamb of God. When Christ offered Himself once for all and sat down, the shadows had reached their purpose and ended forever. No sacrifice is ever offered again, for the substance has come. To rebuild the altar would deny the finished work of the cross.

Part Three

Christ Fulfilled the Priesthood by Becoming Our High Priest

Bound up with the sacrifices was the priesthood, and it too is fulfilled in Christ. Someone had to offer the sacrifices, and under the old order that someone was a son of Aaron, a man who was himself a sinner and who died and had to be replaced. The whole Aaronic priesthood was another shadow, pointing to a Priest who would never sin and never die, a Priest of a higher order altogether.

Hebrews 7:23–25 And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death: But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood. Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.

See the difference between the old priests and the true Priest. The old priests were many, because death kept removing them. Christ holds an unchangeable priesthood, because He lives forever. The old priests could offer a sacrifice, but they could not keep the worshipper saved, for they themselves passed away. Christ saves to the uttermost and ever lives to make intercession. The shadow of a passing priesthood is fulfilled in the substance of a Priest who never passes away.

Hebrews 7:11–12 If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood, (for under it the people received the law,) what further need was there that another priest should rise after the order of Melchisedec, and not be called after the order of Aaron? For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law.

This passage is important, and it must be read carefully so it is not stretched too far. It says that when the priesthood changed, the law concerning the priesthood changed with it. This is exactly right. The rules that governed who could be a priest, how he was consecrated, and how he served had to change, because the Priest is now of the order of Melchizedek and not of Aaron. But notice what kind of law is being changed. It is the priestly law, the law tied to the office of priest. The change of priesthood changes the priestly regulations. It does not reach over and abolish the command not to murder or to keep the day of the Lord holy. The law that changed is the law that governed the very thing being fulfilled.

Teacher’s Note

Hebrews 7:12 is often pulled far out of its setting to argue that the entire law was changed or abolished. Keep it anchored. The whole chapter is about the priesthood, Aaron compared to Melchizedek. The change of law it speaks of is the change in the priestly regulations that necessarily follows when the priesthood itself changes. Read the verse before it and the verse after it and the subject never leaves the priesthood.

State it simply for a class: when the Priest changed from Aaron to Christ, the priestly rules changed with Him. That is what the passage says. It does not say the moral standard of God was set aside, and the same book that says this also says God writes His laws on our hearts.

The Priesthood Fulfilled in Christ

The Aaronic priesthood was a shadow of a perfect and unending Priest. Christ fulfills it as our great High Priest after the order of Melchizedek, who lives forever and saves to the uttermost. The priestly law changed because the Priest changed. This is fulfillment of the office, not abolition of the moral standard.

Part Four

Christ Fulfilled the Moral Law by Upholding It

Now we come to the other kind of fulfillment, and it is completely different from the first. Christ fulfilled the sacrifices by ending them, because their purpose was to point to Him. But Christ fulfilled the moral law in the opposite way, not by ending it but by keeping it perfectly, teaching it truly, and writing it on the hearts of His people. The moral law could not be fulfilled by being set aside, because its purpose was never to point forward to something else. Its purpose is to reveal the righteousness of God, and that purpose never expires.

Matthew 5:21–22 Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not kill; and whosoever shall kill shall be in danger of the judgment: But I say unto you, That whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment. Matthew 5:27–28 Ye have heard that it was said by them of old time, Thou shalt not commit adultery: But I say unto you, That whosoever looketh on a woman to lust after her hath committed adultery with her already in his heart.

Watch what Jesus does with the moral commandments in His great sermon. He does not lower them. He raises them. He takes the command against murder and drives it down into the heart, to the anger that gives birth to murder. He takes the command against adultery and drives it into the lustful look. This is fulfillment as filling full. He fills the commandments out to their deepest meaning, showing that they reach the very thoughts of the heart and not only the outward act. A man does not abolish a law by deepening it. He establishes it more firmly than ever.

Romans 8:3–4 For what the law could not do, in that it was weak through the flesh, God sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and for sin, condemned sin in the flesh: That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.

Here is the deepest part of fulfillment, and it answers everything. The law could not make us righteous, not because the law was weak, but because our flesh was weak and could not keep it. So God sent His Son to do what we could not, and the purpose was stated plainly. He did it so that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us. Not abolished in us, fulfilled in us. The very righteousness the moral law describes is now produced in the believer who walks after the Spirit. Christ keeps the law perfectly, and then by His Spirit He causes that same law to be lived out in His people.

Hebrews 10:16 This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days, saith the Lord, I will put my laws into their hearts, and in their minds will I write them.

The same book that tells us the sacrifices are finished also tells us the moral law is written on the heart. This is no contradiction. It is the two kinds of fulfillment side by side. The sacrifices ended because their work was done. The moral law was written inward because its work goes on forever. One book, one Lord, two different fulfillments, each according to the nature of the thing fulfilled.

Fulfilled by Completion

The sacrifices and the Aaronic priesthood. These pointed forward to Christ. He fulfilled them by becoming the Lamb and the High Priest, and so they reached their purpose and ended forever. Their fulfillment is their completion.

Fulfilled by Upholding

The moral law. This reveals the righteousness of God and points to no future replacement. Christ fulfilled it by keeping it perfectly, deepening it, and writing it on the heart. Its fulfillment is its establishment.

One Word, Two Meanings, No Confusion

The whole knot is untied once you see that the single word fulfil works two ways, depending on what is being fulfilled. A shadow is fulfilled when the substance comes and the shadow is no longer needed. A standard is fulfilled when it is perfectly kept and lived out. The sacrifices were shadows, so their fulfillment ended them. The moral law is a standard, so its fulfillment established it. The same Christ did both, and there is no contradiction in it at all.

This is why we must never reason from the finished sacrifices to a finished moral law. They are fulfilled in opposite ways. To say the Lamb has come, therefore do not steal anymore is a sound sentence. To say the Lamb has come, therefore stealing is now permitted is a foolish one. The cross ends the altar. It does not open the door to sin.

The Whole Counsel

The Shadows Completed, the Standard Upheld

Bring it all together and the picture of Christ and the law stands clear and whole. Jesus said He came to fulfil the law, and He told us plainly that fulfil does not mean destroy. He then fulfilled the law in two ways, each fitting the part He was fulfilling. The sacrifices and the priesthood were shadows that pointed to Him, and He fulfilled them by becoming the true Lamb and the true High Priest, offering Himself once for all and sitting down, so that these shadows are completed and closed forever. We never rebuild the altar, because the substance has come.

The moral law was no shadow. It is the revelation of the righteousness of God, and Christ fulfilled it not by ending it but by keeping it perfectly, by deepening it to the thoughts of the heart, and by writing it upon the hearts of His people through the Spirit. So the believer stands in the good of both fulfillments at once. His sins are covered by the one finished sacrifice, and his heart is being shaped by the moral law that Christ upheld and now writes within him. The shadows are completed in Christ. The standard is upheld by Christ. Both are the work of the one Lord who came not to destroy but to fulfil.

Where It All Rests

Christ fulfilled the sacrifices by becoming the sacrifice, and they are finished. Christ fulfilled the priesthood by becoming the Priest, and the priestly law changed with Him. Christ fulfilled the moral law by keeping it, deepening it, and writing it on the heart, and so it stands more firmly than ever. Fulfillment ended the shadows and established the standard, by the hand of the same Lord.

Do not let the finished altar become an excuse to break the abiding commandments. The cross that ended the sacrifices is the same cross that gives us a new heart to keep the law of love.

Matthew 5:17 Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. Romans 8:4 That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.
