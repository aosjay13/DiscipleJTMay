A Doctrinal Answer

The Two Covenants and the Yoke of Bondage

What Galatians actually says, and what it never said

✦

The Claim Before Us

No book of Scripture is used more often to argue that God’s commandments have been abolished than the letter to the Galatians. The verses are gathered quickly, Hagar and Sarah, the two covenants, the yoke of bondage, the debtor to the whole law, fallen from grace, and the conclusion is announced before the letter has been read. The law is done away, we are told, and anyone who keeps it has left grace behind.

We answer that claim here, and we answer it from the same letter. Not by softening Paul, not by explaining away his hardest words, but by asking the one question that unlocks every line of it. What was the argument in Galatia? Once that question is answered, the letter reads plainly, it agrees with the rest of Scripture, and it agrees with Paul himself.

The short answer is this. Paul is not fighting men who kept God’s commandments. He is fighting men who taught that a Gentile had to be circumcised and become a proselyte in order to be saved. That is the false gospel he destroys, and he destroys it utterly. But a letter written against earning salvation cannot be turned into a letter against obeying God, because the very same letter commands obedience, names sin by the law’s own definition, and warns that those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom.

Part One

The Question the Letter Was Written to Settle

Every letter answers a dispute. Read a letter without knowing the dispute and you will misread it every time. Galatians tells us its dispute in plain words, and it is not whether believers should keep the Sabbath, avoid unclean meat, or honor the feasts of the LORD. It is whether a man is justified before God by faith in Christ or by becoming a son of Abraham through the flesh.

Galatians 2:16 Knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Jesus Christ, even we have believed in Jesus Christ, that we might be justified by the faith of Christ, and not by the works of the law: for by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified. Galatians 3:2–3 This only would I learn of you, Received ye the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith? Are ye so foolish? having begun in the Spirit, are ye now made perfect by the flesh?

Notice the words carefully. Justified. Made perfect. Received the Spirit. Every one of them concerns how a sinner comes to stand accepted before God. Paul never asks whether the Galatians should honor what God commanded. He asks how they were made righteous, and he answers by faith, apart from any work of their own. The same dispute is recorded in the book of Acts, and it is stated there in one sentence.

Acts 15:1 And certain men which came down from Judaea taught the brethren, and said, Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved. Acts 15:5 But there rose up certain of the sect of the Pharisees which believed, saying, That it was needful to circumcise them, and to command them to keep the law to be saved.

There is the whole controversy in six words at the end of each verse. Cannot be saved. To be saved. The error was never obedience. The error was obedience presented as the price of salvation. That is what Paul calls another gospel, and that is what he curses, because it takes the finished work of Christ and adds a payment to it.

The Hinge of the Whole Letter

Galatians is a letter about the mechanism of justification, not about the standard of righteousness. It answers the question, how does a sinner become accepted by God? It never asks, may a believer now break the commandments of God? Confuse those two questions and the letter will seem to say what it never said.

Part Two

The Allegory of the Two Sons

The Claim

“The two covenants are the law and grace. Sinai is bondage, the Jerusalem above is freedom, and the bondwoman with her son must be cast out. So the law is cast out with her.”

The AnswerRead what the allegory is actually about, and read the verse the objection usually skips. Paul is comparing two ways of producing a son of Abraham, not two ways of defining right and wrong. Abraham was given a promise. He grew impatient, and by the strength of his own flesh he produced Ishmael through the bondmaid. Then, when he could do nothing at all, God produced Isaac by promise. Two sons, two mothers, and two entirely different mechanisms.

Galatians 4:22–24 For it is written, that Abraham had two sons, the one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman. But he who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh; but he of the freewoman was by promise. Which things are an allegory: for these are the two covenants; the one from the mount Sinai, which gendereth to bondage, which is Agar.

The dividing line Paul draws is flesh against promise. Ishmael represents what a man produces by his own effort in order to obtain what God has promised. That is the exact error of the Judaizers. They were telling Gentile believers to secure their sonship by a work of the flesh, circumcision, rather than receiving it by promise through faith. The bondage is not the commandment. The bondage is the attempt to generate sonship out of yourself.

And notice which mother Hagar answers to. Paul does not say she is the law. He identifies her with a city and a people.

Galatians 4:25 For this Agar is mount Sinai in Arabia, and answereth to Jerusalem which now is, and is in bondage with her children.
Galatians 4:26 But Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all. Galatians 4:30 Nevertheless what saith the scripture? Cast out the bondwoman and her son: for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the freewoman.

Hagar answers to the Jerusalem of that hour, the religious system that was insisting on the flesh, the very system sending men into Galatia to unsettle the churches. Those are the children in bondage. The command to cast out the bondwoman and her son is a command to expel that false teaching from the congregation, not a command to throw out the words God spoke with His own voice from the mountain.

Teacher’s Note

Take care with allegory. Paul says outright that he is speaking allegorically, and an allegory is given to illustrate one point, not to be pressed at every corner. The point here is stated in the text itself, flesh against promise. Do not let anyone stretch the picture until Hagar becomes the Ten Commandments, because Paul never makes that identification. He identifies her with a covenant arrangement and with a city, and he tells us the difference between the two sons is how each was produced.

There is one more thing worth seeing. Sarah is not lawless. Isaac was circumcised on the eighth day, exactly as God commanded. The freewoman’s household kept the word of God. Freedom in this allegory never meant liberty from what God said. It meant that the son came by promise rather than by human contrivance.

Part Three

The Yoke of Bondage Identified

The Claim

“Stand fast in liberty and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage. The commandments are that yoke, so keeping them is entanglement.”

The AnswerLet the next verse define the yoke. Paul does not leave it to our imagination. The moment after he warns against the yoke, he names it.

Galatians 5:1–3 Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage. Behold, I Paul say unto you, that if ye be circumcised, Christ shall profit you nothing. For I testify again to every man that is circumcised, that he is a debtor to do the whole law.

The yoke is circumcision received as the ground of acceptance with God. That is the entanglement, and Paul spells out why it is deadly. A man who submits to circumcision in order to be justified has stepped onto a road with no mercy at the end of it. He has chosen to be measured by his own performance, and so he is now a debtor to do the whole law, perfectly, without a single failure, forever. No man has ever paid that debt. That is bondage in the truest sense, an obligation you have taken on and can never discharge.

Understand what Paul is not saying. He is not saying that circumcision is now a sin. He circumcised Timothy himself. He is not saying that the law is evil, for he says elsewhere that it is holy, just and good. He is saying that the moment you make any work of your own the basis of your standing, you have taken up a debt that will crush you, and you have set aside the only One who could pay it.

Acts 15:10 Now therefore why tempt ye God, to put a yoke upon the neck of the disciples, which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear? Acts 21:24 Them take, and purify thyself with them, and be at charges with them, that they may shave their heads: and all may know that those things, whereof they were informed concerning thee, are nothing; but that thou thyself also walkest orderly, and keepest the law.

Set those two verses side by side and the matter is settled. Peter calls the demand of the Pharisees a yoke none could bear, and it is the demand described in verse five, circumcision and law keeping in order to be saved. Then years later, at Jerusalem, Paul publicly proves that he himself walks orderly and keeps the law. If Paul had taught that God’s commandments were abolished, that scene is impossible, and Paul is a hypocrite. He was not. He preached free justification and he lived an obedient life, and he saw no contradiction between them.

Two Different Yokes

Scripture speaks of more than one yoke, and they must never be confused. There is the yoke of law-keeping offered as payment for salvation, a burden no man could bear. And there is the yoke the Master Himself offers, which He calls easy, and the burden He calls light. Jesus said, take my yoke upon you, and learn of me. A yoke is not evil in itself. Everything depends on whose it is and what it is for.

The believer is not free from the Master. He is free from the impossible debt, free from condemnation, and free from the delusion that he could ever have earned his own way. That freedom is real, and it is why he now walks gladly in the ways of the One who bought him.

Part Four

Fallen From Grace, and Why It Proves the Opposite

The Claim

“Whosoever of you are justified by the law, ye are fallen from grace. So anyone who keeps the law has fallen from grace.”

The AnswerRead the verse as written, and do not remove the middle of it. Paul does not say, whosoever of you keeps the law. He says, whosoever of you are justified by the law. The condition is the source of a man’s justification, not the manner of his walk.

Galatians 5:4 Christ is become of no effect unto you, whosoever of you are justified by the law; ye are fallen from grace.

Two things cannot both be the ground of your standing. If Christ is your righteousness, you are standing in grace. If your own record is your righteousness, then Christ has been made of no effect to you, because you have declared that you did not need what He accomplished. That is the fall Paul describes. It is a fall from a mechanism, from grace as the means, into works as the means. It has nothing to do with a man who obeys God out of a heart that has already been saved.

And here is the plainest proof of all. In the same letter, only a few lines further, Paul tells us what actually matters, and it is not what an antinomian reading would expect.

Galatians 5:6 For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love. 1 Corinthians 7:19 Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God.

The same writer, the same argument, the same phrase, and then the plain conclusion. Circumcision is nothing, uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of the commandments of God. If Paul believed the commandments were abolished, that sentence could never have left his pen. He sets aside a fleshly badge and lifts up obedience in the very same breath.

Part Five

The Rest of Galatians Undoes the Argument

If Galatians abolished God’s law, then Galatians should be a letter free of commandments, free of sin lists, and free of warnings. It is nothing of the kind. Follow it through and you will find that Paul, having secured justification by faith alone, immediately turns and calls the Galatians to a holy walk defined by the law itself.

  1. Liberty is guarded against abuse at once. “For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another” (Galatians 5:13). Paul saw exactly where careless men would take his words, and he closed the door in the same paragraph.
  2. The law is quoted as the standard of that love. “For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself” (Galatians 5:14). He does not say the law is fulfilled and therefore finished. He says love is how it is carried out, and he proves it by quoting Leviticus.
  3. Sin is named by the law’s own definitions. The works of the flesh in Galatians 5:19–21 are adultery, fornication, idolatry, witchcraft, murders, and the rest, every one of them forbidden by the commandments of God. Paul could not have listed them if the standard defining them had been erased.
  4. The warning is severe and it is aimed at professing believers. “They which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God” (Galatians 5:21). That is not the language of a man who thinks conduct no longer matters.
  5. The fruit of the Spirit is exactly what the law always described. “Against such there is no law” (Galatians 5:23). The Spirit does not produce a life at odds with God’s commandments. He produces the very life those commandments called for.
  6. Paul denies the false conclusion outright. “Is the law then against the promises of God? God forbid” (Galatians 3:21). The strongest denial in his vocabulary is spent defending the law against the very charge men now use his letter to make.
  7. He still speaks of a law to be fulfilled. “Bear ye one another’s burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ” (Galatians 6:2). A man who had abolished all law does not close his letter telling the church to fulfil one.

This is the ruin of the antinomian reading. It requires Paul to abolish the law in chapter four and then enforce it in chapter five. No honest reading of a single short letter can bear that. The only reading that holds the whole thing together is the one Paul gives us himself. Justification comes by faith alone, and the justified man walks in the commandments of God by the Spirit.

Part Six

What Actually Changed Between the Covenants

Something real did change, and we must not pretend otherwise. There are two covenants, and the second is better than the first. But hear what Scripture says the change consisted of, because it is not what most assume.

Jeremiah 31:33 But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the LORD, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people. Hebrews 8:10 For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people.

Read that promise slowly, because it is the death of the argument we are answering. The new covenant does not remove God’s law. It relocates it. The same law that was written on tablets of stone is now written on the heart. If the law had been abolished, the new covenant promise would be empty, for there would be nothing left to write. What changed was not the standard but the place it is kept and the power by which it is kept.

Ezekiel 36:26–27 A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.

Here is the whole difference between Hagar and Sarah applied to the believer. The old arrangement said, do this and live, and men tried to produce righteousness by their own strength, which is Ishmael. The new covenant says, I will give you a new heart, I will put my Spirit within you, and I will cause you to walk in my statutes. That is Isaac. God Himself produces the obedience He requires. It is not a lower standard, it is a greater work.

What did pass away was the priesthood and the sacrificial system that served it, and Scripture says so directly, because those things were shadows pointing to a work that has now been finished once for all. The blood of bulls and goats never took away sin, and it is no longer offered, because the Lamb of God was offered once and forever. But the moral commandments of God, the Sabbath He sanctified at creation, the appointed times He called His own, and the distinctions He drew between clean and unclean were never the shadow of a sacrifice, and no verse anywhere revokes them.

Teacher’s Note

When you teach this, keep the two questions on separate shelves and never let them slide together. What saves a man, and how does a saved man live. Almost every misreading of Galatians comes from an answer given to the first question being applied to the second. If a listener starts using the word law without saying which use he means, stop and ask him. Half of these arguments dissolve the moment the terms are defined.

Part Seven

The Witness of the Whole Bible

A doctrine drawn from a few lines of one letter must be tested against everything else God has said. When we do that here, the antinomian reading collapses, because the rest of Scripture speaks with one voice and it says the opposite.

Matthew 5:17–19 Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled. Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven: but whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven. Romans 3:31 Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law. Romans 7:12 Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.

Jesus anticipated the exact thought and forbade it before it could take root. Think not that I am come to destroy. He then attaches a consequence to teaching otherwise, and the consequence falls on the teacher as much as on the one who breaks. Paul, who wrote Galatians, asks whether faith makes the law void and answers with the strongest denial he has. Then he calls the law holy, just and good. These are not the words of a man who abolished it.

1 John 3:4 Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law. 1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous. Revelation 14:12 Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.

Follow the logic to its end. Sin is the transgression of the law. Remove the law and you have removed the definition of sin, and if there is no sin there is nothing for Christ to have died for. The cross itself requires a standing law, for it was the law’s just penalty that He bore. To abolish the law is to empty the crucifixion of its meaning. And at the very close of the Bible, when the saints are described in the last days, they are identified by two marks held together, the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. Not one or the other. Both.

Where the Reading Leads

Doctrine is tested by where it goes. Ask the man who says the commandments are abolished which ones he still holds. He will almost always keep nine of the ten. He will say murder is sin, theft is sin, adultery is sin, idolatry is sin. Then ask him where Scripture gave him permission to keep nine and discard one. He will not find the verse, because it does not exist. What he has actually done is keep the law he already agreed with and set aside the one that costs him something.

That is not a doctrine drawn from the text. It is a preference dressed in a verse. Scripture never divides God’s commandments into the convenient and the discarded, and a reading that requires such a division has already answered itself.

The Whole Counsel

Free Indeed, and Walking in His Ways

Bring it all together and Galatians stands exactly where it always stood, as one of the strongest defenses of grace ever written, and no enemy of God’s commandments at all. Paul fought a specific lie, that a man must add a work of the flesh to the finished work of Christ in order to be saved. He destroyed that lie completely, and we stand with him against it without one word of hesitation. No sinner is justified by keeping commandments. Not one, not ever.

But the man who is justified freely by the blood of Jesus has been given a new heart, and upon that heart God writes His law with His own hand. He does not obey to become a son, he obeys because he has been made one. Hagar represents the flesh straining to produce what only promise can give. Sarah represents the promise received by faith. And the son of the freewoman, born by promise, was raised in a household that kept the word of God, because freedom was never freedom from His voice.

So when someone hands you Galatians and says the commandments are done away, hand it back opened to chapter five and ask what the works of the flesh are, who will not inherit the kingdom, and why Paul quotes Leviticus to define love. Ask what a man is a debtor to do, and why. The letter answers for itself.

Where It All Rests

The yoke of bondage is the attempt to be justified by your own record, a debt no man can pay. Liberty is standing in the finished work of Christ, forgiven, adopted, and free from condemnation. That liberty was never license, and Paul said so in the same breath he announced it.

We are saved by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone. And the grace that saved us writes the law of God upon the heart and teaches us to walk in it. Cast out the bondwoman, meaning cast out every teaching that would make your salvation a wage. Then walk as a son in your Father’s house, keeping His commandments, not to be loved, but because you are.
