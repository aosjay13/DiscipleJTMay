A Discipleship Teaching

When the Trials Overtake Me

Believing, Trusting, and Resting in God in the Midst of the Storm

✦

Introduction

There comes a season in every life when the troubles do not arrive one at a time, but seem to rush in all at once. The bills, the body, the family, the fears, all of it pressing down until a person feels the waters rising over his head. In those hours the question is not really whether God is good. We have sung that and said it for years. The question that rises up in the dark is whether we will keep believing it when everything we can see seems to argue against it.

This teaching is for that hour. It is about believing in God, trusting Him, and leaning the whole weight of our lives upon Him, not only when the skies are clear, but when the storm is at its worst. We will look at men in Scripture who were overtaken by trials far heavier than most of us will ever carry, and we will see that their faith did not rest on their circumstances. It rested on the unchanging character of God. The Lord never promised His people a life without storms. He promised something better, that He would be with them in the storm, and that He would carry them through.

Section I

Let Not Your Heart Be Troubled

On the night before He went to the cross, the Lord knew His disciples were about to be overtaken by the greatest trial of their lives. Their world was about to come apart. So He gave them a command, and it is a command we need to hear in our own troubles.

John 14:1 Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me.

Notice that He says let not. A troubled heart is something we are told we can refuse. He does not say the trouble will never come knocking. He says we do not have to open the door of our heart and let it move in and take over. And the way we keep it out is by believing. "Ye believe in God, believe also in me." Faith is the foundation, and trust is faith leaning its full weight on the One it believes in.

This is where the whole Christian life begins. We are saved by grace through faith, not by anything we have done. That same faith that first laid hold of Christ for salvation is the faith that holds us steady in the storm. We did not earn our way in, and we do not hold ourselves up by our own strength. We trust the One who saved us.

Proverbs 3:5–6 Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.

The Heart of the Matter

Trust is not the absence of trouble. It is the refusal to let trouble rule the heart, because the heart is fixed on God. We lean not on what we can figure out, but on the One we know.

Section II

Why the Trials Come

The first thing we have to settle is that trials are not a sign that God has forgotten us. The Scriptures tell us plainly to expect them, and even to count them as something God is using for our good. James does not say "if ye fall into" troubles, he says "when."

James 1:2–4 My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing.

The trying of our faith is not the breaking of it, it is the proving of it. A faith that has never been tested is a faith that has never been shown to be real. Peter says the same, and he reaches for the picture of gold being refined in the fire. The fire is not there to destroy the gold, it is there to bring out its worth.

1 Peter 1:6–7 Wherein ye greatly rejoice, though now for a season, if need be, ye are in heaviness through manifold temptations: That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ.

So the believer is not promised a life kept far away from the furnace. He is promised that the furnace will do a good work, and that he will come out of it more precious than he went in. The heat is real, the heaviness is real, but it is "for a season," and it is "if need be." Nothing wasted, nothing pointless, all of it in the hand of a Father who loves His children.

A Word for the One Who Is Weary

It is no lack of faith to feel the weight of a trial. Peter says the saints were "in heaviness," and the Lord Himself sweat as it were great drops of blood in the garden. Trusting God does not mean we pretend the burden is light. It means we carry it to Him instead of carrying it alone. Faith and tears have always walked together, and God is near to the broken in heart.

Section III

Job: Faith That Held When All Was Gone

If ever a man was overtaken by trials all at once, it was Job. In a single day the messengers came one after another, each with worse news than the last. His oxen and donkeys, gone. His sheep, gone. His camels, gone. The servants, killed. And then the worst of all, his ten children, taken in one stroke when the house fell on them. The blows landed so fast he had no time to recover between them. This is what it is to be overtaken.

And here is what this man did. He did not curse God. He fell down and worshipped.

Job 1:21 And said, Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD.

Job did not understand what was happening. He never got an explanation in the moment. But his trust was not in his understanding, it was in his God. Even when his own body was covered in sores and his friends had turned against him, even when he wished he had never been born, he spoke the words that have steadied believers for thousands of years.

Job 13:15 Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him: but I will maintain mine own ways before him.

That is faith with nothing left to lean on but God Himself. And underneath that trust was a settled confidence in two things. First, that his Redeemer was alive and would one day stand upon the earth. Second, that God knew exactly what He was doing, and that the trial had a destination.

Job 19:25 For I know that my redeemer liveth, and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth.
Job 23:10 But he knoweth the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as gold.

What Job Teaches Us

Job could say "he knoweth the way that I take" before he could see where the road was going. He trusted the God who saw the whole path, even while he himself stood in the dark. The end of his story, where the Lord restored him, was written before he ever knew it would come.

Section IV

The Furnace and the Words "But If Not"

Three young men far from home were dragged before the most powerful king on earth and told to bow to his golden image or be thrown into a burning furnace. They had every earthly reason to bend. Their answer to the king is one of the boldest statements of trust in all of Scripture, and it turns on three small words.

Daniel 3:17–18 If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up.

"But if not." There is the heart of real trust. They believed God was able to save them. They also settled it in their hearts that even if He chose not to spare them from the fire, they would not turn from Him. Their faith was not a bargain that said "rescue me and I will serve you." It was a surrender that said "we will serve you whether you rescue us or not." That is trust that the trial cannot break, because it is not resting on the outcome. It is resting on God.

And what did God do? He did not keep them out of the fire. He met them in it.

Daniel 3:25 He answered and said, Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt; and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God.

The fourth man in the fire is the great comfort here. Sometimes God lets the furnace door stay open and walks in to be with us, rather than keeping us from the flames. The fire that was meant to destroy them only burned away the ropes that bound them. They came out without even the smell of smoke on their clothes. This is the promise the Lord made long before through the prophet, and He keeps it still.

Isaiah 43:2 When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee; and through the rivers, they shall not overflow thee: when thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned; neither shall the flame kindle upon thee.
Section V

The Storm on the Sea

The Lord told His disciples to cross over to the other side of the sea. They were doing exactly what He said, and still the storm came. This is worth remembering, because we sometimes think a storm means we have stepped out of God's will. These men were in the very center of it, obeying His word, when the wind rose up against them.

Mark 4:37–38 And there arose a great storm of wind, and the waves beat into the ship, so that it was now full. And he was in the hinder part of the ship, asleep on a pillow: and they awake him, and say unto him, Master, carest thou not that we perish?

"Carest thou not that we perish?" That is the cry of an overtaken heart. The waves were coming over the sides, the ship was filling, and the Lord was asleep. It looked for all the world as though He did not care. But His sleep was not neglect, it was the rest of perfect trust in His Father. And when they woke Him, He did not panic with them. He stood up and spoke to the storm.

Mark 4:39–40 And he arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm. And he said unto them, Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?

Three words to the sea, and the storm that had terrified seasoned fishermen lay down flat. The same voice that spoke the worlds into being spoke peace to the waves. Then He turned to His disciples and asked them why they had been so afraid. The answer is that they had forgotten who was in the boat with them. The very One who could calm the sea was sitting right there the whole time. He had told them they were crossing to the other side, and His word could not fail. They were never going to drown with the Lord of the sea aboard the ship.

The Lesson of the Boat

Fear and faith were both in that boat, and the Lord asked them to choose which would rule. The same Lord is in the boat with us. The storm may rage, but it cannot sink the ship that carries the One who made the sea.

Section VI

The Anchor That Holds

So how do we keep believing when the trials are overtaking us? We do not whip up a feeling. We fix our minds on what is true about God, and we lean there. The peace He gives is tied to where we set our thoughts.

Isaiah 26:3 Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee: because he trusteth in thee.

A mind stayed on God is a mind that keeps coming back to who He is. He is a present help, not a distant one. He is not far off when the trouble hits, He is near, ready, and able.

Psalm 46:1 God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.

The righteous are not promised a life with no afflictions. They are promised that the Lord brings them through every one of them. Not most of them, all of them.

Psalm 34:19 Many are the afflictions of the righteous: but the LORD delivereth him out of them all.

And we have the promise that every single thing the Lord allows into the life of His child is being woven together for good. Not that every thing is good in itself, but that God is working all of it together toward a good end, for those who love Him.

Romans 8:28 And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.

Paul, who knew prison, shipwreck, and beatings, looked his troubles in the face and called them light and momentary next to the weight of glory waiting on the other side. He had learned to look past the seen to the unseen.

2 Corinthians 4:17–18 For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory; While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal.

And what are we to do with the care and the worry that the trials pile on us? We are not told to carry it bravely. We are told to hand it over, because the One we hand it to actually cares for us.

1 Peter 5:7 Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you.

Where the Anchor Holds

Our trust does not rest on our circumstances changing. It rests on God's character never changing. He is present, He is strong, He is working all things for our good, and He carries our care for us. That is solid ground to stand on when everything else is shaking.

In Closing

Trust Him in the Storm

The trials will come, and some of them will come all at once. The Lord never hid that from us. What He promised was not a road without storms, but His own presence on the road, and a good end to every step of it. Job worshipped in his loss. The three young men walked in the fire with a fourth at their side. The disciples watched the sea lie down at a word. Every one of them found that God was bigger than the thing that was overtaking them.

So when the waters are rising over you, do not look first at the size of the wave. Look at the One in the boat. Let not your heart be troubled. Stay your mind on Him, cast your care upon Him, and trust Him whether the deliverance comes today or further down the road. He has never lost one of His own in a storm, and He is not going to start with you.

If you do not yet know this God, that is the place to begin. The same Lord who calmed the sea gave Himself on the cross and rose again, so that anyone who believes on Him would be saved. Come to Him by faith, and you will have an anchor that holds, in this life and forever.
