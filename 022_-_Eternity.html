A Pronomian Teaching

Eternity

The Final State of the Righteous and the Lost

✦

Introduction

Beyond the return of Christ, beyond His reign upon the earth, the eye of faith looks to a further horizon still. There is a state that has no end, a final and everlasting condition for every soul that has ever lived. This is eternity, the unending ages that follow the close of this present world and the judgment of all men. To study it is to look upon the things that abide when all else has passed away, and to weigh how we shall live in light of where we shall dwell for ever.

Scripture does not leave us to guess about these last things. It tells of the resurrection of all the dead, of a great judgment before the throne of God, of a new heaven and a new earth for the redeemed, and of an everlasting separation between the saved and the lost. We will trace what God has revealed, soberly and plainly, that the hope of the righteous may be strengthened and the warning to the lost may be heard while there is yet time to turn.

Section I

The Resurrection of All the Dead

Eternity for the body begins with resurrection. Scripture teaches that all the dead shall rise, both the just and the unjust. The grave is not the final word over any man. There is a resurrection of life for those who are Christ's, and a resurrection of damnation for those who are not. Every soul that has ever lived will stand again, raised to face what lies beyond.

John 5:28–29 Marvel not at this: for the hour is coming, in the which all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, And shall come forth; they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of damnation.
Acts 24:15 And have hope toward God, which they themselves also allow, that there shall be a resurrection of the dead, both of the just and unjust.
Daniel 12:2 And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt.

Mark the two resurrections, the one to life and the other to damnation. This is the great divide that eternity makes plain. The redeemed are raised to everlasting life, their bodies made glorious and incorruptible. The lost are raised to judgment and shame. The hour is coming, said the Lord, when all that are in the graves shall hear His voice and come forth, every one to his appointed end.

All Shall Rise

The grave holds no man for ever. All the dead, both the just and the unjust, shall be raised at the voice of the Son of God, some to the resurrection of life and some to the resurrection of damnation. Eternity is bodily and real, not a vague shadow but a true rising of all the dead.

Section II

The Great White Throne Judgment

After the resurrection comes the judgment. Scripture shows us a great white throne, before which the dead stand to be judged. The books are opened, and men are judged according to their works, every deed brought into the light. This is the final reckoning, where the secrets of all hearts are laid bare before the God who sees all and forgets nothing.

Revelation 20:11–12 And I saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away; and there was found no place for them. And I saw the dead, small and great, stand before God; and the books were opened: and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works.
Hebrews 9:27 And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment.
Revelation 20:15 And whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire.

Notice that two kinds of books are opened. There are the books of works, by which men are judged, and there is the book of life, in which the names of the redeemed are written. The deciding matter is whether a man's name is found in the book of life. Those written there belong to Christ and are saved by His grace. Those not found written there are judged by their works, and no man is saved by his works, for all have sinned.

Teacher's Note

Guard the gospel here with care. The lost are judged according to their works, and by works no man can stand, for all have sinned. The redeemed are not saved by their works but by grace, their names written in the book of life through faith in Christ. Their works follow as the fruit and evidence of saving faith, not as the price of salvation. Teach the judgment soberly, never letting it become a doctrine of salvation by works, and never letting it be dismissed as though deeds do not matter.

Section III

The Everlasting State of the Lost

We come now to a solemn truth that must be spoken plainly, though it grieves us to speak it. Those who die in their sins, whose names are not written in the book of life, pass into an everlasting separation from God. Scripture calls it the second death, the lake of fire, a place of outer darkness and unending loss. This is no light matter, and the Lord Himself spoke of it more than any other.

Revelation 20:14 And death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.
Matthew 25:46 And these shall go away into everlasting punishment: but the righteous into life eternal.
2 Thessalonians 1:9 Who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power.

The hardest weight of this judgment is to be shut out from the presence of the Lord, away from His glory and His goodness for ever. We do not speak of these things to frighten for frightening's sake, but because they are true, and because love warns. The same Lord who is full of grace and mercy declared these things, that men might flee from the wrath to come and lay hold of the life He freely offers.

A Warning Spoken in Love

Those who die without Christ face the second death, an everlasting separation from the presence of the Lord. This sober truth is not told to terrify but to warn, that men might turn while there is time. Love does not hide the danger but points the way of escape.

Section IV

A New Heaven and a New Earth

For the redeemed, eternity is not a vague drifting among the clouds but a renewed creation, a new heaven and a new earth wherein dwelleth righteousness. The present world, marred by sin and groaning under the curse, will give way to a creation made new and clean. There the people of God will dwell in bodies raised and glorified, in a world where sin and its sorrows have no place.

Revelation 21:1 And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.
2 Peter 3:13 Nevertheless we, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness.
Isaiah 65:17 For, behold, I create new heavens and a new earth: and the former shall not be remembered, nor come into mind.

This is the hope set before the believer, a creation in which righteousness is at home. The sin that has spoiled everything will be gone, and the curse lifted at last. The redeemed do not look merely to escape this world but to inherit a better one, the new heavens and new earth that God Himself will make, where His will is done and His righteousness dwells for ever.

Where Righteousness Dwells

The believer's eternal home is a new heaven and new earth wherein dwelleth righteousness. The whole creation, now groaning under the curse, will be made new and clean. This is no flight from the body or the world, but the renewing of all things under the reign of God.

Section V

God Dwelling With His People

The crown and glory of eternity is not the place but the Person. The greatest joy of the new creation is that God Himself will dwell with His people, face to face, with every sorrow wiped away. The long story of sin's separation is over. The fellowship that was broken in the garden is restored and made perfect for ever. To be with God, to see His face, is the believer's deepest and final blessedness.

Revelation 21:3–4 And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God. And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away.
Revelation 22:3–4 And there shall be no more curse: but the throne of God and of the Lamb shall be in it; and his servants shall serve him: And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads.

Hear what is promised. No more death, no more sorrow, no more crying, no more pain, and every tear wiped away by the hand of God. The curse is gone, and His servants shall see His face. This is heaven's heart, not merely a place of rest but the unbroken presence of God with His people. All the longing of the redeemed finds its end in this, to dwell with God and to behold Him for ever.

The Tabernacle of God With Men

The glory of eternity is God dwelling with His people, every tear wiped away, death and sorrow and pain forever gone. His servants shall see His face. The deepest joy of heaven is not the gift but the Giver, the unbroken presence of God Himself with the redeemed.

Section VI

Living Now in Light of Eternity

If these things are so, then eternity must shape the way we live today. The certainty of the resurrection, the judgment, and the world to come is not given to feed idle speculation but to make us holy, watchful, and faithful. The one who truly believes he will stand before God and dwell in a new creation will set his heart on things above and walk in obedience here below.

Colossians 3:1–2 If ye then be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ sitteth on the right hand of God. Set your affection on things above, not on things on the earth.
2 Peter 3:11 Seeing then that all these things shall be dissolved, what manner of persons ought ye to be in all holy conversation and godliness.
1 John 3:2–3 Beloved, now are we the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be: but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is. And every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure.

See how the hope of eternity works in the heart. Every man that has this hope purifies himself, even as Christ is pure. The promise of seeing Him and being made like Him draws the believer to holiness now. We set our affection on things above, we live as those who must give account, and we walk in glad obedience to the commandments of God, knowing that the things we labor for here will follow us into the everlasting day.

Hope That Purifies

The hope of eternity is not idle comfort but a purifying power. The one who knows he shall see Christ and be made like Him sets his heart above and walks in holiness now. What we shall be presses upon what we ought to be, drawing us to obedience in the light of the world to come.

Conclusion

The Everlasting Day

All the dead shall rise, the just and the unjust, and all shall stand before the great white throne to be judged. Those whose names are not found in the book of life pass into the second death, an everlasting separation from the presence of the Lord. But those who are Christ's, washed in His blood and written in His book, inherit a new heaven and a new earth wherein dwelleth righteousness, where God Himself dwells with His people and wipes away every tear.

This is eternity, the unending day toward which all of Scripture moves. We speak its warning in love, that men might flee the wrath to come, and we hold its hope with joy, for our Lord has prepared a place for His own. Let us then live as people of eternity, setting our affection on things above, purifying ourselves as He is pure, and walking in faithful obedience until the day we see His face and dwell with Him for ever.

And they shall see his face; and his name shall be in their foreheads.
