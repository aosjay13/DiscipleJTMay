A Core Pronomian Foundation

Conditional Security and the Fear of God

Resting our whole assurance in Christ while honoring the real seriousness of apostasy and the call to endure to the end

✦

Holding Two Truths at Once

Two truths sit side by side in Scripture, and a believer must hold both or he will be harmed by losing either. The first truth is that our salvation is utterly secure in Christ. The Shepherd holds His sheep, the Father keeps His children, and nothing outside us can tear us from His hand. The second truth is that apostasy is real, that a person can turn away from the living God, that we are called to endure to the end and warned not to fall. Both of these are taught plainly, and the temptation is always to seize one and let go of the other.

Let go of the first and you fall into torment. You live in dread, never sure of God, scanning your own heart for proof that you are saved, finding none that satisfies, worn down by a fear that has no rest in it. Let go of the second and you fall into presumption. You treat grace as a thing you cannot lose no matter how you live, you grow careless about sin, and you ignore the warnings God gave for your good. Neither torment nor presumption is the life God calls us to. He calls us to a settled assurance joined with a holy fear, and the two are not enemies.

This teaching is about holding both at once. We will ground our assurance where it belongs, not in ourselves but in Christ, so that it is solid and not shaken by every failure. We will look honestly at the seriousness of apostasy and the necessity of endurance, so that we do not grow careless. And then we will see how godly fear and true assurance live together in the same heart, not as a contradiction but as the healthy posture of a child who is both deeply secure and rightly reverent before a holy God.

Part One

Our Assurance Is Grounded in Christ, Not in Ourselves

We begin with assurance, and we begin here on purpose, because a believer who is anxious and tormented cannot hear a warning rightly. He needs first to know where his confidence rests. And the great truth that steadies the soul is this: our assurance was never meant to rest on ourselves, on the strength of our faith or the steadiness of our feelings. It rests on Christ, on His finished work and His faithful keeping. The ground of our confidence is outside us, in Him, and that is exactly why it is solid.

John 10:27–29 My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. My Father which gave them me, is greater than all; and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father’s hand.

Hear the strength of this promise. The sheep of Christ shall never perish, and no man is able to pluck them out of His hand or the Father’s hand. This is real security, and we must not weaken it. No enemy, no power, no person, nothing outside you can snatch you away from God. The Shepherd is stronger than every threat, and His grip on His sheep does not fail. When a believer is battered by doubt or fear or the accusations of the enemy, this is his rock. No outside force can take him from God.

Philippians 1:6 Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ. Hebrews 7:25 Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.

See where the confidence comes from. It is God who began the good work, and God who will carry it through to the end. Christ saves to the uttermost, all the way through, and He ever lives to pray for us. Our assurance does not hang on the slender thread of our own performance. It hangs on the unbreakable cord of His faithfulness. This is why the believer can have real peace. The One who holds him does not weaken, does not forget, does not let go from His side. The keeping is His work, and He does it perfectly.

Where Assurance Rests

Our confidence is not in the strength of our faith or the steadiness of our feelings, but in Christ, His finished work, His faithful keeping, and His unfailing intercession. No outside power can pluck us from His hand. Because the ground of assurance is outside us, in Him, it is solid and can bear the full weight of the soul.

Part Two

Apostasy Is Real and the Warnings Are for Us

Now we must hold the other truth with equal honesty. The same Scripture that promises no one can pluck us from God’s hand also warns, soberly and repeatedly, against turning away. These warnings are not idle words, and they are not addressed to unbelievers only. They are written to the people of God, for their good, and we dishonor God if we explain them away to protect a system. Notice carefully: nothing outside us can take us from God, but Scripture speaks of a danger of our own turning, our own departing, our own falling away.

Hebrews 3:12–14 Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God. But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end.

Read who is addressed. Brethren. The warning is to believers, against an evil heart of unbelief that departs from the living God. And notice the little word if. We are made partakers of Christ, if we hold our confidence steadfast to the end. That if is not a threat meant to torment. It is a sober reality meant to keep us awake. The danger is not that God will drop us, for He never does. The danger is that a heart can be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin and depart. So we are told to take heed, and to help one another daily, that none be hardened.

Hebrews 10:26–27 For if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins, But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries.

This is one of the most sobering passages in all of Scripture, and it must not be softened. It speaks of a willful, settled turning back from the truth one has known, a deliberate trampling of the Son of God. For such a hardened departure there remains no other sacrifice, because the person has turned from the only sacrifice there is. The seriousness here is total. To make light of apostasy is to make light of what God treats with the gravest weight. The warnings are real, and they are in the Bible because the danger they warn of is real.

Teacher’s Note

Handle these warning passages with great care, because they can wound a tender conscience even as they fail to wake a hard one. The willful sin of Hebrews 10 is not the daily stumbling of a believer who grieves over his failure and runs back to Christ. It describes a settled, deliberate turning away from the truth, a hardening, a trampling underfoot of the Son of God. The very fact that a believer is troubled by sin and longs to return is evidence that his heart is not hardened.

So as you teach, aim the warning where Scripture aims it, at presumption and careless hardening, not at the struggling saint. The anxious believer needs to hear the promises of Part One. The careless believer needs to hear the warnings of Part Two. Discern which is in front of you.

The Warnings Are Real

Scripture warns believers, soberly and often, against departing from the living God. No outside power can take us, but a heart can be hardened by sin and turn away. These warnings are not idle, and they are not aimed only at unbelievers. They are God’s gracious means of keeping His people awake and pressing them to endure.

Part Three

The Necessity of Endurance

Between the promise that holds us and the warning that wakes us lies the call to endure. Scripture does not present the Christian life as a single moment of decision followed by a guaranteed coast to glory regardless of what follows. It presents a race to be run, a fight to be fought, a course to be finished. The one who is truly Christ’s does not merely begin. He continues. He endures to the end. And this endurance is not how we earn salvation; it is how saving faith shows itself to be alive over the whole course of a life.

Matthew 24:13 But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved. Colossians 1:22–23 To present you holy and unblameable and unreproveable in his sight: If ye continue in the faith grounded and settled, and be not moved away from the hope of the gospel, which ye have heard.

Hear the plain words. He that endures to the end shall be saved. And again, you will be presented holy and unblameable, if you continue in the faith and are not moved away from the hope of the gospel. The word is continue. The Christian life is a continuing, an abiding, a remaining in Christ. This is not a heavy burden laid on us to carry alone. It is the natural path of a living faith, which by its nature holds on, leans on Christ, and keeps following the Shepherd’s voice to the end.

John 15:4 Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine; no more can ye, except ye abide in me. 2 Timothy 4:7 I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith.

Christ’s command is to abide, to remain in Him as a branch in the vine. And Paul at the end of his life could say he had finished the course and kept the faith. This is the shape of the believer’s walk: not a single leap but a lifelong abiding, a fight fought to the finish. And here the two truths meet beautifully. We are kept by Christ, and we endure. We do not endure in our own strength to keep ourselves saved. We endure because He keeps us, and His keeping works itself out in our continuing. The perseverance is real, and it is also His gift.

Endurance Is the Path

The Christian life is a race to finish, a fight to win, a faith to keep to the end. He that endures to the end shall be saved. This endurance does not earn salvation; it is how living faith shows itself alive across a whole life. And we endure not in our own strength but because Christ keeps us, His keeping working itself out in our continuing.

Part Four

How Assurance and Holy Fear Live Together

Now we come to the heart of the matter. How can a believer have real assurance and real fear at the same time without being torn in two? The answer is that the fear God asks of us is not the terror of a slave who dreads punishment. It is the reverence of a son who loves his Father and would not for anything grieve Him or turn from Him. This godly fear does not destroy assurance. It guards it. The two were always meant to live in the same heart.

Philippians 2:12–13 Wherefore, my beloved, as ye have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure.

Look how Paul joins the very things we are tempted to pull apart. Work out your salvation with fear and trembling, he says, and in the same breath, for it is God who works in you. The fear and trembling and the confident keeping of God stand together in one sentence. The reverent fear is not anxiety that God will fail us. It is the holy seriousness of people who know they are dealing with the living God, joined to the deep confidence that He is at work in them. Fear and assurance, side by side, by the inspired hand of Paul.

Romans 11:20–22 Well; because of unbelief they were broken off, and thou standest by faith. Be not highminded, but fear: For if God spared not the natural branches, take heed lest he also spare not thee. Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God.

Here Paul tells us plainly to behold both the goodness and the severity of God, and not to choose only one. He says, be not highminded, but fear. The fear he commands is the cure for presumption, for highmindedness, for taking grace for granted. It is not meant to rob the believer of peace but to keep him humble and dependent. A son who reveres his father is not a son in torment. He is a son who loves rightly, who would not presume on his father’s kindness, and who is therefore most secure in the relationship, not least.

Slavish Fear

The terror of one who dreads punishment and is never sure of God. It scans the heart for proof, finds no rest, and lives in torment. This fear has no place in the believer, for perfect love casts it out.

Godly Fear

The reverence of a son who loves his Father and would not grieve Him or turn away. It guards against presumption, keeps the heart humble and dependent, and lives alongside deep assurance, not against it.

Why the Two Belong Together

Picture a child walking with a loving father along a high mountain path. The child is perfectly secure, for the father holds his hand and will not let go. Yet the child does not therefore run heedlessly toward the edge. His security in his father’s grip and his healthy respect for the height live together in the same small heart, and both are right. Take away the father’s grip and the child is in terror. Take away all respect for the edge and the child is in danger. Together, the held hand and the reverent care make for a safe and joyful journey.

So it is with the believer. The hand of Christ holds him, and that is his assurance. The seriousness of turning away keeps him reverent, and that is his godly fear. He does not earn the grip by his caution, and he does not despise the height because of the grip. He walks secure and reverent at once, held by his Father and honoring the path, all the way home.

The Whole Counsel

Secure in His Hand, Reverent in His Presence

Bring the two truths together and the balance God intends becomes clear. Our assurance is real and deep, grounded not in ourselves but in Christ, in His finished work and His faithful keeping. No power outside us can pluck us from His hand, and the One who began the good work will carry it through. At the same time, the warnings against apostasy are real and sober, written to believers for their good, and the call to endure to the end is woven through Scripture. We are kept by Christ, and we continue in the faith, and these are not two competing facts but one truth seen from two sides. He keeps us, and His keeping shows itself in our endurance.

The fear God asks of us is not the torment of a slave but the reverence of a beloved son. It does not war against assurance; it guards it from presumption. So the believer is freed from both ditches. He is not tormented, scanning his own heart for a confidence it can never supply, because his confidence rests on Christ. And he is not careless, treating grace as a license, because he holds a holy reverence for the God he serves and for the seriousness of turning away. He walks secure in his Shepherd’s hand and reverent in his Father’s presence, and so he runs his race with peace and with care, all the way to the end.

Where It All Rests

Our assurance rests wholly on Christ, who holds His sheep and will not lose them. The warnings against falling away are real, and the call to endure is genuine, and both drive us deeper into Christ rather than into ourselves. Godly fear is not the enemy of assurance but its faithful guard, the reverence of a son who is secure and yet would never presume on his Father’s grace.

Rest your whole weight on Christ, and walk in reverent care before God. Held by His hand and honoring His holiness, endure to the end, for the Shepherd who keeps you is faithful and will bring you home.

John 10:28 And I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand. Romans 11:22 Behold therefore the goodness and severity of God.
