Gifts of the Holy Ghost · Lesson Three

Tongues and Interpretation

The Sign, the Prayer Language, and the Voice in the Assembly

✦

Introduction

In our second lesson we walked through all nine gifts of the Spirit, and we set the last two of them aside for closer study. We come now to those two, the gift of tongues and the interpretation of tongues. No gifts have stirred more confusion or more division in the church than these, and much of that confusion comes from treating every appearance of tongues in Scripture as though it were exactly the same thing. When we read carefully, we find that the Spirit gives this gift in more than one setting and for more than one purpose.

This lesson sets the matter in order by following three threads in the text. First, we look at the day of Pentecost, where tongues came as real languages, spoken and heard, declaring the wonderful works of God. Second, we look at the language of prayer and praise, by which a believer speaks not to men but to God and is built up in private. Third, we look at the use of tongues in the gathered assembly, where one speaks and another interprets, so that the whole body is edified, just as it is by prophecy. Through it all the standard remains what it has always been. The gifts serve a body that walks in love and good order, and they add nothing to a salvation that is already ours by grace through faith.

Section I

The Day of Pentecost: Tongues as Known Languages

The gift of tongues first appears on the day of Pentecost, when the Holy Ghost was poured out upon the gathered believers. What they spoke was not gibberish or noise. They spoke real languages, as the Spirit Himself gave them the words.

Acts 2:4 And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.

A crowd gathered from many nations, and each person heard the believers speaking in his own native language. This was the wonder of it. The believers had not studied these languages, yet visitors from across the known world understood them perfectly.

Acts 2:6 Now when this was noised abroad, the multitude came together, and were confounded, because that every man heard them speak in his own language.
Acts 2:11 ...we do hear them speak in our tongues the wonderful works of God.

Two things stand out here. First, the content of the speech was praise. They declared the wonderful works of God. Second, no interpreter was needed, because the hearers already knew the languages being spoken. The tongues at Pentecost were a sign that drew a crowd and opened the door for Peter to preach the gospel, after which about three thousand souls were added to the church.

What Pentecost Shows

At Pentecost the tongues were genuine human languages, given by the Spirit, declaring the praises of God. They served as a sign to a watching crowd of unbelievers and opened the way for the preaching of Christ.

Section II

The Same Gift Poured Out Again

Pentecost was not the only time tongues accompanied the giving of the Holy Ghost. The same thing happened in the house of Cornelius, when the gospel first came to the Gentiles. The believing Jews who were present knew the Spirit had been poured out because they heard these new believers speak with tongues and magnify God.

Acts 10:46 For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. Then answered Peter.

It happened again at Ephesus, when Paul laid hands on a group of disciples who had not yet received the Holy Ghost. They spoke with tongues and prophesied.

Acts 19:6 And when Paul had laid his hands upon them, the Holy Ghost came on them; and they spake with tongues, and prophesied.

Notice the pattern. In each of these moments, tongues marked the coming of the Spirit and gave voice to praise toward God. This is the same gift we will see Paul instruct the church about in his letter to Corinth, where he gives careful guidance for its right use among the gathered believers.

Section III

Three Settings for One Gift

Before we go further, it helps to hold the whole picture in view. Scripture shows the gift of tongues operating in three settings, each with its own purpose. Keeping these distinct clears away most of the confusion that surrounds this subject.

The Sign

Tongues toward the watching world
  • Known languages at Pentecost
  • A sign to unbelievers
  • Declares the works of God
  • Opens the way for the gospel

The Prayer Language

Tongues toward God in private
  • The believer speaks to God
  • He utters mysteries in the spirit
  • It edifies the believer himself
  • No interpreter is required

The Assembly Voice

Tongues toward the gathered church
  • Spoken aloud in the meeting
  • Must be interpreted
  • Edifies the whole body
  • Governed by order

The same gift, the Spirit-given utterance, serves in all three. What changes is the setting and the purpose, and with them the rules that govern its use. We turn now to the second and third of these, which Paul lays out in detail in First Corinthians chapter fourteen.

Section IV

A Language of Prayer and Praise

Paul describes a use of tongues that is directed not toward people but toward God. When a believer prays in this way, he is not speaking to the congregation. He is speaking to the Lord, uttering things his own mind does not fully grasp.

1 Corinthians 14:2 For he that speaketh in an unknown tongue speaketh not unto men, but unto God: for no man understandeth him; howbeit in the spirit he speaketh mysteries.

This kind of prayer builds up the one who prays. Paul says so plainly, and he does not despise it. He puts it alongside praying and singing with the understanding, so that the believer worships God with both his spirit and his mind.

1 Corinthians 14:4 He that speaketh in an unknown tongue edifieth himself; but he that prophesieth edifieth the church.
1 Corinthians 14:14-15 For if I pray in an unknown tongue, my spirit prayeth, but my understanding is unfruitful. What is it then? I will pray with the spirit, and I will pray with the understanding also: I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the understanding also.

Paul himself valued this private prayer language and used it often. He was not against tongues at all. He simply wanted them kept in their proper place.

1 Corinthians 14:18 I thank my God, I speak with tongues more than ye all.

This building up is inward and spiritual. It strengthens the believer's own walk with God and stirs up his faith, which is why Jude joins praying in the Holy Ghost to building ourselves up in the faith.

Jude 20 But ye, beloved, building up yourselves on your most holy faith, praying in the Holy Ghost.

The Prayer Language

In private, tongues are a means of speaking to God, uttering mysteries in the spirit, and being built up in the inner man. The edifying here is a strengthening of the soul before God, not a path to ease or earthly gain.

Section V

Tongues in the Assembly Must Be Interpreted

When the believers come together, the rule changes. In the gathered church, the goal is always that everyone present is built up, and that cannot happen if words are spoken that no one understands. So Paul gives a clear principle. A tongue spoken aloud in the assembly must be interpreted, or it does the hearers no good.

1 Corinthians 14:5 ...greater is he that prophesieth than he that speaketh with tongues, except he interpret, that the church may receive edifying.

Paul is not saying prophecy is a better gift in itself. He is saying that in the public meeting, a word everyone understands does more good than a word no one understands. An uninterpreted tongue leaves the hearers in the dark. He drives the point home with a simple picture. If a trumpet gives an uncertain sound, no one prepares for battle. If words carry no clear meaning to the hearer, they fall to the ground.

1 Corinthians 14:9 So likewise ye, except ye utter by the tongue words easy to be understood, how shall it be known what is spoken? for ye shall speak into the air.

This is why the speaker is told to pray for the gift of interpretation, so that what he speaks can be understood and the whole gathering can be edified.

1 Corinthians 14:13 Wherefore let him that speaketh in an unknown tongue pray that he may interpret.
Section VI

The Gift of Interpretation of Tongues

This brings us to the ninth gift, the companion to tongues in the public meeting. The interpretation of tongues is the Spirit-given ability to make known the meaning of a message spoken in a tongue, so that the gathered body understands and is built up.

Interpretation of Tongues to render the meaning of a message given in a tongue

Interpretation is not the same as natural translation. A translator works word for word from a language he has studied. An interpreter of tongues receives the sense of the message from the Spirit and gives it to the church in a known language. The aim is not a perfect literal rendering but a faithful conveying of the meaning, so that the body is edified.

When a tongue is spoken aloud and then interpreted, the two gifts together accomplish what prophecy accomplishes. The message that was hidden becomes plain, and the whole gathering can receive it, weigh it, and be built up.

How It Serves Makes a message in tongues understood by the church, so that what was spoken edifies the whole body and no one is left without understanding.

Because interpretation completes the work of a public tongue, the two are always meant to travel together in the assembly. Where there is no interpretation, the tongue is to be kept private, between the believer and God.

Section VII

One Speaks, Another Interprets

Paul gives plain instructions for how tongues and interpretation are to operate when the church gathers. The number is limited, the speaking is taken in turn, and an interpretation must follow. This is the prophetic pattern you asked us to look at closely, where one speaks and the meaning is then made known to all.

1 Corinthians 14:27-28 If any man speak in an unknown tongue, let it be by two, or at the most by three, and that by course; and let one interpret. But if there be no interpreter, let him keep silence in the church; and let him speak to himself, and to God.

Read this carefully, for it sets the whole order. No more than two or three are to speak in a meeting. They speak one at a time, not all at once. After the tongue is given, one interprets, so the message can be understood. And if no one present has the gift of interpretation, the one with a tongue is to stay silent in the gathering and keep that utterance private between himself and God.

The same chapter shows that the speaker keeps control of himself and is not swept away. The gift does not override the person, and a true working of the Spirit never produces chaos.

1 Corinthians 14:32-33 And the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets. For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints.

Order in the Assembly

Two or three at most, each in turn, and every tongue interpreted. If there is no interpreter, the tongue stays private. The Spirit gives utterance, yet the believer remains in control, because God is the author of peace, not confusion.

Section VIII

A Sign to Them That Believe Not

Paul gives one more piece that ties back to Pentecost. He teaches that tongues serve as a sign, and he points to a word spoken long before through the prophet Isaiah, where God said He would speak to a people through other tongues and other lips.

1 Corinthians 14:21 In the law it is written, With men of other tongues and other lips will I speak unto this people; and yet for all that will they not hear me, saith the Lord.
1 Corinthians 14:22 Wherefore tongues are for a sign, not to them that believe, but to them that believe not: but prophesying serveth not for them which believe not, but for them which believe.

This is exactly what we saw at Pentecost. The tongues were a sign that arrested a crowd of unbelievers and turned their attention to the works of God. Inside the gathered church, however, where believers come to be fed, prophecy and interpreted tongues carry the day, because the whole point of the meeting is that everyone understands and is built up.

Section IX

Tongues, Love, and the Whole Counsel

Paul does not forbid tongues. He guards them and gives them their right place. He closes the matter with two balanced commands, holding both gifts in honor while keeping the meeting in good order.

1 Corinthians 14:39-40 Wherefore, brethren, covet to prophesy, and forbid not to speak with tongues. Let all things be done decently and in order.

So we neither chase after tongues as a badge of spiritual rank nor forbid them as though they were dangerous. We seek to build up the body, we keep all things in order, and above all we walk in love, for that is the more excellent way we saw in the last lesson. Without love, even the tongues of men and of angels are only noise.

1 Corinthians 13:1 Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal.

A Word on "Unknown"

In the King James text of First Corinthians fourteen, the word unknown is printed in italics, as in "an unknown tongue." The translators set it in italics to show that they supplied the word for the sake of clear English, since it is not standing for a separate word in the original. They added it to make plain that the tongue was not understood by those who heard it. This is a helpful reminder to read carefully and to let the whole passage, not a single supplied word, shape our understanding.

Tongues Are Not the Measure of Salvation

It is vital to keep this gift in its place. Speaking with tongues is not the proof that a person is saved, nor a mark that one believer is holier than another. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, and no gift adds anything to it. Tongues are a gift for prayer, for praise, and for the building up of the body when interpreted. We pursue them, like all the gifts, for the good of others and the glory of God, and we let love and Christlike character be the thing we treasure most.

Closing

The Gift in Its Proper Place

We have followed the gift of tongues through three settings. At Pentecost it came as known languages, a sign that declared the works of God to a watching world. In private it serves as a language of prayer and praise, by which a believer speaks to God and is built up in the inner man. In the gathered assembly it is joined to interpretation, so that one speaks, another makes the meaning known, and the whole church may receive edifying.

This brings our short series on the gifts of the Holy Ghost to its close. We began with the five callings Christ gave to equip His church, moved through the nine gifts His Spirit distributes among the members, and have now seen these last two set in their right order. Through all of it the aim has been the same. The gifts serve a body that is led, taught, and shepherded under the headship of Christ, a body that does all things decently and in order, and that above every gift walks in love, the way that never fails.

Teacher's Guide

Answer Key

✦

For the teacher. This page prints after the worksheet for your reference during class discussion.

  1. What kind of tongues at Pentecost, and how did the crowd respond?
    They spoke real, known human languages as the Spirit gave utterance (Acts 2:4). Visitors from many nations each heard the believers in their own native language and were amazed (Acts 2:6). The content was praise, declaring the wonderful works of God (Acts 2:11). No interpreter was needed, because the hearers already knew those languages.
  2. Two other times tongues accompanied receiving the Spirit.
    At the house of Cornelius, where the Gentiles spoke with tongues and magnified God (Acts 10:46), and at Ephesus, where Paul laid hands on the disciples and they spoke with tongues and prophesied (Acts 19:6).
  3. The three settings and their purposes.
    First, the sign, the known languages at Pentecost that served as a witness to unbelievers and opened the way for the gospel. Second, the prayer language, by which a believer speaks privately to God and is built up. Third, the assembly voice, spoken aloud in the meeting and interpreted, so the whole body is edified.
  4. To whom does one speak in an unknown tongue, and what is uttered?
    He speaks not unto men, but unto God, for no man understands him. In the spirit he speaks mysteries (1 Corinthians 14:2).
  5. How does the prayer language build up, and Paul's own use?
    He that speaks in a tongue edifies himself (14:4). The spirit prays while the understanding is unfruitful, so Paul resolves to pray and sing with both the spirit and the understanding (14:14-15). Jude joins praying in the Holy Ghost to building ourselves up in the faith (Jude 20). Paul valued it, saying he spoke with tongues more than them all (14:18). The building up is inward and spiritual, a strengthening of the soul before God.
  6. Why must a public tongue be interpreted?
    So that the church may receive edifying (14:5). The goal of the gathering is that everyone understands and is built up. Without interpretation the words are spoken into the air and the hearers gain nothing (14:9). The speaker is therefore told to pray that he may interpret (14:13).
  7. What is interpretation, and how is it unlike translation?
    It is the Spirit-given rendering of the meaning of a message in tongues, so the church understands. Unlike a natural translator who works word for word from a language he has studied, the interpreter receives the sense of the message from the Spirit and conveys it. The aim is faithful meaning, not a literal word-for-word rendering.
  8. The order for tongues in the assembly.
    Let it be by two, or at the most three, and in turn (by course), and let one interpret. If there is no interpreter present, the one with a tongue is to keep silence in the church and speak only to himself and to God (1 Corinthians 14:27-28). The speaker remains in control, for God is the author of peace, not confusion (14:32-33).
  9. Tongues are a sign to whom, and what older word?
    Tongues are a sign to them that believe not (1 Corinthians 14:22). Paul cites the word given through Isaiah, that God would speak to this people with other tongues and other lips (14:21). This matches Pentecost, where the sign arrested a crowd of unbelievers.
  10. Do tongues prove salvation or spirituality? Relation to grace and love?
    No. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, and no gift adds to it. Tongues are not a badge of rank or proof of holiness. Paul says to covet prophecy and not to forbid tongues, doing all things decently and in order (14:39-40). Above all, without love even the tongues of men and of angels are only sounding brass (13:1). We treasure love and Christlike character most.
