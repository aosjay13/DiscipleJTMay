Thorns, Thistles, and the Sower

The Cursed Ground and the Soil of the Heart

✦

Where We Are Going

Two passages of Scripture are usually read far apart. The first is the sentence God spoke over Adam in the garden, where the ground itself was cursed and told to bring forth thorns and thistles. The second is the parable of the sower, where seed is scattered across four kinds of ground and only one of them bears fruit. Most readers treat these as separate subjects, one about the fall of man and one about preaching the word.

They are not separate. They are the same picture seen from two ends. In Genesis the ground is the dirt under Adam's feet. In the parable the ground is the heart inside Adam's children. The thorns that God cursed the earth to grow are the same thorns that choke the word in a man, and our Lord names them outright. This study sets the two passages side by side, follows the thread of the curse from the garden to the human heart, and shows how the only Man who could break that curse first had to wear it on His own head.

The Sentence in the Garden

The Curse on the Ground

When Adam took the fruit, the sentence God handed down did not fall only on the man. It fell on the ground beneath him. God did not curse Adam directly in this part of the judgment; He cursed the earth, and He did it for Adam's sake, so that the very soil a man worked all his life would push back against him and remind him daily of what was lost.

The Curse Pronounced

Genesis 3:17–19 And unto Adam he said, Because thou hast hearkened unto the voice of thy wife, and hast eaten of the tree, of which I commanded thee, saying, Thou shalt not eat of it: cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life; Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field; In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground.

Read the words slowly, because every one of them comes back later. The ground is cursed. It will bring forth thorns and thistles. The man will eat in sorrow and labor in sweat. Notice that thorns are not part of the original creation that God called good. They arrive here, at the fall, as the signature crop of a cursed world. Where the ground was once meant to yield freely to the gardener, it now resists him and grows the very things that tear at him.

Hold This Thought

Thorns are the fruit of a curse. They are what cursed ground produces on its own, without anyone planting them, when it is left to itself. Keep that fixed in your mind, because when we reach the parable of the sower we will meet thorns again, and our Lord will tell us plainly what they are.

From Soil to Soul

The Ground Becomes the Heart

When the Lord Jesus told the parable of the sower, He did something unusual. He told the parable, and then He turned around and interpreted it for His own disciples, so there is no guesswork about what the pieces mean. The seed, He said, is the word. And the ground, He said, is the heart of the one who hears it.

The Sower and His Field

Matthew 13:3–8 Behold, a sower went forth to sow; And when he sowed, some seeds fell by the way side, and the fowls came and devoured them up: Some fell upon stony places, where they had not much earth: and forthwith they sprung up, because they had no deepness of earth: And when the sun was up, they were scorched; and because they had no root, they withered away. And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprung up, and choked them: But other fell into good ground, and brought forth fruit, some an hundredfold, some sixtyfold, some thirtyfold.
Matthew 13:19 When any one heareth the word of the kingdom, and understandeth it not, then cometh the wicked one, and catcheth away that which was sown in his heart.
Luke 8:11 Now the parable is this: The seed is the word of God.

There it is, stated by the Lord Himself. The seed is the word of God, and the place it lands is the heart of a man. The ground in this parable is not a literal field of dirt; it is the inner soil of a person, the condition of the heart that the word of God falls upon. And the moment we understand that, the parable and the garden lock together. The cursed ground of Genesis and the four soils of the parable are describing the same thing. The fall did not only ruin the dirt outside us. It ruined the ground inside us.

The Bridge Between the Two Passages

In Genesis the ground is the earth. In the parable the ground is the heart. The curse that taught the earth to grow thorns is the same fallen condition that teaches the human heart to grow thorns. Every heart that has not been broken up by grace is cursed ground, and cursed ground grows thorns on its own.

Four Kinds of Heart

The Four Soils

The sower scatters the same seed everywhere. The difference is never in the seed; it is always in the ground. Four conditions of heart receive the same word, and three of the four fail. Walk through them slowly, because each one is a real kind of person sitting in a real congregation.

The Wayside

The first seed never even sinks in. It lands on the hard, packed path where feet have walked the soil down to stone, and the birds come and take it away before it can do anything. Our Lord says the wicked one snatches it out of the heart. This is the hearer who lets the word bounce off a heart too hardened to receive it, and the same adversary who deceived Adam in the garden is the bird that robs the seed from the path.

The Stony Ground

The second seed springs up fast and looks the most alive of all, but it has no depth. A shelf of rock sits just under a thin skin of dirt, so the root cannot go down. When the sun rises, which the Lord explains is tribulation and persecution, the green shoot that had no root simply withers. This is the hearer who receives the word with great joy and falls away the moment it costs him something.

The Thorny Ground

The third seed is different from the first two. It lands in real soil, it takes root, and it grows. Nothing is wrong with this seed or this start. But the ground already had thorns in it, and the thorns grow up right alongside the wheat and choke it. The man is not robbed like the wayside and he does not wither like the stony ground; he is slowly strangled. This is the soil we will spend the rest of this study on, because this is where the curse of Genesis shows its face inside the human heart.

The Good Ground

The fourth seed falls into good ground and bears fruit, some thirty, some sixty, some a hundred times what was sown. Luke adds the detail that matters most: this is the heart that hears the word, keeps it, and brings forth fruit with patience. The good ground is not good because it produces a different seed; it receives the same seed the others received. It is good because it lets that seed take root and live.

The Good Ground Defined

Luke 8:15 But that on the good ground are they, which in an honest and good heart, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience.
The Lord Names Them

The Thorns Named

Here is the heart of the whole study. When the Lord interprets the thorny ground, He does not leave the thorns as a vague symbol. He tells us, by name, what the thorns are. And when you gather what He says across the three accounts, you get a complete portrait of the things that grow in a fallen heart and choke the word of God out of it.

The Thorns Identified

Matthew 13:22 He also that received seed among the thorns is he that heareth the word; and the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, choke the word, and he becometh unfruitful.
Mark 4:18–19 And these are they which are sown among thorns; such as hear the word, And the cares of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, and the lusts of other things entering in, choke the word, and it becometh unfruitful.
Luke 8:14 And that which fell among thorns are they, which, when they have heard, go forth, and are choked with cares and riches and pleasures of this life, and bring no fruit to perfection.

Set the three accounts together and the thorns have four names. The cares of this world. The deceitfulness of riches. The lusts of other things. The pleasures of this life. These are the thistles that the cursed ground of the human heart grows all on its own. Nobody has to plant worry, greed, craving, and pleasure in a man. They come up like weeds, because the heart, like the ground, is under the curse.

The Thorn the Lord Named What It Looks Like in a Life How It Chokes the Word
The cares of this world Anxiety over money, work, health, and tomorrow; a mind always crowded with what must be handled. It fills the soil so completely that the word has no room left to grow.
The deceitfulness of riches The quiet lie that more money will finally bring rest, security, and meaning. It promises life and delivers a thorn; the man chases it and starves the seed.
The lusts of other things Cravings for whatever is not God; appetites that keep reaching for the next thing. They draw the strength of the heart away from the one seed that matters.
The pleasures of this life Comfort, entertainment, and ease sought as the aim of living. They keep a man too content with the world to bear fruit for the kingdom.

Notice the verb the Lord uses three times: the thorns choke the word. They do not pull it up by the root and they do not burn it. They surround it, crowd it, and slowly strangle the life out of it until the man becomes, in His own word, unfruitful. The seed was good. The start was real. But the thorns were never dealt with, so the wheat had no air to breathe.

The Same Ground

The Heart as Cursed Ground

Now bring the two passages fully together. In Genesis, God cursed the ground and the ground answered by growing thorns. In the parable, the word falls on the ground of the heart, and the heart answers by growing thorns. The connection is not a clever comparison we are inventing; the Scripture itself draws the line. The book of Hebrews takes the very picture of ground that drinks the rain and then judges that ground by what it grows, herbs or thorns.

Ground Judged by Its Crop

Hebrews 6:7–8 For the earth which drinketh in the rain that cometh oft upon it, and bringeth forth herbs meet for them by whom it is dressed, receiveth blessing from God: But that which beareth thorns and briers is rejected, and is nigh unto cursing; whose end is to be burned.

Here the same rain falls on the same kind of ground, and the ground is known by what comes up. Ground that drinks the rain and yields useful herbs is blessed. Ground that drinks the same rain and yields thorns and briers is near to the curse, and its end is the fire. That is the parable of the sower stated as a warning. The rain is the word and the grace of God poured out again and again on a life, and every heart is finally revealed by whether it brings forth fruit or brings forth thorns.

This is the hard truth under the whole study. Left to itself, the human heart is the cursed ground of Genesis. It does not need to be taught to worry, to love money, to crave, and to chase pleasure. Those thorns come up on their own, the same way thorns came up on their own in a cursed field. A man does not become good ground by trying harder to grow wheat. The ground has to be changed.

The Diagnosis

The thorns in the parable are not an unlucky accident in an otherwise fine field. They are the natural crop of a heart still under the curse that fell in Eden. This is why the word must do more than land on a man; the ground itself must be broken and changed, or it will choke every seed that falls into it.

The Curse Worn and Carried

The Crown of Thorns

If thorns are the badge of the curse, then watch what the soldiers placed on the head of the Lord before they led Him to the tree. They platted a crown of thorns and pressed it onto His brow. Men meant it as mockery. Heaven meant it as a sign. The Son of God walked to His death wearing the exact emblem of the curse that fell in the garden, carrying on His own head the thorns that the cursed ground had been growing ever since.

The Curse Laid on the Sinless One

Matthew 27:29 And when they had platted a crown of thorns, they put it upon his head, and a reed in his right hand: and they bowed the knee before him, and mocked him, saying, Hail, King of the Jews!
Galatians 3:13 Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree.
Luke 22:44 And being in an agony he prayed more earnestly: and his sweat was as it were great drops of blood falling down to the ground.

Look at how exactly the Lord retraced the sentence of Genesis and reversed it. The ground was cursed, so He was made a curse for us. The ground brought forth thorns, so He wore the thorns. Adam was told he would labor in the sweat of his face, and in the garden of Gethsemane the last Adam sweat as it were great drops of blood, falling down to the ground He came to redeem. What fell on Adam, the Lord Jesus took up onto Himself, piece by piece, so that the curse could finally be lifted off the ground of the heart.

The First Adam and the Last

By one man's disobedience the ground was cursed and learned to grow thorns. By one Man's obedience the curse was carried away by the One who wore the thorns Himself. The Sower of the parable is the same Lord who let the thorns be pressed into His own head, so that the seed He sows could finally find ground that bears fruit instead of briers.

And the prophets promised exactly this reversal. The day is coming when the thorn gives way to the tree and the brier gives way to the myrtle, when the ground that was cursed to grow thistles is healed and grows what is good. That promise begins the moment the curse is lifted off a single human heart.

The Promised Reversal

Isaiah 55:13 Instead of the thorn shall come up the fir tree, and instead of the brier shall come up the myrtle tree: and it shall be to the LORD for a name, for an everlasting sign that shall not be cut off.
The Work of the Husbandman

Breaking Up the Fallow Ground

So how does cursed ground ever become good ground? Not by leaving it alone, for left alone it grows only thorns. The ground has to be worked. The prophets call this breaking up the fallow ground, and they tie it directly to seeking the Lord and to refusing to keep sowing among the thorns that are already there.

The Command to the Hearer

Hosea 10:12 Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy; break up your fallow ground: for it is time to seek the LORD, till he come and rain righteousness upon you.
Jeremiah 4:3 For thus saith the LORD to the men of Judah and Jerusalem, Break up your fallow ground, and sow not among thorns.

Notice the warning at the end of Jeremiah's command: sow not among thorns. It is not enough to receive the seed. The thorns that the cursed heart grows on its own, the cares, the lust for riches, the cravings, and the pleasures, have to be torn out and kept out, or the seed will be choked no matter how good it was. This is the daily work of a believer, pulling up the thistles the old nature keeps trying to grow back, so that the word planted in the heart has room to bear.

And the fruit is the proof. Remember that the good ground was never good because it made its own seed; it received the same seed as the rest. It was good because it kept the word and let it bear fruit with patience. That fruit, the keeping of His word, is not how a man earns the seed. It is the evidence that the ground truly received it. We are not saved by the harvest; we are saved by the Sower and His seed. But ground that has really been broken up and planted will show it by what grows there.

The Final Word

Grace is the seed and the rain; it is freely given and it does the saving. The fruit that follows is not the price of the seed but the sign that the ground received it. Good ground keeps the word and bears fruit; thorny ground keeps its thorns and stays unfruitful. The same Lord who wore the thorns is the one who breaks up the cursed ground of a heart and makes it bear a hundredfold.

For the teacher. A natural place to press the listener is the third soil, the thorny ground, because it is the most deceptive. That hearer is not an open rejecter and not a shallow quitter. He looks planted, he looks rooted, and he is quietly being strangled by ordinary, respectable things that no one would call sin. Ask the class which of the four named thorns is growing tallest in their own field right now, and whether they are doing the daily work of pulling it out, or simply sowing more good intentions among thorns they have never broken up.
