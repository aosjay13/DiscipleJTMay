A Pronomian Teaching

Faith and Works

The Living Faith That Works by Love

✦

Introduction

Few subjects have caused more confusion than the relationship of faith and works. Some have torn the two apart, holding faith so high that they cast off all obedience as though it were a threat to grace. Others have leaned so hard on works that they made obedience the price of salvation and lost the gospel altogether. Both have erred, and both have wandered from the plain teaching of Scripture.

The Bible holds faith and works together without contradiction. We are saved by grace through faith alone, apart from any work of our own. Yet the faith that saves is never alone, for it always brings forth obedience as its fruit. A faith that produces no works is no living faith at all, but a dead and empty profession. In this teaching we will set the two in their right places, that the believer may rest wholly on grace and walk faithfully in the commandments of God.

Section I

Salvation Is by Grace Through Faith, Not of Works

We begin where Scripture begins, with the unshakable truth that salvation is the free gift of God. No man earns it. No man deserves it. No work of the flesh, however righteous it may appear, can purchase the favor of God or wash away a single sin. Salvation comes by grace, and it is laid hold of by faith, and it is a gift from first to last.

Ephesians 2:8–9 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.
Romans 3:28 Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law.
Titus 3:5 Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost.

Mark this well, for it is the foundation of all that follows. We are justified by faith without the deeds of the law. The believer does not stand before God on the strength of his obedience but on the finished work of Christ received by faith. If we lose this, we lose the gospel. The door into the kingdom is grace, and the key that opens it is faith, never the works of our own hands.

The Ground of Salvation

Salvation is never earned and never deserved. It is the gift of God, received by faith in Christ alone, apart from the deeds of the law. No man will ever boast before God that he saved himself by his own works.

Section II

Faith Without Works Is Dead

Having settled that works do not save, we must now face the other half of the truth, which many would rather avoid. The same Scripture that denies salvation by works also denies that a workless faith can save. James speaks to this plainly, and his words cut through every false comfort. A faith that produces no works is not a weak faith or a small faith. It is a dead faith, and a dead faith saves no one.

James 2:14 What doth it profit, my brethren, though a man say he hath faith, and have not works? can faith save him?
James 2:17–18 Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone. Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.
James 2:19–20 Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble. But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?

James draws a sharp and humbling picture. The devils believe that God is one, and they believe it well enough to tremble, yet their belief saves them not at all. A bare assent of the mind, a creed held without obedience, is the faith of devils and not the faith of the saved. James calls the man who trusts in such a faith a vain man, empty of the very thing he claims to have.

The Two Errors Named

Section One denies that works can save. Section Two denies that a faith without works is alive. Hold both. The works do not earn salvation, yet a faith that bears no works is dead and cannot save. The same Bible teaches both truths, and the believer must not pit one against the other.

Section III

Abraham and Rahab: Faith Proved by Works

James does not leave us with bare assertion. He brings two witnesses, one a patriarch and one a former harlot, to show how living faith always shows itself in action. Abraham believed God, and his faith was counted to him for righteousness long before he offered his son. Yet when the test came, his faith was proved true and complete by his obedience.

James 2:21–22 Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect?
James 2:23–24 And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness: and he was called the Friend of God. Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only.

Notice carefully the words faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect. The works did not replace his faith, nor did they earn his standing. They proved his faith to be real and brought it to its full and visible expression. Abraham believed, and his believing took hold of his hands and his feet, and he obeyed God even unto the offering of his only son.

James 2:25–26 Likewise also was not Rahab the harlot justified by works, when she had received the messengers, and had sent them out another way? For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.

Body and Spirit

James gives a picture that settles the matter. As the body without the spirit is a corpse, so faith without works is a dead thing. Works are to faith what the breath of life is to the body. Where the works are absent, the faith is not merely sick, it is a lifeless shell.

Section IV

Paul and James Agree

Some have imagined that Paul and James stand at war, the one preaching faith without works and the other preaching works without faith. This is a grave misreading of both. Paul and James look at the same truth from two sides, and together they give us the whole. Paul fights against those who would earn salvation by works. James fights against those who claim a faith that bears no fruit. Neither denies what the other affirms.

Romans 4:2–3 For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God. For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness.

Paul says Abraham was not justified before God by works, that he might have nothing to boast of before his Maker. James says Abraham was justified by works, meaning his faith was shown to be genuine when it acted. Paul speaks of how a sinner is first made right with God, which is by faith alone. James speaks of how a true faith is known and proved, which is by the works it produces. Both quote the same verse about Abraham believing God, and both are perfectly true.

Galatians 5:6 For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love.

Teacher's Note

The key that unlocks the supposed conflict is to see that the word justified is used in two senses. Paul uses it of the sinner being declared righteous before God, which happens by faith apart from works. James uses it of a man's faith being shown and vindicated as real, which happens by the works that flow from it. A man is justified before God by faith, and his faith is justified before men by works. Hold the two senses apart and the harmony is plain.

One Truth, Two Sides

Paul guards the gate so that no man trusts in works to be saved. James guards the house so that no man trusts in a faith that does nothing. The faith Paul preaches is the very faith which worketh by love that James describes. They are two witnesses to one gospel.

Section V

The Works of a Living Faith Are Obedience to God's Law

We must now ask what kind of works a living faith produces. They are not works invented by men, nor traditions of our own devising. The works that flow from true faith are obedience to the commandments of God. For sin is the transgression of the law, and righteousness is the keeping of it. A faith that loves God will keep His commandments, for that is the very shape that love for God takes.

1 John 2:3–4 And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.
1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.
James 1:22 But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.

Here the matter comes full circle. The faith that saves brings forth works, and the works it brings forth are obedience to God's commandments. The believer is not saved by keeping the law, yet because he is saved he keeps the law, for that is the fruit and the proof of the faith God has planted in him. To say I have faith while trampling the commandments of God is the very lie that John exposes.

Not Grievous

John adds a tender word, that His commandments are not grievous. To the heart made new by grace, obedience is not a heavy burden but a delight. The works of a living faith are not the chains of a slave but the glad service of a child who loves his Father.

Section VI

Created for Good Works

Lest anyone think that works are an afterthought or a mere option, Scripture tells us that God saved us for the very purpose of good works. The same passage that denies salvation by works goes on at once to declare that we were created in Christ to walk in them. Works are not the root of our salvation, but they are its appointed fruit, prepared by God beforehand for us to walk in.

Ephesians 2:10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.
Titus 2:14 Who gave himself for us, that he might redeem us from all iniquity, and purify unto himself a peculiar people, zealous of good works.
Matthew 5:16 Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.

See how perfectly the parts fit. Verse eight of Ephesians says we are saved by grace through faith, not of works. Verse ten of the same chapter says we are created unto good works, ordained to walk in them. There is no contradiction. We are not saved by works, and we are saved for works. Christ redeemed us that we might be a people zealous of good works, our light shining before men to the glory of our Father.

Saved For, Not By

We are not saved by good works, but we are most certainly saved for them. God ordained beforehand that His redeemed people should walk in obedience, zealous of every good work, that the world might see and glorify the Father in heaven.

Conclusion

A Living Faith That Works by Love

We have set faith and works each in its proper place. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, apart from the deeds of the law, that no man may boast. Yet the faith that saves is never a faith that sits idle, for a workless faith is the faith of devils, dead and empty and powerless to save. The living faith God gives takes hold of the hands and the feet, and it shows itself in obedience to the commandments of God.

So let no man trust in his works to make him right with God, for that righteousness comes by faith in Christ alone. And let no man rest in a faith that bears no fruit, for such a faith is dead. The true believer is saved by grace, kept by faith, and known by the works of obedience that grace produces in him. This is the faith which worketh by love, and it is the only faith that saves.

For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.
