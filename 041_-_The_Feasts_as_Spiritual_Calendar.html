A Core Pronomian Foundation

The Feasts as Spiritual Calendar

How the appointed times of God shape the believer’s year and spiritual rhythm, kept not as cultural memory but as ongoing practice

✦

A Year With a Shape

Every believer lives by some calendar. The question is only whose. The world has its own year, marked out by its own days, and most people fall into its rhythm without ever choosing it. Their months are shaped by holidays the world invented, by shopping seasons and sporting seasons, by the turning of a secular year that points to nothing higher than itself. A believer can drift through that calendar his whole life and never notice that his time is being shaped by something other than God.

But God gave His people a calendar of His own. He set apart certain times and called them His appointed times, the Moedim, the feasts of the Lord. They are not scattered observances to be remembered one by one and then forgotten. Together they form a calendar, a yearly cycle with a shape and a story, that takes the whole plan of redemption and writes it across the year. To live by this calendar is to let God order your time, so that the rhythm of your year preaches the gospel back to you season by season.

This teaching is not about each feast in turn, for those are taught on their own. It is about the feasts as a whole, as a single spiritual calendar that shapes the believer’s year. We will see that these are God’s appointed times and not merely Israel’s customs. We will see how the spring feasts have already been fulfilled in the first coming of Christ, and how the fall feasts still point ahead to His return. And we will see why keeping this calendar today is ongoing practice and not cultural memory, a living rhythm that orders the believer’s walk and keeps the whole story of God ever before his eyes.

Part One

These Are the Appointed Times of God

The first thing to settle is whose feasts these are. Many call them the Jewish feasts, as though they were the cultural property of one people, like a national holiday that belongs to a particular country. But Scripture does not call them that. When God names them, He calls them His own. They are the feasts of the Lord, His appointed times, set by Him and belonging to Him. This changes everything about how we see them.

Leviticus 23:1–2 And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying, Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, Concerning the feasts of the Lord, which ye shall proclaim to be holy convocations, even these are my feasts.

Hear what God says. These are my feasts. Not Israel’s feasts, not the feasts of the Jews, but the feasts of the Lord. The word translated feasts is the word for appointed times, fixed meetings that God Himself set on the calendar. They are holy convocations, sacred gatherings that God calls His people to keep. He did not ask Israel to invent a set of customs. He appointed times of His own and called His people to meet with Him at them. The feasts belong to God, and so they are for all who belong to God.

Leviticus 23:4 These are the feasts of the Lord, even holy convocations, which ye shall proclaim in their seasons.

Notice the phrase, in their seasons. The feasts are tied to seasons of the year, fixed points around which the year turns. This is what makes them a calendar and not just a list. They mark the spring and they mark the fall, they follow the harvests, they give the year its shape. God did not scatter them randomly. He placed them so that the whole year would be ordered around meeting with Him, the early year and the late year alike held within His appointed rhythm.

Whose Feasts They Are

God calls these times His own feasts, His appointed times, holy convocations that He set on the calendar. They are not the cultural property of one people but the appointed meetings of the Lord with those who are His. Because they belong to God, they belong to all who belong to Him, and they are placed through the year to give it shape.

Part Two

The Spring Feasts Fulfilled in His Coming

The genius of this calendar is that it tells the story of redemption in order, across the year. The feasts fall into two seasons. The spring feasts cluster at the start of the sacred year, and the fall feasts gather near its close. And this is no accident, for the spring feasts were each fulfilled, to the very day, in the first coming of Christ, while the fall feasts await His return. To walk through the spring feasts is to walk through the gospel itself.

1 Corinthians 5:7 Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us.

Passover comes first, and Paul tells us plainly that Christ our Passover was sacrificed for us. The lamb whose blood spared Israel in Egypt was always pointing to the Lamb of God whose blood spares us from death. And it happened on the very day. Christ died at Passover. Then comes Unleavened Bread, the feast of putting away the leaven of sin, fulfilled as the sinless body of Christ lay in the tomb, untouched by corruption. Then Firstfruits, the offering of the first of the harvest, fulfilled when Christ rose from the dead as the firstfruits of all who would rise.

1 Corinthians 15:20 But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept. Acts 2:1 And when the day of Pentecost was fully come, they were all with one accord in one place.

Then, fifty days after Firstfruits, comes the feast of Pentecost, the feast of the wheat harvest, and it too was fulfilled to the day. On the very day of Pentecost the Holy Spirit was poured out, and the harvest of souls began, three thousand gathered in at once. Four spring feasts, four great works of Christ, each fulfilled at its appointed time. The calendar was a prophecy written in seasons, and the first coming of Christ fulfilled the spring of it perfectly.

Teacher’s Note

The fulfillment of the spring feasts to the very day is one of the most powerful evidences you can show a class for the divine authorship of this calendar. Christ did not die at a random time. He died at Passover. The Spirit did not fall at a random time. He fell at Pentecost. The precision is the point. No man arranged this. God set these appointed times centuries in advance, and Christ met each one on schedule.

This is also the cleanest way to teach why the feasts are not empty ritual. They are the framework on which God hung the gospel. To keep them is to walk through the story of Christ in the order God himself laid out.

The Spring Already Fulfilled

Passover, Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, and Pentecost were each fulfilled to the day in the first coming of Christ: His death, His sinless rest in the tomb, His resurrection as the firstfruits, and the outpouring of the Spirit. The spring of the calendar is the gospel of His first coming, written in advance across the early year.

Part Three

The Fall Feasts Awaiting His Return

If the spring feasts were fulfilled in the first coming of Christ, the fall feasts point with equal clarity to His return. After Pentecost there is a long stretch of summer, a season of harvest and waiting, and then the calendar gathers again in the fall for its final three appointed times. These have not yet been fulfilled to the day, and that is why they still stand before us, pointing forward, telling us the story is not finished.

1 Thessalonians 4:16 For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first.

The Feast of Trumpets comes first in the fall, a day announced with the blowing of trumpets, a day of awakening and gathering. It points ahead to the day when the Lord descends with the trump of God and the dead in Christ rise. Then comes the Day of Atonement, the most solemn day of the year, when the people afflicted their souls and the high priest entered the holiest place. It points ahead to the great day of reckoning and cleansing still to come. And last comes the Feast of Tabernacles, the feast of dwelling in booths, the most joyful feast of all.

Revelation 21:3 And I heard a great voice out of heaven saying, Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God.

Tabernacles points to the great consummation, the day when God will tabernacle with His people forever, when He will dwell with men and be their God. The fall feasts are the prophecy of the end of the story, the return of Christ, the gathering of His people, the final cleansing, and the eternal dwelling of God with man. The spring is fulfilled and behind us. The fall is unfulfilled and ahead of us. And we live now in the long summer between, in the season of harvest, keeping the calendar that holds both the memory of His coming and the hope of His return.

The Spring Feasts

Passover, Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, and Pentecost. Fulfilled to the day in the first coming of Christ: His death, His rest in the tomb, His resurrection, and the gift of the Spirit. The gospel of what He has already done.

The Fall Feasts

Trumpets, the Day of Atonement, and Tabernacles. Pointing ahead to the return of Christ: the gathering at the trumpet, the great day of reckoning, and the eternal dwelling of God with His people. The hope of what He is yet to do.

Part Four

Living the Calendar as Ongoing Practice

Now we come to the practical heart of it. If the feasts form a calendar that tells the whole story of redemption, then keeping them is not looking back at a dead custom. It is ongoing practice, a living rhythm that shapes the believer’s year. The difference between cultural memory and ongoing practice is the difference between visiting a museum and living in a home. We do not keep the feasts to remember what an ancient people once did. We keep them because they are the appointed times of our God, and they still preach to us.

Colossians 2:16–17 Let no man therefore judge you in meat, or in drink, or in respect of an holyday, or of the new moon, or of the sabbath days: Which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.

Paul tells us not to let anyone judge us over how we keep these days, and he says they are a shadow of things to come. The shadow still falls forward. The fall feasts especially are a shadow of things still to come, of the return of Christ not yet arrived. So we keep the calendar as people who stand between the fulfilled spring and the unfulfilled fall, remembering and hoping at once. Each year the rhythm carries us again through the death and resurrection of Christ in the spring, and points us again to His return in the fall.

Zechariah 14:16 And it shall come to pass, that every one that is left of all the nations which came against Jerusalem shall even go up from year to year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and to keep the feast of tabernacles.

Here is a striking word about the future. When the King reigns, the nations will go up year by year to keep the Feast of Tabernacles. The feasts are not a fading shadow that vanishes when Christ returns. At least one of them will be kept by all nations in the age to come. This tells us the appointed times of God are woven into His purposes from beginning to end. To keep them now is to practice on earth what will be kept in the kingdom, to fall into step early with the rhythm that will mark eternity.

So what does it mean in practice? It means the believer lets God’s calendar, not the world’s, give shape to his year. When spring comes, he keeps Passover and remembers the Lamb, puts away the leaven of sin, rejoices in the resurrection, and celebrates the gift of the Spirit. Through the summer he labors in the harvest of souls. When fall comes, he hears the trumpet and lifts his eyes for the return of his Lord, he humbles himself in the solemn season, and he rejoices in the hope of dwelling with God forever. The year itself becomes a sermon, preached not in words only but in seasons, and the whole story of God is carried before his eyes every twelve months.

Cultural Memory or Ongoing Practice

The difference is the heart. Cultural memory keeps a custom because it is old, because the ancestors did it, because it ties us to a heritage. The custom points backward to a people and a past. Ongoing practice keeps the appointed times because they belong to God, because they still preach Christ, and because they shape the believer’s walk today and point to what is still coming. The practice points to Christ, past and future, not merely to a heritage.

This is the pronomian way to keep the Moedim. We do not keep them as a museum piece of Israel’s past, nor to earn anything from God, for salvation is by grace alone. We keep them as living appointed times, the calendar of our God, that order our year around the gospel and the blessed hope. They are not nostalgia. They are rhythm, and the rhythm shapes the soul.

The Whole Counsel

Letting God Order the Year

Bring the whole calendar into view and its beauty is plain. The feasts are not Israel’s scattered customs but the appointed times of God, His own feasts, holy convocations set through the year to give it shape. The spring feasts were fulfilled to the very day in the first coming of Christ, His death at Passover, His rest in the tomb at Unleavened Bread, His resurrection at Firstfruits, and the gift of the Spirit at Pentecost. The fall feasts still stand ahead of us, pointing to His return, the trumpet and the gathering, the great day of reckoning, and the eternal dwelling of God with His people. And we live now in the long harvest between, keeping the calendar that holds both the memory and the hope.

To keep this calendar is ongoing practice, not cultural memory. We do not keep the feasts to honor a heritage or to earn a standing with God, for we are saved by grace alone. We keep them because they are the appointed times of our God, because they still preach Christ to us in their seasons, and because they shape the rhythm of the believer’s year around the gospel and the blessed hope. The world will shape your year if you let it, around days that point to nothing. God offers a better calendar, one that writes the whole story of redemption across your year and carries it before your eyes season by season. Let God order the year, and the rhythm of your time will preach the gospel back to you as long as you live.

Where It All Rests

The feasts of the Lord are His appointed times, a calendar that tells the whole story of redemption. The spring is fulfilled in His first coming, the fall awaits His return, and we keep the rhythm between as living practice and not dead memory. Each year the calendar carries us again through the gospel and lifts our eyes again to the blessed hope.

Do not drift through the world’s calendar of empty days. Let God order your year by His appointed times, and walk through the story of Christ, season by season, until the fall feasts are fulfilled at last and the King returns.
