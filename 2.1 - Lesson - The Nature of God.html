The Nature of God

Knowing the God We Serve, and Walking With Him as He Is

✦

Before we can walk with God, we have to know who He is. A wrong picture of God will produce a wrong walk every time. Some men picture a God who is angry and waiting to strike. Others picture a God so soft that sin no longer matters to Him. Neither one is the God of the Bible. Scripture gives us one God, and He has told us plainly what He is like.

I One God, and He Does Not Change

Malachi 3:6  •  For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed.

Many have been taught that the Old Testament gives us a God of wrath and the New Testament a God of love, as though God had a change of heart somewhere between Malachi and Matthew. Scripture will not allow it. The God who thundered on Sinai is the same God who wept at the grave of Lazarus, and Jesus Christ is the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever (Hebrews 13:8). What changed between the Testaments was not the character of God but the unfolding of His plan of redemption in Christ.

II He Told Us Himself What He Is Like

Exodus 34:6–7  •  The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty.

This is God describing God, and notice where He said it. Israel had just made the golden calf, and Moses had climbed the mountain a second time to receive the tables of the law again. At the very moment the LORD hands down His law, He proclaims Himself merciful and gracious. Mercy and commandment came from the same mouth, on the same mountain. But read it all the way through. He forgives iniquity, and He will by no means clear the guilty. Both halves are God.

III His Love Always Comes First

Exodus 20:2  •  I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage.

Before the LORD gave a single commandment at Sinai, He reminded Israel of what He had already done for them. They were not carried out of Egypt because they kept the law. They were delivered first, and then taught how a redeemed people live. That is the same order we find in the gospel, for while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us (Romans 5:8). Obedience is never the price of His love; it is the answer to it.

IV His Law Is His Character in Writing

1 John 5:3  •  For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.

If you want to know what God is like, read what He commands. He forbids lying because He cannot lie. He forbids stealing because He is a giver. He calls us to rest on the seventh day because He Himself rested on the seventh day. The law is holy, and just, and good (Romans 7:12), because the God who gave it is holy, and just, and good. The way a man treats the commandments of God will tell you much about what he believes God to be.

Teacher's Answer Key  ·  Not for Student Distribution

1. Does God ever change?

He does not change. The God of Sinai and the God of Calvary are the same God. What changed between the Testaments is the unfolding of His plan in Christ, not His character.

2. In His own words, what is the LORD like?

Merciful, gracious, longsuffering, abundant in goodness and truth, forgiving iniquity, transgression and sin. And He will by no means clear the guilty. Mercy and justice both belong to Him.

3. What is the nature of God?

God is love.

4. What does His holiness call His people to be?

Holy, as He is holy. Peter repeats the same command in 1 Peter 1:15–16. His love never cancels His holiness; it makes a way for us to walk in it.

5. What had God already done before He gave the commandments?

He had already brought Israel out of Egypt. Redemption came first, then the commandments. They did not obey in order to be delivered; they were delivered, and then taught to obey. Grace before commandment, in both covenants.

6. At what point did God prove His love?

While we were yet sinners. We could not clean ourselves up first. His love came to us in the middle of our sin, and it is that love which changes us.

7. What does Paul say about the law?

That it is holy, just and good, because it came from a holy, just and good God. The commandments are a written portrait of His character.

8. How do we show our love back to God?

By keeping His commandments, and John says they are not grievous. Obedience is the response to His love, never the purchase price of it.

9. What two things did Jesus say to the woman?

"Neither do I condemn thee," and "go, and sin no more." Mercy without the command would leave her in her sin; the command without mercy would leave her condemned. Christ gave both, and in doing so He showed us the Father.

10. What has He promised the one who confesses?

He is faithful and just to forgive our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. His nature toward the repentant is mercy, and He cleanses us so that we can walk in righteousness.

✦ Teacher's Note · If a student raises Romans 5:13

ROMANS 5:13  Some teach from this verse that God overlooked sin until Sinai and only then began holding men accountable, as though God changed. The next verse answers it: death reigned from Adam to Moses. Men were dying for sin long before the tables of stone. God judged the flood generation, He judged Sodom, and He told Cain that sin lieth at the door. Abraham kept His charge, commandments, statutes and laws centuries before Sinai (Genesis 26:5). Sinai did not create God's righteousness. It wrote it down.

GALATIANS 3:24  The law is a schoolmaster to bring us to Christ, that we might be justified by faith. It brings us to Christ for righteousness, because it never could justify us. That does not leave us without a standard once we arrive. Paul settles it plainly: do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law (Romans 3:31).
