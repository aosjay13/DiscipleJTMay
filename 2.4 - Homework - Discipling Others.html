Discipling Others

Leading Someone to Salvation

✦
Name
Date

In Lesson 2 we learned that salvation is the free gift of God's grace, received by humble faith and never earned by works. Now we turn that truth outward. Having received such mercy, we are sent to carry it to others. This homework is meant to prepare your heart and your words so that you can faithfully lead someone else to the same Savior, and then help them grow.

Part I

The Command to Make Disciples

Sharing the gospel is not an option for the believer, it is a direct command from the Lord Himself. Notice that He calls us not only to win converts, but to teach them to obey everything He commanded.

Matthew 28:18–20 And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying, All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen.
Mark 16:15 And he said unto them, Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature.

In your own words, what two things did Jesus command His followers to do? Why is "teaching them to observe all things" just as important as the first decision to follow Him?

Part II

The Message You Carry

To lead someone to salvation, you must be clear about the message itself. The gospel is simple, but it must be complete. Below are the truths a person needs to understand. Check each one as you settle it in your own heart.

  • All have sinned and fall short of God's glory (Romans 3:23), and sin is the breaking of God's Law (1 John 3:4).
  • The Law shows us our sin and our need for a Savior. No one can earn salvation by good works (Romans 3:20).
  • Salvation is the free gift of God's grace, received through faith, not earned (Ephesians 2:8-9).
  • We must repent, humbly turning from sin, and place our faith in Jesus Christ (Luke 18:13-14).
  • Saving grace then empowers us to walk in obedience to God out of love (Ephesians 2:10).
Romans 10:9 That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved.

Write out, in a few sentences, how you would explain the way of salvation to a friend who has never heard it. Keep it simple and true.

Part III

Ready to Give an Answer

People cannot believe in a Savior they have never heard about. God uses our willing voices to carry the good news. We are to be prepared, gentle, and respectful as we share.

Romans 10:13–15 For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved. How then shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? and how shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? and how shall they hear without a preacher? And how shall they preach, except they be sent? as it is written, How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace, and bring glad tidings of good things!
1 Peter 3:15 But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts: and be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear.

What is one fear or hesitation that has kept you from sharing your faith? What truth from these verses helps you overcome it?

Part IV

Sow the Seed, Trust God for the Increase

It is a great relief to know that we are not responsible to save anyone. We are responsible to faithfully share. God alone changes the heart and gives the growth. This frees us to plant and water without fear of failure.

1 Corinthians 3:6–7 I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. So then neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase.

How does knowing that "God gives the increase" change the pressure you feel about someone's response? How does it change the way you pray for them?

Part V

Your Life Is a Witness

Often the most powerful sermon is a changed life. People watch how we live before they listen to what we say. A life of quiet obedience and love opens the door for the words we speak.

Matthew 5:16 Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven.

Honestly check the areas where your daily walk is a strong witness, and circle any you want to grow in.

  • My speech and honesty
  • My patience and kindness with others
  • My obedience to God's commandments
  • The way I handle hardship and trials
  • My love for fellow believers
Part VI

Discipling Beyond the Decision

Leading someone to salvation is the beginning, not the end. A true disciple does not abandon a new believer. We walk alongside them, teaching them to obey, helping them grow, and equipping them to one day disciple others themselves.

2 Timothy 2:2 And the things that thou hast heard of me among many witnesses, the same commit thou to faithful men, who shall be able to teach others also.

Think back to Matthew 28:20. What are some practical ways you could help a new believer learn to "observe all things" the Lord commanded? List at least three.

Part VII

My Personal Action Plan

Write the names of people God has placed in your life who do not yet know Him. Beside each, note one first step you can take, such as praying for them, inviting them, or sharing your own story.

PersonMy Next Step

My Commitment

Having received the free gift of grace, I commit to share that same good news with others, to do so with humility and love, to trust God for the increase, and to help those who believe to grow as faithful disciples of Jesus Christ.

For the Teacher

Answer Key & Leader's Guide

✦ ✦ ✦

Much of this homework is personal and prayerful, so several parts have no single right answer. Use this guide to evaluate understanding, to gently steer responses, and to encourage genuine follow-through. The goal is not a perfect paper but a heart prepared to share grace and to disciple others well.

Part I · The Command to Make Disciples

What two things did Jesus command, and why does teaching obedience matter as much as the first decision?

Look for both halves of the commission. First, to go and make disciples of all nations, preaching the gospel and baptizing. Second, "teaching them to observe all things" the Lord commanded. The student should see that Jesus called for disciples, not merely converts. A decision is only the beginning. Genuine salvation produces a transformed, obedient life, so leading someone to Christ includes helping them learn to walk in His ways.

Part II · The Message You Carry

How would you explain the way of salvation to someone who has never heard it?

A complete, simple explanation should touch each of the five truths in the checklist:

  • All have sinned, and sin is the breaking of God's Law.
  • The Law shows us our sin and our need for a Savior. We cannot earn salvation.
  • Salvation is the free gift of God's grace, received through faith.
  • We must repent and put our faith in Jesus Christ.
  • Grace then empowers us to walk in obedience out of love.

Watch that the explanation stays centered on Christ and on grace, never on earning salvation by works. It should be clear and true, even if worded simply.

Part III · Ready to Give an Answer

What fear has held you back, and what truth helps you overcome it?

This is a personal response. Look for honesty in naming a real hesitation, such as fear of rejection, not feeling qualified, or not knowing enough. The truth that answers it should come from the verses. God is the One who sends us, people cannot believe what they have never heard, and we are simply called to share with meekness, not to have every answer. Affirm any sincere willingness to step out.

Part IV · Sow the Seed, Trust God for the Increase

How does "God gives the increase" change the pressure you feel, and how you pray?

The student should recognize that the burden of saving anyone is not on them. We plant and water faithfully, but God alone changes the heart and gives growth. This frees us from fear of failure and from the temptation to manipulate a response. In prayer it shifts the focus to asking God to soften the person's heart, open their eyes, and draw them, rather than relying on our own persuasion.

Part V · Your Life Is a Witness

Self-examination of the life as a witness.

There is no scoring here. Look for honest self-examination rather than either pride or discouragement. The point is that a changed, obedient, loving life lends weight to the words we speak, and that growth in these areas strengthens our testimony. Encourage the student in their strengths and gently support the areas they marked for growth.

Part VI · Discipling Beyond the Decision

Practical ways to help a new believer learn to "observe all things."

Accept any sincere, practical ideas. Strong answers may include reading the Scriptures together, modeling and teaching prayer, bringing them into the fellowship of the assembly, teaching them God's commandments and how to walk in them, being available to answer questions, and walking alongside them with patience and accountability. The key idea is ongoing relationship and teaching, not a one-time hand-off.

Part VII · My Personal Action Plan

Names of people and next steps, with the commitment.

This is the student's own plan, so there is no key. Encourage them to name real people and realistic first steps, and to commit it to prayer. As a leader, consider following up in a later session to ask how it is going, and offer to pray with them. Gentle, ongoing encouragement matters far more than a graded answer.
