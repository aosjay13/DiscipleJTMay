A Core Pronomian Foundation

The Pronomian Covenant Framework

How the believer stands in the New Covenant, where grace, the law, and obedience are one work of God in Christ

✦

The Frame Before Us

Many believers carry the pieces of the faith in separate hands and never see how they fit together. In one hand they hold grace, and they prize it. In the other hand they hold the commandments of God, and they are not sure what to do with them. Some let the commandments slip away because they fear that holding them will crowd out grace. Others grip the commandments so tightly that grace begins to feel like a reward they must earn. Both have lost the frame that holds the pieces in their proper place.

This teaching gives you that frame. The pronomian believer does not choose between grace and the law of God. He sees them set within a single covenant, established by Christ, sealed in His blood, and written upon the heart by the Spirit. In that covenant, grace is the ground we stand on, the law is the shape of the life we are given, and obedience is the walk of a child who has already been brought into the house. Take any one of these away and the whole frame collapses.

We will build the frame piece by piece. First we will see what a covenant is and why everything rests on it. Then we will see how the New Covenant differs from the old, not by erasing the law of God but by moving it from stone to the heart. Then we will set grace, the law, and obedience in their right order, so that none of them is forced to do the work of another. We close by showing how the believer actually stands and walks within this one covenant, secure and obedient at once.

Part One

A Covenant Is the Ground of Everything

Before we speak of grace or law or obedience, we have to speak of the thing that holds them all. That thing is a covenant. A covenant is a bond that God Himself makes, a sworn relationship in which He binds Himself to a people and sets the terms of how they will live before Him. Salvation has never come to anyone apart from a covenant. God does not save loose individuals on no terms at all. He brings them into a sworn relationship that He establishes and He keeps.

Jeremiah 31:31–33 Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah: Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day that I took them by the hand to bring them out of the land of Egypt; which my covenant they brake, although I was an husband unto them, saith the Lord: But this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel; After those days, saith the Lord, I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.

Read that promise slowly, because the whole frame is already there. God says He will make a new covenant. He says it will not be like the old one that the fathers broke. Then He tells us the one great difference. He will take His law and put it inside the people, writing it on their hearts. Notice what He does not say. He does not say He will throw the law away. He says He will move it. The same law that was once on tablets of stone outside the man will now be written within him. The change is the location of the law and the power to keep it, not the death of the law.

Hebrews 9:15 And for this cause he is the mediator of the new testament, that by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions that were under the first testament, they which are called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance.

A covenant of this kind must be sealed, and it is sealed by blood. Christ is the mediator of the New Covenant, and He sealed it with His own death. This is why everything in the believer's life rests on the covenant and not on his own performance. The bond was cut by Christ, paid for by Christ, and kept by Christ. We do not enter it by climbing up to God. We enter it because the blood was shed and the door was opened, and we came in by faith.

Why the Covenant Comes First

Grace, the law, and obedience cannot be understood on their own. They are parts of one covenant that God makes, seals in the blood of Christ, and keeps by His Spirit. Start with the covenant and the pieces fall into place. Start with the pieces alone and they fall into confusion.

Part Two

The New Covenant Writes the Law on the Heart

Here is where many go wrong, and where the pronomian frame brings clarity. People hear the words new covenant and assume that new must mean the old has been thrown out, law and all. But the promise itself tells us plainly what is new and what is not. What is new is where the law is kept and who gives the power to keep it. What remains is the law of God as the standard of a righteous life. The New Covenant does not lower the standard. It plants the standard inside the believer.

Ezekiel 36:26–27 A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.

Look at what God promises to do in the believer. He gives a new heart. He puts His Spirit within. Then He names the result of His own work. He causes us to walk in His statutes and to keep His judgments and do them. The very thing some say has been done away with, His statutes and His judgments, is named here as the thing the Spirit produces in us. The New Covenant was never the end of God's law. It is the means by which God's law is finally kept, not by a stony heart driven from the outside, but by a heart of flesh moved from within.

Hebrews 8:10 For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people.

The book of Hebrews quotes the promise of Jeremiah word for word and applies it to us in Christ. God puts His laws into our mind and writes them on our hearts. This is the heart of the New Covenant, and it is the heart of the pronomian frame. The same God who once spoke His law from the mountain now speaks it into the inner man. The law has not changed its character. It has changed its address. Once it stood over us as a witness against our sin. Now it lives within us as the desire of a renewed heart.

What was fulfilled and set aside

We must speak carefully here, because the New Covenant did bring an end to certain things, and we honor the truth by naming them rightly. The priestly service of Aaron, the daily sacrifices, the offerings of bulls and goats, all of these pointed forward to Christ and found their end in Him. They were shadows of the substance, and when the substance came, the shadows had finished their work. Christ offered Himself once for all, and no sacrifice is ever offered again.

Hebrews 10:11–14 And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God; From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool. For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.

This is the careful line the pronomian believer walks, and it keeps him from two ditches. On one side is the man who says the whole law is finished, sacrifices and commandments alike, so the believer is free from the standard of God. On the other side is the man who would drag back the altar and the priesthood, as though Christ had not finished the work once for all. Both have missed it. The sacrificial system is fulfilled and closed in Christ. The moral law of God, His statutes and judgments and commandments, is written on the heart and remains the shape of a faithful life.

Teacher’s Note

When you teach this part, make the distinction out loud before anyone can force a false choice on you. Say plainly that two different things are being discussed under the one word law. The priestly and sacrificial ordinances pointed to Christ and ended in His finished work, never to be repeated. The moral commandments of God, the standard of righteousness, are written on the heart in the New Covenant and remain binding as the shape of the believer's walk.

If a listener cannot tell these two apart, every passage about the law will seem to contradict another. Once he can tell them apart, the whole counsel of God settles into harmony. Keep returning to this distinction. It is the hinge the entire frame turns on.

Part Three

Grace, the Law, and Obedience in Their Right Order

Now we set the three pieces in the order the covenant gives them. The confusion in the church almost always comes from putting one of these pieces where another belongs. We ask the law to do the work of grace, or we ask grace to excuse us from the law, or we ask obedience to purchase what only grace can give. Set each piece in its place and the tension that troubles so many disappears.

Grace is the ground we stand on

Ephesians 2:8–9 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. Romans 5:2 By whom also we have access by faith into this grace wherein we stand, and rejoice in hope of the glory of God.

Grace is first because grace is the ground. A sinner does not work his way into the covenant. He is brought in by the gift of God, received through faith in the finished work of Christ, with nothing of his own to offer and nothing to boast in. This grace is not a single moment we leave behind. It is the ground we stand on, the place we live, the wealth we never earned and can never lose by failing to earn it. Everything else in the believer's life is built on this ground and never replaces it.

The law is the shape of the life we are given

Romans 7:12 Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good. Romans 8:4 That the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh, but after the Spirit.

The law was never the ground of salvation, and it was never meant to be. Its place in the covenant is different and just as good. The law is the shape of the righteous life that God writes into the believer. It tells us what love looks like when it is lived out. It names what is holy and what is sin. When the Spirit causes us to walk after Him, the righteousness of the law is fulfilled in us, not as a debt we pay but as a life we are given. The law describes the very life that grace produces.

Obedience is the walk of a child already in the house

John 14:15 If ye love me, keep my commandments. 1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.

Obedience comes last, not because it matters least, but because it is the fruit and not the root. The believer does not obey to get into the house. He obeys because he has already been brought into the house and given the heart of a child. The keeping of the commandments flows out of love, and the love is itself the gift of God. This is why the commandments are not grievous to the renewed heart. A child obeys his father not to earn a place at the table, but because the place is already his and he loves the one who set it.

The False Frame

Grace and the law are made enemies. To keep grace, the law is thrown out. Or to keep the law, grace is turned into a wage. Obedience is asked to purchase what it can never buy, and the believer is left anxious or careless.

The Pronomian Frame

Grace is the ground, the law is the shape of the life, obedience is the walk of a child. All three sit inside one covenant sealed by Christ. None does the work of another, and the believer stands secure and walks faithfully at once.

The One Word That Settles It

If you want a single word to hold the whole frame, let it be the word given. The covenant is given by God, not earned. The new heart is given, not worked up. The law is given to that heart, written on the inside. The power to obey is given by the Spirit. Even the faith by which we receive it all is given. Once you see that every piece is given, you stop trying to earn what was never for sale, and you stop throwing away what God has freely placed within you.

The man who understands grace does not ask how little he can obey now that he is forgiven. He asks how he can walk worthy of the One who gave him everything. Same covenant, two opposite hearts, and the heart is revealed by the question it asks.

Part Four

How the Believer Stands and Walks in the One Covenant

We come now to the believer himself, standing inside this covenant, and we ask the practical question. How does he live? The answer is that he stands in grace and walks in obedience, and both at once, because both belong to the same covenant. He never has to choose between feeling secure and being faithful. The covenant gives him both in the same breath.

Romans 6:14–15 For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace. What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace? God forbid.

Read both verses together, because people love to quote the first and forget the second. To be under grace and not under the law means we are no longer under the law as a system of earning, no longer crushed beneath a standard we cannot keep in our own strength, no longer condemned by it. But Paul immediately guards the truth from abuse. Does being under grace mean we are free to sin? God forbid. Grace does not release us into lawlessness. It releases us from the law as a taskmaster so that we may keep it as sons, freely and gladly, by the Spirit.

Romans 3:31 Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law. Titus 2:11–12 For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, Teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world.

See how faith and the law are not opposed. Faith does not throw the law out. Faith establishes it, sets it on its true foundation in the heart of the believer. And grace itself is the teacher of holiness. The grace that saves us is the same grace that trains us to deny ungodliness and to live righteously. So the believer who truly understands the covenant is not pulled in two directions. The grace that holds him is the very grace that shapes him.

Hebrews 13:20–21 Now the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant, Make you perfect in every good work to do his will, working in you that which is wellpleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ.

Here is the whole frame in a single blessing. God works in us by the blood of the everlasting covenant. He makes us able for every good work. He produces in us the very will of God. From first to last it is His covenant, His blood, His working, His Spirit. The believer's part is to stand in the grace that holds him and to walk in the obedience the Spirit produces, knowing that the same God who began the covenant will keep it to the end.

Why This Is Not Bondage

Some fear that calling the believer to keep God's commandments drags him back under a yoke. But the yoke Scripture warns against is the attempt to be justified by law-keeping, to earn before God what only Christ can give. That is bondage, for it is a debt no man can pay. To walk in the statutes of God out of a new heart, by the power of His Spirit, freely and gladly, is not bondage at all. It is the freedom of a child in the Father's house, doing the will of the Father because he loves Him and belongs there, not because he is a hired servant afraid of losing his wage.

The Whole Counsel

One Covenant, Held Together in Christ

Bring the pieces together and the frame stands complete. There is one covenant, made by God, sealed in the blood of Christ, and kept by the Spirit. Within that covenant, grace is the ground we stand on, freely given and never earned. The law of God is the shape of the righteous life, written now upon the heart rather than only on stone. Obedience is the walk of a child who has already been brought into the house, fruit of the love that God Himself poured in. The sacrificial system that pointed to Christ is fulfilled and closed forever, and the moral standard He upheld remains, kept now from the inside by a renewed heart.

So when someone asks whether the believer lives by grace or by the law, the pronomian answer refuses the false choice. We live by grace, and that grace writes God's law upon our hearts and produces in us the obedience the law always described. Take away grace and the law becomes a hopeless ladder. Take away the law and grace loses the shape of the life it was meant to produce. Take away obedience and the whole profession is exposed as empty. Held together as the covenant holds them, they are not in tension at all. They are one work of God in Christ, from the first gift to the final walk.

Where It All Rests

We do not earn the covenant; we are brought into it by the blood of Christ. We do not keep ourselves by our own strength; the Spirit writes the law within and causes us to walk in it. Grace is the ground, the law is the shape, obedience is the walk, and Christ is the one who holds them all together.

Stand in the grace that was given you, and walk in the obedience the Spirit produces in you. Both belong to the same covenant, and the God who began it is faithful to keep it to the end.

Hebrews 13:20 Now the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant. Jeremiah 31:33 I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.
