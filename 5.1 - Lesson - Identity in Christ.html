Discipleship Class · Level 1 · Lesson 5

Identity in Christ

A New Creature, and Learning to Walk as One

✦

In our last lesson we sat down and counted the cost of following Jesus. Now we turn to the other side of that coin: what God does inside the person who comes to Him. Many sincere believers live as if nothing really changed when they were saved. They decide who they are by how they feel, by what they used to do, or by what other people say about them. Scripture gives us a truer mirror. It tells us who we are in Christ, and it shows us how to walk in it.

I

A New Creature

2 Corinthians 5:17•Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.

To be in Christ is to be joined to Him in a living union. Paul says the person in that union is a new creature. Notice what the verse does not say. Your face does not change, your height does not change, and your memories are not wiped away. A man who could not carry a tune before he was saved usually cannot carry one the morning after. The change Paul describes happens on the inside, in the heart and spirit of a man. God promised it centuries earlier:

Ezekiel 36:26–27•A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.

Look at what the new spirit is for. God did not give a new heart so that we could feel different and live the same. He gave it to cause us to walk in His statutes. The new creature is a person whose very nature now leans toward God and His ways, with His law written on the heart (Jeremiah 31:33).

II

Spirit, Soul, and Body

1 Thessalonians 5:23•And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly; and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Paul names three things in this prayer. The body is the part everyone can see. The soul is what most people think of as the real you: the mind, the will, and the emotions. Scripture also speaks of the spirit, and the Word of God is sharp enough to divide between soul and spirit (Hebrews 4:12). When God made Adam, He formed a body from the dust, breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and man became a living soul (Genesis 2:7). The Hebrew word for that breath, neshamah, is the same word translated spirit in Proverbs 20:27: The spirit of man is the candle of the LORD. James adds that the body without the spirit is dead (James 2:26). The spirit is the life God breathed into us.

III

Dead in Sins, Made Alive With Christ

Ephesians 2:4–5•But God, who is rich in mercy, for his great love wherewith he loved us, Even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ, (by grace ye are saved;)

Before Christ, Paul says, we were dead in trespasses and sins (Ephesians 2:1). We were still walking and breathing, so he is not talking about the body. At its root, death in Scripture means separation. At the grave the body returns to the dust, and the spirit returns to God who gave it (Ecclesiastes 12:7). Spiritual death is the separation sin brings between a man and his Maker: your iniquities have separated between you and your God (Isaiah 59:2). God told Adam that in the day he ate he would surely die (Genesis 2:17). That same day Adam hid himself from the presence of the LORD (Genesis 3:8), and his body began its long road back to the dust (Genesis 3:19).

But God, rich in mercy, made us alive together with Christ. Jesus called it being born again: that which is born of the Spirit is spirit (John 3:5–6). Because we are sons, God has sent forth the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying, Abba, Father (Galatians 4:6), and that same Spirit bears witness with our spirit that we are the children of God (Romans 8:16).

IV

Washed, Sanctified, Justified

1 Corinthians 6:11•And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.

Paul had just named fornicators, idolaters, thieves, drunkards, and others who shall not inherit the kingdom of God (1 Corinthians 6:9–10). Then he says, such were some of you. That is past tense. Whatever you were, you have been washed clean, set apart for God, and declared righteous. A few verses later he says, he that is joined unto the Lord is one spirit (1 Corinthians 6:17). Your past no longer gets to name you. God has made us accepted in the beloved (Ephesians 1:6), and the mystery hidden for ages is now made known: Christ in you, the hope of glory (Colossians 1:27).

V

Righteousness Given, Righteousness Lived

2 Corinthians 5:21•For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.

Here is the great exchange. Jesus, who knew no sin, was made sin for us, so that we could be made the righteousness of God in Him. Our right standing with God was never earned. It was given, and it is received by faith (Philippians 3:9). Paul keeps that truth side by side with another one: we are saved by grace and not of works, and we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them (Ephesians 2:8–10).

Someone who reads the Bible as it is written does not have to choose between grace and obedience. The righteousness God gives is the root; the righteousness we live out is the fruit. John says it plainly: he that doeth righteousness is righteous, even as he is righteous (1 John 3:7).

VI

Put On the New Man

Ephesians 4:22–24•That ye put off concerning the former conversation the old man, which is corrupt according to the deceitful lusts; And be renewed in the spirit of your mind; And that ye put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness.

God made the new man in a moment, but the mind is renewed over time (Romans 12:2). So Paul tells believers to put off the old man, be renewed in their thinking, and put on the new man every day. God has already made the garment; our part is to wear it. The Word is the mirror that shows us who we are, and the man who looks into the perfect law of liberty and keeps on in it is blessed in his deed (James 1:25).

We have been sealed with the holy Spirit of promise (Ephesians 1:13), and that is exactly why Paul says, grieve not the holy Spirit of God (Ephesians 4:30), and why he tells us to cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit (2 Corinthians 7:1). Knowing who we are in Christ is never a license to live carelessly. It is the reason to live clean: every man that hath this hope in him purifieth himself, even as he is pure (1 John 3:3).

✦ To Carry With You ✦

Your past does not name you, and your feelings do not define you. God does. Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new. 2 Corinthians 5:17

Teacher's Answer Key · Not for Student Distribution

1. What is he, what passed away, and what became new?

A new creature (a new creation). The old things have passed away, and all things have become new. The change is inward, in the heart and spirit, not in a person's looks or memories.

2. What does God give and take away, and what does His Spirit cause?

He gives a new heart and a new spirit, and takes away the stony heart, giving a heart of flesh in its place. His Spirit within us causes us to walk in His statutes, keep His judgments, and do them. The new birth produces obedience.

3. What three parts, what does he pray, and until when?

Spirit, soul, and body. He prays that God would sanctify us wholly and that all three be preserved blameless until the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

4. Our condition, God's riches, what He did, and how we are saved?

We were dead in sins, separated from God. God is rich in mercy because of His great love for us. He made us alive together with Christ, and we are saved by grace.

5. Past, present, or future, and what three things happened?

Were is past. That is who some of us used to be. We were washed, sanctified (set apart and made holy), and justified (declared righteous) in the name of the Lord Jesus and by the Spirit of our God. That is now our present condition.

6. What has God sent, why, and what does the Spirit cry?

The Spirit of His Son, because we are sons. The Spirit cries, Abba, Father. We relate to God as children to a Father, not as strangers.

7. What was Jesus made, and whose righteousness do we have?

He who knew no sin was made sin for us. We are made the righteousness of God, and we have it in Him, in Christ, received by faith.

8. Whose workmanship, created for what, and what are we to do?

God's workmanship. We were created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God ordained beforehand that we should walk in them. Grace saves us, and grace sets us walking.

9. Put off, renewed, put on, and how was the new man created?

Put off the old man, corrupt by deceitful lusts. Be renewed in the spirit of your mind. Put on the new man, which after God is created in righteousness and true holiness. God made the new man; we put it on daily.

10. What are we now, what will we be, and what do we do meanwhile?

We are the sons of God now. When He appears we shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is. Until then, everyone with this hope purifies himself, even as He is pure.

✦ Teacher's Note · “If I’m New, Why Do I Still Struggle?”

Romans 12:2 · Galatians 5:16–17 This question will come, and it is an honest one. God made the new man in a moment, but the mind must be renewed day by day, and the flesh still wars against the Spirit. Struggle is not proof that nothing changed; the old man never fought sin at all. Point students to what the new man was created for, righteousness and true holiness (Ephesians 4:24), and to the promise that God’s Spirit will cause us to walk in His statutes (Ezekiel 36:27). The answer to the struggle is not to doubt the new birth but to walk in the Spirit, feed on the Word, and confess sin quickly when we fall (1 John 1:9).

✦ Teacher's Note · Weighing Some Claims in the Source Material

“In Christ” over 300 times In the KJV New Testament the exact words in Christ appear about 78 times. The count passes 200 only when in him, in whom, and in the Lord are added, and not all of those speak of union with Christ. The point is still true, since Paul leans on this idea constantly, but don't build anything on the number.

Genesis 2:17, spiritual death only? The source says Adam did not die physically that day, only spiritually. He was cut off from God that day (Genesis 3:8), but death also entered his body: unto dust shalt thou return (Genesis 3:19), and he died at nine hundred thirty years (Genesis 5:5). Teach both, as Paul does (Romans 5:12).

“Your spirit did not sin” The source teaches that once sealed, sin cannot touch the spirit, so failure only touches the body and mind. Correct this openly. The very verse used for spirit, soul, and body is a prayer that the spirit be preserved blameless (1 Thessalonians 5:23), which would be needless if it could not be defiled. Paul commands us to cleanse ourselves from filthiness of the flesh and spirit (2 Corinthians 7:1), and the same letter that speaks of sealing warns, grieve not the holy Spirit of God (Ephesians 4:30). The new birth is a finished work of God; abiding in Him is our daily walk (John 15:4–6). When a believer sins, the answer is confession and cleansing (1 John 1:9), not a claim that the spirit was never touched.

Two kinds of righteousness The source says God does not accept us on our own righteousness, which is true (Philippians 3:9). But it then says our lived righteousness matters only to people like a boss or spouse. Scripture says otherwise: he that doeth righteousness is righteous (1 John 3:7), and the fine linen of the saints is their righteousness (Revelation 19:8). The source also quotes Ephesians 2:10 only up to unto good works. The verse goes on, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them. Read it whole.
