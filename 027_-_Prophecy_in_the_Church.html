A Pronomian Teaching

Prophecy in the Church

The Living Voice of God Among His People

✦

Introduction

God has always spoken to His people through His prophets, and He has not changed. From Moses to Samuel, from Isaiah to John, the Lord raised up men and women through whom He declared His word. When the Spirit was poured out at Pentecost, the gift of prophecy was given to the church, and the sons and daughters of God's people began to prophesy as Joel had foretold. The living voice of God among His people did not fall silent with the close of the apostolic age.

Prophets still exist, and God still performs His word. This is the conviction we hold from Scripture, that the gift of prophecy continues in the church until the Lord returns. Yet with this gift comes a solemn duty to discern the true from the false, for the Bible warns again and again of false prophets who speak out of their own hearts. We will look at the reality of prophecy today, the tests by which true and false are known, the order under which it must operate, and the purpose God intends it to serve among His people.

Section I

God Still Speaks: Prophecy Poured Out on the Church

At Pentecost, Peter declared that the outpouring of the Spirit fulfilled the prophecy of Joel, that in the last days God would pour out His Spirit upon all flesh, and His people would prophesy. This was not a gift for one generation only, but for the whole age of the Spirit, for all whom the Lord our God shall call. The gift of prophecy belongs to the church throughout these last days, and we are living in them still.

Acts 2:17–18 And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith God, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams: And on my servants and on my handmaidens I will pour out in those days of my Spirit; and they shall prophesy.
1 Corinthians 12:28 And God hath set some in the church, first apostles, secondarily prophets, thirdly teachers, after that miracles, then gifts of healings, helps, governments, diversities of tongues.
Ephesians 4:11–13 And he gave some, apostles; and some, prophets; and some, evangelists; and some, pastors and teachers; For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ: Till we all come in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ.

Notice that God set prophets in the church, and gave them for the building up of the body till we all come to the fulness of Christ. That work is not yet finished, and so the gifts continue. The same God who poured out His Spirit on the day of Pentecost has not withdrawn His gift. He still speaks to and through His people, and prophets still stand among us, for God still performs His word.

The Gift Continues

The outpouring of the Spirit at Pentecost gave the gift of prophecy to the church for all the last days. God set prophets in His body for its building up, and that work is not yet complete. Prophets still exist, and God still performs His word among His people.

Section II

The First Test: Does It Agree With God's Word and Commandments

Because the gift is real, the danger of counterfeit is also real, and Scripture commands us to test every prophecy. The first and greatest test is whether the word agrees with what God has already revealed. A true word from God will never contradict His written word or lead His people away from His commandments. Moses gave this test plainly, that even if a sign comes to pass, if the prophet draws the people away from obedience to God, he is false.

Deuteronomy 13:1–3 If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder, And the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them; Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams: for the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul.
Isaiah 8:20 To the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them.

This is a vital test, and it is thoroughly in keeping with the truth that God's commandments stand. A prophet may even give a sign that comes to pass, yet if he turns the people from the law and the word of God, he is false and must not be heard. The measure of all prophecy is the written word of God. To the law and to the testimony, says Isaiah, if they speak not according to this word, there is no light in them.

Teacher's Note

Make this test the first and highest. Any prophecy, however moving or impressive, that contradicts the written word of God or turns the people away from His commandments is false, no matter what signs accompany it. Scripture itself is the unchanging standard against which all claimed revelation is measured. No true word of God will ever overturn the word of God already given.

Section III

The Second Test: Does It Come to Pass, and What Fruit Does It Bear

Scripture gives a further test, that the word of a true prophet comes to pass, for God performs the word of His messengers. Alongside this, the Lord taught that prophets are known by their fruits, by the character of their lives and the harvest of their ministry. A true prophet's words prove true, and his life bears the fruit of righteousness, while the false is exposed by failed words and corrupt fruit.

Deuteronomy 18:21–22 And if thou say in thine heart, How shall we know the word which the LORD hath not spoken? When a prophet speaketh in the name of the LORD, if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not spoken, but the prophet hath spoken it presumptuously: thou shalt not be afraid of him.
Isaiah 44:26 That confirmeth the word of his servant, and performeth the counsel of his messengers; that saith to Jerusalem, Thou shalt be inhabited; and to the cities of Judah, Ye shall be built, and I will raise up the decayed places thereof.
Matthew 7:15–16 Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles?

God performs the counsel of His messengers, confirming the word of His true servants. The false prophet speaks presumptuously, and his words fall to the ground. And the Lord adds the test of fruit, for false prophets may wear sheep's clothing, but their inward nature and their works betray them in time. A true prophet's word comes to pass, and his life and ministry bear good fruit unto God.

Known by Their Fruits

God confirms the word of His true servants and performs what they declare in His name. The false prophet's word fails, and his corrupt fruit exposes him though he wear sheep's clothing. By the truth of the word and the fruit of the life, the true is known from the false.

Section IV

Prophecy Must Be Judged and Operate Under Order

Even genuine prophecy in the church is not to be received without examination. Paul gave clear instruction that prophecy is to be judged, weighed, and tested by the assembly, and that it must operate in order and not in confusion. The prophet is subject to the church and its leaders, not above them. The gift never sets a man beyond accountability, for the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets.

1 Corinthians 14:29–31 Let the prophets speak two or three, and let the other judge. If any thing be revealed to another that sitteth by, let the first hold his peace. For ye may all prophesy one by one, that all may learn, and all may be comforted.
1 Corinthians 14:32–33 And the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets. For God is not the author of confusion, but of peace, as in all churches of the saints.
1 Thessalonians 5:19–21 Quench not the Spirit. Despise not prophesyings. Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.

See the balance Paul strikes. We are not to despise prophesyings nor to quench the Spirit, yet we are to prove all things and hold fast only what is good. The prophet speaks, and the others judge. The gift operates under the order and authority of the church, in peace and not in confusion, for God is not the author of confusion. No true prophet resents being weighed, for he too desires that only the genuine word of God stand.

Judged, Not Unaccountable

Genuine prophecy is to be judged and proved by the church, operating in order under its leaders. The spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets. We neither despise prophecy nor receive it blindly, but prove all things and hold fast what is good.

Section V

The Purpose of Prophecy: Edification, Exhortation, and Comfort

God gives prophecy for a purpose, and that purpose is the good of His people. Paul tells us plainly that prophecy serves to build up, to stir up, and to comfort the church. It is not given to exalt the prophet, to satisfy curiosity, or to display wonders, but to edify the body of Christ and draw hearts to God. True prophecy strengthens the saints and turns them toward the Lord.

1 Corinthians 14:3 But he that prophesieth speaketh unto men to edification, and exhortation, and comfort.
1 Corinthians 14:24–25 But if all prophesy, and there come in one that believeth not, or one unlearned, he is convinced of all, he is judged of all: And thus are the secrets of his heart made manifest; and so falling down on his face he will worship God, and report that God is in you of a truth.

Mark the purpose, edification, exhortation, and comfort. And see its power even over the unbeliever, who, hearing the word, is convicted, falls down, and worships God, declaring that God is truly among His people. Prophecy that comes from God builds up the saints, calls them to faithfulness, comforts the weary, and reveals the presence of God so that hearts are turned to Him. This is the fruit of the gift rightly used.

For the Building of the Body

Prophecy is given to edify, exhort, and comfort the church, not to exalt the one who speaks. Its aim is the strengthening of the saints and the turning of hearts to God. When it is genuine, even the unbeliever is convicted and falls down to worship, knowing God is present.

Section VI

Humility, Love, and the Supremacy of Scripture

Finally, all prophecy must be held in humility and exercised in love, and it must always bow to the supremacy of the written word of God. The prophet knows in part and prophesies in part, and so must walk humbly, never claiming a perfection that belongs to Scripture alone. The gift without love profits nothing, and any word that exalts itself above the word of God is to be rejected.

1 Corinthians 13:9 For we know in part, and we prophesy in part.
1 Corinthians 13:2 And though I have the gift of prophecy, and understand all mysteries, and all knowledge; and though I have all faith, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing.
2 Peter 1:19–20 We have also a more sure word of prophecy; whereunto ye do well that ye take heed, as unto a light that shineth in a dark place, until the day dawn, and the day star arise in your hearts: Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation.

Peter calls the Scripture a more sure word of prophecy, the fixed light to which we do well to take heed. All present prophecy stands beneath it and is measured by it. The one who prophesies must do so in humility, knowing he sees only in part, and in love, without which even the greatest gift is nothing. Held this way, under the authority of Scripture and in the bond of love, prophecy remains a blessing to the church and a means by which God still speaks to His people.

Beneath the More Sure Word

Prophecy is exercised in humility, for we know and prophesy only in part, and in love, without which the gift is nothing. It always stands beneath the more sure word of Scripture, which is the fixed and final standard. So held, prophecy blesses the church and honors God.

Conclusion

God Still Speaks, and His Word Stands Sure

The God who spoke through the prophets of old poured out His Spirit upon the church at Pentecost, and He has not withdrawn the gift. Prophets still exist, and God still performs His word among His people. Yet because the gift is real, the counterfeit is real also, and Scripture commands us to test every word. The true is known by its agreement with God's written word and commandments, by its coming to pass, and by the good fruit of the one who speaks it.

Genuine prophecy is to be judged by the church, operating under order and authority, never above accountability, and always for the edification, exhortation, and comfort of the saints. It is exercised in humility and love, and it bows always to the more sure word of Scripture, which is the fixed and final standard. So let us neither despise prophesyings nor receive them blindly, but prove all things and hold fast that which is good, that the living voice of God might continue to build up His people until the day dawns and the day star arises in our hearts.

Despise not prophesyings. Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.
