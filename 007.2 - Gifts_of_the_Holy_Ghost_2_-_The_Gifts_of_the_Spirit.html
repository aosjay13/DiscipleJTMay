Gifts of the Holy Ghost · Lesson Two

The Gifts of the Spirit

The Nine Manifestations of the Spirit in First Corinthians Twelve

✦

Introduction

In our first lesson we saw the five callings the risen Christ gave to His church. Apostle, prophet, evangelist, pastor, and teacher were given to equip the saints, to build up the body, and to bring the people of God into the full stature of the Lord. Those callings answer the question of how the body is led and matured. Now we turn to a different question. Once the body is equipped, how does the Spirit work through its members?

That is where this second lesson begins. Christ gave leaders to equip the saints, and the Holy Ghost pours out gifts through the saints themselves, so that the equipped body can actually function. In First Corinthians chapter twelve, Paul lists nine of these gifts, calling them manifestations of the Spirit. They are not talents a person develops on his own and they are not rewards for being more holy than others. They are workings of one and the same Spirit, given to serve the whole body. In this lesson we will look at all nine, understand what each one does, and see the order in which they were meant to operate.

Section I

Concerning Spiritual Gifts

Paul opens the subject by saying that he does not want the church to be uninformed about these gifts. They are too important to be left to guesswork or to be ignored.

1 Corinthians 12:1 Now concerning spiritual gifts, brethren, I would not have you ignorant.

Before he lists a single gift, Paul sets down a test that anchors everything that follows. The Holy Spirit always exalts the lordship of Christ. Any working that drags people away from Him is not the Spirit of God.

1 Corinthians 12:3 ...no man can say that Jesus is the Lord, but by the Holy Ghost.

The First Test

The true gifts of the Spirit always honor the lordship of Christ and line up with His word. This is the measure by which everything that claims to be of the Spirit must be weighed.

Section II

One Spirit, Many Gifts, Given to Profit All

Paul stresses that the gifts are many but their source is one. There is variety in the gifts, variety in how they are carried out, and variety in their results, yet behind all of it stands the one God working through His one Spirit.

1 Corinthians 12:4-6 Now there are diversities of gifts, but the same Spirit. And there are differences of administrations, but the same Lord. And there are diversities of operations, but it is the same God which worketh all in all.

Then he tells us the reason these gifts are given, and it is not personal display. The Spirit manifests Himself through a believer for the good of everyone present.

1 Corinthians 12:7 But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal.

Finally, Paul makes clear who decides which gift goes to whom. The believer does not assign these to himself, and no one earns them. The Spirit distributes them as He chooses.

1 Corinthians 12:11 But all these worketh that one and the selfsame Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will.

Three Settled Truths

The gifts come from one Spirit. They are given to profit the whole body, not to exalt the one who carries them. And they are distributed as the Spirit wills, not as a reward we can earn or a prize we can claim.

Section III

A Way to See the Nine

Paul lists the nine gifts together in a single passage. Reading them side by side helps us see the whole picture before we take each one in turn.

1 Corinthians 12:8-10 For to one is given by the Spirit the word of wisdom; to another the word of knowledge by the same Spirit; To another faith by the same Spirit; to another the gifts of healing by the same Spirit; To another the working of miracles; to another prophecy; to another discerning of spirits; to another divers kinds of tongues; to another the interpretation of tongues.

A simple and time honored way to study these nine is to sort them into three groups of three. One group reveals, one group acts in power, and one group speaks. This grouping is a teaching aid rather than a command of Scripture, but it helps us hold all nine in view and understand how each serves the body.

Section IV

The Revelation Gifts

These three gifts reveal something the believer could not have known on his own. They make plain what God knows, so that His people are guided, warned, and protected.

The Word of Wisdom a fragment of God's wisdom for a present need

This is not the same as the general wisdom we are told to ask for in prayer. It is a supernatural utterance of God's wisdom for a specific moment, showing what to do or how to proceed when human understanding is not enough. It often gives direction for the road ahead.

We see it when the early church faced a sharp dispute and the leaders, gathered together, reached a settled answer that brought peace and order to the whole body.

Acts 15:28 For it seemed good to the Holy Ghost, and to us, to lay upon you no greater burden than these necessary things.
How It Serves Gives God's direction and counsel for a present situation, showing the body what to do when natural wisdom falls short.
The Word of Knowledge a fragment of God's knowledge of facts unknown to the speaker

This gift reveals facts that the believer had no natural way of knowing. It is a piece of God's own knowledge, given to meet a need, to confirm His care, or to expose what is hidden. The Lord showed it plainly when He spoke to the woman at the well.

John 4:18 For thou hast had five husbands; and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband: in that saidst thou truly.

It also appears when Peter knew, without being told, that Ananias and Sapphira had lied about their gift to the church. The knowledge came not from investigation but from the Spirit.

How It Serves Reveals facts no one could know naturally, to guide, to comfort, or to bring hidden things into the light.
Discerning of Spirits to perceive the source behind a working or a word

This gift gives the ability to perceive what spirit is truly at work, whether something comes from God, from a person's own flesh, or from an evil spirit. It guards the body from deception, which is why Scripture commands us to test the spirits.

1 John 4:1 Beloved, believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world.

Paul exercised this gift at Philippi when he perceived the evil spirit behind a slave girl who followed him, even though her words sounded true on the surface.

How It Serves Perceives the true source behind words and works, protecting the body from deception and false ministry.
Section V

The Power Gifts

These three gifts do not merely reveal or speak. They act. Through them God displays His power, meeting needs that no human strength could meet.

The Gift of Faith a sudden, God-given confidence to believe Him for a specific thing

This is not the saving faith that brings us to Christ, which every believer already has. This is a special surge of faith given for a moment, a confidence that does not waver in the face of impossibility. It often rises just before God acts in power.

We see it in Peter at the temple gate, who spoke with unshakable certainty to a man who had never walked.

Acts 3:6 ...silver and gold have I none; but such as I have give I thee: In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk.
How It Serves Supplies unwavering confidence in God for a specific need, standing firm where natural faith would falter.
The Gifts of Healing supernatural healings of sickness and infirmity

Notice that Paul writes gifts of healing, in the plural. Each healing is its own gift from God, given as He wills. Through this gift the Lord restores the body, just as He did again and again in the ministry of Jesus and the apostles.

Acts 10:38 ...who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil; for God was with him.

Healing is always the work of God's mercy, not a power the believer owns or controls. The glory belongs to the Lord who heals.

How It Serves Brings God's healing to the sick and afflicted, as a mercy from His hand and a witness to His power.
The Working of Miracles acts of power that go beyond the ordinary course of nature

This gift brings about works that natural law cannot explain, displays of God's power that reveal His hand at work. We see it throughout Scripture, from the deliverance at the sea to the mighty works in the book of Acts, such as the raising of Dorcas.

Acts 9:40 ...and turning him to the body said, Tabitha, arise. And she opened her eyes: and when she saw Peter, she sat up.

Miracles bear witness to the living God and confirm His word, drawing eyes upward to Him rather than to the one through whom the power flowed.

How It Serves Displays God's power beyond natural means, confirming His word and turning hearts toward Him.
Section VI

The Vocal Gifts

These three gifts speak. Through them the Spirit gives utterance for the building up of the body. The last two, tongues and interpretation, will receive their own full lesson next, so here we introduce them in their place among the nine.

Prophecy Spirit-given utterance that builds up the body

We met prophecy in our first lesson as a calling, but here it appears as a gift the Spirit may give more widely. Its purpose is plainly stated, and it is always for the good of God's people.

1 Corinthians 14:3 But he that prophesieth speaketh unto men to edification, and exhortation, and comfort.

True prophecy builds up, stirs up, and comforts, and it must always agree with the word already given. A word that turns people away from the truth of Scripture is not from the Spirit of God.

How It Serves Speaks God's word to edify, exhort, and comfort the body, always tested against Scripture.
Divers Kinds of Tongues speaking in languages given by the Spirit

This gift is the speaking of languages the Spirit supplies, whether languages known to other people or utterance unknown to the speaker. When tongues are spoken aloud in the gathered assembly, Scripture teaches that they are to be interpreted so that everyone is built up.

1 Corinthians 14:5 ...greater is he that prophesieth than he that speaketh with tongues, except he interpret, that the church may receive edifying.

We will study this gift carefully in our next lesson, from what happened at Pentecost, to its place in private prayer, to its right use in the assembly.

How It Serves Gives Spirit-supplied utterance, which in the assembly is joined to interpretation so the body is edified.
Interpretation of Tongues to render the meaning of a message spoken in tongues

This gift makes a message in tongues understood, so that what was spoken can edify the whole gathering. It is not a word for word translation in the natural sense, but a Spirit-given rendering of the meaning. Paul therefore instructs that one who speaks in tongues in the assembly should pray that he may interpret.

1 Corinthians 14:13 Wherefore let him that speaketh in an unknown tongue pray that he may interpret.

Tongues and interpretation work as a pair in the public meeting, and together they edify the body just as prophecy does. This too we will take up fully in the next lesson.

How It Serves Renders the meaning of a message in tongues, so the whole assembly is built up and not left without understanding.
Section VII

The Gifts Serve the Body

Right after listing the nine gifts, Paul turns to the picture of the body. He does this on purpose. The gifts were never meant to set one believer above another. They were given so that the many members, each with a different working, would serve one another as one body.

1 Corinthians 12:12 For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of that one body, being many, are one body: so also is Christ.

No member can claim to stand on its own, and no member may be despised. The eye cannot tell the hand that it is not needed, and the head cannot tell the feet that it has no use for them. Every gift supplies something the others do not.

1 Corinthians 12:21 And the eye cannot say unto the hand, I have no need of thee: nor again the head to the feet, I have no need of you.

The Body's Order

The gifts are tools for serving one another in one body. They do not make a believer more loved by God or more saved than another. They are given so that the body, joined together, is built up in love.

Section VIII

The More Excellent Way

Paul ends the chapter by pointing past the gifts themselves to something greater. He tells the church to desire the best gifts, and then immediately lifts their eyes to a higher path.

1 Corinthians 12:31 But covet earnestly the best gifts: and yet shew I unto you a more excellent way.

That more excellent way is love, and the whole of the next chapter is given to it. Paul says plainly that a believer may speak with tongues, prophesy, understand all mysteries, and have faith to move mountains, yet without love it all amounts to nothing.

1 Corinthians 13:1-2 Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal... and have not charity, I am nothing.

Here we hold the right balance. The gifts are real, good, and meant to be desired, but they are not the measure of a person's standing with God. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, and no gift adds anything to it. The gifts are tools for service, while the abiding mark of a Spirit-led life is the fruit of the Spirit, which is love, joy, peace, and the rest. A believer may carry a gift and still need to grow in love. So we seek the gifts to serve the body, and above all we walk in love, for that is the way that never fails.

Gifts and Fruit Are Not the Same

It is wise to keep a clear line between the gifts of the Spirit and the fruit of the Spirit. A gift is given in a moment to serve others, and a person may operate in one without being mature. Fruit grows slowly in a yielded heart and reveals true character. We are never told to test a believer by how many gifts he carries. We are told to know people by their fruit. So pursue the gifts for the good of the body, but let love and Christlike character be the thing you treasure most.

Closing

One Spirit, Working in All

The nine gifts of First Corinthians twelve are the workings of one and the same Spirit, given to the members of the body so that the equipped church can serve and be built up. Some reveal, some act in power, and some speak, yet all of them flow from one Spirit, distributing to every man as He will, and all of them are given to profit the whole.

We have placed each gift in its setting and seen the order in which they were meant to operate. In our next lesson we will take up the last two in close detail, the gift of tongues and the interpretation of tongues. We will trace them from the day of Pentecost, where many languages were spoken and heard, to their place in private prayer, and to their right use in the gathered assembly, where one speaks and another interprets, that the church may be edified.

Teacher's Guide

Answer Key

✦

For the teacher. This page prints after the worksheet for your reference during class discussion.

  1. Why is the manifestation given, and who benefits?
    It is given to every man to profit withal, meaning for the common good of the whole body. The gifts are not for private display or to exalt the one who carries them. They are tools for serving everyone present.
  2. Who decides which gifts a believer receives?
    The Spirit Himself, dividing to every man severally as he will. The believer does not assign these to himself, and no one earns them by being more holy. They are distributed by the sovereign choice of the Spirit.
  3. What are the three revelation gifts, and what does each reveal?
    The word of wisdom reveals God's wisdom and direction for a present situation. The word of knowledge reveals facts no one could know naturally. Discerning of spirits reveals the true source behind a word or work, whether of God, of the flesh, or of an evil spirit.
  4. Word of knowledge versus word of wisdom, with examples.
    The word of knowledge reveals facts (Jesus knew the woman at the well had five husbands, John 4:18). The word of wisdom shows what to do with what is known, giving God's direction (the settled answer at the Jerusalem council, Acts 15:28). Knowledge informs, wisdom directs.
  5. How is the gift of faith different from saving faith?
    Saving faith brings a person to Christ and belongs to every believer. The gift of faith is a sudden, God-given surge of confidence for a specific moment, an unwavering certainty that often rises just before God acts in power, as with Peter at the temple gate (Acts 3:6).
  6. Why "gifts of healing," plural?
    Because each healing is its own gift from God, given as He wills, rather than a single power the believer holds and controls. Healing is always the work of God's mercy, and the glory belongs to the Lord who heals.
  7. What does discerning of spirits do and protect against?
    It perceives the true spirit at work behind words and deeds, and it protects the body from deception and false ministry. This is why Scripture commands us to try the spirits whether they are of God (1 John 4:1). Paul used it at Philippi to perceive the evil spirit in the slave girl.
  8. Why the body picture, and why "no need of thee"?
    Paul turns to the body to show that the gifts were never meant to set one believer above another. The body is one with many members, each supplying what the others do not. No member can stand alone or be despised, so the eye cannot say to the hand, nor the head to the feet, "I have no need of thee" (1 Corinthians 12:21).
  9. What is the more excellent way, and the gifts' worth without it?
    The more excellent way is love (charity), the subject of chapter thirteen. Without love, even tongues, prophecy, all knowledge, and mountain-moving faith amount to nothing (1 Corinthians 13:1-2). The believer becomes as sounding brass.
  10. Do gifts prove salvation or spirituality? How do they relate to grace and fruit?
    No. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, and no gift adds to it. Gifts are tools for service given in a moment, and a person may carry one without being mature. The abiding mark of a Spirit-led life is the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23), which grows in a yielded heart. We know people by their fruit, not by their gifts.
