A Message on Faith

Abraham and Sarah: Unwavering Faith

How Doubt, Laughter, and Human Weakness Became the Very Portrait of Faith

✦

Preface: The Original Story

Abraham did not start out as a hero of faith. He started out as an old man in a foreign land, being told by God to leave his home, his family, and everything familiar, and go to a place God had not even named yet. Genesis 12 opens with that call, and Abraham obeyed it. He packed up and left, trusting a promise he could not see fulfilled and could not fully understand.

Years passed, and the promise God gave him, that he would become a great nation with descendants beyond counting, still had not come to pass. He and Sarah had no child. In Genesis 15, Abraham finally said what he was thinking. He told God plainly that he did not see how the promise could happen, since he remained childless and his own servant looked set to become his heir. God did not scold him for saying it out loud. He simply took him outside, pointed to the night sky, and told him to count the stars if he could. Then Scripture records the turning point of the whole story. Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him for righteousness.

But belief did not mean the doubt disappeared for good. In Genesis 16, tired of waiting on God's timing, Abraham and Sarah tried to make the promise happen on their own, through Sarah's servant Hagar. It caused real pain in their household and consequences that lasted for generations. Then in Genesis 17, when God repeated the promise and said Sarah herself would bear the child, Abraham fell on his face laughing, thinking to himself that a man of a hundred years and a wife of ninety could hardly have a son. In Genesis 18, when the Lord's messengers said the same thing to Sarah, she laughed too, from inside her tent, then denied it when she was confronted.

And yet the child came. Isaac was born exactly as God had said, to two people who had doubted, laughed, and tried to force the promise their own way. That is the real story of Abraham and Sarah. Not two people with flawless confidence, but two people who kept coming back to trust God even after doubting Him, and who watched Him keep His word anyway.

Part One

Romans 4: Faith That Did Not Waver

Paul looks back at Abraham to show what real faith looks like, and he does not skip over the hard part. He names the exact thing Abraham had to overcome, his own body worn out and as good as dead, and Sarah's womb, which by every natural measure was also dead.

Romans 4:18–21 Who against hope believed in hope, that he might become the father of many nations, according to that which was spoken, So shall thy seed be. And being not weak in faith, he considered not his own body now dead, when he was about an hundred years old, neither yet the deadness of Sara's womb. He staggered not at the promise of God through unbelief, but was strong in faith, giving glory to God, and being fully persuaded that, what he had promised, he was able also to perform.

What This Means

Paul is not describing a man who never questioned anything. He is describing a man who looked straight at impossible circumstances and chose to trust God's word over what his own eyes and body told him. That is the model for anyone who doubts themselves today. Not the absence of hard facts working against you, but a decision to believe God anyway.

Part Two

Hebrews 11: Faith Counted Even Through Sarah

Hebrews gives Sarah her own line in the chapter of faith, and that matters. She is not just mentioned as part of Abraham's story, she is commended for her own faith by name.

Hebrews 11:8–9, 11–12 By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went. By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country, dwelling in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise. Through faith also Sara herself received strength to conceive seed, and was delivered of a child when she was past age, because she judged him faithful who had promised. Therefore sprang there even of one, and him as good as dead, so many as the stars of the sky in multitude, and as the sand which is by the sea shore innumerable.

What This Means

Sarah laughed too, back in Genesis 18. But Hebrews does not hold that against her. It says she judged him faithful who had promised. The doubt happened, and the faith still gets the final word in her story. That is worth saying plainly to anyone doubting themselves tonight. A doubting moment is not the end of your story either.

Part Three

James 2: Faith Proven by Action

James points to Abraham to show that real faith moves. It is not only something Abraham felt inwardly, it is something he did, and that action is what showed his faith was genuine.

James 2:21–23 Was not Abraham our father justified by works, when he had offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Seest thou how faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect? And the scripture was fulfilled which saith, Abraham believed God, and it was imputed unto him for righteousness; and he was called the Friend of God.

What This Means

James is not contradicting Paul here, he is completing the picture. Abraham's faith was not only a private belief, it moved him to actually act on what God said, even at Mount Moriah with Isaac. For someone struggling with self-doubt, this matters because it shows faith does not need to feel strong before it moves. Abraham obeyed, and the obedience is what showed his faith was real.

Part Four

Galatians 3: The Blessing Passed Down Through Faith

Paul ties every believer back to Abraham directly, not through bloodline, but through the same kind of faith.

Galatians 3:6–7, 9 Even as Abraham believed God, and it was accounted to him for righteousness. Know ye therefore that they which are of faith, the same are the children of Abraham. So then they which be of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham.

What This Means

This is the connecting point for your congregation. They are not distant spectators of Abraham's story, they are called his children through the very same kind of faith he had, doubts, laughter, mistakes, and all. If Abraham's faith counted, and it is the same faith available to them, their doubt does not disqualify them either.

Part Five

1 Peter 3: Sarah as an Example for Believers

Peter holds Sarah up directly as a model, and the principle of trust without being ruled by fear applies to the whole church, not to any one group alone.

1 Peter 3:5–6 For after this manner in the old time the holy women also, who trusted in God, adorned themselves, being in subjection unto their own husbands: even as Sara obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, whose daughters ye are, as long as ye do well, and are not afraid with any amazement.

Teaching Note

The phrase "not afraid with any amazement" is worth sitting on. Sarah's faith was not about having no fear ever. It was about not letting fear control her in the end. That is a realistic, achievable picture of faith for someone who battles self-doubt. Not fearlessness, but fear that does not get the final say.

Tying It Together

Doubt Was Never Allowed to Be the Final Word

The New Testament writers had every reason to leave out the uncomfortable parts of Abraham and Sarah's story, the doubt in Genesis 15, the plan with Hagar, the laughing in Genesis 17 and 18. They did not. They looked at the whole life and still called it faith.

That is the point to land for anyone doubting themselves tonight. Scripture does not require a spotless record of confidence before it calls something faith. It requires a heart that keeps turning back to trust God, even after doubting, even after getting it wrong, even after laughing at the promise. Abraham is called the father of faith, and Sarah is commended by name, not because they never doubted, but because doubt was never allowed to be the final word.

Consider closing on Galatians 3:9, "they which be of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham." Your church people are not distant admirers of a great man's faith. They are heirs of that same kind of faith, doubts included.
