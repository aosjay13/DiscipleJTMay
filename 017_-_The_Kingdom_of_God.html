A Pronomian Teaching

The Kingdom of God

The Reign of God, Now and Not Yet

✦

Introduction

When the Lord Jesus began to preach, the first word out of His mouth was the kingdom. He came proclaiming that the kingdom of God was at hand, and He taught His disciples to pray that the kingdom would come. From beginning to end the gospel is the good news of a King and His reign. To understand the kingdom of God is to understand the great theme that runs through the whole of Scripture, the rule of God breaking into a rebel world to reclaim it for Himself.

The kingdom of God is the reign of God. It is His kingly rule, His authority owned and obeyed. There is a sense in which that kingdom is already here, present wherever God is truly King over willing hearts. And there is a sense in which it is still to come, awaiting the day when Christ returns and every knee shall bow. We will hold both together, the kingdom now and the kingdom not yet, that the believer may live as a faithful subject of the King in the days before the King appears.

Section I

The Kingdom Is the Reign of God

The kingdom of God is first of all the rule of God Himself. Before it is a place, it is a reign. God is King over all that He has made, and His throne is established for ever. The kingdom is where His will is owned and done, where His authority is honored, where He governs as the rightful Lord of all. This rule is eternal, for God has always reigned and always will.

Psalm 103:19 The LORD hath prepared his throne in the heavens; and his kingdom ruleth over all.
Psalm 145:13 Thy kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and thy dominion endureth throughout all generations.
Daniel 4:34 And I blessed the most High, and I praised and honoured him that liveth for ever, whose dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom is from generation to generation.

Mark the breadth of it. His kingdom ruleth over all, and His dominion endures throughout all generations. The kingdom is not a small or distant thing. It is the everlasting rule of the living God over the whole of His creation. When we speak of the kingdom coming, we do not mean that God begins to reign where He never reigned before, but that His rightful rule is owned and submitted to where rebellion once held sway.

Reign Before Realm

The kingdom of God is the kingly rule of God. It is His authority honored and His will obeyed. God has always been King over all, and the coming of His kingdom is the spread of His owned and willing rule among those who once stood in rebellion.

Section II

The King Has Come

The Old Testament looked forward to a King from the line of David whose throne would have no end. When Jesus came, the long expectation was answered. The angel told Mary that her Son would receive the throne of David and reign for ever. In Christ the King Himself stepped into the world, and the kingdom drew near in His own Person.

Luke 1:32–33 He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest: and the Lord God shall give unto him the throne of his father David: And he shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.
Mark 1:14–15 Now after that John was put in prison, Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God, And saying, The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel.

Hear the King's own announcement. The time is fulfilled, the kingdom of God is at hand, and the call goes out to repent and believe. The works of Jesus proved the kingdom had drawn near, for where He cast out devils and healed the sick, the power of the King was breaking the grip of the enemy. Wherever He went, the rule of God advanced against the kingdom of darkness.

Matthew 12:28 But if I cast out devils by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God is come unto you.

The Kingdom Drew Near in Him

In the coming of Jesus the King Himself arrived. The throne promised to David is His, and His reign has no end. Where He worked, the rule of God broke in upon the works of darkness, and men were called to repent and believe the gospel of the kingdom.

Section III

Entering the Kingdom: The New Birth and Repentance

How does a man come under the rule of God and enter His kingdom? Not by birth into a nation, nor by any work of the flesh, but by being born again. The kingdom is entered through the new birth, which God works in the heart by His Spirit, and through repentance and faith in the King. The proud and the self-righteous shut themselves out. The kingdom is opened to those who come as little children, owning their need and bowing to the King.

John 3:3, 5 Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God... Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.
Matthew 18:3 And said, Verily I say unto you, Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven.
Colossians 1:13 Who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son.

See what happens at conversion. God delivers the sinner from the power of darkness and translates him into the kingdom of His dear Son. He changes kingdoms. He leaves the dominion of the enemy and comes under the rule of Christ. This is the present and personal entrance into the kingdom, a real change of allegiance worked by God in every soul that is born again.

A Change of Kingdoms

To be saved is to be moved from one kingdom to another. The believer was under the power of darkness, and God has carried him over into the kingdom of His dear Son. Salvation is not only the forgiveness of sins but the changing of a man's King and the changing of the realm he belongs to.

Section IV

The Kingdom Present Among His People

There is a true sense in which the kingdom of God is already present in this age. It is not yet present in its fullness, with the King reigning visibly over all the earth, but it is present wherever God is owned as King in the hearts and lives of His people. The kingdom is among us now, working quietly like leaven and growing like a seed, advancing in the world though it is not yet seen in its glory.

Luke 17:20–21 And when he was demanded of the Pharisees, when the kingdom of God should come, he answered them and said, The kingdom of God cometh not with observation: Neither shall they say, Lo here! or, lo there! for, behold, the kingdom of God is within you.
Romans 14:17 For the kingdom of God is not meat and drink; but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost.

The present form of the kingdom is not yet outward splendor but inward reality, righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Ghost. Where God rules in a heart, the kingdom is there, bearing the fruit of His rule. The Lord taught that the kingdom in this age grows from small beginnings, like the smallest of seeds becoming a great tree, spreading through the world before the day of its open glory.

Mark 4:30–32 And he said, Whereunto shall we liken the kingdom of God? or with what comparison shall we compare it? It is like a grain of mustard seed, which, when it is sown in the earth, is less than all the seeds that be in the earth: But when it is sown, it groweth up, and becometh greater than all herbs, and shooteth out great branches; so that the fowls of the air may lodge under the shadow of it.

The Kingdom Now

The kingdom is already present wherever God is owned as King. It comes not yet with outward observation but in righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost. It grows from small beginnings, spreading like seed and leaven through the world before the day of its open glory.

Section V

The Kingdom Yet to Come in Glory

Though the kingdom is present now in part, its fullness is still to come. The day is appointed when Christ shall return in power and glory, and the kingdom shall be seen by all. Then the rule of God will no longer be hidden or owned only by the faithful few, but openly established over all the earth. The Lord taught us to pray for this very day, that the kingdom would come and the will of God be done on earth as it is in heaven.

Matthew 6:10 Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.
Daniel 7:13–14 I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.
Revelation 11:15 And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.

This is the great hope set before us. The kingdoms of this world shall become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ, and He shall reign for ever and ever. When Christ returns, He will establish His kingdom in open power, and the saints shall reign with Him. The reign of God hidden now will be made manifest then, and every knee shall bow to the King.

Revelation 20:6 Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years.

Teacher's Note

The fullness of the kingdom comes at the return of Christ, who appears after the tribulation and before the outpouring of God's wrath to gather His people and establish His reign. We hold this hope with confidence but without setting dates, for the Lord said no man knows the day or the hour. Watch and be ready, and let the certainty of the coming kingdom shape how we live, rather than chasing after times and seasons the Father has kept in His own power.

Section VI

Living as Subjects of the King

If we belong to the kingdom of God, then we are to live as faithful subjects of the King even now, in the days before His open reign. We seek His kingdom first, above every earthly concern. We obey His commandments, for a kingdom is governed by the law of its King. And we walk in righteousness, looking for the day when faith gives way to sight and we see our King face to face.

Matthew 6:33 But seek ye first the kingdom of God, and his righteousness; and all these things shall be added unto you.
Matthew 7:21 Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven.

The King draws a clear line. It is not the one who merely calls Him Lord who enters the kingdom, but the one who does the will of the Father. To own Christ as King is to obey Christ as King. The subjects of His kingdom are marked out by lives that bow to His rule and keep His commandments, not as a way to earn entrance, but as the true and living proof that they belong to Him.

2 Peter 1:10–11 Wherefore the rather, brethren, give diligence to make your calling and election sure: for if ye do these things, ye shall never fall: For so an entrance shall be ministered unto you abundantly into the everlasting kingdom of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.

Seek First the Kingdom

The citizen of the kingdom seeks it first, above all earthly things, and shows he belongs to the King by doing the will of the Father. To call Christ Lord is to obey Him as Lord. Faithful subjects look for the everlasting kingdom and live now in glad submission to its King.

Conclusion

The King Reigns, and the King Is Coming

The kingdom of God is the reign of God, eternal and over all. In Christ the King came near, breaking the power of darkness and calling men to repent and believe. Those who are born again are delivered from the dominion of the enemy and translated into the kingdom of His dear Son. So the kingdom is here now, present wherever God is owned as King, growing quietly through the world in righteousness and peace and joy in the Holy Ghost.

Yet the fullness is still to come. When Christ returns in glory, the kingdoms of this world shall become the kingdoms of our Lord, and He shall reign for ever and ever. We wait for that day with confidence, watching and ready, without grasping after dates the Father has kept in His own hand. Until then we seek His kingdom first, obey our King, and live as faithful subjects of the One who reigns now and is surely coming to reign in open glory.

Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven.
