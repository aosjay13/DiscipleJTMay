A Doctrinal Foundation

Grace, Obedience, and the Law’s Role

How the believer is saved, and how the believer walks

✦

The Tension Before Us

Few questions cause more confusion in the church than this one. If we are saved by grace alone, apart from our works, then why does Scripture call us to keep God’s commandments? And if we are called to keep His commandments, then how can we say we are saved by grace alone? Many feel pulled between two cliffs. On one side stands the fear of earning salvation by our own effort. On the other stands the fear of a careless faith that names Christ but ignores His ways.

The good news is that Scripture never asks us to choose between grace and obedience. They are not rivals. They answer two different questions. Grace answers the question, how is a sinner saved. Obedience answers the question, how does a saved man live. When we keep these two questions clear, the tension dissolves, and the whole counsel of God stands in perfect harmony.

This teaching sets the matter in order. First we establish that grace is the sole mechanism of salvation. Then we show that obedience is the fruit and evidence of a living faith, never its purchase price. Then we set the law in its proper place, neither thrown away nor turned into a ladder to heaven. We close by showing how grace, far from excusing disobedience, is the very power that produces it.

Part One

Grace Is the Mechanism of Salvation

Begin where Scripture begins, with the helplessness of man. No person has ever stood before God on the strength of his own record. The law is holy and good, yet no sinner has kept it well enough to be declared righteous by it, because the law shows us our sin without giving us the power to undo it. Salvation, then, had to come as a gift, or it could not come at all.

Ephesians 2:8–9 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast. Titus 3:5 Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost.

Notice how plainly the Spirit guards against boasting. Salvation is a gift, it is not of ourselves, it is not of works. The reason is stated outright, so that no man may boast. If a single sinner could earn his place by keeping commandments, then heaven would ring with the praise of men rather than the praise of God. The whole design of the gospel is built to take the credit out of our hands and lay it upon Christ alone.

Romans 3:20 Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin. Romans 11:6 And if by grace, then is it no more of works: otherwise grace is no more grace. But if it be of works, then is it no more work: otherwise work is no more work.

Romans draws the line as sharply as it can be drawn. Grace and earning cannot mix as a means of being saved. If you add your works to the scale as payment, you have not improved grace, you have cancelled it, for grace by its very nature is unearned. This is the bedrock. A man is brought from death to life by the mercy of God, received through faith in the finished work of Jesus Christ, and by nothing he has done or could do.

The Settled Foundation

Salvation is by grace through faith in Christ alone. It is received, never achieved. Obedience does not buy it, water baptism does not seal it, and good works do not maintain it as a wage. Whatever we say in the rest of this study, we never move this stone.

Part Two

Obedience Is the Fruit and Evidence of Faith

If grace alone saves, a fair question follows. Why then does Scripture speak so constantly of obedience, of keeping the commandments, of doing the will of God? The answer is that obedience is not the root of salvation; it is the fruit. A living faith works. It cannot help but work, because the same God who saves a man also changes him and writes His ways upon the heart. Where there is true saving faith, obedience follows as surely as fruit follows a healthy tree.

James 2:17–18 Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone. Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works. James 2:26 For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.

James does not contradict Paul. Paul tells us how a man is justified before God, by faith apart from the works of the law. James tells us how that faith is proven before men and tested in real life, by the works it produces. A faith that never moves the hands or the feet is not a second kind of faith; it is a dead faith, a corpse that has the shape of belief but none of its life. Works do not give faith its life. Works show that the life is there.

1 John 2:3–4 And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him. John 14:15 If ye love me, keep my commandments.

Here is the test of a true profession. The man who claims to know God yet has no care for His commandments has shown that his claim is empty. Obedience is the evidence by which love is known. Jesus did not say, keep my commandments and I will love you. He said, if you love me, you will keep them. The keeping flows from the love, which itself is the gift of God poured into a heart that has been saved.

Matthew 7:16–18 Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles? Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit. A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit.

The picture of the tree settles the whole matter. Fruit does not make a tree alive; fruit proves that the tree is already alive. A man is not made a good tree by hanging fruit on his branches. He is made a good tree by the grace of God, and then the good fruit appears as the natural sign of what he has become. Obedience is the fruit, not the seed.

Grace: The Mechanism

Answers: how is a sinner saved? Received by faith, apart from works, so that no man may boast. It is the gift, the cause, the foundation, and it never changes with our performance.

Obedience: The Evidence

Answers: how does a saved man live? Produced by the faith that saved him, shown in keeping God’s commandments. It is the fruit, the proof, the sign that the life of God is truly present.

Teacher’s Note

Hold the order firmly when you teach this, because the entire balance rests on it. Grace produces obedience; obedience never produces grace. The moment you reverse the order, you have either built a works religion or excused a dead faith. Keep saying it plainly: we are saved to obey, we are not saved by obeying.

Watch for the listener who hears “faith without works is dead” and concludes that works keep him saved. Gently correct it. James is giving a test of whether faith is real, not a second payment added to the price Christ already paid in full.

Part Three

The Proper Role of the Law

If we are not saved by keeping the law, some conclude the law has no further use, and that the believer may set it aside. Scripture will not allow this. The law was never given as a way to earn salvation; that was never its purpose, and it cannot do what it was never meant to do. Its true roles remain, and they are good.

The law defines sin

Without the law we would not even know what sin is. It is the standard that names right and wrong, the mirror that shows a man his true condition. This role does not end when a man is saved; it continues to guide him in the way of righteousness.

Romans 7:7 What shall we say then? Is the law sin? God forbid. Nay, I had not known sin, but by the law: for I had not known lust, except the law had said, Thou shalt not covet. 1 John 3:4 Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.

Read those two verses together and a clear truth appears. Sin is defined as the breaking of God’s law. If the law were abolished, then sin itself would have no meaning, for there would be nothing left to transgress. The very fact that Scripture still calls us away from sin proves that the standard which defines sin still stands.

The law is holy, just, and good

Romans 7:12 Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good. 1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.

The law is not the enemy of grace; it is the description of a righteous life. The problem was never with the law, which is holy and good. The problem was always with us, who could not keep it in our own strength. Grace does not lower the standard. Grace gives us a new heart that delights in the standard and a new power to walk in it.

Love is the fulfilling of the law

Romans 13:8, 10 Owe no man any thing, but to love one another: for he that loveth another hath fulfilled the law... love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the fulfilling of the law. Matthew 5:17–18 Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.

Some read the word fulfil and think it means abolish. It means the opposite. To fulfil the law is to fill it full, to live it out completely, to show what it looks like when kept perfectly in love. Jesus did not come to throw the law away; He came to embody it and to give His people the power to walk in it. And love, far from setting the commandments aside, is the very thing that keeps them, for love does no harm to a neighbor and so fulfils all that the law requires.

Teacher’s Note

When you teach the ongoing role of the law, draw the careful distinction that keeps you on solid ground. The moral commandments of God, the standard of righteousness shown in the Ten Commandments and confirmed throughout the prophets and the apostles, remain binding as the shape of a faithful life. What was fulfilled and set aside in Christ were the Aaronic and Levitical priestly ordinances and the animal sacrifices, which pointed forward to Him and find their end in His finished work.

Make this distinction yourself before a listener forces a false choice on you. The man who says “the law is done away” and the man who says “you must keep the sacrifices” have both missed it. Christ ended the shadow; the moral standard He upheld remains.

What the Law Can and Cannot Do

The law cannot justify a sinner, cannot give life, and cannot save. That was never its work. What it can do is show us our sin, define righteousness, guide the believer’s walk, and reveal the character of the God we love. The error is not in keeping the law; the error is in trusting it to save.

Part Four

Grace Is the Power That Produces Obedience

We come now to the heart of the matter, the truth that finally dissolves the tension. Grace and obedience are not opponents straining against each other. Grace is the very power by which obedience becomes possible. The old covenant gave the law on tablets of stone, outside the man. The new covenant writes that same law upon the heart, inside the man, so that he obeys not by gritted teeth but by a renewed nature.

Titus 2:11–12 For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world.

Read carefully what grace does. The grace of God does not teach us to live as we please. It teaches us to deny ungodliness and to live soberly and righteously. Grace is not the permission to sin; it is the schoolmaster of holiness. The man who has truly received grace has received the very thing that turns him from his old ways and trains him in the ways of God.

Hebrews 8:10 For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people. Ezekiel 36:26–27 A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you... And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.

This is the great promise of the new covenant, and it answers everything. God does not merely forgive and then leave us to struggle alone against an impossible standard. He gives a new heart, He puts His Spirit within, and He causes us to walk in His statutes. The obedience He requires, He also supplies. So the believer keeps the commandments not to become a son, but because he has been made a son and given the heart of one.

Romans 6:1–2 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein? Romans 3:31 Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.

Paul anticipates the very abuse some try to make of grace. If grace covers sin, shall we sin all the more so grace may abound? His answer is the strongest he can give, God forbid. A man who is truly dead to sin cannot make sin his home. And faith does not throw out the law; faith establishes it, sets it on its true foundation, and writes it where it belongs, upon a willing heart.

The Question That Reveals the Heart

There is a simple way to tell whether a person has understood grace. Ask what grace makes him want to do. The man who has misunderstood it asks, how much can I get away with now that I am forgiven? The man who has truly received it asks, how can I please the God who saved me when I deserved nothing? Same grace, two opposite responses, and the response tells you which heart has actually been changed.

Grace abused as a license to sin is a sign that grace was never truly received, for the grace of God teaches the very opposite. Where grace has done its work, the heart longs to walk in the ways of the One who showed such mercy.

Why This Is Not a Return to Bondage

Some fear that calling believers to obedience drags them back under a yoke of bondage. But the bondage Scripture warns against is the attempt to be justified by law-keeping, to earn before God what only Christ can give. That is bondage indeed, for it is a debt no man can pay. To walk in God’s commandments out of a new heart, by the power of His Spirit, freely and gladly, is not bondage at all. It is the freedom of a son in his Father’s house, doing the will of the Father because he loves Him, not because he fears being cast out as a hired servant who failed to earn his wage.

The Whole Counsel

Saved by Grace, Walking in Obedience

Bring the three threads together and the tension is gone. A sinner is saved by grace through faith in Christ alone, with nothing of his own to offer and nothing to boast in. That faith, being a living faith, produces obedience, the fruit and evidence that the life of God is truly present. And the law, never a ladder to heaven, remains the holy standard that defines sin, guides the walk, and reveals the character of the God we love. Grace gives the new heart, the Spirit gives the power, and the believer walks in the commandments of God as a son, not a slave.

So when someone presses the question, are we saved by grace or by obedience, the faithful answer refuses the false choice. We are saved by grace alone, and that same grace makes us obedient. Take away grace and obedience becomes a hopeless effort to earn what cannot be earned. Take away obedience and faith is exposed as dead, a profession with no life behind it. Held together as Scripture holds them, they are not in tension at all. They are the cause and the fruit of one saving work of God.

Where It All Rests

We do not obey in order to be saved. We obey because we have been saved. The cross paid a debt we could never pay, and the Spirit now produces in us the obedience the law always called for. Grace is the root, obedience is the fruit, and the law remains the holy standard that both honor.

Let no one use grace to excuse sin, and let no one use obedience to claim a wage. Receive the gift with empty hands, and then walk worthy of the calling, by the power of the One who saved you.

Ephesians 2:10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them. 1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.
