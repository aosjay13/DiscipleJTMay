A Core Pronomian Foundation

The Gospel and Obedience

Reconciling grace alone with the call to obey, and how a living faith cannot help but bear the fruit of keeping God’s commandments

✦

The Apparent Contradiction

Put two verses side by side and many believers feel the ground shift under them. Paul says a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law. James says faith without works is dead. One seems to say works have no part in salvation. The other seems to say works are everything. Set them as enemies and you will spend your life torn between them, leaning first toward a grace that excuses everything and then toward a striving that earns nothing. The trouble is not in the verses. The trouble is in reading them as if they answered the same question.

The gospel is the good news that God saves sinners by grace, as a free gift, received through faith in the finished work of Christ. That is settled and it never moves. Yet the same gospel calls every believer to obey, to keep the commandments, to walk in the ways of God. How can both be true at once? How can salvation be entirely a gift and the obedient life still be required? This is the question that has unsettled sincere believers for centuries, and it has a clear and beautiful answer.

The answer is found in the nature of saving faith itself. Faith is not a bare opinion that sits quietly in the mind. Living faith is a trust that takes hold of Christ and is joined to Him, and being joined to Him, it cannot help but bear His fruit. Obedience is not a second thing added on to faith to complete the transaction. Obedience is what living faith looks like when it breathes and moves. This teaching will show that grace alone and the call to obey are not rivals at all. They are the root and the fruit of one living thing.

Part One

The Gospel Is Grace Alone, and It Does Not Move

We lay the foundation first, and we lay it deep, because everything in this teaching is built on it. The gospel saves by grace alone. A sinner brings nothing to the table but his sin. He cannot pay, cannot earn, cannot contribute even the smallest part of his rescue. Salvation comes to him as a gift, freely given by God and received by faith. If we ever blur this, we have lost the gospel itself, and no amount of talk about obedience can put it back.

Ephesians 2:8–9 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. Romans 4:4–5 Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt. But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.

Paul could not make it plainer. Salvation is a gift, not of ourselves, not of works, and the reason is stated openly, so that no man may boast. Then he draws the sharpest possible line. If you work for it, the reward is a debt owed to you, a wage you have earned. But salvation is not a wage. It is given to the one who works not, but believes. God justifies the ungodly, not the deserving, and He counts faith for righteousness. There is no room here for earning. The gift is free or it is not a gift at all.

Titus 3:5 Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost.

This is the unmovable stone. We are not saved by works of righteousness which we have done. Not the good deeds we did before we believed, not the obedience we offer after. The cause of our salvation is the mercy of God, applied by the washing of regeneration and the renewing of the Holy Spirit. Hold this firmly, because in the parts that follow we will speak much of obedience, and a careless listener may think we are sneaking works back in as the price of salvation. We are not. The price was paid in full by Christ. What follows is about the life that this free salvation produces, never about the payment, which is finished.

The Stone That Never Moves

Salvation is by grace alone, received through faith, given as a gift, never earned by works. God justifies the ungodly and counts faith for righteousness. Everything this teaching says about obedience is built on this foundation and never replaces it. The gift is free, or it is not grace at all.

Part Two

Saving Faith Is a Living Thing, Not a Bare Opinion

Here is the hinge on which the whole question turns. When the Bible says we are saved by faith, what kind of faith does it mean? This is where so many go wrong. They imagine faith as a single thought in the mind, a bare agreement that certain facts are true. A man says he believes that Jesus is the Son of God, and he supposes that this agreement, all by itself, is saving faith. But Scripture knows a faith like that and gives it a name. It calls it the faith of devils.

James 2:19 Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.

The demons hold perfectly correct beliefs about God. They know He is one. They know who Christ is. Their theology is exact, and it saves them not at all. Why? Because their belief is mere agreement with a fact, and it has not changed them, not bowed them, not joined them to God in trust and love. Saving faith is something altogether different. It is not the cold agreement that a thing is true. It is a living trust that lays hold of Christ, leans the whole weight of the soul upon Him, and is joined to Him as a branch to the vine.

John 15:5 I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing. Galatians 5:6 For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love.

See how Paul himself describes the faith that matters. It is faith which worketh by love. Not idle faith, not bare opinion, but a faith that works, and works by love. And the picture of the vine shows why. A branch joined to the vine does not strain to produce grapes. The life of the vine flows into it, and fruit comes as the natural result of the union. So it is with the believer. Joined to Christ by living faith, the life of Christ flows in, and obedience grows out, not by gritted effort to earn anything, but as the fruit of a living connection.

Teacher’s Note

This is the single most important point in the whole study, so plant it firmly and return to it often. The entire apparent contradiction dissolves the moment a person sees that saving faith is living trust, not bare agreement. Devils agree with the facts and are not saved. The believer trusts and is joined to Christ, and the joining bears fruit.

When someone struggles with Paul and James, do not start by comparing the two verses. Start here, with the nature of faith. Once a listener sees that the faith Paul praises is the same living, working faith that James describes, the two apostles are no longer at odds. They were never describing two different faiths. They were describing the same living faith from two sides.

Two Kinds of Belief

There is a belief that merely agrees with facts, and the devils have it. And there is a living faith that trusts Christ, leans on Him, and is joined to Him. Only the second saves, and the second cannot help but bear fruit, because it is union with the living Christ. The kind of faith decides everything.

Part Three

How Paul and James Agree Perfectly

Now we can take up the two apostles who are so often pitted against each other, and we will find they stand in full agreement. The key is to see that they are answering two different questions. Paul asks how a sinner is justified before God. James asks how a living faith is known and proven. Once you see the two questions, the two answers fit together without a seam.

Romans 3:28 Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law. James 2:24 Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only.

At first glance these seem to collide head on. But look closely at the words. Paul speaks of justification before God, the verdict that declares a sinner righteous, and he says it comes by faith apart from the works of the law. He is talking about how we are accepted. James speaks of a faith that stands the test of real life, and he says a faith proven by works is the only faith that justifies a man in the sense of showing him to be truly righteous. James is talking about how true faith is demonstrated. Paul looks at the root. James looks at the fruit. Both are needed, and they never contradict.

James 2:17–18 Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone. Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works.

Hear what James actually says about the faith he rejects. He calls it dead. He does not say works are added to a living faith to make it save. He says a faith without works was never alive to begin with. It is a corpse, having the shape of faith but none of its life. James issues a challenge that cannot be met. Show me your faith without your works. It cannot be done, for faith that produces nothing has nothing to show. But faith that lives can be seen, because it works. James and Paul are not two voices in conflict. They are one voice describing the root and the fruit of the same living tree.

Paul: The Root

Answers how a sinner is justified before God. By faith, apart from the works of the law, received as a gift so no man may boast. He is looking at the cause of our acceptance, which is grace laid hold of by faith.

James: The Fruit

Answers how a living faith is known and proven. By the works it produces, since a faith that bears no fruit was never alive. He is looking at the evidence of true faith, which is obedience flowing from the root.

Abraham settles it for both of them, and it is striking that both apostles use the very same man. Paul points to Abraham believing God and being counted righteous, before he had done any work, as proof that righteousness comes by faith. James points to Abraham offering up Isaac, years later, as proof that his faith was a living faith that worked. The same Abraham, the same faith, seen first at its root and then at its fruit. He was justified by faith, and that faith showed itself alive by obedience. There is no quarrel between the apostles, only the two ends of one living faith.

Part Four

Why Obedience Necessarily Follows

We have seen that living faith bears fruit. Now we ask why this is not merely likely but certain. Why must obedience follow saving faith, every time, without exception? The answer is that salvation is not only a pardon written in heaven. It is also a change worked in the heart. The same act of God that forgives the sinner also makes him new, gives him a new heart, and puts His Spirit within. A changed heart cannot help but produce a changed life.

Ezekiel 36:26–27 A new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony heart out of your flesh, and I will give you an heart of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them.

This is the promise that makes obedience certain. God does not merely forgive and then leave the sinner to fight an impossible battle in his old strength. He gives a new heart and puts His Spirit within, and He names the result with His own mouth. He will cause us to walk in His statutes and to keep His judgments and do them. The obedience is not something we manufacture to prove ourselves. It is something God produces in the heart He has remade. Where He has truly given the new heart, the walking in His statutes follows, because He has promised to cause it.

Philippians 2:13 For it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure. Ephesians 2:10 For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

Look at how completely the work belongs to God. He works in us both the willing and the doing. We are His workmanship, created in Christ for good works that He prepared beforehand for us to walk in. The very verse that follows the great statement of grace, saved by grace through faith not of works, immediately says we were created for good works. Grace does not leave us empty of works. Grace creates us unto works. The good life is not the price of the gift. It is the purpose of the gift.

1 John 2:3–4 And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.

This is why obedience also gives us assurance. John tells us how we may know that we truly know God. We know it if we keep His commandments. The keeping does not earn the knowing. The keeping proves the knowing is real. So a believer troubled about whether his faith is genuine need not stare endlessly into his own heart. He can look at the fruit. Is there a love for God and a turning toward His ways that was not there before? That fruit is the evidence that the root is alive. And a man who claims to know God while caring nothing for His commandments is told plainly that his claim is false.

The Question That Tells the Truth

There is a simple way to test whether grace has truly been received. Ask what it makes a person want. The one who has misunderstood grace asks how much he can get away with now that he is forgiven. The one who has truly received it asks how he can please the God who saved him when he deserved nothing. Same grace, two opposite hearts, and the response reveals which heart has actually been changed.

This is why a faith that uses grace as a license to sin is a faith that was never alive. The grace of God teaches the very opposite, training us to deny ungodliness and to live righteously. Where grace has done its true work, the heart longs to walk in the ways of the One who showed such mercy. The longing itself is the fruit of the root.

The Whole Counsel

The Root and the Fruit of One Living Thing

The apparent contradiction is gone once we see the whole picture. The gospel saves by grace alone, as a free gift received through faith, and this never changes. But the faith that receives the gift is a living faith, a trust that lays hold of Christ and is joined to Him, not the bare agreement that the devils have. And living faith, being union with the living Christ, cannot help but bear His fruit. Obedience is not a second payment added to grace. It is the natural and certain result of a heart that grace has made new.

So Paul and James stand together, the one showing the root and the other the fruit of the same tree. We are justified before God by faith apart from works, and that very faith proves itself alive by the works it produces. The believer keeps the commandments of God not to purchase a salvation already given, but because the God who saved him has written His law on the heart and put His Spirit within, causing him to walk in the ways of his Lord. Grace is the gift. A new heart is part of the gift. And obedience is what the new heart does. Take away grace and obedience becomes a hopeless effort to earn the unearnable. Take away obedience and the faith is exposed as dead. Held together as the gospel holds them, they are simply the root and the fruit of one living thing.

Where It All Rests

We are saved by grace alone, through a faith that is alive. A living faith is joined to Christ, and union with Christ bears the fruit of obedience as surely as a branch in the vine bears grapes. We do not obey to be saved. We obey because we have been saved and made new. Grace is the root, obedience is the fruit, and both belong to the one work of God.

Never use the gift to excuse sin, and never turn obedience into a wage. Receive salvation with empty hands, and then let the living faith within you do what living faith always does. It works by love.

Galatians 5:6 For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision availeth any thing, nor uncircumcision; but faith which worketh by love. James 2:26 For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also.
