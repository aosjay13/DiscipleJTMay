Pagan Holidays

A Scriptural Warning · Tried by Scripture, Traced through History

✦

Preamble

This study sets forth, by the plain testimony of Scripture and the witness of history, why the popular holidays of the world are rooted in pagan worship and the traditions of men. God does not leave the manner of His worship to human invention. He commands His people to flee the customs of the heathen and to keep only the days He Himself has appointed. What follows examines each holiday in turn, showing both its origin and the word of God that stands against it.

Foundation

The Standard of Worship

Before any single holiday is examined, the governing rule must be settled. God commands His people not to imitate the religious practices of the nations, and not to borrow the forms of false worship and apply them to Him. This is the very heart of the matter. When Israel made the golden calf, the people called it a feast unto the LORD, yet God counted it as sin, because the form of worship had been borrowed from the nations. A pagan custom does not become holy by attaching God's name to it. We worship God the way He commands, not the way the heathen served their gods, and not by adding our own traditions on top of His word.

Nimrod and Babel

The Root in Babylon

The tangle of pagan worship behind these holidays has a single source. After the flood, Nimrod, the mighty hunter before the LORD, built the first kingdom of the world, and the beginning of that kingdom was Babel in the land of Shinar. From Babel his power spread to Erech, Accad, and on into the cities of Assyria. Babel was the cradle of organized rebellion against God, the place where men raised a tower to make a name for themselves, and from there the LORD scattered them across the face of the earth. They carried the seeds of Babylon's false worship into every land they entered.

At the center of that worship stood a god and a goddess, a divine pair that surfaces again and again under changing names. The god was worshipped among the Canaanites as Baal, the lord of the storm and the sun. The goddess was Ashtoreth, the queen of heaven, known in Babylon as Ishtar and to the Greeks as Astarte. Scripture names the two of them together as the idols Israel kept turning back to, for they forsook the LORD and served Baal and Ashtaroth. Wherever the nations spread, this god-and-goddess pattern spread with them.

Within the worship of this goddess runs a mother-and-child theme, the queen of heaven and her divine son. In the traditions that grew up around Babylon, the queen was remembered by the name Semiramis, the wife of the kingdom's founder and the mother of the child-god. That son was identified with Tammuz, the dying-and-rising god of the turning seasons, the same Tammuz that God showed the prophet Ezekiel being mourned by women at the very door of His temple. The image of the goddess and her son was carried from Babylon into Egypt, into Greece, and into Rome, long before it was ever dressed in the clothing of the church.

Here is the fountain from which the rest flows. The winter birthday of the sun, the spring rites of the fertility goddess and her son, the cakes baked for the queen of heaven, and the festivals of the dead all rise out of the worship that began at Babel. When God calls His people to come out from among the nations, He is calling them out of Babylon and out of all her customs.

Genesis 11:9 Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.

Jeremiah 44:17 ...to burn incense unto the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her, as we have done, we, and our fathers, our kings, and our princes...
Revelation 17:5 And upon her forehead was a name written, MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH.
The Winter Solstice

Christmas

The twenty-fifth of December was not the day of Christ's birth, and the early believers did not observe it. The date was the day the Romans kept the birthday of the unconquered sun, the chief festival of their sun worship, set at the turning of the winter solstice. Just before it came the Roman festival of revelry held in mid to late December, with its feasting, gift giving, and license. The evergreen tree, the burning log, the holly, and the mistletoe all carry pre-Christian European associations with solstice observance and the worship of nature.

Scripture itself argues against the season. At the time of Christ's birth the shepherds were in the open field, keeping watch over their flocks by night, which points away from the cold and rain of late December. The deeper problem is not the calendar but the union of the name of Christ with the customs of sun worship and the traditions of men. The prophet Jeremiah describes the very practice the heathen used, cutting a tree out of the forest, fastening it so that it cannot move, and adorning it with silver and gold.

Scriptural References

Luke 2:8 And there were in the same country shepherds abiding in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night.
Jeremiah 10:2–4 Thus saith the LORD, Learn not the way of the heathen... For the customs of the people are vain: for one cutteth a tree out of the forest, the work of the hands of the workman, with the axe. They deck it with silver and with gold; they fasten it with nails and with hammers, that it move not.
Deuteronomy 12:31 Thou shalt not do so unto the LORD thy God: for every abomination to the LORD, which he hateth, have they done unto their gods.
A note on the tree. Jeremiah 10 is describing the carving and adorning of an idol, not a modern Christmas tree directly. It is cited here because it shows the same impulse God condemned, taking a tree from the forest and decking it with silver and gold as an object of devotion. Argue it as the pattern God forbade, not as a literal prophecy of the holiday, and the point cannot be turned aside.
The Spring Fertility Rites

Easter

The English name Easter is tied by the eighth-century writer Bede to a goddess honored in the spring. Whatever the exact source of the name, the season itself was marked across the ancient world by the worship of a fertility goddess, and the customs of that worship are the very ones that gather around the holiday today.

That goddess was known by many names across many lands. In Babylon she was Ishtar; among the Canaanites she was Ashtoreth, whom the Scriptures call the queen of heaven; the Greeks named her Astarte. She was the goddess of fertility, love, and the renewal of spring, and her worship ran through the very nations God told Israel not to imitate. Solomon fell into the worship of Ashtoreth, and Israel turned again and again to serve her. The prophet Jeremiah rebuked the people for kneading dough and baking cakes to the queen of heaven, an offering of spring breads still echoed in the special cakes and buns of the season.

Bound up with this goddess was her consort, Tammuz, a god whose yearly death and supposed return were mourned and then celebrated as the earth came back to life in spring. God showed the prophet Ezekiel women weeping for Tammuz at the very door of His temple, and in the same vision He showed men with their backs to His house, worshipping the sun toward the east. The egg, an old symbol of new life and fertility, and the sunrise service that faces the rising sun, both belong to this worship of spring renewal and the sun, not to the resurrection of Christ.

The text of Scripture makes the substitution plain. The King James Bible uses the word Easter only once, in Acts 12:4, where the original word is the Passover. A pagan term was laid over the biblical feast. The resurrection of Christ is real and central, yet God already gave the feast that points to it. Christ is our Passover, and the day He appointed needs no fertility symbols, no cakes to a goddess, and no sunrise rites added to it.

Scriptural References

1 Kings 11:5 For Solomon went after Ashtoreth the goddess of the Zidonians...
Jeremiah 7:18 ...and the women knead their dough, to make cakes to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto other gods, that they may provoke me to anger.
Ezekiel 8:14 ...behold, there sat women weeping for Tammuz.
Ezekiel 8:16 ...with their backs toward the temple of the LORD, and their faces toward the east; and they worshipped the sun toward the east.
Acts 12:4 ...intending after Easter to bring him forth to the people.
1 Corinthians 5:7 ...For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us.
Deuteronomy 12:30 ...that thou enquire not after their gods, saying, How did these nations serve their gods? even so will I do likewise.
The Feast of the Dead

Halloween

Of all these days, Halloween wears its origin most openly. The thirty-first of October descends from the ancient Celtic festival that marked the end of the harvest and the beginning of the dark half of the year. It was believed that on this night the boundary between the living and the dead grew thin, and that the spirits of the dead returned. The bonfires, the costumes worn to imitate or ward off the dead, and the offerings left out for wandering spirits all belong to this festival of the dead. Centuries later the church placed its day for the saints on the first of November, making the night before All Hallows' Eve, yet the old customs of death, divination, and spirits carried straight through.

There is no way to baptize this day. It honors death, the dead, witchcraft, and divination, and Scripture names every one of these an abomination. The believer is called to have no fellowship with the works of darkness, but to reprove them.

Scriptural References

Deuteronomy 18:10–12 There shall not be found among you any one that maketh his son or his daughter to pass through the fire, or that useth divination, or an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch... or a necromancer. For all that do these things are an abomination unto the LORD.
Leviticus 19:31 Regard not them that have familiar spirits, neither seek after wizards, to be defiled by them: I am the LORD your God.
Galatians 5:19–21 Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these... Idolatry, witchcraft... they which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God.
Ephesians 5:11 And have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them.
The Lupercalia

Valentine's Day

The fourteenth of February is popularly traced to an ancient Roman fertility festival held in mid February, a rite involving sacrifices and customs tied to a pastoral god. The chain from that festival to the modern day is debated among historians, so the stronger point lies elsewhere. The day exalts romantic love through the image of the winged boy with his bow and arrow, who in pagan story is the god of desire, the son of the goddess of love. To set a day on the calendar under that figure is to keep before the eyes a symbol drawn straight from the worship of false gods, and to feed the lust of the flesh and the lust of the eyes that Scripture warns against.

The believer is called not to be conformed to this world. A day built on a pagan love-god and on appetite is the world's pattern, not God's.

Scriptural References

Deuteronomy 12:30–31 ...that thou enquire not after their gods... Thou shalt not do so unto the LORD thy God.
1 John 2:15–16 Love not the world... For all that is in the world, the lust of the flesh, and the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life, is not of the Father, but is of the world.
Romans 12:2 And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind...
A note on the Lupercalia. The link between Valentine's Day and the Roman Lupercalia is a traditional association, not a proven line of descent. Present it as the popular claim, and let the weight of the section fall on the pagan love-god imagery and the call not to be conformed to the world, which are harder to dispute.
The Worship of Janus

New Year's Day

The month of January is named for the Roman god of doorways, beginnings, and endings, pictured with two faces, one looking back and one looking forward. The Romans opened their civil year with rites and revelry in his honor, and the modern New Year still bears his name and his theme of looking back and ahead at the turning of the year. Yet God set the beginning of the year in the spring, at the time of the Passover, by His own reckoning. To begin the year in the dead of winter under the name of a false god follows the calendar of the nations rather than the appointment of God, and God commands that the names of other gods not even be heard in the mouths of His people.

Scriptural References

Exodus 12:2 This month shall be unto you the beginning of months: it shall be the first month of the year to you.
Exodus 23:13 ...and make no mention of the name of other gods, neither let it be heard out of thy mouth.
Deuteronomy 12:32 What thing soever I command you, observe to do it: thou shalt not add thereto, nor diminish from it.
The Call

Come Out and Be Separate

The pattern is unmistakable. Each of these days takes a practice born in the worship of false gods or the customs of the nations and dresses it in religious or sentimental clothing. The name of Christ on a sun-worship date does not sanctify it. A spring fertility rite does not become the resurrection. A festival of the dead does not become harmless because candy is added. God's standard has not changed. He calls His people to prove all things, to hold fast that which is good, and to abstain from all appearance of evil. He calls us to come out from among them and to be separate.

God has not left His people without days to keep. He gave His own appointed times, the Sabbath and the feasts of the LORD, which He calls holy convocations. These are the days the believer is to keep, and they point to Christ from beginning to end. For the full account of these biblical days and how the assembly is to observe them, see the companion document Moedim in the Church and the article on the Moedim in the Bylaws of Faith and Practice.

Scriptural References

2 Corinthians 6:14, 17 Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship hath righteousness with unrighteousness?... Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, saith the Lord.
1 Thessalonians 5:21–22 Prove all things; hold fast that which is good. Abstain from all appearance of evil.
Revelation 18:4 Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues.
Leviticus 23:2 ...Concerning the feasts of the LORD, which ye shall proclaim to be holy convocations, even these are my feasts.
