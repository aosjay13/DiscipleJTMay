From Babylon to America

The Prophecy Movie Analysis

✦

Purpose of this Study

The film From Babylon to America walks the viewer through the whole sweep of human history as a single prophetic story. It begins at the Tower of Babel, runs through the great statue of Daniel 2 and the four beasts of Daniel 7, lands on the two beasts of Revelation 13, and ends with a final test of worship between the seal of God and the mark of the beast. Along the way it makes some very specific claims: that the little horn of Daniel is the papacy, that the beast from the earth is the United States, and that Sunday worship, once it is enforced by law, becomes the mark of the beast.

This study lays out that whole argument from start to finish, fuller than the film itself has room for, with every key passage of Scripture quoted in full so the reader can see exactly what the Bible says. Then each claim is weighed. Some of what the film teaches stands squarely on the plain text, and we will say so gladly, especially about the seventh-day Sabbath. Some of it rests on interpretation that sincere believers have held for centuries but that the text does not demand. And a few of its historical claims rest on later tradition rather than on Scripture or early records. Those are flagged openly in the Teacher’s Notes so that every hearer can test them independently (Acts 17:11).

The Method

I.

How the Film Reads Prophecy

Before looking at any single claim, it helps to know what kind of glasses the film is wearing. Bible students have generally read the prophecies of Daniel and Revelation in four broad ways. The preterist reading sees most of these prophecies as fulfilled in the first century, around the fall of Jerusalem in AD 70. The idealist reading treats them as timeless pictures of the struggle between good and evil, tied to no particular events. The futurist reading holds that the final beast, the mark, and the great tribulation still lie ahead at the close of the age. And the historicist reading, which this film follows, sees the prophecies unfolding step by step across the whole span of history from the prophet’s day to the return of Christ.

Historicism was the dominant Protestant way of reading prophecy from the Reformation into the 1800s. Today it is carried most strongly by Seventh-day Adventist teachers, and the film’s argument follows that line of teaching closely, from the identity of the little horn to the Sunday law scenario at the end.

The engine that drives historicism is called the day-year principle. Where a prophecy gives a period of days, the historicist reads each prophetic day as a literal year. Two passages are usually offered as the foundation for this rule.

The Day-Year Passages

Numbers 14:34 After the number of the days in which ye searched the land, even forty days, each day for a year, shall ye bear your iniquities, even forty years, and ye shall know my breach of promise.
Ezekiel 4:6 And when thou hast accomplished them, lie again on thy right side, and thou shalt bear the iniquity of the house of Judah forty days: I have appointed thee each day for a year.

This ministry reads the last things through a Post-Tribulation, Pre-Wrath framework. In that framework the man of sin is a future figure who is revealed at the close of the age, sits in the temple of God, and makes war on the saints for a short, set season before Christ returns to gather His elect and pour out the wrath of God (2 Thessalonians 2:3–4; Matthew 24:29–31). That means where the film says a prophecy was fulfilled in the Middle Ages, we will often say the Middle Ages gave a foreshadowing of it, while the full weight of it still lies ahead. Holding both a pattern in history and a final fulfillment at the end is not a dodge. Scripture itself does this, as when John says the spirit of antichrist was already at work in his own day while the antichrist was still to come (1 John 2:18).

Teacher’s Note

Notice what Numbers 14:34 and Ezekiel 4:6 actually do. In both places God assigns a day for a year inside a specific judgment, and He says so plainly. Neither passage states a universal rule that every prophetic day everywhere must be read as a year. The strongest support for reading some prophecies in years is Daniel 9:24–27, where the seventy weeks are best understood as weeks of years, reaching to the coming of Messiah the Prince.

So present the day-year principle as a pattern the text allows in certain places, not a key the text demands for every number. When the same period of 1,260 days is also given as 42 months and as time, times, and half a time (Revelation 11:2–3; 12:6, 14; 13:5), the natural reading is a literal three and a half years, which is how our end time study reads it.

Part One of the Film

II.

Babel and the Birth of Idolatry

The film opens after the flood. The descendants of Noah gather on the plain of Shinar, and under the leadership of Nimrod they build the Tower of Babel in open rebellion against God. According to the film, after Nimrod died a violent death his wife Semiramis claimed he had risen as the sun god, and that her son Tammuz had been conceived by divine power. This mother and child religion, the film says, spread from Babylon across the whole earth as the root of pagan sun worship and of every counterfeit virgin birth story, all designed by Satan to muddy the true promise of the Seed of the woman (Genesis 3:15).

Start with what Scripture actually records about Nimrod and Babel.

Nimrod and the Tower

Genesis 10:8–10 And Cush begat Nimrod: he began to be a mighty one in the earth. He was a mighty hunter before the LORD: wherefore it is said, Even as Nimrod the mighty hunter before the LORD. And the beginning of his kingdom was Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar.
Genesis 11:1–4 And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech. And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there. And they said one to another, Go to, let us make brick, and burn them throughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter. And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.
Genesis 11:5–9 And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded. And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do. Go to, let us go down, and there confound their language, that they may not understand one another’s speech. So the LORD scattered them abroad from thence upon the face of all the earth: and they left off to build the city. Therefore is the name of it called Babel; because the LORD did there confound the language of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD scatter them abroad upon the face of all the earth.

Scripture ties Babel to the beginning of Nimrod’s kingdom, but it never says in so many words that he built the tower. That connection comes from ancient Jewish tradition, and the clearest early witness to it is the historian Josephus, writing in the first century.

From the Historical Record

Josephus, Antiquities of the Jews, Book 1, Chapter 4, Section 2 (Whiston translation) “Now it was Nimrod who excited them to such an affront and contempt of God. He was the grandson of Ham, the son of Noah, a bold man, and of great strength of hand. He persuaded them not to ascribe it to God, as if it was through his means they were happy, but to believe that it was their own courage which procured that happiness. He also gradually changed the government into tyranny, seeing no other way of turning men from the fear of God, but to bring them into a constant dependence on his power. He also said he would be revenged on God, if he should have a mind to drown the world again; for that he would build a tower too high for the waters to be able to reach! and that he would avenge himself on God for destroying their forefathers!”

From Babel the rest of the Bible traces a real line of false worship. The goddess called the queen of heaven was honored with cakes and drink offerings in Jeremiah’s day, and God showed Ezekiel women weeping for Tammuz at the door of His own house, with men beside them bowing to the rising sun. By the end of the Bible, Babylon has become the name for the whole world system of false religion, the mother of harlots that makes the nations drunk with her wine.

Babylon’s Worship in Scripture

Jeremiah 7:18 The children gather wood, and the fathers kindle the fire, and the women knead their dough, to make cakes to the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto other gods, that they may provoke me to anger.
Ezekiel 8:14 Then he brought me to the door of the gate of the LORD’S house which was toward the north; and, behold, there sat women weeping for Tammuz.
Ezekiel 8:16 And he brought me into the inner court of the LORD’S house, and, behold, at the door of the temple of the LORD, between the porch and the altar, were about five and twenty men, with their backs toward the temple of the LORD, and their faces toward the east; and they worshipped the sun toward the east.
Jeremiah 51:7 Babylon hath been a golden cup in the LORD’S hand, that made all the earth drunken: the nations have drunken of her wine; therefore the nations are mad.
Revelation 17:5 And upon her forehead was a name written, MYSTERY, BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH.

The film is right that Satan is a counterfeiter. From the first promise in the garden, the enemy has known that a Deliverer would come through the seed of the woman, and he has worked to blur that hope with imitations. Paul warns that Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light (2 Corinthians 11:14).

The Promise Satan Counterfeits

Genesis 3:15 And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.
Isaiah 7:14 Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.
Teacher’s Note

Here is where the film leans on a source that will not bear the weight. Scripture never names a wife for Nimrod, never mentions a son, never describes his death, and never says he was worshipped as a risen sun god. The story that Semiramis was Nimrod’s wife, that she deified him after his death, and that Tammuz was her miraculously conceived son comes to us mainly through Alexander Hislop’s book The Two Babylons (1853, expanded 1858), especially Chapter II, where he builds the case by linking names and symbols across many cultures.

The older records tell a different story. The classical writers make Semiramis the wife of Ninus, the legendary founder of Nineveh, not Nimrod (Diodorus Siculus, Library of History, Book 2, Chapters 4–20), and Herodotus mentions her only briefly as a queen of Babylon (Histories, Book 1, Chapter 184). Historians connect the name to Sammu-ramat, an Assyrian queen who served as regent roughly 811 to 806 BC, many centuries after Babel. And in the old Sumerian and Babylonian texts, Tammuz (Dumuzid) is the husband and consort of the goddess Inanna, known later as Ishtar, not her son, and no ancient text presents him as virgin-born.

So build on what is solid. Babel was a real rebellion, God scattered the nations from there, the queen of heaven and Tammuz were real objects of worship that God called abominations, and Babylon is Scripture’s own name for the whole system of false religion. That case stands firm without Hislop. The Semiramis and Tammuz storyline should be taught, if at all, as a later tradition, clearly labeled, and never as the load-bearing wall. This is the same line our Pagan Holidays series takes.

Part Two of the Film

III.

The Great Image of Daniel 2

In the second year of his reign, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon dreamed a dream that troubled him so deeply he could not sleep, and he demanded that his wise men tell him both the dream and its meaning (Daniel 2:1–5). None could. God revealed it to Daniel in a night vision, and Daniel stood before the king and told him everything. This is the first of the two great outlines of history in the book of Daniel, and the film uses it as the backbone of everything that follows.

The Dream

Daniel 2:31–35 Thou, O king, sawest, and behold a great image. This great image, whose brightness was excellent, stood before thee; and the form thereof was terrible. This image’s head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass, His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay. Thou sawest till that a stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet that were of iron and clay, and brake them to pieces. Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshingfloors; and the wind carried them away, that no place was found for them: and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth.

The Interpretation

Daniel 2:36–40 This is the dream; and we will tell the interpretation thereof before the king. Thou, O king, art a king of kings: for the God of heaven hath given thee a kingdom, power, and strength, and glory. And wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven hath he given into thine hand, and hath made thee ruler over them all. Thou art this head of gold. And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth. And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron: forasmuch as iron breaketh in pieces and subdueth all things: and as iron that breaketh all these, shall it break in pieces and bruise.
Daniel 2:41–45 And whereas thou sawest the feet and toes, part of potters’ clay, and part of iron, the kingdom shall be divided; but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron, forasmuch as thou sawest the iron mixed with miry clay. And as the toes of the feet were part of iron, and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong, and partly broken. And whereas thou sawest iron mixed with miry clay, they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men: but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay. And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever. Forasmuch as thou sawest that the stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, and that it brake in pieces the iron, the brass, the clay, the silver, and the gold; the great God hath made known to the king what shall come to pass hereafter: and the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure.

Daniel himself names the first kingdom: Nebuchadnezzar and his Babylon are the head of gold (Daniel 2:38). The book goes on to name the next two. On the night Babylon fell, the handwriting on the wall announced that the kingdom was given to the Medes and Persians (Daniel 5:28), and in a later vision the angel Gabriel names both Media and Persia and then Grecia outright (Daniel 8:20–21). The fourth kingdom is not named in Daniel, but it is the power that swallowed up the Greek kingdoms and was ruling the world when Christ was born, the Rome of Caesar Augustus (Luke 2:1).

Daniel Names the Kingdoms

Daniel 5:28 PERES; Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians.
Daniel 8:20–21 The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia. And the rough goat is the king of Grecia: and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king.
Luke 2:1 And it came to pass in those days, that there went out a decree from Caesar Augustus, that all the world should be taxed.

The metals get cheaper as they go down, from gold to silver to brass to iron, but they get harder and stronger. Each empire was less glorious than the last and more crushing. Then the feet and toes show the fourth kingdom divided, part strong and part brittle, peoples mingled together by marriage and alliance but never truly joined. The film reads the feet as the broken pieces of the Western Roman Empire after its fall in AD 476, the nations of Europe that have never been welded back into one empire despite every attempt.

Then comes the stone cut out without hands. It is a kingdom God sets up Himself, not built by human effort, and it does not reform the image or blend with it. It strikes the feet, grinds the whole image to powder, and grows into a mountain that fills the earth. This is the kingdom of God, which Christ announced at His first coming and will bring in full at His return, when the kingdoms of this world become the kingdoms of our Lord and of His Christ (Revelation 11:15).

Teacher’s Note

The sequence of Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, and Rome is one of the oldest and most widely held readings in the church. Jerome, writing around AD 407, gives exactly this order in his Commentary on Daniel at chapter 2, and the Reformers held it almost to a man. Where the film and this ministry part ways is not the four kingdoms but the timing of the feet and toes.

Notice that the stone strikes the image on its feet, and that God sets up His kingdom in the days of these kings (Daniel 2:44). A futurist reading sees the ten toes as a final ten-king arrangement that stands at the very end, in the days when Christ returns, which matches the ten kings of Revelation 17:12 who have received no kingdom as yet but rule one hour with the beast. Both readings agree on the main point: the line of human empires runs straight to the kingdom of God, and nothing man builds will stand when that stone falls.

A small note on wording: the film’s summary says bronze. The King James Bible says brass. Both refer to the same kind of copper alloy, but when quoting the text in class, use the text.

Part Two of the Film

IV.

The Four Beasts of Daniel 7

About fifty years after Nebuchadnezzar’s dream, in the first year of Belshazzar, God gave Daniel himself a vision that covers the same ground from a different angle. Nebuchadnezzar saw the empires as a man sees them, a gleaming image of precious metals. Daniel saw them as God sees them, a parade of devouring beasts rising out of a storm-tossed sea.

The Vision

Daniel 7:1–4 In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon Daniel had a dream and visions of his head upon his bed: then he wrote the dream, and told the sum of the matters. Daniel spake and said, I saw in my vision by night, and, behold, the four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea. And four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another. The first was like a lion, and had eagle’s wings: I beheld till the wings thereof were plucked, and it was lifted up from the earth, and made stand upon the feet as a man, and a man’s heart was given to it.
Daniel 7:5–8 And behold another beast, a second, like to a bear, and it raised up itself on one side, and it had three ribs in the mouth of it between the teeth of it: and they said thus unto it, Arise, devour much flesh. After this I beheld, and lo another, like a leopard, which had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl; the beast had also four heads; and dominion was given to it. After this I saw in the night visions, and behold a fourth beast, dreadful and terrible, and strong exceedingly; and it had great iron teeth: it devoured and brake in pieces, and stamped the residue with the feet of it: and it was diverse from all the beasts that were before it; and it had ten horns. I considered the horns, and, behold, there came up among them another little horn, before whom there were three of the first horns plucked up by the roots: and, behold, in this horn were eyes like the eyes of man, and a mouth speaking great things.

The angel explains the beasts in a single line: they are four kings, or kingdoms, which rise out of the earth (Daniel 7:17). The lion with eagle’s wings is Babylon, the king of beasts and the king of birds together, and its wings being plucked and a man’s heart given to it recalls Nebuchadnezzar being humbled and then restored with a sound mind (Daniel 4:33–34). The bear raised up on one side is Medo-Persia, a union of two peoples in which the Persians came to dominate the Medes, and the three ribs in its mouth are commonly taken as its three great conquests, Lydia, Babylon, and Egypt. The leopard with four wings is Greece under Alexander, who conquered the known world with astonishing speed, and its four heads are the four kingdoms his generals carved out of his empire after his death in 323 BC, exactly as Daniel’s later vision describes (Daniel 8:8, 22).

The fourth beast is different. Daniel can find no animal to compare it to. It has iron teeth, matching the legs of iron in the image, and it crushes and stamps what it does not devour. It has ten horns, and out of them rises a little horn with eyes like a man and a mouth speaking great things. The film identifies this beast as Rome, and the ten horns as the tribal kingdoms that took over the Western Empire as it crumbled.

The Judgment and the Son of Man

Daniel 7:13–14 I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him: his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed.
Daniel 7:17–18 These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth. But the saints of the most High shall take the kingdom, and possess the kingdom for ever, even for ever and ever.

Jesus took the title Son of man from this very chapter and applied it to Himself before the high priest (Matthew 26:64). The beasts rise out of the sea and are destroyed. The Son of man comes with the clouds of heaven and receives a kingdom that never ends, and He shares it with His saints. The two visions, image and beasts, line up like this.

Teacher’s Note

The film’s dates are sound as historical markers. Babylon fell to Cyrus in 539 BC. Alexander broke Persia at the battle of Gaugamela in 331 BC. Rome crushed the Macedonian kingdom at the battle of Pydna in 168 BC, which is the usual marker for Rome’s dominance over the Greek world. And the last emperor in the West was deposed in AD 476. Keep in mind that the eastern half of the empire continued at Constantinople until 1453, so the date of 476 marks the breakup of Rome in Western Europe specifically, which is where the historicist reading focuses.

Part Two of the Film

V.

The Little Horn and Its Ten Marks

Daniel was not satisfied to leave the fourth beast alone. He wanted to know the truth about it, and above all about the little horn that rose among the ten. The angel’s answer is one of the most detailed character sketches in all of prophecy, and the film builds its identification of the little horn on it point by point.

Daniel Asks About the Fourth Beast

Daniel 7:19–22 Then I would know the truth of the fourth beast, which was diverse from all the others, exceeding dreadful, whose teeth were of iron, and his nails of brass; which devoured, brake in pieces, and stamped the residue with his feet; And of the ten horns that were in his head, and of the other which came up, and before whom three fell; even of that horn that had eyes, and a mouth that spake very great things, whose look was more stout than his fellows. I beheld, and the same horn made war with the saints, and prevailed against them; Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom.
Daniel 7:23–25 Thus he said, The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth, which shall be diverse from all kingdoms, and shall devour the whole earth, and shall tread it down, and break it in pieces. And the ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings that shall arise: and another shall rise after them; and he shall be diverse from the first, and he shall subdue three kings. And he shall speak great words against the most High, and shall wear out the saints of the most High, and think to change times and laws: and they shall be given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of time.
Daniel 7:26–27 But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end. And the kingdom and dominion, and the greatness of the kingdom under the whole heaven, shall be given to the people of the saints of the most High, whose kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and all dominions shall serve and obey him.

From these verses the film draws ten identifying marks and argues that the papacy, the Roman Catholic Church acting as a church and a state together, fits every one of them. Here is each mark, the text it rests on, and the film’s application.

1. A little horn at first It begins small among the others (Daniel 7:8). The film points to the papal state, and today to Vatican City, the smallest sovereign state on earth.
2. Rises among the ten It comes up out of the fourth beast’s own head, among the ten horns (Daniel 7:8, 24). The film places it among the kingdoms of broken-up Western Rome.
3. Rises after the ten Another shall rise after them (Daniel 7:24). The film dates its rise after the fall of the West in AD 476.
4. Uproots three horns Three of the first horns are plucked up by the roots, and he shall subdue three kings (Daniel 7:8, 24). The film names the Heruli, the Vandals, and the Ostrogoths, three kingdoms that held to the Arian teaching and stood in the way of Rome’s bishop.
5. Diverse from the others He shall be diverse from the first (Daniel 7:24). The film says it differs because it is a religious power and a civil power at the same time, a church that is also a state.
6. Eyes like a man In this horn were eyes like the eyes of man (Daniel 7:8). The film says a single man stands at its head, the pope, and that its look is more stout than his fellows (Daniel 7:20).
7. Speaks great words against the most High A mouth speaking great things (Daniel 7:8, 25). The film points to the claims to stand in the place of God on earth and to forgive sins, the same two charges the religious leaders wrongly laid against Christ, who truly was God (Mark 2:7; John 10:33).
8. Wears out the saints He makes war with the saints and prevails against them (Daniel 7:21, 25). The film points to the persecution of dissenters through the Middle Ages and the Inquisition.
9. Thinks to change times and laws He shall think to change times and laws (Daniel 7:25). The film points to the transfer of the day of rest from the seventh day to the first, and to the removal of the second commandment’s ban on images from Catholic catechisms.
10. Rules for time, times, and half a time They shall be given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of time (Daniel 7:25). The film reads this as 1,260 years, from AD 538 to 1798.

The Charges of Blasphemy

Mark 2:7 Why doth this man thus speak blasphemies? who can forgive sins but God only?
John 10:33 The Jews answered him, saying, For a good work we stone thee not; but for blasphemy; and because that thou, being a man, makest thyself God.
1 Timothy 2:5 For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus;
Teacher’s Note

Grade the ten marks individually rather than accepting or rejecting them as one package. Several of them describe real, documented history. Rome’s bishop did rise to civil power as the Western Empire collapsed. The Heruli fell in 493, the Vandals in 534, and the Ostrogoths were broken in the 530s and finally defeated in 553, all by powers friendly to Rome’s bishop. The papacy did claim both civil and spiritual rule. Dissenters were persecuted, sometimes to death. And the change from the seventh day to the first as the church’s day of rest is openly claimed by Catholic writers, as Part XI shows.

What is interpretive is the step from those facts to the claim that these events are the fulfillment Daniel saw. The ninth mark, the change of the second commandment, deserves care in class. Catholic catechisms do fold the ban on graven images into the first commandment and split the tenth into two, but Catholic teaching would say it has not removed that text from Scripture. State it accurately so no one can dismiss the whole lesson over one overstatement.

Part Two of the Film

VI.

The 1,260 Years

The same period appears seven times in Daniel and Revelation, in three different forms. It is called time, times, and half a time, or the dividing of time. It is called forty and two months. And it is called a thousand two hundred and threescore days.

One Period, Three Ways

Revelation 12:6 And the woman fled into the wilderness, where she hath a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days.
Revelation 12:14 And to the woman were given two wings of a great eagle, that she might fly into the wilderness, into her place, where she is nourished for a time, and times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent.
Revelation 13:5 And there was given unto him a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies; and power was given unto him to continue forty and two months.
Revelation 11:2–3 But the court which is without the temple leave out, and measure it not; for it is given unto the Gentiles: and the holy city shall they tread under foot forty and two months. And I will give power unto my two witnesses, and they shall prophesy a thousand two hundred and threescore days, clothed in sackcloth.
Daniel 12:7 And I heard the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river, when he held up his right hand and his left hand unto heaven, and sware by him that liveth for ever that it shall be for a time, times, and an half; and when he shall have accomplished to scatter the power of the holy people, all these things shall be finished.

The arithmetic is simple. A time is one year, times is two years, and half a time is half a year, so three and a half years in all. Counted in the prophetic year of 360 days, that is 1,260 days, and 42 months of 30 days comes to the same 1,260. Revelation 12 uses both forms for the same flight of the woman, which proves they are one period. Applying the day-year principle, the film turns 1,260 days into 1,260 years.

The film then anchors that span to two dates. For the beginning it points to AD 538. Five years earlier, in 533, the emperor Justinian had written to Pope John II recognizing the bishop of Rome as head of all the holy churches, a letter preserved in Justinian’s own law code (Code of Justinian, Book 1, Title 1, Law 8). Then in March of 538 Justinian’s general Belisarius broke the Ostrogothic siege of Rome, removing the last power in the city that stood against the bishop’s rule. For the end it points to 1798. In February of that year the French general Louis-Alexandre Berthier, acting under the revolutionary government Napoleon served, marched into Rome and proclaimed a republic. Pope Pius VI was removed from power and carried off, and he died a prisoner in Valence, France, in August 1799. From 538 to 1798 is exactly 1,260 years.

Teacher’s Note

The events are real, and the arithmetic of 538 to 1798 works. What is interpretive is the choice of endpoints. Papal power was growing well before 538, and it did not vanish after 1798; a new pope was elected in 1800. Historicist writers over the centuries have proposed other starting points as well, such as 533 or 606, and have arrived at different ending dates as a result. That does not make the 538 to 1798 reading foolish, but it does mean it is a proposed fit, not something the text itself dates.

Our end time study reads the 1,260 days as a literal period of three and a half years at the close of the age. The best reason is Daniel’s own framing. The time, times, and half ends when all these things shall be finished (Daniel 12:7), and the very next verses tie the count to the abomination that maketh desolate (Daniel 12:11), the same abomination Jesus placed right before the great tribulation and His coming (Matthew 24:15, 21, 29–31).

Weighing Part Two

VII.

Weighing the Little Horn

Before disagreeing with the film, it is only fair to recognize the company it keeps. The identification of the papacy as the antichrist power was not invented by any modern denomination. It was the standard Protestant position for three hundred years.

Going Deeper · The Reformers on the Little Horn

Martin Luther wrote in the Smalcald Articles (1537, Part II, Article IV) that the papacy was the very antichrist, because it exalted itself over Christ and claimed that no one could be saved without submitting to it. John Calvin, John Knox, the translators of the Geneva Bible in their margin notes, John Wesley, and Isaac Newton in his Observations upon the Prophecies of Daniel (1733) all read the little horn in much the same way. The Westminster Confession of Faith, the great statement of English and Scottish Protestants, put it in these words:

Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 25, Section 6 (1646)“There is no other head of the Church but the Lord Jesus Christ: nor can the Pope of Rome, in any sense, be head thereof; but is that Antichrist, that man of sin, and son of perdition, that exalteth himself, in the Church, against Christ and all that is called God.”

So the film stands in a long line. The question is not whether a system that claimed the place of God, persecuted the saints, and changed God’s times and laws fits the pattern of the little horn. It does, and strikingly so. The question is whether that is the whole fulfillment, or a historical foreshadowing of a final fulfillment still to come. Four things in the text point us toward the second.

The horn’s career runs to the end. The little horn makes war with the saints and prevails against them until the Ancient of days comes and the saints possess the kingdom (Daniel 7:21–22). Its dominion is taken away and destroyed at the judgment that brings in the everlasting kingdom (Daniel 7:26–27). The text shows no gap of centuries between the end of its power and the coming of the kingdom.
Paul’s man of sin is destroyed at Christ’s coming. The Lord shall consume him with the spirit of His mouth and destroy him with the brightness of His coming (2 Thessalonians 2:8). He is not a power that fades out in 1798; he is still standing when Christ appears.
Daniel ties the three and a half years to the abomination and the end. The time, times, and half runs until all these things are finished, and it is counted from the setting up of the abomination that maketh desolate (Daniel 12:7, 11).
Jesus put the abomination in the future. Speaking decades after Daniel and centuries before the Middle Ages, Christ told His disciples that when they see the abomination of desolation stand in the holy place, great tribulation will follow, and immediately after that tribulation come the cosmic signs and His appearing (Matthew 24:15, 21, 29–31).

The Man of Sin and the Abomination

2 Thessalonians 2:3–4 Let no man deceive you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition; Who opposeth and exalteth himself above all that is called God, or that is worshipped; so that he as God sitteth in the temple of God, shewing himself that he is God.
2 Thessalonians 2:7–8 For the mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way. And then shall that Wicked be revealed, whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming:
Daniel 12:11 And from the time that the daily sacrifice shall be taken away, and the abomination that maketh desolate set up, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety days.
Matthew 24:15 When ye therefore shall see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place, (whoso readeth, let him understand:)
Matthew 24:21 For then shall be great tribulation, such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time, no, nor ever shall be.
1 John 2:18 Little children, it is the last time: and as ye have heard that antichrist shall come, even now are there many antichrists; whereby we know that it is the last time.

Paul says the mystery of iniquity was already at work in his own day, and John says many antichrists had already come. The spirit of antichrist has worked through every age, and it found one of its clearest historical expressions in a church-state power that claimed God’s prerogatives and hunted down those who kept His word. We can honor the Reformers’ warning and still hold that the man of sin himself, his forty-two months, and his mark are yet to come.

Teacher’s Note

Teach this with a clear distinction between a system and the people inside it. God’s own call in Revelation is addressed to His people who are still in Babylon: Come out of her, my people (Revelation 18:4). There are sincere believers in every tradition who love the Lord and have simply never been shown what the Scripture says about His commandments. The warning is against a teaching and an authority that sets itself over the word of God, not a license to despise our Catholic neighbors. Speak the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15).

Part Three of the Film

VIII.

The Beast from the Sea

The film now moves from Daniel to Revelation, and the link between them is unmistakable. John sees a beast rise out of the sea with seven heads and ten horns, and it looks like a leopard, with the feet of a bear and the mouth of a lion. Those are Daniel’s first three beasts named in reverse order, as though John were looking back at them from further down the line, all rolled into one creature that also carries the fourth beast’s ten horns. Every school of interpretation agrees that Revelation 13 picks up where Daniel 7 left off.

The First Beast

Revelation 13:1–4 And I stood upon the sand of the sea, and saw a beast rise up out of the sea, having seven heads and ten horns, and upon his horns ten crowns, and upon his heads the name of blasphemy. And the beast which I saw was like unto a leopard, and his feet were as the feet of a bear, and his mouth as the mouth of a lion: and the dragon gave him his power, and his seat, and great authority. And I saw one of his heads as it were wounded to death; and his deadly wound was healed: and all the world wondered after the beast. And they worshipped the dragon which gave power unto the beast: and they worshipped the beast, saying, Who is like unto the beast? who is able to make war with him?
Revelation 13:5–8 And there was given unto him a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies; and power was given unto him to continue forty and two months. And he opened his mouth in blasphemy against God, to blaspheme his name, and his tabernacle, and them that dwell in heaven. And it was given unto him to make war with the saints, and to overcome them: and power was given him over all kindreds, and tongues, and nations. And all that dwell upon the earth shall worship him, whose names are not written in the book of life of the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world.
Revelation 13:9–10 If any man have an ear, let him hear. He that leadeth into captivity shall go into captivity: he that killeth with the sword must be killed with the sword. Here is the patience and the faith of the saints.

The film points out that this beast shares the little horn’s features: a mouth speaking great things and blasphemies, war against the saints, and the same span of forty-two months. So it identifies the sea beast with the little horn, and both with the papacy. The sea, it says, stands for a crowded region of the world, and Revelation itself explains that waters mean peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues (Revelation 17:15). The seven heads are later called seven mountains on which the woman sits (Revelation 17:9), which many readers have tied to Rome, the city of seven hills.

The film then reads the deadly wound as the blow of 1798. When Berthier took the pope captive and the papal states were swept away, the church lost the civil power it had held for centuries. And many historicist teachers go on to say the wound began to heal on February 11, 1929, when the Lateran Treaty between the Vatican and the Italian government under Mussolini recognized Vatican City as a sovereign state. Since then, they point out, popes have been received by kings and presidents, addressed world bodies, and drawn crowds in the millions, as all the world wondered after the beast (Revelation 13:3).

Revelation Explains Its Own Symbols

Revelation 17:9 And here is the mind which hath wisdom. The seven heads are seven mountains, on which the woman sitteth.
Revelation 17:12–13 And the ten horns which thou sawest are ten kings, which have received no kingdom as yet; but receive power as kings one hour with the beast. These have one mind, and shall give their power and strength unto the beast.
Revelation 17:15 And he saith unto me, The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues.
Teacher’s Note

The Lateran Treaty and the events of 1798 are well documented history. Their connection to the deadly wound is interpretation. Notice what Revelation 17:12 says about the ten horns in John’s own day: they had received no kingdom as yet, and they will reign one hour with the beast, giving their power to him. That points to a coalition of rulers at the end rather than to tribes that rose and fell fifteen centuries ago. It also fits the picture in Revelation 13:7–8 of a power given authority over all kindreds, tongues, and nations, with all the earth worshipping it. That is broader than any church has ever held, even at the height of medieval power.

The most balanced way to teach it: the medieval papacy gives a clear preview of the beast’s character, a religious and political power claiming worship and persecuting the saints, and the final beast will show that same character on a worldwide scale for a literal forty-two months.

Part Three of the Film

IX.

The Beast from the Earth

Then John sees a second beast. This one comes up out of the earth rather than the sea. It has two horns like a lamb, but it speaks as a dragon. It exercises all the authority of the first beast, works great wonders even to calling fire down from heaven, commands the world to make an image to the first beast, and forces everyone to take a mark in the right hand or forehead in order to buy or sell.

The Second Beast

Revelation 13:11–14 And I beheld another beast coming up out of the earth; and he had two horns like a lamb, and he spake as a dragon. And he exerciseth all the power of the first beast before him, and causeth the earth and them which dwell therein to worship the first beast, whose deadly wound was healed. And he doeth great wonders, so that he maketh fire come down from heaven on the earth in the sight of men, And deceiveth them that dwell on the earth by the means of those miracles which he had power to do in the sight of the beast; saying to them that dwell on the earth, that they should make an image to the beast, which had the wound by a sword, and did live.
Revelation 13:15–18 And he had power to give life unto the image of the beast, that the image of the beast should both speak, and cause that as many as would not worship the image of the beast should be killed. And he causeth all, both small and great, rich and poor, free and bond, to receive a mark in their right hand, or in their foreheads: And that no man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark, or the name of the beast, or the number of his name. Here is wisdom. Let him that hath understanding count the number of the beast: for it is the number of a man; and his number is Six hundred threescore and six.

The film makes a four-part case that this beast is the United States of America. First is timing: the second beast appears just as the first goes into captivity, and the United States declared its independence in 1776, adopted its Constitution in 1789, and added the Bill of Rights in 1791, all in the years around 1798. Second is location: the first beast came from the sea, the crowded Old World, while this one rises from the earth, the thinly settled lands of the Western Hemisphere. Third is character: two horns like a lamb picture a young and gentle power, and the film says the two horns stand for the two great principles of the American founding, civil liberty and religious liberty, including the separation of church and state. Fourth is its future: though it looks like a lamb, it will one day speak as a dragon, turning from protecting freedom of worship to enforcing worship by law.

Revelation, however, gives this second beast another name. Three more times it appears in the book, and each time it is called the false prophet.

The Second Beast Is the False Prophet

Revelation 16:13–14 And I saw three unclean spirits like frogs come out of the mouth of the dragon, and out of the mouth of the beast, and out of the mouth of the false prophet. For they are the spirits of devils, working miracles, which go forth unto the kings of the earth and of the whole world, to gather them to the battle of that great day of God Almighty.
Revelation 19:20 And the beast was taken, and with him the false prophet that wrought miracles before him, with which he deceived them that had received the mark of the beast, and them that worshipped his image. These both were cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone.
Revelation 20:10 And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.
Matthew 7:15 Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves.

Revelation 19:20 settles who the second beast is by describing him with the same deeds as Revelation 13: the one who wrought miracles before the beast and deceived those who took the mark and worshipped the image. He is a prophet, a religious figure, and he is taken alive at Christ’s return and cast into the lake of fire with the beast. That reads naturally of a person, or at least of a religious movement headed by one, and awkwardly of a nation. His lamb-like horns fit the Lord’s own warning about wolves in sheep’s clothing. He looks like the Lamb and speaks for the dragon. That is exactly how false prophecy has always worked.

Teacher’s Note

Handle national identifications with humility. Believers in nearly every generation have been sure the beast was a power of their own day, and in nearly every generation that confidence had to be revised. That history should make us careful. At the same time, nothing in Scripture rules out any nation, including our own, becoming a willing tool of the beast’s system at the end. A nation born with lamb-like freedoms can certainly learn to speak like a dragon.

The safe ground is what the text says plainly: a religious deceiver will work real wonders, promote the worship of the beast, and enforce it by economic pressure and the threat of death. Teach the people to recognize the pattern, whoever carries it out, and never tie the prophecy to a date or a headline (Matthew 24:36; Acts 1:7).

Part Four of the Film

X.

The Seal of God

The climax of the film is a contrast between two marks of ownership. Before the four winds of judgment are let loose, God’s servants are sealed in their foreheads (Revelation 7:3). Those who follow the beast receive his mark in the forehead or the hand (Revelation 13:16). The film asks a simple question: what is the seal of God?

Its answer starts with how an official seal works. A king’s or a government’s seal, the film says, contains three things: the name of the ruler, his title, and the territory he rules. It then looks through the Ten Commandments and finds all three in only one place, the fourth commandment. There God gives His name, the LORD, His title, the one who made them, and His territory, heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is. So the film concludes that the seventh-day Sabbath is the seal of God.

The Fourth Commandment

Exodus 20:8–11 Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates: For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day, and hallowed it.

The film is on strong ground in calling the Sabbath God’s sign. Scripture says so in plain words, more than once, and it says the Sabbath is the mark that shows who sanctifies His people and whose people they are. Isaiah even speaks of sealing the law among the Lord’s disciples.

The Sabbath Is God’s Sign

Exodus 31:13–17 Speak thou also unto the children of Israel, saying, Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you. Ye shall keep the sabbath therefore; for it is holy unto you: every one that defileth it shall surely be put to death: for whosoever doeth any work therein, that soul shall be cut off from among his people. Six days may work be done; but in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD: whosoever doeth any work in the sabbath day, he shall surely be put to death. Wherefore the children of Israel shall keep the sabbath, to observe the sabbath throughout their generations, for a perpetual covenant. It is a sign between me and the children of Israel for ever: for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed.
Ezekiel 20:12 Moreover also I gave them my sabbaths, to be a sign between me and them, that they might know that I am the LORD that sanctify them.
Ezekiel 20:20 And hallow my sabbaths; and they shall be a sign between me and you, that ye may know that I am the LORD your God.
Isaiah 8:16 Bind up the testimony, seal the law among my disciples.

Revelation adds its own description of the seal. The servants of God are sealed in their foreheads, and the 144,000 who stand with the Lamb have His Father’s name written there. And Paul tells every believer that when we heard the gospel and believed, we were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise.

How Scripture Describes the Seal

Revelation 7:2–3 And I saw another angel ascending from the east, having the seal of the living God: and he cried with a loud voice to the four angels, to whom it was given to hurt the earth and the sea, Saying, Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads.
Revelation 14:1 And I looked, and, lo, a Lamb stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having his Father’s name written in their foreheads.
Ephesians 1:13 In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise,
Ephesians 4:30 And grieve not the holy Spirit of God, whereby ye are sealed unto the day of redemption.

Now put those together with one more passage. When God gave His commandments, He told Israel to bind His words as a sign upon their hand and as frontlets between their eyes. The hand is what we do. The forehead is what we think and choose. So when Revelation speaks of God’s name in the forehead and the beast’s mark in the forehead or hand, it is using the same language God used for His law. The final test is over whose word governs the mind and the hands.

The Hand and the Forehead

Deuteronomy 6:6–8 And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes.
Ezekiel 9:4 And the LORD said unto him, Go through the midst of the city, through the midst of Jerusalem, and set a mark upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations that be done in the midst thereof.

Here is the careful way to say it. The Holy Spirit is the seal God places on every true believer. God’s name, His ownership, is written on His servants. And the Sabbath is the sign God Himself chose to show that ownership, the outward mark of a people who belong to the Creator and are sanctified by Him. A believer sealed by the Spirit will bear the fruit of the Spirit, and that fruit includes loving God enough to keep His commandments, the seventh day among them (John 14:15). The Sabbath does not save anyone. We are saved by grace through faith, and then we walk in the good works God prepared for us (Ephesians 2:8–10).

Grace First, Then Obedience

Ephesians 2:8–10 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.
John 14:15 If ye love me, keep my commandments.
Teacher’s Note

The name, title, and territory argument is a helpful observation, and it is true that the fourth commandment is the only one that names God as Creator of heaven and earth. But notice that it is drawn from how earthly seals work, not from a definition Scripture gives. Use it as an illustration, not as a proof. The proof is in the plain statements that the Sabbath is God’s sign (Exodus 31:13, 17; Ezekiel 20:12, 20).

Also guard against any teaching that makes day-keeping the ground of salvation. Scripture calls the Holy Spirit the seal of the believer (Ephesians 1:13). The Sabbath is the sign that follows from belonging to God, not the price of belonging to Him.

Part Four of the Film

XI.

The Change of the Sabbath and the Mark

Daniel said the little horn would think to change times and laws (Daniel 7:25). The film argues that the clearest example is the change of the weekly day of rest from the seventh day to the first. On the first half of that claim, that God never made the change, Scripture is plain. God blessed and sanctified the seventh day at creation. Jesus called Himself Lord of the Sabbath, not its abolisher. The women who followed Jesus rested on the Sabbath according to the commandment after His death. And Hebrews says a Sabbath rest still remains for the people of God (Hebrews 4:9).

The Day God Never Changed

Genesis 2:2–3 And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made. And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.
Mark 2:27–28 And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath: Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath.
Luke 23:56 And they returned, and prepared spices and ointments; and rested the sabbath day according to the commandment.
Mark 7:7–9 Howbeit in vain do they worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. For laying aside the commandment of God, ye hold the tradition of men, as the washing of pots and cups: and many other such like things ye do. And he said unto them, Full well ye reject the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition.

So how did the first day take the place of the seventh? History shows the change came gradually, through civil law and church council, not through any word of Christ or His apostles. The first civil Sunday law came from the emperor Constantine in AD 321. About four decades later a church council went further and forbade Christians to rest on the Sabbath at all.

From the Historical Record

Constantine’s Sunday Law, March 7, AD 321, Code of Justinian, Book 3, Title 12, Law 2 (as translated in Philip Schaff, History of the Christian Church, Volume 3, Section 75) “On the venerable Day of the Sun let the magistrates and people residing in cities rest, and let all workshops be closed. In the country, however, persons engaged in agriculture may freely and lawfully continue their pursuits; because it often happens that another day is not so suitable for grain-sowing or for vine-planting; lest by neglecting the proper moment for such operations the bounty of heaven should be lost.”
Council of Laodicea, about AD 363, Canon 29 (Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, Volume 14) “Christians must not judaize by resting on the Sabbath, but must work on that day, rather honouring the Lord’s Day; and, if they can, resting then as Christians. But if any shall be found to be judaizers, let them be anathema from Christ.”

Notice what Constantine called the day: the venerable day of the sun. And the Roman Catholic Church has never been shy about taking responsibility for the change. Its own teachers have said plainly that the Bible does not command Sunday and that the church made the change on its own authority.

In Catholic Writers’ Own Words

James Cardinal Gibbons, Archbishop of Baltimore, The Faith of Our Fathers (1876), Chapter VIII “But you may read the Bible from Genesis to Revelation, and you will not find a single line authorizing the sanctification of Sunday. The Scriptures enforce the religious observance of Saturday, a day which we never sanctify.”
Peter Geiermann, The Convert’s Catechism of Catholic Doctrine (1910), on the Third Commandment “Q. Which is the Sabbath day? A. Saturday is the Sabbath day. Q. Why do we observe Sunday instead of Saturday? A. We observe Sunday instead of Saturday because the Catholic Church transferred the solemnity from Saturday to Sunday.”

The film also quotes a letter from the office of Cardinal Gibbons, dated 1895, calling the change the mark of the church’s ecclesiastical authority. It is from that phrase that the film draws its central conclusion: if Rome calls the change of the Sabbath its mark of authority, and Revelation warns against the mark of the beast, then Sunday observance, once it is imposed by law, is the mark of the beast.

The Warning of the Third Angel

Revelation 14:9–11 And the third angel followed them, saying with a loud voice, If any man worship the beast and his image, and receive his mark in his forehead, or in his hand, The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation; and he shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels, and in the presence of the Lamb: And the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever: and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image, and whosoever receiveth the mark of his name.

Weigh the pieces separately. It is stated in Scripture that the Sabbath is God’s sign and was never changed by God. It is documented history that the church of Rome made the change and claims it. It is stated in Scripture that the final conflict centers on worship, that the mark is received in the forehead or hand, the same places God said His law belongs, and that the saints who resist are those who keep the commandments of God (Revelation 12:17; 14:12). Put together, the view that the Sabbath will be at the heart of the final test is a reasonable reading. But it is still a reading. Revelation never names a day of the week as the mark, and it adds details that point to a future act of worship toward a living image under threat of death (Revelation 13:14–15).

Teacher’s Note

Two cautions for class. First, the 1895 letter is widely quoted, but the original is hard to verify, and different versions name different signers. The Gibbons book and the Geiermann catechism are published and easy to check, and they make the same point, so lean on those.

Second, do not tell sincere Sunday-keeping believers that they bear the mark of the beast. Even the tradition the film comes from teaches that no one receives the mark today. By their own account it becomes the mark only when worship is enforced by law under the beast’s power and chosen with full knowledge, after the warning of Revelation 14 has gone out to the whole world. We can say plainly that Sunday rest is a tradition of men that set aside a commandment of God (Mark 7:7–9), and invite people to return to the Sabbath, without pronouncing a sentence Scripture reserves for the end.

The Film’s Forecast

XII.

The Image of the Beast and the Sunday Laws

The film ends its prophetic outline with a forecast. The United States, the lamb-like second beast, will one day speak like a dragon. It will build an image to the beast by joining church and state in the same pattern the papacy once did, and it will enforce worship on the first day of the week through what the film calls a National Sunday Law. Those who refuse will first be shut out of buying and selling, and finally face the threat of death (Revelation 13:15–17).

This is not a scenario invented from nothing. Sunday laws have a long history in the Western world, and in America in particular.

Going Deeper · The History of Sunday Laws

After Constantine’s law of 321, Sunday rest was enforced by civil law across Christian Europe for centuries. The English colonists brought the practice to America, and many colonies passed laws requiring Sunday rest and church attendance in the 1600s. These came to be called blue laws, and they carried on as state laws after independence.

In 1888 Senator Henry W. Blair of New Hampshire introduced a national Sunday-rest bill in the United States Senate, backed by a coalition of Protestant reform groups. It never came to a vote, but it alarmed many religious liberty advocates at the time, and Seventh-day Adventists were among its most active opponents.

In 1961 the Supreme Court upheld Maryland’s Sunday closing laws in McGowan v. Maryland, 366 U.S. 420, ruling that although the laws began with a religious purpose, they now served the secular purpose of a common day of rest. Many such laws have since been repealed, though some states still restrict certain Sunday sales.

So the history shows that civil governments have enforced Sunday before, including in this country, and could do so again. What the history cannot do is prove that a national Sunday law is the image of the beast. Scripture describes the image as something made at the false prophet’s command, given life so that it speaks, and used to demand worship on pain of death (Revelation 13:14–15). How exactly that will look in its day is not spelled out, and the Lord has not asked us to guess the details or the timing.

Watching Without Setting Dates

Matthew 24:36 But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only.
Acts 1:7 And he said unto them, It is not for you to know the times or the seasons, which the Father hath put in his own power.
1 Thessalonians 5:21 Prove all things; hold fast that which is good.
Teacher’s Note

Treat this part of the film as speculation built on interpretation, and label it that way. It is worth watching laws that bind conscience in matters of worship, and believers should be prepared to obey God rather than men if that day comes (Acts 5:29). But do not build panic on headlines or tie the prophecy to any proposed law, election, or date. The Lord’s call is to watch and be ready, not to predict.

The Film’s Conclusion

XIII.

The Call to Endurance

The film closes on the right note. It turns to Revelation 14, where three angels fly through heaven with God’s final message to the world before the harvest of the earth. The first angel carries the everlasting gospel and calls every nation to fear God and worship Him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters. Those words echo the fourth commandment almost word for word (Exodus 20:11). The second angel announces that Babylon is fallen. The third warns against the beast and his mark. And then comes the description of the people who stand firm through it all.

The Three Angels and the Saints

Revelation 14:6–8 And I saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the everlasting gospel to preach unto them that dwell on the earth, and to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people, Saying with a loud voice, Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters. And there followed another angel, saying, Babylon is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she made all nations drink of the wine of the wrath of her fornication.

Revelation 12:17 And the dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, which keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ.
Revelation 22:14 Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life, and may enter in through the gates into the city.

Here the film and this ministry stand shoulder to shoulder. The people of God at the end are marked by two things held together: the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. Not the commandments without faith, which would be dead religion, and not faith without the commandments, which would be a faith that will not stand when the pressure comes. The dragon makes war against exactly this people, which tells us that keeping God’s commandments, the Sabbath included, is not a side issue in the last days. It is the line of battle.

Where our framework adds to the film is in the order of events. The saints do not escape the great tribulation; they endure it. That tribulation is the wrath of Satan through the beast against those who keep God’s commandments. Immediately after it come the signs in the sun, moon, and stars, the appearing of the Son of man, and the gathering of His elect by the angels with a great trumpet sound. Only then does the wrath of God fall on the kingdom of the beast, because God has not appointed His people to wrath (1 Thessalonians 5:9).

The practical call is the same whether the historicist or the futurist reading proves right in its details. Come out of Babylon now. Return to the commandments of God now, including the day He blessed at creation. Hold fast the faith of Jesus now, resting in His grace and not in our own strength. The believer who is already walking in obedience by faith will not have to decide under pressure what he has refused to decide in peace.

A Closing Word

From Babylon to America is a serious attempt to take the prophecies of Daniel and Revelation at their word and to connect them to real history, and it deserves a serious hearing. Its strongest moments are the ones where it simply lets Scripture speak: the rise and fall of empires foretold in advance, the character of a power that claims the place of God, the Sabbath as God’s own sign, and the final call to keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. On those points we stand with it.

Where it builds on Hislop’s legends, or fixes prophecies to particular nations and centuries with more certainty than the text allows, we hold back and say why. The goal is not to win an argument about the Middle Ages. It is to be a people who search the Scriptures daily, prove all things, and hold fast to what is good, so that whenever the last test comes, we are already standing where God told us to stand.
