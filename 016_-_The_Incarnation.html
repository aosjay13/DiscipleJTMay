A Pronomian Teaching

The Incarnation

The Word Made Flesh, God and Man in One Person

✦

Introduction

At the heart of the gospel stands a mystery so deep that angels desire to look into it, that the eternal God took upon Himself the nature of man. This is the incarnation, the truth that the Son of God, without ceasing to be God, became also true man and dwelt among us. He was not a man who became God, nor a phantom that only seemed to have flesh. He was God the Son made flesh, born of a woman, laid in a manger, yet upholding all things by the word of His power.

This doctrine is no small matter for the mind alone. Upon it the whole of our salvation rests. Only one who was truly man could stand in the place of men and bear their sins. Only one who was truly God could bear an infinite weight and give His offering infinite worth. Take away either nature and the atonement falls. We will trace what Scripture teaches of the Word who was made flesh, and see why He had to be what He was to save us.

Section I

The Word Was God from the Beginning

Before there was a manger in Bethlehem, the Son existed eternally as God. He did not begin His existence when He was born. He who was laid in the manger had no beginning at all, for He was with God and was God before the world was made. John opens his gospel by carrying us back behind all created things to the eternal Word.

John 1:1–3 In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.
Colossians 1:16–17 For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him: And he is before all things, and by him all things consist.

Mark the weight of these words. The Word was God, and all things were made by Him. He is before all things, and by Him all things hold together. This is no creature, however exalted. This is the eternal God, the Maker of heaven and earth, the one in whom the whole creation consists. Keep this firmly in mind, for it is this very One who will be made flesh.

Eternal and Divine

The Son did not come into being at Bethlehem. He is the eternal Word, who was with God and was God, by whom all things were made. The incarnation is the eternal God taking up our nature, not a creature being promoted to deity.

Section II

The Word Was Made Flesh

The same eternal Word, without laying aside His deity, took to Himself a true human nature. He was made flesh. He entered our world not as a visitor wearing the appearance of a man, but as a real man with body and soul, who hungered and wept and grew weary as we do. The Maker of the body was born with a body. The one who upholds all things was carried as a babe.

John 1:14 And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.
Philippians 2:6–7 Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men.
Hebrews 2:14 Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil.

See how Paul describes it. He who was in the form of God, equal with God, made Himself of no reputation and took the form of a servant. He did not cease to be God when He became man. He added our nature to His own, veiling His glory in flesh that He might dwell among us and walk the road we walk.

He Took, He Did Not Lose

In the incarnation the Son took up what He did not have before, a human nature, without losing what He always was, true God. He emptied Himself not by subtracting His deity but by adding our humanity and humbling Himself within it. The glory was veiled, never surrendered.

Section III

Born of a Virgin

The manner of His coming was itself a wonder foretold long before. He was conceived not by the will of man but by the power of the Holy Ghost, and born of a virgin. This was no afterthought. The prophet Isaiah had spoken of it centuries before, that a virgin should conceive and bear a son whose name would declare God with us.

Isaiah 7:14 Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.
Matthew 1:22–23 Now all this was done, that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet, saying, Behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son, and they shall call his name Emmanuel, which being interpreted is, God with us.
Luke 1:35 And the angel answered and said unto her, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee: therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God.

The virgin birth is not a strange tale to be explained away. It is the appointed way by which the eternal Son entered our race. Conceived by the Holy Ghost, He took true flesh from His mother, yet was preserved from the sin that passes to all who are born of two earthly parents. Thus the name Immanuel, God with us, is no poetry but plain truth. The child born of Mary was God dwelling among His people.

God With Us

By the virgin birth the Son took real human flesh while remaining the holy Son of God. The sign given through Isaiah was fulfilled in the manger. The name Immanuel tells the whole story in a word, God with us.

Section IV

Truly Man, Tempted and Tried

We must not let His deity swallow up the truth of His humanity. He was a real man in every part of our nature, sin only excepted. He grew as a child grows. He hungered and thirsted. He was weary and slept. He wept at the grave and sweat as it were great drops of blood. He was tempted in all points as we are, and felt the full weight of it, yet without sin.

Luke 2:52 And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man.
Hebrews 4:15 For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin.
Hebrews 2:17 Wherefore in all things it behoved him to be made like unto his brethren, that he might be a merciful and faithful high priest in things pertaining to God, to make reconciliation for the sins of the people.

This is a great comfort to the believer. Our Lord is no stranger to our weakness. He was made like unto His brethren in all things, that He might be a merciful high priest who understands our trials from the inside. When we come to Him in our weakness, we come to one who has walked our road, felt our temptations, and overcame.

Teacher's Note

Hold both natures firmly and do not let one cancel the other. Jesus is not part God and part man, nor God hiding inside a man, nor a man with a spark of the divine. He is fully God and fully man, two whole natures joined in one Person for ever. When Scripture shows Him weary or weeping, that is His true humanity. When it shows Him forgiving sin and stilling storms, that is His true deity. One Person, two natures, never confused and never divided.

Section V

Why He Had to Be Both God and Man

The incarnation was not a display of power for its own sake. It was necessary for our salvation. The work that had to be done could only be done by one who was both God and man. As man, He could stand in the place of men, take our nature, and offer up a life and death that belonged to our race. As God, His offering carried infinite worth, able to answer for the sins of a multitude no man could number.

1 Timothy 2:5 For there is one God, and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus.

A mediator must lay his hand on both parties. To stand between God and men, He must touch both. As the man Christ Jesus He takes hold of us, sharing our flesh and blood. As the eternal Son He takes hold of God, being Himself God. Thus He bridges the gulf that sin had opened, joining in His own Person the God who was offended and the men who had offended.

Hebrews 10:5 Wherefore when he cometh into the world, he saith, Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me.
Galatians 4:4–5 But when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, To redeem them that were under the law, that we might receive the adoption of sons.

The Mediator Must Be Both

Only a true man could die in the place of men. Only the eternal God could give that death infinite worth. The one mediator is the man Christ Jesus who is also God, and because He is both, He alone could reconcile us to the Father.

Made Under the Law

Paul says the Son was made under the law, to redeem them that were under the law. He kept perfectly the commandments we had broken, fulfilling all righteousness in our nature. The obedience we owed and failed to render, He rendered in full, that we might be redeemed and received as sons.

Section VI

The Mystery of Godliness

The early church confessed this truth as the great mystery of godliness. It is beyond our full understanding, yet it is plainly revealed and firmly to be believed. God was manifest in the flesh. The invisible became visible, the eternal entered time, the Maker was made. We do not explain away the wonder, we bow before it and worship.

1 Timothy 3:16 And without controversy great is the mystery of godliness: God was manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels, preached unto the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up into glory.
Hebrews 1:3 Who being the brightness of his glory, and the express image of his person, and upholding all things by the word of his power, when he had by himself purged our sins, sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high.

Here is the whole of it in a single breath. The brightness of God's glory, the express image of His person, the one who upholds all things, stooped down to purge our sins by Himself, and then sat down at the right hand of God. The one who became flesh for us has returned in glory, and He bears our nature still, our brother and our God, our mediator for ever.

Believed On in the World

The mystery is not given us merely to admire but to receive. God was manifest in the flesh, and He is to be believed on in the world. The proper response to the incarnation is faith in the Son who came, and a life laid down in glad obedience to Him.

Conclusion

God With Us, Our Mediator For Ever

The eternal Word, by whom all things were made, was made flesh and dwelt among us. He did not cease to be God when He took our nature, but joined to His deity a true and complete humanity, born of a virgin, tempted and tried, sin only excepted. He came as the one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus who is also the eternal Son, that He might lay His hand on both and bring us home to the Father.

Take away His deity and His offering is only the death of a man, of no power to save. Take away His humanity and He cannot stand in our place at all. But He is both, fully God and fully man, two natures in one Person for ever, and so He is mighty to save. Let us then believe on Him who was manifest in the flesh, and bow before the great mystery of godliness, and serve with glad obedience the God who became our brother to redeem us.

And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, full of grace and truth.
