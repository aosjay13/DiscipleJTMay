Repentance

A Biblical Teaching on Turning Back to the Creator

✦

Introduction

To understand the true nature of repentance, we must first establish the objective standard of righteousness. God has given commandments as an expression of his intrinsic moral nature. These commandments exist for the good of humanity and for his glory, and they are revealed in the Law of Moses and throughout the sixty-six book Bible. Rooted in Pronomian theology, we affirm that God's standard of righteousness remains unchanging and that His commandments remain a lamp unto our feet. Therefore, any biblical discussion on repentance must begin and end with our relationship to God's eternal Law.

I. The Biblical Definition of Repentance

Biblically, we define repentance not merely as a singular confession, but as the continual state of living free from sin. The Scriptures provide a crystal clear definition of what sin actually is: sin is the transgression of God's Law. Because sin is defined as breaking the commandments of the Creator, it logically follows that true repentance is a sustained return to observing His commandments and walking in His ways. We wholly reject the notion that obedience to God's commandments is irrelevant to faithful living.

Scriptural References

1 John 3:4 Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.
Romans 6:1–2 What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid...

II. Repentance is More Than Mere Regret

There is a profound difference between experiencing emotional regret and walking in biblical repentance. Regret is an internal feeling of sorrow; repentance is an active, outward demonstration of returning to God's standard. We define backsliding as turning away from God's Commandments to pursue one's own will. Such departure requires immediate repentance. Mere regret leaves a person in their sin, whereas true repentance compels a believer to forsake their own will and return to the established order of the Law, knowing that where a tree falls, so shall it lie.

Scriptural References

2 Peter 2:21 For it had been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them.

III. Inseparable from Grace and Faith

Understanding the necessity of keeping the Law does not negate grace; rather, it establishes what a grace-filled life looks like. We affirm that salvation is a gift of God's grace, made available only through the Messiah. Mankind is unable to achieve salvation through his own effort. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, and not of works, lest any man should boast.

However, following salvation, the believer must deeply desire to follow God's Commandments. We believe that true biblical faith inherently produces obedience. Sanctification requires us to be doers of the word and not hearers only, recognizing that faith void of obedient works to God's law is dead. Repentance is the bridge between the grace that saves us and the faith that produces righteous works. We are not saved by keeping the Law, but because we are saved by grace, we repent of our transgressions and return to the Law as the perfect standard of living.

Scriptural References

Ephesians 2:8–9 For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.
James 1:22 But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.
James 2:20 But wilt thou know, O vain man, that faith without works is dead?
1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.
