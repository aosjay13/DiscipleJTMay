I The True Definition of Eternal Life

When we think of "eternal life," we often simply think of living forever. However, Jesus gave us the exact definition of what eternal life truly is:

John 17:3  •  And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent.

1. According to Jesus' own words, what is the true definition of eternal life?

II The Test of Knowing God

Eternal life is an active, present-day relationship with the Father. Many equate knowing God with simply having a feeling or an emotional experience. However, the New Testament gives us an explicit test for knowing the Father, and it is inseparable from covenant obedience:

1 John 2:3–5  •  And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments. He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him. But whoso keepeth his word, in him verily is the love of God perfected: hereby know we that we are in him.

2. How do we know that we have truly come to know Him? What does the Bible call a person who claims to know Him but does not keep His commandments?

III The Barrier of Sin Removed

If eternal life is walking in an obedient relationship with God, what stopped us from having it? Our sin. Before we can fully appreciate salvation, we must understand what we are being saved from. Sin must be explicitly defined using the scriptural standard:

1 John 3:4  •  Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law.

John 3:16  •  For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.

3. His blood paid the ultimate penalty for our Law-breaking. According to 1 John 3:4, what is the biblical definition of sin? Why was it necessary for Jesus to come into the world?

IV The Present Reality of Life

1 John 5:11–12  •  And this is the record, that God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. He that hath the Son hath life; and he that hath not the Son of God hath not life.

4. According to these verses, when does this eternal life begin for the believer?

V The Abundant Life in God's Word

It is easy to misinterpret "abundant life" as simply a life free from earthly struggles or financial poverty. However, true biblical peace and spiritual abundance are inherently tied to loving and walking in God's Instructions:

John 10:10  •  The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.

Psalm 119:165  •  Great peace have they which love thy law: and nothing shall offend them.

5. According to Psalm 119:165, what kind of life and peace belongs to those who love God's Law? How does keeping God's commandments protect us from the "thief" who comes to steal, kill, and destroy?

VI The Call to Sanctification and Works

Salvation and eternal life require more than just mental agreement; they require walking as Jesus walked. The defining mark of a saint who possesses eternal life involves a dual commitment to grace and ongoing obedience:

Revelation 14:12  •  Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus.

6. Do you believe that God sent Jesus to shed His blood to pay the penalty for your law-breaking (sin)? Are you ready to live as a true saint, keeping the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus?

VII Holding Fast and Personal Reflection

While eternal life begins the moment you put your faith in Jesus, Scripture presents it as a relationship that must be maintained through covenant faithfulness and repentance. We have an ongoing responsibility to remain in active fellowship with the Savior:

Hebrews 3:12–14  •  Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God. But exhort one another daily... lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end.

Ezekiel 18:21, 24  •  But if the wicked will turn from all his sins that he hath committed, and keep all my statutes, and do that which is lawful and right, he shall surely live, he shall not die... But when the righteous turneth away from his righteousness, and committeth iniquity... in his sin that he hath sinned, in them shall he die.

FOR TEACHER USE ONLY  •  DO NOT DISTRIBUTE TO STUDENTS
1. According to Jesus' own words, what is the true definition of eternal life? (John 17:3)
Eternal life is intimately knowing the Father, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom He has sent. It is a present-tense, active, covenant relationship with our Creator, rather than simply a future destination after death.
2. How do we know that we have truly come to know Him? What does the Bible call a person who claims to know Him but does not keep His commandments? (1 John 2:3-4)
We know that we have truly come to know Him if we keep His commandments. If a person claims to have an intimate relationship with God ("I know Him") but does not walk in obedience to His commandments, the Bible calls that person a "liar," and declares that the truth is not in them. Intimacy with God cannot be separated from obedience to His Law.
3. According to 1 John 3:4, what is the biblical definition of sin? Why was it necessary for Jesus to come into the world?
The biblical definition of sin is the transgression (breaking) of God's Law. It was necessary for Jesus to come because our Law-breaking separated us from a holy God, earning us spiritual death. Jesus came to shed His blood, pay the penalty for our transgressions, and rescue us from perishing so that we could be restored to eternal life.
4. According to 1 John 5:11-12, when does this eternal life begin for the believer?
Eternal life begins right now, in the present. The text says "He that hath the Son hath life" (present tense). The moment we receive Jesus Christ and enter into a covenant relationship with Him, we possess eternal life.
5. What kind of life belongs to those who love God's Law? How does keeping God's commandments protect us from the "thief"?
Those who love God's Law have "great peace" (the abundant life), and "nothing shall offend them." Keeping God's commandments acts as a protective boundary for our lives. When we walk in God's instructions, we close the doors to the enemy, preventing the destruction, strife, and brokenness that come from lawlessness.
6. Do you believe that God sent Jesus to shed His blood... Are you ready to live as a true saint?
This is a personal confession of faith and commitment. The student's answer should reflect a sincere "Yes," acknowledging both their faith in Jesus' atoning blood for salvation and their commitment to walk in the commandments of God as a true saint.
7. Personal Reflection: What is one area of your life where you feel the Holy Spirit calling you to align with His commandments?
This is an open-ended personal response. Look for students to name a practical area of biblical obedience, such as keeping the seventh-day Sabbath, walking in financial integrity, honoring parents, speaking truth, or treating others with love and mercy, where they desire to align their actions with God's Word.
