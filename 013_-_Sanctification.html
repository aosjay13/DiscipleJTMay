A Pronomian Teaching

Sanctification

The Believer Set Apart and Made Holy

✦

Introduction

When a soul is saved by grace through faith, the work of God in that life has only begun. Justification is the moment God declares the believer righteous on account of Christ. Sanctification is the work that follows, the lifelong setting apart of that believer unto God and the steady making of him holy. The first is finished in an instant and never repeated. The second is begun at the new birth and carried on by the Holy Ghost until the believer is brought home.

To be sanctified is to be set apart. It carries two truths together. The believer is set apart by God for God, taken out of the common use of the world and claimed as His own. And the believer is then made holy in practice, his heart and his walk brought ever closer to the standard of God's own righteousness. We will trace both, for Scripture holds them together and never tears them apart.

Section I

What Sanctification Means

The word sanctify means to set apart, to make holy, to consecrate to a sacred use. In Scripture, things were sanctified when they were taken from common use and given wholly to God. The Sabbath was sanctified, the tabernacle and its vessels were sanctified, and the people of Israel were called to be a sanctified nation. To sanctify a thing was to mark it off as belonging to God and no longer to the world.

So it is with the believer. At salvation God claims him, sets His mark upon him, and separates him from the mass of the perishing world. This is the believer's standing. He is holy because God has set him apart. Yet the same Scriptures that declare him set apart also call him to live the part, to become in his daily walk what God has already declared him to be.

1 Thessalonians 4:3–4 For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication: That every one of you should know how to possess his vessel in sanctification and honour.
Leviticus 20:7–8 Sanctify yourselves therefore, and be ye holy: for I am the LORD your God. And ye shall keep my statutes, and do them: I am the LORD which sanctify you.

The Heart of It

Notice the order in Leviticus. The LORD says I am the LORD which sanctify you, and in the same breath He says ye shall keep my statutes, and do them. God is the one who sets His people apart, and the people show that they are set apart by keeping His commandments. The work is His, and the walk is theirs.

Section II

The Work of the Holy Ghost

Sanctification is not something the believer manufactures by his own strength. It is the work of the Holy Ghost within him. The same Spirit who seals the believer at salvation then labors in him to put sin to death and to bring forth holiness. The believer is not passive, for he must yield and obey, but the power to change comes from God and not from the flesh.

2 Thessalonians 2:13 But we are bound to give thanks alway to God for you, brethren beloved of the Lord, because God hath from the beginning chosen you to salvation through sanctification of the Spirit and belief of the truth.
1 Peter 1:2 Elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through sanctification of the Spirit, unto obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ: Grace unto you, and peace, be multiplied.
Romans 8:13 For if ye live after the flesh, ye shall die: but if ye through the Spirit do mortify the deeds of the body, ye shall live.

Mark the words of Romans, that we mortify the deeds of the body through the Spirit. The killing of sin is real work, and the believer does it, yet he does it through the Spirit and not by raw willpower. This is the joining of God's power and the believer's obedience that runs through the whole of sanctification.

Sealed, Then Sanctified

The Spirit who sanctifies is the same Spirit who sealed the believer at the new birth. The sealing marks him as God's possession. The sanctifying makes him fit for the Master's use. The one secures him, the other shapes him, and both are the gracious work of God toward those He has claimed.

Section III

Set Apart in Standing, Made Holy in Walk

Scripture speaks of sanctification in two ways, and the believer must hold both. In one sense the believer is already sanctified, set apart once for all by God at the moment of salvation. In another sense the believer is being sanctified, growing day by day into the holiness God requires. The first is his settled position before God. The second is his daily progress in practice.

1 Corinthians 6:11 And such were some of you: but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God.

Here Paul tells the Corinthians they are already sanctified, set apart, washed, claimed by God. Yet to those same believers he gives command after command to forsake sin and pursue holiness. They are set apart in standing, and they are called to grow holy in walk. There is no contradiction. The settled standing is the ground, and the daily growth is the fruit.

2 Corinthians 7:1 Having therefore these promises, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God.
Hebrews 12:14 Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord.

Both Held Together

The believer is holy because God has set him apart, and the believer pursues holiness because God commands him to grow. To claim the standing while despising the walk is to deceive oneself. To labor at the walk while doubting the standing is to fall into bondage. Grace gives the standing, and grace empowers the walk.

Section IV

The Standard of Holiness Is God's Law

If the believer is called to grow in holiness, we must ask what holiness looks like. We are not left to guess or to invent our own measure. The standard of holiness is the unchanging righteousness of God, and that righteousness is revealed in His commandments. Sin is the transgression of the law, and holiness is the keeping of it. To grow in sanctification is to grow in conformity to the very commandments that express God's holy character.

1 Peter 1:15–16 But as he which hath called you is holy, so be ye holy in all manner of conversation; Because it is written, Be ye holy; for I am holy.
Romans 7:12 Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good.

Peter draws his call to holiness straight out of the commandments God gave, for the words be ye holy, for I am holy are quoted from the law itself. The believer who would be holy is therefore the believer who returns to the commandments of God as the lamp unto his feet. This is no return to bondage and no attempt to earn salvation. It is the saved soul learning to walk in the holiness God has set apart for him.

1 John 2:6 He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also so to walk, even as he walked.

Teacher's Note

Guard this point carefully against two errors. The first error says that because we keep the commandments we are sanctified, making holiness the cause of salvation. The second error says that because we are saved we may ignore the commandments altogether. Both are wrong. We keep the commandments because God has sanctified us, and our obedience is the evidence and the outworking of the holiness He has already given. The Law is the standard of the walk, never the ground of the standing.

Section V

A Lifelong and Growing Work

Sanctification is not finished in a day. It is the patient work of a lifetime, in which the believer is changed from one degree of glory to another. There will be growth, and there will be struggle. The flesh wars against the Spirit, and the believer must take up the fight daily. Yet the direction of a sanctified life is always upward and onward, pressing toward the likeness of Christ.

2 Corinthians 3:18 But we all, with open face beholding as in a glass the glory of the Lord, are changed into the same image from glory to glory, even as by the Spirit of the Lord.
Philippians 1:6 Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ.
Philippians 3:13–14 Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended: but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before, I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus.

Paul, far along in his walk, still said he had not yet arrived but pressed on toward the mark. So it is for every believer. The work is real, the progress is real, and the One who began it will finish it. We do not sanctify ourselves into salvation. We grow, by the Spirit, into the holiness for which our salvation has set us apart.

Both Gift and Pursuit

Holiness is given and holiness is pursued. God sets the believer apart as a gift of grace, and God then calls the believer to chase after holiness with all his heart. The pursuit never earns the gift. The pursuit proves that the gift is real and living within.

Section VI

The Fruit and the End of Sanctification

What does a sanctified life produce, and where does it lead? The fruit of sanctification is holiness in the present and the fullness of eternal life at the last. As the believer yields to the Spirit and keeps the commandments of God, sin loses its grip and righteousness takes root. The end of that road is everlasting life with God, the soul made fit at last for His holy presence.

Romans 6:22 But now being made free from sin, and become servants to God, ye have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life.
1 Thessalonians 5:23 And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly; and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

The aim of the whole work is that the believer be preserved blameless unto the coming of the Lord. God sets him apart, the Spirit makes him holy, the commandments mark out his path, and the goal is a life wholly given to God and kept until Christ returns. This is sanctification, begun at the new birth and carried on without ceasing until the day we see Him.

Fruit Unto Holiness

Romans says the believer made free from sin has his fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life. Sanctification bears real fruit now in a changed and obedient life, and it leads at the last to the everlasting life God has promised. The road of holiness runs all the way home.

Conclusion

Set Apart by Grace, Made Holy by the Spirit

Sanctification is the steady work of God in the saved soul. He sets the believer apart at salvation, claiming him as His own and declaring him holy. Then by His Spirit He labors within that believer to make him holy in walk, conforming him to the righteousness revealed in the commandments. The standing is settled and never moves. The walk grows day by day until the believer is brought home.

Let no one trust in his own holiness to save him, for that work belongs to the grace of God received by faith. And let no saved soul despise the call to holiness, for the same God who saved us has set us apart to be holy, and His Spirit gives us the power to obey. We are sanctified by God, we are being sanctified by His Spirit, and we shall be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord.

Follow peace with all men, and holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord.
