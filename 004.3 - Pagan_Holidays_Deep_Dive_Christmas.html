The Origins of Christmas

The Festival of the Sun Across the Ancient World

✦

Continuation of Part I

Part I named Christmas as the first of the pagan holidays and traced its season to the worship of the sun, with roots reaching back to Babel and the kingdom of Nimrod. This deep dive follows that thread out across the nations. Long before any church kept December the twenty-fifth as the birth of Christ, the peoples of Babylon, Egypt, the Celtic and Norse lands, Persia, and Rome were already keeping a festival at this very season, and they kept it for the same reason. At the dead of winter the sun reaches its lowest point, seems to die, and then climbs again. To nations that worshipped the heavens, this was the death and rebirth of the sun god. What follows shows how that single impulse, scattered out of Babylon, produced the same midwinter worship in land after land, and how its customs were finally gathered up and given the name of Christ.

The Common Root

The Hinge of the Year

The deepest point of winter held both dread and hope for the ancient world. Day by day through the autumn the sun rose lower and the daylight grew shorter, until around the twenty-first of December the sun seemed to stand still, to die, and then slowly to climb again. To peoples who watched the heavens and worshipped what they saw there, this was no mere astronomy. It was the death and rebirth of the sun itself, the most important event of their religious year. So at this one season, in lands that had never met one another, the same festival appeared: fires kindled against the darkness, evergreens brought in as signs of life, feasting and gift-giving, and the worship of the sun god under a hundred different names.

This is the heart of the matter. Christmas did not invent the midwinter festival. It inherited one that was already ancient and already everywhere. The table below sets the festivals of the nations side by side, all of them clustered at the same turning of the year.

Land or People Festival What Was Honored
Babylon & Mesopotamia Midwinter rites of the sun and of Tammuz The reborn sun; the death and return of Tammuz, consort of the goddess Ishtar
Egypt Solstice rites of Ra and Horus The strengthening of the sun god; the mother Isis and her son Horus
Persia & the Roman army Worship of Mithras, the sun The sun god of the soldiers, bound up with the cult of the unconquered sun
The Celts & Druids Midwinter (Alban Arthan) and the Yule The return of the sun; the sacred evergreen, oak, and mistletoe
Norse & Germanic Yule (geol), twelve days of midwinter The turning of the year; the Yule log and evergreen; feasting and drinking
Rome (Republic on) Saturnalia, Dec. 17–23 Saturn, god of sowing; feasting, gift-giving, and license
Rome (3rd c. on) Dies Natalis Solis Invicti, Dec. 25 The birthday of the unconquered sun, set at the solstice
A note on the strongest case. Some popular teaching claims that gods such as Horus and Mithras were born of a virgin on December the twenty-fifth, attended by wise men, and that the church simply copied their stories point for point. Those specific parallels do not hold up; Horus's birth was kept in late summer and his mother was not a virgin, and Mithras was said to be born from a rock, with no ancient record fixing him to December. The case in this study does not rest on them and does not need them. What is solid and documented is this: the winter solstice was honored across the ancient world as the rebirth of the sun, December the twenty-fifth was the Roman feast of the birth of the unconquered sun, and the customs of the season come straight out of that sun worship. Teach the documented case, and it cannot be turned aside.
The Fountainhead

Babylon and the Reborn Sun

As Part I showed, the river of false worship rises at Babel. In the religion of Babylon the sun was honored as a god, and the dying and rising of the seasons was bound up with Tammuz, the consort of the goddess Ishtar. His yearly death was mourned, and his return was celebrated as the life of the year came back with the strengthening sun. At the center of it all stood the same image Part I traced from Babel outward: the mother and her son, the queen of heaven and the child-god, the very pattern the prophets later condemned in Israel.

According to the traditions that grew up around Babylon in later ages, the founder-king himself was remembered as born at the season of the winter solstice, and an evergreen was said to spring up as a sign of his rebirth. Whether or not every detail of that later legend can be proven from the ancient records, the core is plain in Scripture and history alike. Babylon worshipped the sun and the mother and child, it kept the rhythm of the dying and reviving god, and from Babylon that worship was carried into every land when the LORD scattered the people from the tower.

Jeremiah 44:17 ...to burn incense unto the queen of heaven, and to pour out drink offerings unto her...

The Land of the Sun

Egypt and the Returning Light

Egypt was a land built upon the worship of the sun. Ra, the sun god, was honored as the supreme deity, and the whole of Egyptian religion turned upon the daily death of the sun in the west and its rebirth in the east, and upon its yearly weakening and strengthening through the seasons. At the dead of winter, when the sun stood lowest, the Egyptians watched for its return as the renewal of life itself.

In Egypt also stood the image that runs through all these nations. The goddess Isis was portrayed holding her son Horus, the divine child, an image so enduring that it was carried down through the centuries and the empires long after Egypt's gods were forgotten by name. This is the same mother and child of Babylon under new names, the queen and her son. The Egyptians did not keep the exact December date that Rome would later fix, but they kept the same impulse: the veneration of the sun at the turning of the year and the worship of the mother and her child.

Handle Horus with care. The honest Egyptian connection is the sun worship of Ra and the mother-and-child image of Isis and Horus, both well documented and both tying straight back to Babylon. Avoid the popular claim that Horus was born of a virgin on December the twenty-fifth with wise men in attendance, since the records place his birth in late summer and his mother Isis was the wife of Osiris. The solar religion and the mother-child motif are the parts that stand.

The Northern Lands

The Celts, the Druids, and the Yule

Far from Babylon, in the forests of northern Europe, the same midwinter festival appeared among the Celtic and Germanic peoples. The Celts and their Druid priests marked the winter solstice as the rebirth of the sun and held the evergreen sacred, for it alone seemed to keep its life while every other tree stood bare and dead. The Roman writer Pliny recorded how the Druids cut the mistletoe from the sacred oak with a golden sickle, holding it to be a plant of divine power. The holly and the ivy, green through the dead of winter, were honored in the same way.

Among the Norse and Germanic peoples the festival was called Yule, a span of roughly twelve days of midwinter feasting. The great Yule log was kindled and kept burning to welcome the returning sun, and the halls were decked with evergreen boughs. There was deep drinking, feasting, and revelry, and tales were told of the gods riding through the winter sky. When these peoples were later folded into Christendom, they did not abandon the Yule. They kept its log, its greenery, its twelve days, and its feasting, and only changed the name to Christmas.

Scriptural References

Deuteronomy 12:30 ...that thou enquire not after their gods, saying, How did these nations serve their gods? even so will I do likewise.
The Gathering Point

Rome: Saturnalia and the Unconquered Sun

It was in Rome that all these streams gathered into the festival the world now keeps. The oldest of Rome's midwinter feasts was the Saturnalia, held from the seventeenth to the twenty-third of December in honor of Saturn, the god of sowing. For these days the ordinary rules of life were overturned. There was public sacrifice and a great public banquet, masters waited on their servants, schools and courts were closed, the homes were hung with greenery, and the people gave one another gifts and gave themselves over to feasting and license. Nearly every custom later called Christmas can be seen here already in full.

In the third century the emperors raised the worship of the sun to the center of Roman religion. The feast of the birth of the unconquered sun, the Dies Natalis Solis Invicti, was kept on the twenty-fifth of December, the day reckoned as the winter solstice, when the sun was reborn. The cult of Mithras, a sun god especially loved by the Roman soldiers, was bound up with this same devotion to the sun, though the ancient record does not actually fix Mithras's birth to that date. December the twenty-fifth had become, for the whole Roman world, the birthday of the sun.

The Substitution

The Church Takes the Sun's Day

For the first three centuries, the followers of Christ did not keep His birth at all. The Scriptures nowhere command it, nowhere record the day, and the early believers regarded the keeping of birthdays as a custom of the heathen rather than of the godly. When some did begin to ask when Christ was born, no one agreed; several different dates were proposed across the empire, and the matter was unsettled for generations.

By the middle of the fourth century, a Roman calendar from the year 354 records the birth of Christ being kept on the twenty-fifth of December, the very day of the feast of the unconquered sun. The name of Christ was set upon the day of the sun, and the customs of Saturnalia and of the northern Yule came in along with the date: the feasting, the gifts, the greenery, the burning log, and the revelry. The shell of the pagan festival remained; only the label was changed.

This did not go unnoticed in later ages. When the Puritans of England and New England read their Bibles closely in the seventeenth century, they saw the holiday for what it was, a thing of human invention with no warrant in the word of God, draped in customs drawn from the worship of the sun. For a season they outlawed its keeping altogether, holding that a day God never appointed had no claim upon His people.

The Verdict

The Customs and the Verdict

When the customs of Christmas are laid out and traced to their sources, not one of them comes from the Scriptures, and not one of them honors the birth of Christ in any way He commanded. Each comes instead from the midwinter worship of the nations.

Custom What It Was Where It Came From
The evergreen tree Greenery brought indoors as a sign of life through the dead of winter Norse and Celtic Yule; Roman greenery; the tree of Jeremiah 10
The Yule log A great log kindled to welcome the returning sun Norse and Germanic Yule
Mistletoe A sacred plant cut from the oak and hung for its power The Celtic Druids, recorded by Pliny
Holly & ivy Evergreen decking of the midwinter feast Roman Saturnalia and European midwinter
Gift-giving The exchange of presents during the festival Roman Saturnalia and the Roman new year
Feasting & revelry Days given over to eating, drinking, and license Roman Saturnalia and Norse Yule
December 25 The date of the celebration itself The Roman feast of the birth of the unconquered sun

The verdict of Scripture is the same as it was in Part I. God forbids His people to worship Him in the manner the nations worshipped their gods, and He forbids the borrowing of pagan forms to dress up as devotion to Him. The shepherds in the field by night tell us the season is wrong. The tree cut from the forest and adorned tells us the custom is forbidden. And the day of the sun's birth tells us whose festival this truly was. The birth of Christ is a glorious truth, but it is not honored by keeping the festival of the sun under a new name. As Part I concluded, the call of God is to come out from among the nations and to keep the days He Himself appointed. For those appointed times, see the companion document Moedim in the Church and the article on the Moedim in the Bylaws of Faith and Practice.
