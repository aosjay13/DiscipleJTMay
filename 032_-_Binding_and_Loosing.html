A Pronomian Teaching

Binding and Loosing

The Authority Christ Gave to His Church

✦

Introduction

When the Lord Jesus spoke of giving the keys of the kingdom of heaven to His church, and of binding and loosing on earth and in heaven, He granted a real and weighty authority to His people. This is one of the great promises of Scripture concerning the power and responsibility entrusted to the church. Yet it is also one of the most misunderstood, twisted by some into a kind of magic formula and ignored by others as too mysterious to grasp.

We will take the words of Christ as they stand and let Scripture interpret Scripture. The authority to bind and loose is genuine, given by the King to His people, but it is always exercised under His lordship, in agreement with heaven, and according to His word. It is not a power by which men command God or control the unseen world by their own will. We will look at what Christ gave, how it is exercised, and how the believer is to walk in the authority entrusted to him, in humility and in step with the will of God.

Section I

The Keys of the Kingdom

The Lord first spoke of binding and loosing when He gave to His church the keys of the kingdom of heaven. A key is a symbol of authority, the right to open and to shut. In giving the keys, Christ entrusted to His people the authority to declare the terms of the kingdom, opening its door to those who come to Christ in faith and shutting it against unbelief and sin.

Matthew 16:19 And I will give unto thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever thou shalt loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven.
Revelation 3:7 These things saith he that is holy, he that is true, he that hath the key of David, he that openeth, and no man shutteth; and shutteth, and no man openeth.

Notice that the key of David belongs first and supremely to Christ Himself, who opens and shuts with final authority. The keys He gives to His church are a delegated authority, exercised in His name and under His rule. The church does not own the kingdom or rule it by its own right, but holds the keys as a steward holds the keys of his master's house, opening and shutting according to the master's will.

A Delegated Authority

The keys of the kingdom are the authority to open and shut, given by Christ to His church. They are not the church's own power but a delegated trust, held in His name and exercised under His rule, opening the kingdom to faith and shutting it against unbelief and sin.

Section II

Binding and Loosing in Agreement With Heaven

We must look carefully at the words the Lord used, for they tell us how this authority works. The church binds and looses on earth what is bound and loosed in heaven. Heaven is not waiting to follow the church's lead, as though men could compel God to ratify whatever they decide. Rather the church, walking in step with God, declares on earth what God has already determined in heaven. We follow heaven's lead, not the reverse.

Matthew 18:18 Verily I say unto you, Whatsoever ye shall bind on earth shall be bound in heaven: and whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven.
1 John 5:14 And this is the confidence that we have in him, that, if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us.

The authority to bind and loose is therefore not a blank check for the church to do whatever it pleases and expect heaven to obey. It is the privilege of acting in agreement with heaven, declaring and enforcing on earth the will of God already settled above. This is the same principle that governs all true prayer, that we ask according to His will. The power lies not in our words but in our agreement with the God who holds all authority.

Teacher's Note

Guard the people against a common error here. Binding and loosing is not a formula by which men command God or seize control of the unseen world by their own will. Heaven does not bow to earth. The church declares on earth what God has determined in heaven, walking in agreement with His will and His word. Any teaching that turns these words into a means of bending God to human desires has missed the Lord's meaning entirely. The authority is real, but it is the authority of agreement with God, not power over Him.

Section III

Binding and Loosing in the Discipline of the Church

The clearest setting in which the Lord placed these words is the discipline of the church. In Matthew the eighteenth chapter, the promise of binding and loosing comes directly after His instruction for dealing with a sinning brother. The church, acting rightly according to His word, has the authority to declare a person bound in his sin while unrepentant, or loosed and restored when he repents.

Matthew 18:15–17 Moreover if thy brother shall trespass against thee, go and tell him his fault between thee and him alone: if he shall hear thee, thou hast gained thy brother. But if he will not hear thee, then take with thee one or two more, that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established. And if he shall neglect to hear them, tell it unto the church: but if he neglect to hear the church, let him be unto thee as an heathen man and a publican.
John 20:23 Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them; and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained.

See how binding and loosing flows out of the discipline of the church. When the church, following the Lord's pattern, declares a sin retained against the unrepentant or remitted to the repentant, it acts with the authority of heaven, for it is declaring what God Himself has determined concerning sin and repentance. This is not the church inventing judgments of its own, but faithfully announcing God's verdict according to His word.

Declaring God's Verdict

In the discipline of the church, binding and loosing means declaring a person bound in unrepentant sin or loosed in repentance. The church does not create these judgments but announces what God has already determined, acting in agreement with heaven according to the Lord's own pattern.

Section IV

The Authority of Agreement in Prayer

Immediately after speaking of binding and loosing, the Lord gave a precious promise about agreement in prayer. Where two or three gather in His name, agreeing together according to His will, He is present among them and their prayer carries great power. The authority of the church is exercised not only in discipline but in united, believing prayer offered in agreement with God.

Matthew 18:19–20 Again I say unto you, That if two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them of my Father which is in heaven. For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.
John 14:13–14 And whatsoever ye shall ask in my name, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son. If ye shall ask any thing in my name, I will do it.

Notice that the prayer is offered in His name and in agreement with one another, and the Lord Himself is present in the midst. To ask in His name is to ask in line with His character and His will, not merely to attach His name to our own desires. When the church gathers and agrees in such prayer, it exercises real authority, for the King is present and the prayer ascends in agreement with heaven. This is the heart of the church's power in prayer.

Gathered in His Name

The church's authority is exercised in united prayer offered in Christ's name and in agreement with His will. Where two or three gather in His name, He is present among them. To ask in His name is to ask according to His character and will, and such prayer carries the authority of heaven.

Section V

Authority Over the Powers of Darkness

The authority Christ gives His people extends to the spiritual realm, over the powers of darkness that war against the kingdom. The Lord gave His disciples power over unclean spirits, and that authority belongs to His church still, exercised in His name and by His power. We do not face the enemy in our own strength, but in the authority of the One who has already triumphed over all the powers of darkness.

Luke 10:19 Behold, I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy: and nothing shall by any means hurt you.
Colossians 2:15 And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it.
Mark 16:17 And these signs shall follow them that believe; In my name shall they cast out devils; they shall speak with new tongues.

All this authority rests on the victory of Christ, who spoiled principalities and powers and triumphed over them at the cross. The believer exercises authority over the enemy not by his own merit or might, but in the name of Jesus and on the ground of His finished work. The power is real, but it is His power, given to His people, and it operates only in submission to Him and in line with His will.

In His Name and His Victory

The authority over the powers of darkness rests entirely on the victory of Christ, who triumphed over them at the cross. The believer stands against the enemy in the name of Jesus and on the ground of His finished work, never in his own strength. The power is Christ's, exercised in submission to Him.

Section VI

Exercised in Humility, Under His Lordship

Finally, all the authority Christ gives is to be exercised in humility, under His lordship, and according to His word. It is never a power to be used for our own ends, nor a cause for pride or presumption. The sons of Sceva learned the danger of using the name of Jesus as a mere formula without true submission to Him. Real authority belongs only to those who are truly under His authority.

Acts 19:13–15 Then certain of the vagabond Jews, exorcists, took upon them to call over them which had evil spirits the name of the Lord Jesus, saying, We adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preacheth. And there were seven sons of one Sceva, a Jew, and chief of the priests, which did so. And the evil spirit answered and said, Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye?
James 4:7 Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.

Mark the order in James, submit yourselves to God, and then resist the devil. Authority over the enemy flows from submission to God. The sons of Sceva used the name without that submission and were overcome. The believer who walks under the lordship of Christ, in obedience to His word and in humble dependence on Him, may exercise real authority. But it is always His authority, exercised in His name, for His glory, and according to His will, never our own.

Submission Before Authority

Authority flows from submission. We submit to God first, and then we may resist the devil. The sons of Sceva used the name of Jesus without that submission and were overcome. Real authority belongs only to those who walk under the lordship of Christ, in obedience and humility.

Conclusion

Real Authority, Under the Lordship of Christ

Christ gave His church the keys of the kingdom and the authority to bind and loose, a real and weighty trust. Yet it is a delegated authority, exercised in agreement with heaven, not a power by which men command God. The church binds and looses on earth what God has determined in heaven, declaring His verdict in the discipline of the assembly, ascending to Him in united prayer offered in the name of Christ, and standing against the powers of darkness on the ground of His finished victory.

Through all of it, the authority belongs to Christ and is exercised under His lordship, in His name, according to His word, and for His glory. It flows from submission to God and is held in humility, never in pride or presumption. Let us then walk in the authority our King has given, not to serve ourselves but to advance His kingdom, always in step with heaven and always under the One who holds the key of David, who opens and no man shuts, and who shuts and no man opens.

Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.
