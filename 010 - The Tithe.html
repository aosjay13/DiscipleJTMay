A Teaching Guide

The Tithe

The Lord's Tenth, from Abraham to the Believer Today

✦

Introduction

Few subjects stir as much confusion in the church as the tithe. Some treat it as a heavy burden left behind with the old covenant, and others wave it about as a money slogan with no roots in Scripture at all. Both miss the mark. The tithe is older than the law of Moses, older than the nation of Israel, and older than the Levitical priesthood. It begins with a man of faith giving a tenth to a priest who points us straight to Christ.

In this study we will trace the tithe through the whole of Scripture. We will see it given by Abraham before the law was ever written, vowed by Jacob, woven into the worship of Israel, defended by the prophets, named by the Lord Jesus Himself, and explained in the letter to the Hebrews. By the end we want one thing to be settled in every heart, that giving the Lord His portion is not a chain but a joy, and that it flows from love rather than fear.

Section I

What the Tithe Is: The Lord's Tenth

The word tithe simply means a tenth. To tithe is to set apart one part out of ten and give it to the Lord. It is not a vague gesture of generosity but a measured portion, a clear and definite tenth of what comes into our hands.

Scripture treats this tenth as something that already belongs to God. We do not give Him the tithe as if we were doing Him a favor with our own property. We return to Him a portion that He calls His own. The law of Moses states this plainly, and the principle reaches back long before Moses ever lived.

Leviticus 27:30 And all the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land, or of the fruit of the tree, is the LORD'S: it is holy unto the LORD.

Notice the language. The tithe is the LORD'S, and it is called holy. To keep it back is not to hold on to what is ours, it is to keep what has already been set apart as His. Once we understand that the tenth belongs to God, the whole question changes. We are not asking how much of our money we must part with. We are asking whether we will be faithful with what was never fully ours to begin with.

The Definition

A tithe is a tenth, set apart and given to the Lord. Scripture calls this tenth holy and says it belongs to Him. Faithfulness with the tithe is a matter of stewardship, not of charity.

Section II

The Tithe Before the Law: Abraham and Melchizedek

The most important fact about the tithe is also the most overlooked. It did not begin with Moses. The first tithe in Scripture is given by Abraham, centuries before the law was handed down at Sinai and long before the tribe of Levi was set apart for the priesthood. When some say the tithe was a part of the law that has passed away, this single event answers them. You cannot abolish with the law something that was already in practice before the law existed.

After Abraham returned from rescuing his nephew Lot, he was met by a mysterious figure named Melchizedek, who was both a king and a priest of the most high God.

Genesis 14:18–20 And Melchizedek king of Salem brought forth bread and wine: and he was the priest of the most high God. And he blessed him, and said, Blessed be Abram of the most high God, possessor of heaven and earth: and blessed be the most high God, which hath delivered thine enemies into thy hand. And he gave him tithes of all.

Abraham gave a tenth of all the spoils to this priest of God. No commandment had been written ordering him to do so. He gave the tithe as an act of worship and honor, recognizing that his victory had come from the hand of God. This is the heart of the tithe in its purest form, a man of faith honoring the Lord with a tenth, freely and gladly, before any law required it.

The Foundation

The tithe predates the law of Moses. Abraham tithed by faith, not by commandment. A practice that came before the law was not created by the law, and it does not vanish when the shadows of the law are fulfilled.

Section III

Jacob's Vow at Bethel

Abraham was not the only patriarch to tithe before the law. His grandson Jacob did the same. Fleeing from his brother, Jacob lay down to sleep and saw a ladder reaching to heaven. When he awoke, he made a solemn vow to the God who had appeared to him, and at the center of that vow was the tithe.

Genesis 28:20–22 And Jacob vowed a vow, saying, If God will be with me, and will keep me in this way that I go, and will give me bread to eat, and raiment to put on, So that I come again to my father's house in peace; then shall the LORD be my God: And this stone, which I have set for a pillar, shall be God's house: and of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenth unto thee.

Here is a second witness, again long before Sinai. Jacob promises that of everything God gives him, he will return a tenth. Two of the great fathers of the faith, Abraham and Jacob, both marked their walk with God by giving Him a tenth. This was not the dead weight of legal duty. It was a free expression of trust and gratitude toward a God who provides.

A Second Witness

By the mouth of two witnesses a matter is established. Both Abraham and Jacob tithed by faith, apart from the law, marking their relationship with God by returning a tenth of all He gave.

Section IV

The Tithe Within the Law of Moses

When the law was given, the tithe was not invented but folded into the worship of the nation. God assigned the tithe to the tribe of Levi, who served at the tabernacle and later the temple. The Levites owned no land of their own, so the tithes of the people became their provision while they gave themselves fully to the service of God.

Numbers 18:21 And, behold, I have given the children of Levi all the tenth in Israel for an inheritance, for their service which they serve, even the service of the tabernacle of the congregation.

The tithe also carried a purpose of joy and remembrance. Part of it was eaten before the Lord at the place He chose, so the people would learn to fear and honor Him. And every third year a portion was set aside for the Levite, the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow, so that the needy in the land would be fed.

Deuteronomy 14:22–23 Thou shalt truly tithe all the increase of thy seed, that the field bringeth forth year by year. And thou shalt eat before the LORD thy God, in the place which he shall choose to place his name there... that thou mayest learn to fear the LORD thy God always.

So under Moses the tithe did several things at once. It supported the priesthood, it taught the people to honor God, and it provided for the poor. The administration ran through Levi, but the underlying principle, that a tenth belongs to the Lord, was the same principle Abraham had already lived out by faith.

Teacher's Note

It is worth distinguishing the principle of the tithe from the Levitical machinery that once administered it. The tenth belonging to God is older than Levi and continues after Levi. The specific arrangement of carrying the tithe to the temple and feeding the Levites was tied to a priesthood and a temple system that has changed. Keep this distinction sharp, because it is the answer to the objection that the tithe ended when the temple fell.

Section V

The Storehouse and the Charge of Malachi

Through the prophet Malachi, God spoke some of the strongest words in all of Scripture about the tithe. The people had grown careless and were holding back what belonged to Him. God called this by a startling name. He called it robbery.

Malachi 3:8–10 Will a man rob God? Yet ye have robbed me. But ye say, Wherein have we robbed thee? In tithes and offerings. Ye are cursed with a curse: for ye have robbed me, even this whole nation. Bring ye all the tithes into the storehouse, that there may be meat in mine house, and prove me now herewith, saith the LORD of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it.

Two things stand out. First, to keep back the tithe is treated as taking what belongs to another, since the tithe is already the Lord's. Second, God gives a rare invitation. He says prove me now. He calls His people to test His faithfulness by their obedience and to watch how He provides. This is not a promise of riches on demand, and it must never be twisted into a scheme for personal gain. It is the simple assurance that God honors the faith of those who honor Him.

A Word of Balance

The blessing of Malachi is real, but it is not a vending machine. We do not give in order to get rich. We give because the tenth is His and because we trust Him to provide. The blessing He pours out is far wider than money. It is the steady peace of a soul that knows it has been faithful, and the quiet confidence that the One who owns heaven and earth will not let His faithful children be forsaken.

Section VI

What the Lord Jesus Said About the Tithe

Some assume the Lord Jesus set the tithe aside. The opposite is true. When He rebuked the religious leaders of His day, He did not tell them to stop tithing. He told them to keep tithing while also tending to the deeper matters of the heart that they were neglecting.

Matthew 23:23 Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.

Listen closely to the Lord's own words. He says these ought ye to have done, speaking of judgment, mercy, and faith, and not to leave the other undone, speaking of the tithe. With His own mouth He affirmed both. The error of the Pharisees was never that they tithed. Their error was that they tithed carefully while ignoring mercy and justice. The Lord corrected the imbalance. He did not cancel the tithe.

The Lord's Verdict

Jesus placed the tithe and the heart side by side and said do both. He never taught that giving the Lord His tenth had ended. He taught that it must be joined to mercy, justice, and faith, never used as a substitute for them.

Section VII

The Order of Melchizedek and Why the Tithe Remains

We now come to the heart of the whole study. If the tithe was once carried to Levi at the temple, and Levi's priesthood has been fulfilled and set aside in Christ, does the tithe still stand? The letter to the Hebrews answers this with great care, and the answer rests on the figure of Melchizedek.

Hebrews reminds us that Melchizedek received tithes from Abraham, and that even Levi, who would later receive tithes from Israel, was in a sense present in Abraham at that moment and paid tithes through him. In other words, the Melchizedek priesthood is greater than and older than the Levitical one.

Hebrews 7:9–10 And as I may so say, Levi also, who receiveth tithes, payed tithes in Abraham. For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him.

The point is this. The tithe was given to Melchizedek before Levi ever existed, and now Christ Himself is our high priest after that very order. The priesthood of Levi has passed, but the priesthood of Melchizedek stands forever, and so does the tithe that belongs to it.

Hebrews 7:17 For he testifieth, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec.

So the believer today does not bring the tithe to a temple that no longer stands, nor to a Levitical priest whose service has been fulfilled. We honor the Lord through the eternal priesthood of Jesus Christ, who lives forever to make intercession for us. The temple changed and the priesthood changed, but the principle that came down through Abraham to Melchizedek was never bound to the law of Moses in the first place. It rests on an order that has no end.

The Heart of the Matter

The tithe belongs to the order of Melchizedek, an order older than Levi and one that endures forever in Christ. The Levitical administration was fulfilled, but the tithe that ran through Melchizedek did not fall with it. It continues for the believer who serves under our eternal high priest.

Teacher's Note

This is the cleanest way to handle the common claim that the tithe was abolished as part of the law. Grant freely that the Levitical and temple arrangement has passed. Then show that the tithe did not originate there. It came through Abraham to a priest after the order of Melchizedek, the very order Christ now holds. What is rooted in an eternal priesthood does not expire with a temporary one.

Section VIII

Tithes and Offerings: Two Kinds of Giving

Scripture often pairs the tithe with offerings, yet the two are not the same. Recall the words of Malachi, where God said the people had robbed Him in tithes and offerings. Two distinct things are named. Understanding the difference keeps our giving healthy and clear.

The tithe is the fixed tenth that already belongs to the Lord. It is the baseline of faithful stewardship, the portion we return rather than the portion we decide. An offering is what we give above and beyond the tithe, freely, as our heart moves us and as need arises. The tithe shows our faithfulness. The offering shows our generosity.

2 Corinthians 9:7 Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.

This verse speaks directly to the spirit of the offering, given freely and cheerfully as one purposes in his own heart. The tithe sets the floor of our giving, and offerings rise above it. A believer who only ever gives the bare tenth and never gives beyond it has missed the joy of openhanded generosity, while a believer who gives spontaneous offerings yet withholds the tithe has skipped the first step of faithfulness. The two belong together.

The Distinction

The tithe is the Lord's set tenth, returned in faithfulness. An offering is given above the tithe, freely and cheerfully. One is the foundation of giving and the other is its overflow.

Section IX

The Heart Behind the Gift

It would be a grave mistake to walk away from this study thinking the tithe is mainly about money. It is about the heart. Where our treasure goes, our heart follows, and how we handle the Lord's tenth reveals what we truly trust.

Matthew 6:21 For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.

The tithe was never meant to be paid in fear or under threat. It is the response of a grateful child who knows that every good thing has come from the Father's hand. We do not give to earn our salvation. We are saved by grace through faith, not by any work of our own, and giving the tithe adds nothing to that finished work. Rather, the faithful tithe is fruit. It is one of the natural marks of a heart that has been changed, a love that gives because it has first been loved.

When the tithe is given grudgingly, the giver gains little, even if the right amount leaves his hand. When it is given freely, in trust and thanksgiving, it becomes a true act of worship. The same tenth can be a burden or a blessing, and the difference lies entirely in the heart.

Fruit, Not Wage

Giving the Lord His tenth does not purchase His favor, for His favor was bought by the blood of Christ and given freely to us. The tithe is the fruit of a saved life, not the price of salvation. We give because we have been loved, not in order to be loved. Keeping this order right guards the heart against both legalism and fear.

Section X

Living It Out: A Faithful Steward

How then do we put this teaching into practice? The pattern of Scripture is simple and steady. We give the Lord the first and the best, not the leftovers, and we give it with gladness.

Proverbs 3:9–10 Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase: So shall thy barns be filled with plenty, and thy presses shall burst out with new wine.

Honoring the Lord with the firstfruits means we set His portion apart first, before our other spending begins, rather than seeing what happens to be left at the end of the month. Faithfulness is a settled habit, not an accident. When the tithe comes off the top, it teaches us week by week that God comes first in all things, including our finances.

A faithful steward also remembers that all of it belongs to God, not only the tenth. The tithe is the part we return in a special way, but the remaining nine tenths are still held in trust from Him and are to be used wisely, honestly, and with care for others. The tither who is greedy and careless with the rest has missed the lesson. The whole life, and the whole purse, are to be lived before the Lord.

The Pattern to Follow

Give the Lord the first portion, not the last. Set apart the tenth before other spending, give it cheerfully, and steward the remainder faithfully as well. The whole life belongs to God, and the tithe is the regular, honest confession that we know it.

Conclusion

A Tenth Returned in Love

From Abraham on the field of victory, to Jacob beneath the open heaven, to Israel at the temple, to the words of the Lord Jesus, and to the eternal priesthood of Christ after the order of Melchizedek, the witness of Scripture is steady and clear. The tithe is the Lord's, it is older than the law, and it endures for the believer who serves under our living high priest.

Yet we close where every right teaching on giving must close, at the heart. The tithe was never a price to be paid for God's love, for that love was poured out at the cross and given freely. It is the fruit of a thankful life, the glad return of a tenth by a child who trusts his Father to provide. Give it in faith, give it in joy, and you will find that you cannot outgive the God who owns heaven and earth.

Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase.
