The Mazzaroth
and the Signs in the Heavens

What Scripture Says, What History Shows,
and Where the Twelve Signs Really Came From

Introduction

There is a teaching circulating widely among believers that the twelve constellations of the zodiac were placed in the sky by God as a picture book of the gospel, and that Adam, Seth, and Enoch read the whole plan of redemption in the stars long before a word of Scripture was written. It is a beautiful idea. Many sincere brethren hold it. Books have been written on it, sermons preached from it, and charts drawn up matching each sign to a Hebrew month and a prophetic meaning.

This teaching examines that claim honestly. We will look at every place Scripture speaks of the Mazzaroth and the constellations, we will trace where the twelve signs actually came from in recorded history, and we will test the popular claims against both. Some of what we find will confirm what the heavens truly declare. Some of it will not survive the examination.

That is not a loss. We do not need the zodiac to preach Christ. We have the complete, infallible written Word, and it is sufficient. Our concern here is that a teacher stands accountable for what he puts in the mouths of God's people, and no argument built on sand will help a soul when the storm comes. What follows is offered so that this congregation can speak about the heavens with confidence, with reverence, and without repeating claims that cannot be defended.

I.

One Word, One Verse: What Mazzaroth Actually Is

The word Mazzaroth appears exactly one time in all of Scripture. It is not a doctrine, a system, or a book. It is a single word in a single verse, and the verse belongs to one of the most humbling passages in the Bible.

Job 38:31–33 "Canst thou bind the sweet influences of Pleiades, or loose the bands of Orion? Canst thou bring forth Mazzaroth in his season? or canst thou guide Arcturus with his sons? Knowest thou the ordinances of heaven? canst thou set the dominion thereof in the earth?"

Notice first who is speaking and why. This is God answering Job out of the whirlwind. From chapter thirty-eight forward, the Lord asks Job question after question that Job cannot possibly answer. Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth? Have you walked in the depths of the sea? Can you command the morning? The entire point of the passage is that man does not command the heavens and does not understand them. God does.

So the one verse in Scripture that names the Mazzaroth uses it to prove that man cannot handle the heavens. That is a striking place to begin a teaching that claims man was given the heavens as a scroll to read.

As for what the word means, the honest answer is that we do not know with certainty. Translators have rendered it as the constellations generally, as the twelve signs, as a particular star or star group, and as the planets. The King James translators left it untranslated rather than guess. A closely related form, mazzalot, appears in Second Kings, and there the King James renders it "the planets." We will look at that verse shortly, because its context matters a great deal.

Hold This

Scripture uses the word Mazzaroth once, gives no definition, lists no signs, assigns no meanings, and places the word inside a rebuke about the limits of human understanding. Everything built beyond that point is built by men, not by the text.

Teacher's Note

This is a good place to remind a class of a rule that protects us everywhere in Scripture: we never stake a doctrine on a single Hebrew word, and we never build a system on a word whose meaning is genuinely uncertain. When a teaching requires one obscure term to carry the whole weight, that is a warning sign, not a foundation.

II.

The Purpose God Assigned to the Lights

Scripture is not silent about why the lights are there. God tells us plainly on the fourth day of creation.

Genesis 1:14–15 "And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years: And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so."

Four purposes are given: to divide day from night, to mark signs, to mark seasons, and to mark days and years. The word rendered "seasons" is the same word used for the appointed times of the Lord, the holy convocations commanded in Leviticus. That connection is real and important, and we will return to it near the end of this study. It is the strongest and soundest link between the heavens and the faith of a believer.

But notice what Genesis does not say. It does not say the lights were given to tell a story. It does not say they encode a message, a sequence, or a plan. They mark time and they give light, and they serve as signs in the sense that Scripture elsewhere shows us, which we will examine in section eight.

What the Heavens Do Declare
Psalm 19:1–4 "The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge. There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard. Their line is gone out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world. In them hath he set a tabernacle for the sun,"

This psalm is quoted constantly in support of a gospel written in the constellations, so let us read it as it stands. What do the heavens declare? The glory of God and the work of His hands. What is the nature of that speech? It has no speech and no language, and it is heard everywhere on earth without translation. That is precisely the opposite of a coded message requiring an interpreter, a chart, and a list of Hebrew star names to unlock.

Then look at what David does next. Beginning in verse seven of the same psalm, he turns from the heavens to the law of the Lord, which is perfect, converting the soul. The psalm has two halves on purpose. The heavens declare that God is glorious. The written Word declares who He is and what He requires. David does not blur the two, and neither should we.

Psalm 147:4 "He telleth the number of the stars; he calleth them all by their names." Isaiah 40:26 "Lift up your eyes on high, and behold who hath created these things, that bringeth out their host by number: he calleth them all by names by the greatness of his might, for that he is strong in power; not one faileth."

God names the stars. That is glorious, and it is true. It does not follow that He revealed those names to men, or that the names men later gave the stars are the names God uses. Both passages make the point that God's knowledge of the host of heaven is beyond ours, not that ours matches His.

Hold This

The heavens give what theologians call general witness. They testify that a Creator exists and is glorious, and that testimony reaches every man on earth. They do not preach the cross, name the Messiah, or explain how a sinner is justified. That knowledge comes only through the written Word and the preaching of it.

III.

The Constellations Scripture Names

Scripture does name a handful of star groups. It is worth seeing the complete list, because it is short.

Job 9:9 "Which maketh Arcturus, Orion, and Pleiades, and the chambers of the south." Amos 5:8 "Seek him that maketh the seven stars and Orion, and turneth the shadow of death into the morning, and maketh the day dark with night: that calleth for the waters of the sea, and poureth them out upon the face of the earth: The LORD is his name:"

Add the Mazzaroth passage in Job 38 and one reference in Isaiah 13 to "the constellations" in general, and that is essentially the whole of it. Pleiades, Orion, Arcturus, the chambers of the south, and one uncertain term.

Now consider what is missing. Not one of the twelve signs is named in Scripture. Not the Virgin, not the Lion, not the Balances, not the Ram. No sequence is given. No meanings are attached to any constellation anywhere in the Bible. And in every single passage where these star groups are mentioned, the point being made is the same: God made them, God controls them, and man does not.

Bonus

Amos gives us a pattern worth preaching on its own. He points to Orion and the seven stars, and immediately says "Seek him that maketh" them. The creation always points past itself to the Creator. The moment a man stops at the sign and begins studying the sign for its own sake, he has stepped off the path Amos laid down.

IV.

The Line God Drew Around the Heavens

If God had given mankind a redemptive message written in the constellations, we would expect Scripture to encourage the study of them. Instead we find the opposite. The warnings are frequent, severe, and consistent from the Law through the Prophets into the New Testament.

Deuteronomy 4:19 "And lest thou lift up thine eyes unto heaven, and when thou seest the sun, and the moon, and the stars, even all the host of heaven, shouldest be driven to worship them, and serve them, which the LORD thy God hath divided unto all nations under the whole heaven." Deuteronomy 18:10–12 "There shall not be found among you any one that maketh his son or his daughter to pass through the fire, or that useth divination, or an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch, Or a charmer, or a consulter with familiar spirits, or a wizard, or a necromancer. For all that do these things are an abomination unto the LORD: and because of these abominations the LORD thy God doth drive them out from before thee."

Now to the related word we set aside earlier. When righteous Josiah cleansed the land, this is what he swept away.

2 Kings 23:5 "And he put down the idolatrous priests, whom the kings of Judah had ordained to burn incense in the high places in the cities of Judah, and in the places round about Jerusalem; them also that burned incense unto Baal, to the sun, and to the moon, and to the planets, and to all the host of heaven."

The word rendered "the planets" here is the near relative of Mazzaroth. This is the only other place the root shows up in this form, and it appears in a list of things a godly king destroyed. That is significant. The two occurrences of this word family in Scripture are a rebuke to Job and an idolatry Josiah burned out of Judah. Neither one is a commendation.

The Prophets Are Blunt
Isaiah 47:13–14 "Thou art wearied in the multitude of thy counsels. Let now the astrologers, the stargazers, the monthly prognosticators, stand up, and save thee from these things that shall come upon thee. Behold, they shall be as stubble; the fire shall burn them; they shall not deliver themselves from the power of the flame: there shall not be a coal to warm at, nor fire to sit before it." Jeremiah 10:2 "Thus saith the LORD, Learn not the way of the heathen, and be not dismayed at the signs of heaven; for the heathen are dismayed at them."

Isaiah names three trades and condemns all three: those who read the stars, those who watch them, and those who forecast by the months. Jeremiah tells Israel plainly not to learn the way of the nations regarding the signs of heaven. Note carefully that Jeremiah does not say the heathen invented the signs and Israel should reclaim them. He says the way the heathen handle the heavens is a way Israel must not learn.

Romans 1:20–23 "For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse: Because that, when they knew God, they glorified him not as God, neither were thankful; but became vain in their imaginations, and their foolish heart was darkened. Professing themselves to be wise, they became fools, And changed the glory of the uncorruptible God into an image made like to corruptible man, and to birds, and fourfooted beasts, and creeping things."

Paul tells us exactly how much creation reveals and exactly where men went wrong with it. Creation reveals God's eternal power and divine nature, enough to leave every man without excuse. Men then took that testimony and turned it into images of men, birds, and beasts. Read the list of the twelve signs and see how thoroughly it matches Paul's description: a man, a maiden, twins, a lion, a bull, a ram, a goat, a scorpion, fishes, a crab. Paul is not describing a divine picture book that got corrupted. He is describing the corruption itself.

Hold This

There is a real and necessary distinction here. Observing the heavens is lawful and even commanded. Israel had to watch the moon to keep the appointed times. Sailors and farmers watched the stars. Reading the heavens for hidden knowledge, fortune, or messages is forbidden. The difference is not how sincere the reader is. The difference is whether he is receiving what God plainly assigned the lights to do, or extracting from them what God never put there.

V.

Where the Twelve Signs Came From

The popular teaching claims the twelve signs are primeval, given to Adam or Seth or Enoch, preserved in the earliest star names, and only later stolen and corrupted by Babylon and Egypt. That is a historical claim, and historical claims can be checked. When we check it, the record runs the other direction.

The Documented Record
  • Mesopotamia, roughly 1000 BC. The oldest substantial star catalog we possess is a Babylonian text listing the constellations along the path of the moon. It does not contain twelve signs. It lists around eighteen constellations in that band, of uneven size, tied to omen practice and to the gods of Babylon.
  • Babylon, the fifth century BC. This is when the twelve-sign system as we know it appears. Babylonian astronomers reduced the older band to twelve equal divisions of thirty degrees each, matching an administrative calendar of twelve thirty-day months. The twelve-sign zodiac is a Babylonian mathematical convenience, and it has a traceable birthday.
  • Greece, the fourth century BC onward. Greek astronomers borrowed the twelve-sign system from Babylon, and it passed from there into the Roman world, into India, and eventually into the Arabic-speaking world.
  • The Hebrew names. The Hebrew names now attached to the twelve signs are not ancient Hebrew astronomy. They are Hebrew renderings of the Babylonian and Assyrian names. Moznayim simply means scales, and the Assyrian name it renders also means scales. Betulah means a maiden. Shor means a bull. Sartan means a crab. These are translations, not revelations.
  • The first Jewish list. The earliest surviving Jewish source that names all twelve in Hebrew is a mystical text called Sefer Yetzirah, a work of debated date but certainly many centuries after the close of the Old Testament. It has no claim to Mosaic or prophetic authority.

Teacher's Note

That last point deserves care in front of a class. The list in Sefer Yetzirah does not begin with the Virgin in Elul. It begins with Taleh, the Ram, in Nisan, and runs forward through the year in the order of the months. The sequence that starts with the Virgin and ends with the Lion is not the Jewish sequence and is not attested anywhere in antiquity. It was assembled in the nineteenth century, and we will see by whom in the next section.

The Star Names Problem

Much of the gospel-in-the-stars material rests on the supposed original meanings of individual star names. This is where the whole structure is weakest, and a teacher should understand why before repeating any of it.

The star names commonly cited are overwhelmingly Arabic, and they came into European use through medieval translations of Greek astronomy. They are also, in the main, ordinary descriptive names. Denebola in the constellation of the Lion comes from an Arabic phrase meaning the tail of the lion, because that is where the star sits. It does not mean the Judge who comes. Names like this describe position and appearance, the same way we would say the north star.

Beyond that, we simply cannot get behind the record. There is no surviving source that preserves star names from before Babylon. The claim that the earliest names carried gospel meanings cannot be verified, because the earliest names are gone. A teaching that requires evidence which does not exist is not a teaching we can put our weight on.

Bonus

One example shows the problem clearly. The Balances, Libra, were not originally a separate sign at all. In the older Babylonian and Greek record, those stars formed the claws of the Scorpion. The two brightest still carry Arabic names meaning the southern claw and the northern claw. The Balances were split off into their own sign in the Roman period.

So the sign said to represent God's unchanging standard of righteousness, weighing man and finding him wanting, is the one sign in the twelve that we can document as a late human rearrangement of somebody else's scorpion.

VI.

Where the Gospel in the Stars Teaching Came From

This teaching is often presented as an ancient truth recovered. It has a documented origin, and it is recent.

  • Frances Rolleston, 1862. An English woman with an interest in classical languages compiled a book of notes titled Mazzaroth, or the Constellations. She died before finishing it, and it was published largely as she left it. Her method was to compare the sounds of Arabic star names to Hebrew words that sounded similar, and to assign meanings on that basis. Everything that follows descends from her work.
  • Joseph Seiss, 1882. An American Lutheran minister took Rolleston's notes and organized them into a readable book, The Gospel in the Stars. A great deal of the material commonly repeated today actually originates with Seiss rather than with any ancient source.
  • E. W. Bullinger, 1893. An English clergyman who had corresponded with Rolleston published The Witness of the Stars and included the theory in the appendices of his study Bible, which carried it to a very wide audience.
  • The twentieth century forward. Well-known teachers picked it up and popularized it further. Very little genuinely new work has been added in over a century. Nearly every modern presentation traces back through these same three books.

Hold This

No church father taught this. No rabbi taught this. No apostle mentioned it. No prophet alluded to it. It appears for the first time in the eighteen sixties, in a book of unfinished notes, built on comparing the sounds of Arabic words to Hebrew words. That is not the recovery of an ancient revelation. That is a Victorian theory.

Teacher's Note

We have handled this same pattern before in this series, and it is worth naming the pattern itself. When a claim about ancient history has a single nineteenth-century book behind it and no earlier witness, we treat it as a tradition of men, not as fact, no matter how many later authors repeat it or how devout those authors were. Sincerity is not evidence. Repetition is not evidence.

Bonus

It is worth saying plainly that these were not wicked people or false teachers in the ordinary sense. Seiss and Bullinger were believers who loved the Lord and wanted to show that the whole creation testifies of Christ. Their motive was good. Their method was not, and a good motive does not make a weak argument strong. We can honor their intent and still decline to teach their conclusions.

VII.

Testing the Specific Claims

General objections are one thing. Let us take the particular claims that circulate most often and examine each on its own.

The Sequence Begins with the Virgin and Ends with the Lion

This ordering is essential to the whole scheme, because it is what makes the twelve signs tell a story from the virgin birth to the returning King. But no ancient source orders them this way. The Babylonian order, the Greek order, and the Jewish order in Sefer Yetzirah all begin with the Ram and run forward through the year. The Virgin-to-Lion arrangement was constructed in the nineteenth century to produce the desired narrative.

Verdict: Not ancient. Constructed to fit the theory.

Sartan Originally Meant the Sheepfold, Later Corrupted into a Crab

Sartan is the ordinary Hebrew word for a crab. The Babylonian name for the same group of stars also meant a crab. There is no documented earlier stage in which it meant an enclosure or a sheepfold. The claim comes from the nineteenth-century sound-alike method, not from any text.

Verdict: The crab is the older reading, not the corruption.

The Tribal Standards in Numbers 2 Bore the Zodiac Signs

Numbers 2 tells us the tribes camped by their standards, and it never tells us what was pictured on them. Scripture does compare Judah to a lion in Genesis 49, and that comparison is genuine. But Genesis 49 compares Dan to a serpent, not to an eagle or a scorpion. The four-banner arrangement of lion, man, ox, and eagle comes from later rabbinic tradition, and its attachment to the zodiac traces back to Rolleston herself.

Verdict: Tradition, and late tradition at that. Not Scripture.

The Months Align Perfectly with the Prophetic Meanings

The alignments do not hold up under their own logic. Nisan is the first month of the biblical year and the month of the Passover Lamb, yet in the Virgin-to-Lion arrangement the Lamb falls at position eight. If the sequence were God's own order, the first month would not land two thirds of the way through the story. And the sign that sits over any given month shifts across centuries because of the slow wobble of the earth's axis, so any month-to-sign chart is a snapshot of one era, not a fixed decree.

Verdict: The scheme contradicts itself at the most important month.

Ancient Star Maps Show a Man Crushing the Scorpion's Head

There is a figure in that region of the sky standing over the Scorpion, and that image is genuinely old. But it is the Greek figure Ophiuchus, the serpent-bearer, and the Babylonian figure behind it was a god of their pantheon. Reading Genesis 3:15 out of it requires deciding in advance what the picture should mean. The promise of the seed who bruises the serpent's head is precious and certain, and it rests on the words God spoke in the garden. It does not need a Babylonian constellation to hold it up.

Verdict: The picture is real. The interpretation is imported.

Job 38:32 Proves God Ordained the Twelve Signs

The verse names Mazzaroth without defining it, and the meaning is genuinely uncertain among translators. Even if it did refer to a zodiac, it would not follow that Job knew of twelve, that his twelve were our twelve, or that he read a gospel in them. And the verse is a rebuke. God is telling Job he cannot bring the Mazzaroth forth in its season.

Verdict: The verse cannot bear this weight.

Teacher's Note

A fair question will come up: could God have placed some testimony in the arrangement of the stars that we simply cannot recover? He could have. We cannot prove a negative, and we should not pretend to. What we can say is that Scripture never claims it, no ancient witness records it, and the reconstructions offered to us do not survive examination. We are not required to have an opinion on what God might have done. We are required to teach what He said.

VIII.

The Signs the Heavens Genuinely Give

Having cleared the ground, we can now build on it. Scripture does speak of signs in the heavens, and it does so with real power. This is the material a teacher should be preaching, and it is better than anything the zodiac scheme offers.

The Star of the King
Numbers 24:17 "I shall see him, but not now: I shall behold him, but not nigh: there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel, and shall smite the corners of Moab, and destroy all the children of Sheth." Matthew 2:1–2 "Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judaea in the days of Herod the king, behold, there came wise men from the east to Jerusalem, Saying, Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him."

Here is a genuine heavenly sign at the birth of Christ. But notice how God handled it. The star did not explain anything. It brought the wise men to Jerusalem, where they had to ask where the King would be born, and the answer came from the written Word through the scribes quoting Micah. The star got them moving. The Scripture told them where to go. That is the biblical relationship between the heavens and the Word, and it is exactly backward from the zodiac scheme.

The Woman, the Sun, and the Twelve Stars
Genesis 37:9 "And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me." Revelation 12:1–2 "And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars: And she being with child cried, travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered."

Scripture does use celestial imagery, and it interprets its own imagery. Joseph's dream gives us the key: sun, moon, and stars stand for the patriarch, his wife, and the tribes. When John sees a woman crowned with twelve stars, the twelve are the tribes of Israel, because Genesis already told us so. That is how symbolism is meant to work. The Bible supplies the meaning of its own pictures rather than sending us to a Babylonian chart.

The Signs Before the Day of the Lord
Joel 2:30–31 "And I will shew wonders in the heavens and in the earth, blood, and fire, and pillars of smoke. The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and terrible day of the LORD come." Luke 21:25 "And there shall be signs in the sun, and in the moon, and in the stars; and upon the earth distress of nations, with perplexity; the sea and the waves roaring;" Matthew 24:29–30 "Immediately after the tribulation of those days shall the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be shaken: And then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory."

These are the signs in the heavens that Scripture actually commands us to watch for, and they are unmistakable. They require no chart and no expert. When the sun goes dark and the moon turns to blood, no one will need a book to interpret it. The Lord did not hide the sign of His coming in a puzzle.

Hold This

Every genuine heavenly sign in Scripture works the same way. It is given at God's initiative, at a moment of His choosing, and it points to something the written Word has already explained. It is never a standing code for men to decipher on their own schedule. And we set no dates, ever, from any of it.

IX.

The Lights and the Appointed Times

There is one connection between the heavens and the walk of a believer that is completely solid, and it deserves the emphasis that the zodiac scheme has been stealing from it.

Genesis 1:14 "And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:" Psalm 104:19 "He appointed the moon for seasons: the sun knoweth his going down." Leviticus 23:4 "These are the feasts of the LORD, even holy convocations, which ye shall proclaim in their seasons."

The word behind "seasons" in Genesis 1:14 and Psalm 104:19 is the same word behind "seasons" in Leviticus 23. God set the sun and moon in the sky so that His people would know when His appointed times had arrived. That is not a hidden meaning drawn out by clever reading. It is the stated purpose, given on the fourth day of creation and then used exactly that way through the whole of Scripture.

The new moon marks the month. The barley and the equinox govern the placement of the first month. The Passover falls on the fourteenth day of that month, and the count to Pentecost runs from the sheaf of the firstfruits. Every one of these depends on watching the lights God appointed. Israel did not need an astrologer. They needed to look up and count.

Hold This

The heavens are God's clock, and He commanded His people to read it. The lights tell us when to gather, not what will happen to us. That single sentence marks the whole boundary between obedient observation and forbidden divination.

Bonus

Consider how much this raises the honor of the heavens rather than lowering it. The sun and moon are not decoration and they are not a fortune teller's tool. They are the instruments by which the Creator summons His people to His feasts. Every Sabbath sunset and every new moon is the Creator keeping an appointment with His own house.

That is a far greater dignity than being turned into a picture book.

X.

How to Handle This in the Assembly

Many believers have encountered this teaching and found it moving. Some in the congregation may have charts, books, or videos on it. How we correct matters as much as what we correct.

  • Do not make this a test of fellowship. A brother who believes there is a gospel written in the stars is mistaken about a matter of evidence. He is not denying the faith. Treat him as a brother and reason with him as one.
  • Honor the good motive. Those drawn to this teaching are usually drawn by a right instinct, that all creation testifies of Christ. That instinct is correct. Affirm it, then show them where Scripture actually locates that testimony.
  • Show, do not merely assert. Open Job 38 and read the context. Read Psalm 19 all the way through both halves. Read Romans 1 and let Paul describe the images himself. The passages do the work.
  • Guard the line firmly. Wherever this teaching has begun to slide into reading horoscopes, checking birth signs, or looking to the heavens for personal guidance, it must be dealt with plainly. That is the forbidden thing, and no Christian veneer over it changes what it is.
  • Give them something better. Do not leave a hole where the chart used to be. Take them to the star at Bethlehem, to the woman crowned with twelve stars, to the darkened sun and the sign of the Son of man, and to the appointed times marked out by the moon. There is more genuine wonder in the actual text than in any reconstruction.
Acts 17:11 "These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so." 1 Thessalonians 5:21 "Prove all things; hold fast that which is good." Colossians 2:8 "Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ." Deuteronomy 29:29 "The secret things belong unto the LORD our God: but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law."

That last verse is the settled resting place for this whole study. There are secret things. God has them, and they are His. What He has revealed, He gave to us and to our children, and He gave it for a purpose: that we may do the words of His law. The revealed things are enough. They have always been enough.

Conclusion

The Heavens Point Upward, and the Word Points to Christ

The heavens are magnificent, and they testify. They tell every man on earth, without a word and without a translator, that there is a Creator of eternal power and glory. They mark the days, the years, and the appointed times of the Lord. They will yet be darkened and shaken at the coming of the Son of man. All of that is true, and it is worth preaching.

But they do not preach the cross. They do not name the Lamb. They cannot tell a sinner that the blood of Jesus Christ cleanses from all sin. That word was given by inspiration, written down, preserved, and placed in our hands, and it is sufficient. When men go looking for a fuller gospel in the arrangement of the stars, they are looking past the very thing God gave them for the very thing He never did.

We hold the whole counsel of God in the Scripture. We keep His Sabbaths and His appointed times by the lights He set for that purpose. We watch for the signs He told us to watch for, and we set no dates. And when we lift our eyes to the night sky, we do what Amos told us to do: we seek Him that maketh the seven stars and Orion, and we give the glory to Him alone.
