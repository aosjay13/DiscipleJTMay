Discipleship Class · Level 1 · Lesson 6

What Happens When a Christian Sins?

Forgiven Freely, Kept by Faith, and Called to Walk in the Light

✦

In our last lesson we learned who we are in Christ: a new creature, washed, sanctified, and justified. Now we have to face an honest question that every believer runs into sooner or later. What happens when a Christian sins? Not the sinner who has never come to God, but the one who has been born again, who loves the Lord, and who still stumbles.

Men tend to fall into one of two ditches here. Some believe that every sin cancels their salvation, so they live in fear, getting saved again every Sunday and never sure where they stand. Others decide that since they are saved, sin no longer matters at all. Both are wrong, and both are dangerous. Scripture walks a straight road between them, and that road is what we will follow today.

I

We Will Stumble, and We Must Not Pretend Otherwise

1 John 1:8–9•If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

John was writing to believers, and he includes himself: if we say. No one in this room has reached a place where he cannot fail. A man who claims he has no sin has not become holy; he has become deceived. The Bible defines sin plainly: sin is the transgression of the law (1 John 3:4). By that measure, every one of us has things to bring to God.

What makes the believer different from who he was before conversion is not that he never sins. It is that sin now grieves him. Before, he could sin and sleep well. Now there is a new heart in him that wants to please God, and when he falls, it hurts. That grief is not a sign that something has gone wrong with your salvation. It is one of the clearest signs that something has gone right.

II

Righteousness Is a Gift, Not a Wage

Romans 4:2–5•For if Abraham were justified by works, he hath whereof to glory; but not before God. For what saith the scripture? Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness. Now to him that worketh is the reward not reckoned of grace, but of debt. But to him that worketh not, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness.

If we could earn our right standing with God, we would have something to brag about. We would stand in heaven and say, Lord, thank You for the cross, but don't forget what I did. God has shut that door. Boasting is excluded, Paul says, by the law of faith (Romans 3:27). The wages of sin is death, but eternal life is the gift of God (Romans 6:23), and a gift is something you receive, not something you are paid.

Look at who God justifies: the ungodly. He did not wait for us to clean ourselves up before He declared us righteous. Abraham believed God, and that faith was counted to him for righteousness (Genesis 15:6). That is where our standing begins. And the faith that God counted was a living faith. Scripture says Abraham obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws (Genesis 26:5), and James says his faith wrought with his works, and by works was faith made perfect (James 2:22). His obedience did not buy his righteousness. It showed that his faith was real.

III

The Blessed Man of Psalm 32

Romans 4:6–8•Even as David also describeth the blessedness of the man, unto whom God imputeth righteousness without works, Saying, Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not impute sin.

To impute means to put something to a person's account. God puts righteousness to our account without works, and He refuses to put our sin there. In the Greek of verse 8, Paul uses a strong double negative. It is not that God might hold it against us some days and not others. He will by no means count it.

But Paul is quoting David, and it is worth opening Psalm 32 to see who that blessed man is. David goes on: in whose spirit there is no guile (Psalm 32:2). Then he tells his own story. When he kept silent about his sin, his bones waxed old and God's hand was heavy on him day and night. Then something changed:

Psalm 32:5•I acknowledged my sin unto thee, and mine iniquity have I not hid. I said, I will confess my transgressions unto the LORD; and thou forgavest the iniquity of my sin. Selah.

The man to whom the Lord will not impute sin is the man who stops hiding it. He is not the man who pretends he has none, and he is not the man who no longer cares. He is honest before God, he brings his sin into the light, and he finds that God has already made a way to cover it. He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy (Proverbs 28:13).

IV

The New Covenant: His Law Written, Our Sins Remembered No More

Hebrews 10:16–17•This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days, saith the Lord, I will put my laws into their hearts, and in their minds will I write them; And their sins and iniquities will I remember no more.

The writer of Hebrews is quoting Jeremiah 31:33–34, the great promise of the new covenant. Notice that it comes as one promise with two halves. God says He will write His laws on our hearts, and He says He will remember our sins no more. Many people hold on to the second half and forget the first.

God does not forgive us so that we can go on living without His law. He forgives us and then writes that law inside us, so that what was once a rule on stone becomes the desire of a new heart. Forgiveness deals with the sins of the past. The law written on the heart turns us away from sin in the future. When a Christian stumbles, both halves of the covenant go to work: the blood cleanses him, and the law in his heart convicts him and draws him back to the right path.

V

One Offering, Never Repeated

Hebrews 10:14•For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified.

Under the old system, the sacrifices for sin were offered again and again, and the writer of Hebrews says that if they could have finished the job, the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins (Hebrews 10:2). Every new sacrifice was a fresh reminder of guilt. Christ's offering is different. He offered Himself once, and that one offering does not need to be repeated.

That is why a believer who stumbles does not need to be saved all over again. Jesus is not crucified afresh each time we fail. The blood that cleansed us at the start is the same blood that keeps cleansing us as we walk with Him. It purges our conscience from dead works, and Hebrews tells us what for: to serve the living God (Hebrews 9:14). A clean conscience is not a reason to stop caring about sin. It is the freedom to serve God without the weight of old guilt.

Notice also the last words of the verse. In the Greek, them that are sanctified is a continuing form, those who are being sanctified. The offering is finished and complete. Our sanctification is a work God carries on in us day by day as we keep walking with Him.

VI

When We Fall: An Advocate With the Father

1 John 2:1–2•My little children, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous: And he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world.

John gives us both sides in one breath. First, I write these things that ye sin not. The goal is never to sin. Second, if any man sin, we have an advocate. An advocate is one who stands beside you and speaks on your behalf. When a believer falls, he does not fall out of the family. He has a Brother at the Father's right hand pleading his case, and that Brother's own blood is the propitiation, the full payment that satisfied God's justice.

This is what we do when we sin. We do not run from God; we run to Him. If we walk in the light, as he is in the light, the blood of Jesus Christ cleanses us from all sin (1 John 1:7). Peter denied the Lord three times, and Jesus had already prayed that his faith would not fail, and told him, when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren (Luke 22:32). Peter wept, came back, and went on to preach at Pentecost. A just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again (Proverbs 24:16). The righteous man is not the one who never falls. He is the one who keeps getting back up and going to God.

VII

Stumbling Is Not the Same as Walking Away

Romans 6:1–2•What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid. How shall we, that are dead to sin, live any longer therein?

Paul saw this question coming. If grace covers sin, why not keep on sinning? His answer is God forbid. There is a great difference between a believer who trips and falls while walking toward God and a person who has turned around and is walking away from Him. The first needs a hand up. The second needs to repent.

Hebrews 10:26•For if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins,

This verse sits in the same chapter that tells us we are perfected for ever by one offering. The writer is not contradicting himself. The believer who abides in Christ is safe in Christ. But the one who knowingly and stubbornly goes back to a life of sin is stepping away from the only sacrifice there is. Jesus said, If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered (John 15:6). Peter warns that it would have been better for some never to have known the way of righteousness than to turn from it after knowing it (2 Peter 2:21).

These warnings are not given to make sincere believers afraid every time they stumble. They are given so that no one treats grace as a license. The name of Jesus tells us what He came to do: he shall save his people from their sins (Matthew 1:21). He saves us from them, not in them.

VIII

Kept by the Power of God

Jude 24–25•Now unto him that is able to keep you from falling, and to present you faultless before the presence of his glory with exceeding joy, To the only wise God our Saviour, be glory and majesty, dominion and power, both now and ever. Amen.

So what holds us in place when we fail? Not our own record. We are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation (1 Peter 1:5). Our faith rests in Jesus, and He is able to keep us from falling and to bring us all the way into the presence of His glory. When you trust Him as your Savior, you are not trusting Him to get you started and then leave the rest to you. You are trusting Him to carry you all the way home.

That is why sin should not be the center of a believer's attention. God should be. We run this race looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith (Hebrews 12:2). The man who keeps his eyes on Jesus will be honest about his sin when it shows up, quick to confess it, and quick to get back up. And the closer he walks with God, the less room sin has to grow.

✦ To Carry With You ✦

When you fall, do not run from God. Run to Him. Be honest about your sin, confess it, get back up, and keep your eyes on Jesus. And if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous. 1 John 2:1

Teacher's Answer Key · Not for Student Distribution

1. What happens if we say we have no sin, and what does God do when we confess?

We deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. When we confess, God is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. He does not only pardon the sin; He cleans us up from it.

2. Whom does God justify, and what is counted for righteousness?

The ungodly. God did not wait for us to become good before He declared us righteous. Our faith in Him is what is counted for righteousness, not our works.

3. What was put to Abraham's account, and how did he receive it?

Righteousness, a right standing with God. He received it by believing God (Genesis 15:6), not by earning it, so he has nothing to boast about before God. His faith was a living faith that went on to obey (Genesis 26:5; James 2:22).

4. With works or apart from them, and what did David do?

Apart from works. God imputes righteousness without works (Romans 4:6). David acknowledged his sin, stopped hiding it, and confessed it to the LORD, and God forgave the iniquity of his sin. The blessed man is the one who is honest with God about his sin.

5. How is righteousness received, and what does gift imply?

As a gift. A gift is freely given, at no cost to the one who receives it. If it were earned it would be wages, not a gift (Romans 4:4; Romans 6:23).

6. What two things does God promise, and why together?

He will put His laws into our hearts and write them in our minds, and He will remember our sins and iniquities no more. They come together because God does not forgive us so we can keep living without His law. He forgives the past and writes His law within us so that we walk in His ways from here on (Jeremiah 31:33–34).

7. How many offerings, how long, and is a new sacrifice needed?

One offering. Believers are perfected for ever. No new sacrifice is needed and we do not get saved all over again each time we stumble. The same blood keeps cleansing us as we walk in the light (1 John 1:7). The words them that are sanctified describe people who are being sanctified, so God keeps working in us day by day.

8. Why did John write, and who is our advocate?

So that we sin not. If any man does sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, who speaks on our behalf. He is the propitiation for our sins, the payment that fully satisfied God's justice. When we fall, we run to Him, not away from Him.

9. Does grace permit sin, and what is the difference?

No. God forbid. We are dead to sin and cannot keep living in it. A believer who stumbles is still facing God; he grieves over his sin, confesses it, and gets back up (Proverbs 24:16). A person who sins wilfully has turned his back and chosen sin with full knowledge of the truth, stepping away from the only sacrifice there is. The safe place is abiding in Christ (John 15:6), and the door is open to anyone who turns back.

10. Who keeps you, how, and how far?

God is able to keep us from falling. We are kept by the power of God, through faith. To trust Jesus as Savior is to trust Him to take us all the way home, to present us faultless before the presence of His glory. He is the author and the finisher of our faith (Hebrews 12:2).

✦ Teacher's Note · If a Student Asks, Do I Need to Be Saved Again Every Time I Sin?

Hebrews 10:14 · 1 John 1:9 No, and say it plainly, because some in the room may have lived for years under that fear. A child who disobeys his father is still his father's child. He needs to make it right, but he does not need to be born again a second time. One offering perfected for ever them that are sanctified. When a believer sins, the answer is confession and cleansing, not a new conversion.

Luke 15:24 Be just as plain about the other side. The prodigal son was still a son while he was in the far country, but his father said, this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found. A man who walks away from God and stays away is in real danger, and that is why the father ran to meet him the moment he turned home. Use this to comfort the sincere and to wake up the careless.

1 John 3:6–9 A student may ask about whosoever abideth in him sinneth not. The verbs in this passage are in a continuing tense in the Greek. John is describing a man who goes on living in sin as a way of life, not a believer who stumbles and confesses. Read it alongside 1 John 1:8–2:1, written by the same man in the same letter.

✦ Teacher's Note · Weighing Some Claims in the Source Material

Romans 4:8, "never, not ever" The source says verse 8 uses an emphatic negative in the Greek, and that is accurate: Paul uses a strong double negative, by no means. But Paul is quoting Psalm 32, and the psalm shows who the blessed man is. He is the one in whose spirit there is no guile, who acknowledged his sin and confessed it (Psalm 32:2, 5). The promise is real, but it describes the honest, confessing believer. It is not a blanket promise that God will overlook the sin of a man who once believed and then turns away (Ezekiel 18:24; 2 Peter 2:20–21).

"Even when you sin and don't have time to confess it" The source says faith in Jesus holds us in right standing even when we sin and have no time to confess. There is truth here. God is not standing over us with a stopwatch, and a believer who is walking in the light is under the cleansing blood (1 John 1:7). Confession is not a magic formula we must finish before the clock runs out. It is a heart that agrees with God about sin. But be careful that students do not hear this as sin no longer mattering. The same letter that promises cleansing also says, these things write I unto you, that ye sin not (1 John 2:1).

Hebrews 10:2, "no more conscience of sins" The source uses this verse to say sin should not be our focus. In context, the verse is about the old sacrifices never fully clearing the worshipper's conscience. Christ's blood does, so we are free from the torment of guilt that needs a fresh sacrifice. That is true and freeing. But the same chapter warns against wilful sin (Hebrews 10:26), and Paul tells believers to examine themselves (2 Corinthians 13:5). God is our focus, and a man focused on God will still deal honestly with his sin.

Hebrews 10:14, "them that are sanctified" In the Greek this is a present participle, those who are being sanctified. The source quotes the verse for the finished work of the cross, which is right. Add the other half: the offering is complete, and our sanctification is ongoing.

Abraham and works The source says Abraham was counted righteous not by what he did but by believing God. That is true of Genesis 15:6, and it is Paul's point in Romans 4. But do not leave students thinking Abraham's faith had no works. God said Abraham kept my commandments, my statutes, and my laws (Genesis 26:5), and James says his faith was made perfect by works (James 2:21–23). Faith is the root; obedience is the fruit.

Hebrews 10:16, the law written on the heart The source quotes this verse only to reach verse 17, your sins I will remember no more. Read both. The new covenant does not remove God's law. It moves it from stone to the heart (Jeremiah 31:33). Forgiveness and obedience are two halves of the same promise.

The last question The source's last question offered church, heaven, or Russia as choices. That has been replaced here with Jude 24 and 1 Peter 1:5, which make the same point from Scripture: we trust Him to take us all the way home.
