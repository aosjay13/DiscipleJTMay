A Doctrinal Foundation

The Nature and Authority of God’s Law

Why it was given, why it endures, and what it reveals about the One who gave it

✦

Before We Begin

Many people speak of God’s law as though it were a burden He laid on Israel and then quietly set aside. They picture it as a heavy yoke, a list of rules that served its day and has since expired. But Scripture never speaks of the law that way. It calls the law holy, just, and good. It calls it perfect, sure, and everlasting. It treats the law not as a passing arrangement but as a window into the very heart of God.

If we want to understand the law rightly, we have to ask the right questions. Not only what does it command, but why was it given. Not only what does it require of us, but what does it tell us about the One who wrote it. When we ask those questions, we find that the law is far more than a code of conduct. It is a revelation of God’s own character, written down so that His people could know Him and walk with Him.

This teaching takes up four matters in order. First, why God gave the law at all. Second, why the law endures, sharing in the unchanging nature of God Himself. Third, how the law reveals who God is. And fourth, the principle that separates what was a shadow from what remains in full force. Hold these four together, and the law stops looking like a burden and starts looking like a gift.

Part One

Why God Gave the Law

God did not give the law to make men miserable, and He did not give it to give us a way to earn His favor. He gave it because He loves His people and wants them to live well. A good father does not hand his children rules to crush them. He gives them boundaries because he knows what will bless them and what will harm them. The law is the wisdom of a loving God put into plain words so that His people would know how to live.

Scripture gives us several reasons the law was given, and they fit together. The first is that the law shows us how to love. When a man asked Jesus to name the greatest commandment, He answered with love for God and love for neighbor, and then said that on these two hang all the law and the prophets. The whole law is simply love spelled out in detail. It tells us what love looks like when it touches our worship, our speech, our marriages, our money, and our treatment of the weak.

Deuteronomy 10:12–13 And now, Israel, what doth the LORD thy God require of thee, but to fear the LORD thy God, to walk in all his ways, and to love him, and to serve the LORD thy God with all thy heart and with all thy soul, to keep the commandments of the LORD, and his statutes, which I command thee this day for thy good?

Notice the last three words of that verse. The commandments were given for our good. They were never meant to be a chain around the neck of God’s people. They were meant to be a path that leads to life, and the One who marked out the path knows the ground better than we do.

The Law Defines Sin

A second reason the law was given is that it tells us what sin is. Without a standard, we have no way to know when we have done wrong. We may feel guilty or feel proud, but our feelings are not a reliable measure. The law gives us a fixed line. It tells us plainly what God counts as righteousness and what He counts as transgression. Apart from that line, sin has no clear shape.

1 John 3:4 Whosoever committeth sin transgresseth also the law: for sin is the transgression of the law. Romans 3:20 Therefore by the deeds of the law there shall no flesh be justified in his sight: for by the law is the knowledge of sin.

This is a point worth holding tightly. If the law has passed away, then sin itself has no definition, and the cross loses its meaning, because Christ died for our transgression of that very law. The law remains the mirror that shows us our true condition. It cannot wash the face it reveals, but it can show us that the face needs washing, and that drives us to the only One who can cleanse it.

The Law Leads Us to Christ

This brings us to a third reason. The law was given to lead us to the Messiah. When the law shows us our sin and we find we cannot fix ourselves, we are left looking for a Savior. Paul describes the law as a schoolmaster, a guardian that brings the child along until he is brought to Christ. The law was never the destination. It was the faithful guide that points beyond itself to the One who saves.

Galatians 3:24 Wherefore the law was our schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ, that we might be justified by faith.
Hold This Clearly

The law was never given as a ladder to climb up to God. No one is saved by keeping it, because no one keeps it perfectly. It was given to teach us how to love, to define our sin, and to drive us to Christ. Once we are saved by grace, the same law remains as the shape of a faithful life, no longer our judge, now our guide.

Part Two

The Law Shares God’s Unchanging Nature

Here we reach the heart of the matter. The law endures because it flows out of the character of God, and the character of God does not change. A law that simply reflected the customs of one nation in one era could fade with that era. But a law that reflects who God is must last as long as God lasts, and God lasts forever.

Malachi 3:6 For I am the LORD, I change not; therefore ye sons of Jacob are not consumed. Hebrews 13:8 Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever.

If God does not change, then His standard of right and wrong does not change either. What was an abomination to Him in the days of Moses is an abomination to Him now, because His holiness has not shifted. What pleased Him then pleases Him still. The moral law is not a matter of taste or season. It is the steady expression of a God who is the same across all generations.

Scripture says this about the law in the plainest terms. It does not call the commandments temporary. It calls them everlasting, sure, and standing fast forever. These are not the words we use for things that expire.

Psalm 111:7–8 The works of his hands are verity and judgment; all his commandments are sure. They stand fast for ever and ever, and are done in truth and uprightness. Psalm 119:160 Thy word is true from the beginning: and every one of thy righteous judgments endureth for ever.

The Messiah Himself made this point as clearly as it can be made. He did not come to abolish the law. He came to fulfill it, to fill it full of meaning and to live it out perfectly. And He warned that not the smallest stroke of it would fall away while heaven and earth still stand.

Matthew 5:17–18 Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.
Teacher’s Note

When teaching Matthew 5:17, do not let the word fulfil be read as do away with. The very next verse rules that out, since it says not one jot or tittle will pass while heaven and earth remain. To fulfil the law is to fill it up, to obey it fully and to reveal its true depth, not to cancel it. Lean on verse 18 to anchor this, because the structure of the passage settles the meaning better than any single word study.

So the unchanging nature of God is the foundation under the whole teaching. The law is permanent because the One who wrote it is permanent. Strip away that foundation and the law floats free, ready to be reshaped by every passing opinion. Keep that foundation in place and the law stands as firm as the throne of God.

Going Deeper

This same principle explains why certain commands predate Mount Sinai and therefore reach beyond it. The Sabbath was set apart at creation, before there was an Israel. The distinction between clean and unclean animals was known to Noah, who took the clean animals by sevens onto the ark. Abraham gave a tenth to Melchizedek long before the Levitical priesthood existed. These were not invented at Sinai. They were written into the order of the world from the beginning, which tells us they belong to God’s settled character and not to one temporary covenant.

Part Three

The Law Reveals the Character of God

If the law flows out of who God is, then reading the law is one way of coming to know Him. Every commandment is a small portrait of its Author. When God says do not steal, He is telling us that He values what is right and just. When He says do not bear false witness, He is telling us that He is a God of truth. The commandments are not arbitrary. Each one shows us something about the One who spoke it.

Scripture makes the connection directly. God calls His people to be holy because He is holy. The standard He sets for us is simply a reflection of what He is in Himself. He is not asking us to reach a bar He does not live up to. He is inviting us to share in His own nature.

Leviticus 19:2 Speak unto all the congregation of the children of Israel, and say unto them, Ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy.

Because the law mirrors God, Scripture uses the same words for the law that it uses for God Himself. God is righteous, and the law is righteous. God is true, and the law is truth. God is good, and the law is good. You cannot praise the law without praising its Author, and you cannot honor the Author while despising His law.

Psalm 19:7–8 The law of the LORD is perfect, converting the soul: the testimony of the LORD is sure, making wise the simple. The statutes of the LORD are right, rejoicing the heart: the commandment of the LORD is pure, enlightening the eyes. Psalm 119:142 Thy righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and thy law is the truth.

Paul reaches the same conclusion. He had every reason to find fault with the law if it deserved blame, yet he calls it holy and the commandment holy, just, and good. He even says the law is spiritual, meaning it belongs to the realm of God and not merely to the flesh. The problem was never with the law. The problem was with us.

Romans 7:12, 14 Wherefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good. ... For we know that the law is spiritual: but I am carnal, sold under sin.
The Pattern Is Plain

The way we treat God’s law reveals the way we feel about God. To love His commandments is to love Him, because His commandments are the open expression of His heart. This is why Jesus said that the one who keeps His commandments is the one who loves Him. Obedience is not the opposite of love. It is love made visible.

John 14:15 If ye love me, keep my commandments. 1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments: and his commandments are not grievous.

Part Four

Shadow and Substance: What Passed and What Remains

Now we come to the question that troubles many sincere believers. If the law is unchanging, then why does the New Testament speak of certain things being fulfilled or set aside? The answer is found in a single principle. Some parts of the law were given as shadows that pointed forward to the work of Christ, and some parts express the eternal righteousness of God. When the substance arrives, the shadow has done its work. But righteousness does not pass away, because God does not pass away.

Think of a shadow on the ground. The shadow proves that something real is casting it. A shadow of a man tells you a man is near. When the man himself steps into view, you no longer study his shadow, because the reality has come. This is exactly the language Scripture uses for one specific part of the law, the system of animal sacrifice and the priestly service that went with it.

Hebrews 10:1 For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect.

The blood of bulls and goats could never take away sin. Those sacrifices were a shadow, given to teach the people that sin requires death and that a perfect offering was coming. When Christ offered Himself once for all, the shadow met its substance. We no longer bring a lamb to an altar, because the Lamb of God has come. The priesthood that served those altars changed when the eternal Priest after the order of Melchizedek took His seat, and with that priesthood the rules governing it changed as well.

Hebrews 7:12 For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law. Hebrews 9:9–10 Which was a figure for the time then present, in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices ... which stood only in meats and drinks, and divers washings, and carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation.

Read those words carefully. The things imposed only until the time of reformation were the carnal ordinances, the sacrifices and the priestly washings tied to the altar. These were the parts of the law that pointed to the cross and were satisfied at the cross. They were the shadow. They have given way to the substance, which is Christ.

But the moral commandments of God are not a shadow of anything. They do not point forward to a coming event that will retire them. They express the settled righteousness of God, and they remain in full force. The command to worship God alone, to honor His name, to keep His Sabbath, to honor father and mother, to refuse adultery, theft, murder, and false witness, none of these were waiting for a substance to arrive. They are the substance. They are the eternal righteousness of an unchanging God.

The Shadow That Passed

Given to point forward to Christ and satisfied in His finished work:
  • Animal sacrifices and offerings
  • The Aaronic and Levitical priesthood
  • The altar service and ritual washings
  • The carnal ordinances of the temple

The Substance That Remains

The eternal righteousness of God, unchanging as His character:
  • The moral commandments of God
  • The seventh-day Sabbath
  • The appointed times and feast days
  • The clean and unclean distinctions
Teacher’s Note

Colossians 2:16–17 is often quoted to argue that the Sabbath and feasts were abolished, because Paul calls them a shadow of things to come. Handle this verse with care. Paul says these things are a shadow of things to come, and then adds that the body is of Christ. The point is not that the days are gone, but that their meaning is found in Christ. A shadow still falls only because the body is real and present. So the right lesson is to keep the days while looking to Christ as the substance they reveal, not to discard the days as if the shadow had no body behind it.

Keep the distinction sharp. What was fulfilled and set aside was the priestly and sacrificial system, named plainly as carnal ordinances in Hebrews 9. The moral law, the Sabbath, the feasts, and the dietary distinctions are never called carnal ordinances and are never retired in Scripture.

So the principle holds together without contradiction. The law is unchanging because God is unchanging. The parts of it that were shadows have met their substance in Christ, exactly as they were designed to do. And the parts that express the eternal righteousness of God remain in full force, because the righteousness of God does not expire. Nothing here is thrown away. The shadow gave way to the body, and the body confirms that the shadow was real.

In Summary

God gave His law because He loves His people and wanted to show them how to live, how to recognize sin, and where to find their Savior. The law endures because it is the written expression of an unchanging God, and what reflects His character cannot pass away while He remains. To read the law is to read the heart of its Author, for He is holy, righteous, true, and good, and so is His law.

The shadow that pointed to Christ, the sacrifices and the priestly service, found its fulfillment at the cross and gave way to the substance. But the eternal righteousness of God remains, and His commandments still stand fast forever. We do not keep them to be saved. We keep them because we are saved, and because we love the One whose character they reveal. If ye love me, keep my commandments.
