A Doctrinal Teaching

When Bad Things Happen to Good People

The Ruler of This Present World, the Suffering of the Righteous, and the Hope of the Age to Come

✦

Introduction

Every honest believer comes to it sooner or later. A good man buries a child. A faithful woman is handed a diagnosis she did not deserve. A family that loves God loses everything, while the proud and the cruel seem to prosper without a care. And the heart cries out the oldest question of all: if God is good, and God is in control, then why do such terrible things fall on people who have done nothing to bring them on? This is not a question for the faithless only. It is a question the saints of God have asked from the beginning, and the Scriptures do not turn away from it.

The Bible gives us an answer, but it is not a small one. To understand why bad things happen to good people, we have to understand who is at work in this present world, where he came from, what he wants, and why the Lord has allowed him to hold the sway he holds, for a little while longer. We will see that the world we live in is not yet the world as God means it to be, that an enemy has set himself up as its ruler, and that his time is short. The trouble of the righteous is real, but it is not the end of the story, and it is not the verdict of heaven on the one who suffers.

Section I

The Question the Righteous Have Always Asked

We should begin by saying plainly that this question is not rebellion. Some of the most godly men in Scripture have asked it, and the Lord did not strike them down for it. Job is the great example. Heaven itself calls him a good man, not by his own boast but by the testimony of God.

Job 1:1 There was a man in the land of Uz, whose name was Job; and that man was perfect and upright, and one that feared God, and eschewed evil.

This is the verdict of God on Job before the trouble ever comes. He was upright. He feared God. He turned from evil. And yet upon this very man fell loss after loss, until he was sitting in ashes with everything stripped away. Job is the proof, written at the front of the book, that suffering is not always the wages of secret sin. The good can suffer terribly. That is the whole reason his story is in our Bible.

The psalmist Asaph felt the same sting when he looked around and saw the wicked living at ease. It nearly knocked him off his feet.

Psalm 73:2–3 But as for me, my feet were almost gone; my steps had well nigh slipped. For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked.

The prophet Habakkuk took the question straight to God and did not soften it. He could not understand how a holy God could watch the wicked swallow up the man more righteous than himself, and say nothing.

Habakkuk 1:13 Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil, and canst not look on iniquity: wherefore lookest thou upon them that deal treacherously, and holdest thy tongue when the wicked devoureth the man that is more righteous than he?

Hold On To This

The question of why the good suffer is an honest question, and Scripture treats it honestly. God never rebukes Job for his pain, never rebukes Asaph for his confusion, never rebukes Habakkuk for asking. What He rebukes is the false comfort of Job's friends, who insisted that suffering must always be punishment for sin. Be very slow to lay that burden on a hurting soul. It is the lie the Lord Himself condemned.

Section II

The Adversary Who Would Be Like God

To answer the question, we have to meet the one behind so much of the trouble in this world. Before there was a fallen man, there was a fallen spirit. Scripture pulls back the curtain and shows us a created being of great beauty and high place who was not content to be a creature. He wanted the throne itself.

Isaiah 14:12–14 How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations! For thou hast said in thine heart, I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God: I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the north: I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High.

Read those five "I will" statements slowly. There is the whole of his rebellion in one breath. He did not merely want to disobey God; he wanted to be like God, to take a throne that was never his, to be worshipped in the place of his Maker. The prophet Ezekiel shows us the same being, perfect and beautiful in his beginning, until pride was found in him.

Ezekiel 28:14–15 Thou art the anointed cherub that covereth; and I have set thee so: thou wast upon the holy mountain of God; thou hast walked up and down in the midst of the stones of fire. Thou wast perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, till iniquity was found in thee.
Ezekiel 28:17 Thine heart was lifted up because of thy beauty, thou hast corrupted thy wisdom by reason of thy brightness: I will cast thee to the ground...

Here, then, is the root of the enemy we are speaking of. He was made good, and he made himself evil through pride. His ambition has never changed. He still wants to be like the most High, still craves the worship that belongs to God alone, and still hates the One who made him. When he could not strike the throne of God directly, he turned his hatred toward the creation God loved, and most of all toward man, who was made in God's own image.

Section III

The God of This World

Now we come to a truth that many believers have never been taught, though Scripture states it plainly more than once. In this present age, Satan holds a real and terrible power over the world system. The Bible does not whisper this; it calls him by titles of rulership.

2 Corinthians 4:4 In whom the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not, lest the light of the glorious gospel of Christ, who is the image of God, should shine unto them.

The god of this world. Three times the Lord Jesus Himself names him the prince of this world.

John 12:31 Now is the judgment of this world: now shall the prince of this world be cast out.
John 14:30 Hereafter I will not talk much with you: for the prince of this world cometh, and hath nothing in me.

Paul calls him the prince of the power of the air, the spirit at work in those who will not obey God.

Ephesians 2:2 Wherein in time past ye walked according to the course of this world, according to the prince of the power of the air, the spirit that now worketh in the children of disobedience.

And John, writing to the church, draws the line as sharply as it can be drawn. The believer belongs to God, but the world as a system lies in the grip of the wicked one.

1 John 5:19 And we know that we are of God, and the whole world lieth in wickedness.

The Key That Unlocks the Question

Much of the trouble in this world makes more sense once we see this. We are not yet living in the kingdom of God upon the earth. We are living in a world whose course is set by a usurper, a defeated but still active enemy who is permitted, for now, to work his will among those who do not belong to God. Bad things happen to good people in part because good people still live in enemy-held territory.

Section IV

How the Ruler Came to His Throne

If Satan is the prince of this world, we must ask how a created and condemned spirit ever came to hold such a place. The answer takes us back to the garden. When God made man, He gave him dominion over the earth. The world was put under the rule of mankind, who bore the image of God.

Genesis 1:26 And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.

That dominion was real, and it was meant to be exercised under God. But in the fall, man listened to the serpent rather than to his Maker, and in turning from God he turned the world over to the very one whose voice he obeyed. The deceiver gained a foothold he was never given by right, but seized through man's surrender. From that day the curse spread out over the whole creation, and creation itself has been groaning under it ever since.

Romans 8:20–22 For the creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who hath subjected the same in hope, because the creature itself also shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God. For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now.

This is why the ground brings forth thorns, why bodies grow sick and break down, why the earth shakes and the storm destroys. The whole creation is under a bondage it did not choose, waiting to be set free. When Satan tempted the Lord Jesus in the wilderness, he laid claim to the kingdoms of this world, and Jesus did not argue that he held no power at all. He simply refused to bow.

Luke 4:5–6 And the devil, taking him up into an high mountain, shewed unto him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. And the devil said unto him, All this power will I give thee, and the glory of them: for that is delivered unto me; and to whomsoever I will I give it.

A Teaching Note

Notice carefully who is speaking in Luke 4:6. It is the devil, and he is a liar from the beginning, so we do not take his words as pure truth. Yet the Lord answered the temptation not by denying that the kingdoms were in the enemy's hand, but by refusing to worship him for them (Luke 4:8). The weight of Scripture, with its plain titles of "god of this world" and "prince of this world," tells us the enemy does hold a real, permitted sway over the world system in this age. We should hold this as the clear teaching of Scripture, while remembering it is a sway that is borrowed, temporary, and already under sentence.

Section V

The Thief Who Comes to Destroy

Now we come to the heart of what the enemy wants. He is not a neutral landlord of the world. He is a destroyer, and he hates everything God has made. The Lord Jesus drew the contrast in the plainest terms, setting the enemy's purpose against His own.

John 10:10 The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.

Steal, kill, and destroy. That is his whole errand. Whatever God builds, the enemy wants to tear down. Whatever God loves, the enemy wants to wound. And since man is the crown of creation, made in the image of the God he hates, man is the special target of his rage. Peter warns the church that this enemy is on the prowl, looking for someone to ruin.

1 Peter 5:8 Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour.

Even the book of Revelation names him by what he does. The angel of the bottomless pit is given a name that means Destroyer, for that is the very nature of the one who rules the darkness of this present world. He cannot create, so he defaces. He cannot give life, so he takes it. He cannot be God, so he tries to ruin the handiwork of God out of spite.

Hold On To This

Much of the suffering that falls on the innocent is not the hand of God reaching down to punish. It is the malice of an enemy reaching out to destroy. When sickness, accident, cruelty, and loss strike the godly, we are watching the destroyer at his old work. This does not excuse our own sin where sin is present, but it forbids us from reading every tragedy as a verdict from heaven. Often it is an attack from the pit, permitted for a season, and not a sentence from the throne.

Section VI

Why God Allows It, For Now

Here we reach the hardest part of the question. If God is greater than Satan, and He surely is, then why does He allow the destroyer to do his work at all? Why not end it today? Scripture does not give us every reason, but it gives us enough to stand on, and the first thing it shows us is that the enemy is never out of bounds. In the book of Job we see that Satan could not touch one hair of Job's head without God's permission, and even then only within strict limits.

Job 1:12 And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, all that he hath is in thy power; only upon himself put not forth thine hand. So Satan went forth from the presence of the LORD.
Job 2:6 And the LORD said unto Satan, Behold, he is in thine hand; but save his life.

This is a great comfort hidden in a hard book. The enemy is a chained dog. He can reach only as far as the chain allows, and not one inch farther. Whatever falls on the child of God has passed through the permission of a Father who has set the limits Himself. The Lord also tells us that He has chosen, for now, to let the good and the evil grow together until the harvest, rather than tearing up the field before its time.

Matthew 13:30 Let both grow together until the harvest: and in the time of harvest I will say to the reapers, Gather ye together first the tares to burn them: but gather the wheat into my barn.
Matthew 13:38–39 The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the world; and the reapers are the angels.

So in this present age the wheat and the tares share the same field, the same weather, the same world. The sun rises on the wicked and the rain falls on the unjust right alongside the righteous, because the day of separation has not yet come.

Matthew 5:45 ...for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust.

And in His mercy God uses even these hard things in the life of His own. He does not waste our suffering. The trial of our faith, painful as it is, is doing a work in us that ease never could, refining what is real and burning away what is not.

1 Peter 1:6–7 Wherein ye greatly rejoice, though now for a season, if need be, ye are in heaviness through manifold temptations: That the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ.
James 1:2–4 My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; Knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing.

A Word of Pastoral Care

We must say this gently and clearly. To tell a grieving believer that their suffering is doing them good is true in God's hands, but it is not a club to swing at a wounded heart. Job's comforters had pieces of truth, yet God was angry with them, because they used truth to crush a hurting man instead of to lift him. When you sit with someone in their pain, lead with the love of a Father who has set limits on the enemy and wastes nothing, not with a lecture on why they hurt. Let God do the explaining in His own time. Your task is to weep with those who weep, and to point them to the One who has already broken the destroyer's power.

Section VII

The Serpent's Head Is Already Bruised

We have looked long at the darkness, so now we must lift our eyes to the light, for the darkness does not have the last word. From the very moment of man's fall, God promised a Deliverer who would crush the serpent. The first gospel was preached in Eden, spoken by God over the head of the enemy himself.

Genesis 3:15 And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.

The serpent would strike the heel of the Promised One, and he did, at the cross. But in that very wounding, the seed of the woman crushed the serpent's head. What looked like the enemy's victory was his ruin. At Calvary the Lord Jesus stripped the powers of darkness of their weapons and put them to open shame.

Colossians 2:15 And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it.

This was the whole purpose of His coming. He did not come merely to forgive and then leave the destroyer on his throne. He came to undo the destroyer's work and to break his power for ever.

1 John 3:8 He that committeth sin is of the devil; for the devil sinneth from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that he might destroy the works of the devil.
Hebrews 2:14 Forasmuch then as the children are partakers of flesh and blood, he also himself likewise took part of the same; that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil.

The Turning Point of the Whole Story

The enemy still prowls, but he is a defeated foe. The cross has already settled the verdict. He is like a beaten ruler who has lost the war but has not yet been removed from the field, still raging in the time that remains to him. The believer does not fight for victory; the believer fights from a victory that Christ has already won. That changes everything about how we face the trouble of this present age.

Section VIII

Until the End of This Age

The reign of the destroyer is on a clock, and the clock is running down. His sway lasts only through this present evil age, which the Lord came to deliver us out of.

Galatians 1:4 Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from this present evil world, according to the will of God and our Father.

A day is coming, and it is fixed, when the borrowed throne is taken away and the rightful King takes up the rule of the earth. The kingdoms that the enemy claimed on the mountain will be handed to the One who refused to bow for them.

Revelation 11:15 And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever.

The destroyer himself will be seized, bound, and at last cast for ever into the place prepared for him. The one who wanted to be like the most High will be put under the feet of the saints.

Revelation 20:10 And the devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever.

And the apostle promises that this crushing is not only future and far off, but soon, and that the people of God will share in it.

Romans 16:20 And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly. The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen.

When that day comes, the curse on creation will be lifted, the groaning will end, and the King will reign in righteousness over a world made right. In that kingdom the law of God will go forth from Zion, and the will of God will be done on earth as it is in heaven.

Isaiah 2:3 ...for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.

The End of the Question

This is why the suffering of the righteous is real but not final. We live in the short, dark window between the enemy's defeat at the cross and his removal at the coming of the King. The bad things that happen to good people belong to this passing age, an age under a usurper whose sentence is already written. The age to come belongs entirely to God, and there the destroyer has no part at all.

In Summary

Stand Fast Until the King Returns

So why do bad things happen to good people? Because this present world is held, for now, by an enemy who hates God and everything God has made. He once tried to be like the most High and was cast down, and ever since he has lived to steal, to kill, and to destroy. Through man's fall he gained a borrowed throne over the world system, and the whole creation groans under the bondage that followed. The good suffer because the good still live in his territory, in a world that is not yet what God will make it.

But hear the rest, for it is the better part. The destroyer is a chained dog, who can reach only as far as the Father permits. He is a defeated foe, whose head was crushed at the cross. And he is a doomed ruler, whose throne ends at the close of this age, when the kingdoms of this world become the kingdoms of our Lord. The trouble of the righteous is real, yet it is brief, it is bounded, and it is not the verdict of heaven upon them.

Until that day, the believer is not told to fear and is not told to despair. We are told to resist the devil, and he will flee from us. We are told to put on the whole armour of God and stand. And above all we are told to take heart, for the One who has already overcome speaks these words over every good soul who suffers in an evil age: "In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world."
