Before We Begin

Chapter one showed us the whole world at once, the six great days and the God who spoke them into being. Chapter two now walks us back in close. It is not a second and rival story of creation, as some have claimed. It is a closer look at the sixth day, drawing near to the man, the garden that was made for him, and the wife who was given to him. Where chapter one stood back to show the sweep of it all, chapter two kneels down in the dust beside the man that God has made.

Three gifts are set in place in this chapter, and all three come before there is any sin in the world. God gives the gift of rest, the gift of work, and the gift of marriage. Every one of them is good, every one of them is older than the fall, and every one of them still stands today. We have not outgrown a single one.

And as always, we read with our eyes on Christ. The rest of the seventh day points to the rest we find in Him. The man of dust points ahead to the last Adam who came down from heaven. And the bride brought forth from the sleeping man’s side points to the church, brought forth from the opened side of her Saviour. Let us walk slowly and see it.

Genesis 2:1–3

The Seventh Day: God Rests and Blesses It

Genesis 2:1–3 1Thus the heavens and the earth were finished, and all the host of them. 2And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made. 3And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made.

The work is finished. The heavens and the earth are complete, down to the last of their host, every star and every living thing. And then God does something that ought to stop us in our tracks. He rests. Not because He was weary, for the everlasting God does not faint and is never tired. He rested to set a pattern, to lay down a rhythm for the world He had just made. He worked six days, and on the seventh He rested, and He wrote that rhythm into the very first week of time.

Then He does two things to that seventh day that He does to no other. He blessed it, and He sanctified it. To sanctify is to set apart as holy, to mark a thing off for a special and sacred use. So before there was a nation of Israel, before there was a law given at the mountain, before there was a single commandment written on stone, God had already blessed and set apart the seventh day. The Sabbath is not a later invention handed to one people. It is a gift laid down at the foundation of the world, for the whole race of man.

Hold On To This

The Sabbath was made in the garden, not at the mountain. God rested, blessed, and sanctified the seventh day at the end of creation week, long before there was a Jew or a law. That is why the Lord Jesus could say that the sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath. It is a gift as old as the world, given to everyone who bears the image of God.

Mark 2:27 27And he said unto them, The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath:

A Rest That Points Beyond Itself

The seventh day of rest was always a signpost pointing to something greater. It taught the world to stop striving and to trust the God who provides. And the New Testament tells us there remains a rest for the people of God, a rest we enter by faith in Christ, ceasing from our own works as God ceased from His. Every Sabbath we keep is a small taste of that great and lasting rest, and a rehearsal of the thousand-year rest that is still to come, when Christ shall reign and the whole earth shall keep His Sabbath.

Genesis 2:4–7

The Man of Dust and the Breath of Life

Genesis 2:4–7 4These are the generations of the heavens and of the earth when they were created, in the day that the LORD God made the earth and the heavens, 5And every plant of the field before it was in the earth, and every herb of the field before it grew: for the LORD God had not caused it to rain upon the earth, and there was not a man to till the ground. 6But there went up a mist from the earth, and watered the whole face of the ground. 7And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.

Notice the name of God changes here. In chapter one He is called God, the mighty Maker who speaks worlds into being. From here He is called the LORD God, and that first word, set in small capitals, stands for the personal and covenant name of God. It tells us that the great Maker of the heavens is also the near and personal Lord who bends down to form a man with His own hands. He is not only the God who is high above all. He is the Lord who draws close.

And see how He makes the man. He does not speak him into being from a distance, as He did the light and the seas. He stoops down, takes the dust of the ground, and forms him like a potter working the clay. Then He puts His face to the face of that lifeless shape and breathes into it the breath of life. Man is made of two things held together. He is dust from below, humble and earthy, and he is breath from God above, living and precious. Take away the breath of God and the man is only dust again. Every breath you draw is on loan from the One who first gave it.

1 Corinthians 15:45 45And so it is written, The first man Adam was made a living soul; the last Adam was made a quickening spirit.

Paul reaches back to this very verse and calls Adam the first man, a living soul. Then he sets beside him the last Adam, the Lord Jesus Christ, who is a quickening spirit, the One who gives life. The first Adam received his life. The last Adam gives life to others. The first man was of the earth, earthy. The second man is the Lord from heaven. Adam is here at the head of the old creation so that we will understand Christ at the head of the new.

Teacher’s Note

Some claim that chapter two is a second creation account that contradicts chapter one, pointing to the order of things. Read the two together and the trouble melts away. Chapter one gives the wide view of all six days in order. Chapter two narrows in on the sixth day, on the making of the man and the planting of his garden home. It was never meant to repeat the whole sequence. When verse nineteen speaks of the LORD God forming the beasts, it is not laying down a new order but reminding us that the animals He had already made were now brought to the man to be named. The two chapters are one story told first from far off, and then up close.

Genesis 2:8–14

The Garden, the Trees, and the Rivers

Genesis 2:8–9 8And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had formed. 9And out of the ground made the LORD God to grow every tree that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food; the tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.

God plants a garden and sets the man in it. Look at the kindness of it. The trees are pleasant to the sight and good for food, so they feed the eye and the body both. Our God is not a hard master who gives only what is bare and needful. He made a home full of beauty and delight and put His child in the middle of it. Everything the man could want was there for the taking.

But two trees stand out from the rest. In the midst of the garden grows the tree of life, and near it the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. These two trees set the stage for everything that follows. One offers life, the other will bring death, and the man will have to choose whom he will trust. The tree of life will disappear from the story after the fall, guarded by a flaming sword, and we will not see it again until the last pages of the Bible, where it stands once more in the city of God, its leaves for the healing of the nations. What was lost in the first garden is restored in Christ.

Genesis 2:10–14 10And a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into four heads. 11The name of the first is Pison: that is it which compasseth the whole land of Havilah, where there is gold; 12And the gold of that land is good: there is bdellium and the onyx stone. 13And the name of the second river is Gihon: the same is it that compasseth the whole land of Ethiopia. 14And the name of the third river is Hiddekel: that is it which goeth toward the east of Assyria. And the fourth river is Euphrates.

A river flowed out of Eden to water the garden and then parted into four great heads. The writer even tells us of the gold and the precious stones of those lands. This is history, not fable. Eden was a real place, watered by real rivers, rich with real treasure. The Bible plants its feet on the ground and asks us to believe that these things happened in the world God made.

Teacher’s Note

People have long tried to pin Eden to a spot on the map using these four rivers. Two of the names, Hiddekel and Euphrates, match rivers we still know today, but the others are harder to place, and the flood of Noah reshaped the face of the whole earth, moving and burying much of what once was. So we should hold any map of Eden loosely. The point of these verses is not to hand us a set of directions, but to root the garden in real geography and real history. Where exactly it stood, we cannot now say for certain, and we do not need to.

Genesis 2:15–17

To Dress It and to Keep It

Genesis 2:15–17 15And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it. 16And the LORD God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: 17But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.

God gives the man work to do. He is to dress the garden and to keep it, to tend it and to guard it. Mark this well. Work came before the fall. It is not part of the curse. Labour is a good gift, given to a sinless man in a perfect garden, so that he could share in the joy of tending what God had made. The curse would later add sweat and thorns and toil to the work, but the work itself was always a blessing. A man was made to have something worth doing.

Then comes the first command ever given to man, and see how it is shaped. God does not begin with what is forbidden. He begins with a wide open generosity. Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat. Yes and yes and yes, a whole garden of yes, and only one no. The one tree is held back, not because God is stingy, but because love always leaves room for trust. A command with no choice in it is no test of the heart at all. God gave the man nearly everything, and asked him to trust Him with the one thing held back.

Hold On To This

God’s commands are not the fence of a jailer. They are the boundary of a Father who loves us. He filled the garden with good things and freely gave them all, and He drew one line, for the man’s own good. Obedience here was never the price of God’s kindness, for the kindness came first and the garden was already given. Obedience was simply the answer of a trusting heart to a good and giving God. It is no different for us. We do not keep His word to earn His love. We keep it because we already have it.

And there is a warning fixed to the one tree. In the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die. God is honest with the man about what sin will cost. Death is the wage of turning from the God who is life. This is not cruelty. It is the plain truth spoken in love, the way a good father warns a child away from the fire. Everything now hangs on whether the man will believe his Maker.

Genesis 2:18–20

It Is Not Good That the Man Should Be Alone

Genesis 2:18–20 18And the LORD God said, It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him an help meet for him. 19And out of the ground the LORD God formed every beast of the field, and every fowl of the air; and brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof. 20And Adam gave names to all cattle, and to the fowl of the air, and to every beast of the field; but for Adam there was not found an help meet for him.

All through chapter one God looked at His work and called it good. Now for the first time He names something that is not good. It is not good that the man should be alone. Man was made for fellowship, made to love and to be loved, and standing by himself he was incomplete. So God sets out to make him a help meet for him. That old word meet simply means fitting, suitable, a match. She would be a helper who answered to him, who stood face to face with him as his equal and his counterpart, the one creature in all the world made to fit the empty place at his side.

First God brings the animals to the man to be named. This was Adam’s honour, to give names to every living creature, an act of the dominion God had granted him. But as the long parade passed by, every beast with its mate, the lack in the man’s own life grew plain. Among all the cattle and the fowl and the beasts of the field, there was not found a help meet for him. Not one of them could stand beside him as his equal. He needed someone of his own kind, and God was about to give her.

Genesis 2:21–25

Bone of My Bones: The Making of Marriage

Genesis 2:21–23 21And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept: and he took one of his ribs, and closed up the flesh instead thereof; 22And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman, and brought her unto the man. 23And Adam said, This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh: she shall be called Woman, because she was taken out of Man.

God puts the man into a deep sleep, opens his side, takes a rib, and from that rib He builds a woman. There is a tenderness in where she was taken from. She was not made from his head to rule over him, nor from his feet to be trampled under him, but from his side, from near his heart, to stand beside him and be loved by him. When God brings her to the man, his first words are a song of joy. This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh. At last, here is one who is truly like me, truly mine.

The Bride From the Wounded Side

Do not miss the picture hidden here. The first bride was brought forth from the opened side of a man while he lay in a deep sleep. And there is a second Adam. The church, the bride of Christ, was brought forth from the opened side of her Saviour as He slept the sleep of death upon the cross, when the soldier pierced His side and out came blood and water. As Eve was taken from Adam, so the church is taken from Christ, bone of His bone and flesh of His flesh. Paul says that marriage itself is a great mystery, and that he speaks concerning Christ and the church. The very first wedding in the garden was already telling the love story of the Saviour and His people.

Genesis 2:24–25 24Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh. 25And they were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed.

Out of that first marriage God speaks the pattern for every marriage that would ever follow. A man shall leave his father and his mother, cleave to his wife, and the two of them shall become one flesh. Here is the whole shape of it in one verse. One man and one woman, leaving the old home to build a new one, joined so closely that they are counted as one. This is not a custom that men invented and can now remake to suit the age. It is the order God laid down in the garden. The Lord Jesus Himself pointed straight back to this verse when He taught on marriage, and settled the matter by the word of the beginning.

Ephesians 5:31–32 31For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh. 32This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church.

The chapter ends in a quiet and beautiful line. They were both naked, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed. There was nothing to hide, no guilt, no fear, no shame between them or before God. This is the world as it was meant to be, open and unafraid, walking with God in the cool of the day. Hold that picture gently in your mind, because in the very next chapter it will be broken, and the shame will come. But it was not so at the first. At the first there was only peace.

Where This Chapter Leads

Genesis two hands us three gifts from before the fall, and time has not taken one of them away. The gift of rest, when God blessed and set apart the seventh day. The gift of work, when He gave the man a garden to tend. And the gift of marriage, when He built the woman and brought her to the man to be one flesh. These were very good in the garden, and they are good still. The world may forget them or fight against them, but they stand as firm as the God who gave them.

And each gift is pointing home to Christ. The Sabbath rest is a foretaste of the rest we have in Him. The man of dust prepares us for the last Adam, the Lord from heaven. And the bride taken from the sleeping man’s side is the picture of the church, drawn from the pierced side of her Saviour. Read this chapter with your eyes open, and you will find Jesus in the garden long before He walks its paths in the flesh.
