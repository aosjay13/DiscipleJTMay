Gifts of the Holy Ghost · Foundation Lesson

The Baptism in the Holy Ghost

Being Fully Immersed in God, with the Evidence of Speaking in Tongues

✦

Introduction

Before we ever study the gifts of the Spirit one by one, we need to understand the doorway through which the fullness of the Spirit comes. That doorway is the baptism in the Holy Ghost. The word baptize means to dip, to plunge, to bury under. When a thing is baptized it is not merely sprinkled or touched on the surface; it is put all the way under, so that it comes out changed. To be baptized in the Holy Ghost, then, is to be fully immersed in God Himself, soaked through and saturated by His presence and His power.

The Scriptures show us that there is more than one work of the Spirit, and that these works are not the same thing under a different name. When a person is saved, the Holy Spirit takes that person and places him into the body of Christ. This is one baptism, and the agent who performs it is the Spirit. But there is another, where the Lord Jesus takes the believer and immerses him into the Holy Ghost and fire. This is a second work, with a different Baptizer and a different result, and it is the one that opens the believer to the full operation of the gifts. In this lesson we will set these two works side by side, follow the pattern of them through the book of Acts, and see why speaking in tongues stands as the consistent biblical evidence of this immersion.

Section I

Two Baptisms, Two Baptizers

The clearest way to begin is to notice that Scripture speaks of the Spirit and baptism in two distinct ways. In one, the Holy Spirit is the One doing the baptizing, and the place He baptizes us into is the body of Christ. In the other, Jesus is the One doing the baptizing, and the element He baptizes us with is the Holy Ghost. These are not the same event told twice. They have different actors, and they accomplish different things.

1 Corinthians 12:13 For by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body, whether we be Jews or Gentiles, whether we be bond or free; and have been all made to drink into one Spirit.

Here the Spirit is the Baptizer and the body of Christ is where He puts us. Now read the words of John the Baptist, who points away from himself to the One coming after him.

Matthew 3:11 I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance: but he that cometh after me is mightier than I, whose shoes I am not worthy to bear: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost, and with fire.

Notice the turn. Here Jesus is the Baptizer, and the Holy Ghost and fire are what He baptizes us with. So we have two baptizers and two results. The Spirit baptizes us into Christ; Christ baptizes us into the Spirit. Keeping these straight is the key that unlocks the whole subject.

The First Work

At the moment of salvation
  • The Baptizer is the Holy Spirit
  • We are baptized into the body of Christ
  • It joins us to the Lord and to His people
  • We are sealed and made His own

The Second Work

A distinct, fuller immersion
  • The Baptizer is the Lord Jesus
  • We are baptized with the Holy Ghost and fire
  • It clothes us with power for service
  • It opens the gifts and drives us to God's will
Section II

Baptized by the Spirit into the Body of Christ

When a person believes the gospel and is saved, something immediate and permanent happens. The Holy Spirit takes that person and places him into the one body of Christ. He is no longer an outsider; he is now a member of the household of God, joined to the Lord and to every other believer. This is the work described in First Corinthians twelve, and it belongs to every believer without exception. There are no Christians who have not been baptized by the Spirit into the body, because that very baptism is what makes a person part of the body in the first place.

Alongside this, the believer is sealed. To be sealed is to be marked as belonging to God, kept and guaranteed by His own Spirit who now lives within.

Ephesians 1:13 In whom ye also trusted, after that ye heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation: in whom also after that ye believed, ye were sealed with that holy Spirit of promise.

The Heart of It

At salvation the Holy Spirit indwells the believer and baptizes him into the body of Christ. This is not earned and not optional. It is the Spirit's own act of grace, joining the believer to the Lord forever the moment he believes.

We must hold this firmly, because it guards us against pride. The first baptism is not a reward for the spiritual; it is the common possession of every saved person. Whatever we say next about a fuller immersion, we never say that the body of Christ is divided into those who have the Spirit and those who do not. Every believer has the Spirit. The question the rest of this lesson takes up is whether every believer has yet been immersed in the Spirit in that further measure the Lord Jesus pours out.

Section III

Baptized by Jesus with the Holy Ghost and Fire

All four Gospels record John the Baptist drawing the same contrast. John baptized with water, but the One coming after him would baptize with the Holy Ghost. This was not a small footnote in John's preaching; it was the very thing that set Jesus apart as mightier than the prophet who announced Him.

Luke 3:16 John answered, saying unto them all, I indeed baptize you with water; but one mightier than I cometh, the latchet of whose shoes I am not worthy to unloose: he shall baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire.

After His resurrection, Jesus took up John's promise and told His disciples to wait for it. He made plain that water baptism and Holy Ghost baptism are two different things, and that the second was still ahead of them even though they were already His followers.

Acts 1:5 For John truly baptized with water; but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence.

This is striking. The men He spoke to had left everything to follow Him. They had believed on Him, walked with Him, and been sent out by Him. Yet the baptism with the Holy Ghost was still future for them. That tells us plainly that this immersion is a work distinct from coming to faith, something the Lord pours out upon those who already belong to Him. He then tied it to power and to purpose.

Acts 1:8 But ye shall receive power, after that the Holy Ghost is come upon you: and ye shall be witnesses unto me both in Jerusalem, and in all Judaea, and in Samaria, and unto the uttermost part of the earth.

The Heart of It

The baptism with the Holy Ghost is poured out by Jesus upon those who are already His. Its purpose is power, and its aim is witness. It does not save, for they were already saved; it equips and sends.

Section IV

What It Means to Be Fully Immersed in God

The picture behind the word baptism is total immersion. When John baptized people in the Jordan, they went down into the water and came up out of it. The water surrounded them entirely. The same picture carries over when Jesus baptizes us with the Holy Ghost. We are not lightly touched by God; we are plunged into Him, covered and filled, so that His presence saturates us.

There is a difference between having a cup of water and being put under the river. At salvation the Spirit comes to dwell within, a well of living water rising up inside the believer. In the baptism with the Holy Ghost that same Spirit overflows, until the believer is not only filled within but immersed without, surrounded by the power of God for the work He has given.

John 7:38-39 He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water. (But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive...)

A Word on the Picture

Think of a sponge held over a basin. A few drops will dampen it, but it is only when the sponge is pushed all the way under and held there that it is filled to its very edges and begins to pour out on every side. This is the difference the baptism makes. The believer who is fully immersed in God does not merely contain a measure of the Spirit; he overflows with Him, and that overflow becomes a blessing to everyone around.

Section V

The Pattern Through the Book of Acts

What Jesus promised in Acts one comes to pass through the rest of the book. As we trace it, a clear pattern emerges. Again and again the baptism with the Holy Ghost comes upon those who already believe, and again and again it is marked by speaking in tongues.

Jerusalem the day of Pentecost

The waiting disciples were filled, and the sign that accompanied it was tongues, real languages given by the Spirit, declaring the wonderful works of God to a gathered crowd.

The Word And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance. (Acts 2:4)
Samaria a subsequent receiving

The Samaritans had believed Philip's preaching and had been baptized in water, yet the Spirit had not yet fallen upon them in this way. Peter and John came, prayed, and laid hands on them, and only then did they receive. This shows the baptism as a step that can follow conversion, not always at the same instant.

The Word Then laid they their hands on them, and they received the Holy Ghost. (Acts 8:17)
Caesarea the house of Cornelius

While Peter was still preaching, the Holy Ghost fell on the Gentiles. The Jewish believers present knew it had happened because they heard the Gentiles speak with tongues and magnify God. Tongues were the visible proof that the same gift had been given.

The Word For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. (Acts 10:46)
Ephesus Paul and the twelve disciples

Paul found disciples and asked plainly whether they had received the Holy Ghost since they believed. The question itself assumes that believing and receiving this baptism can be two separate moments. When Paul laid hands on them, they spoke with tongues and prophesied.

The Word And when Paul had laid his hands upon them, the Holy Ghost came on them; and they spake with tongues, and prophesied. (Acts 19:6)

The Pattern

In each account the baptism comes upon those who already belong to the Lord, and in the accounts that describe the moment plainly, the believers speak with tongues. The setting changes from Jews to Samaritans to Gentiles to disciples of John, but the gift and its evidence remain the same.

Section VI

The Evidence of Speaking in Tongues

Why do we point to tongues as the evidence of this baptism? Not because tongues are the greatest gift, and not because a believer who speaks in tongues is holier than one who does not. We point to tongues because that is what the record itself shows us, over and over, as the sign that accompanied the outpouring. When the Spirit fell at Pentecost, they spoke with tongues. When He fell on Cornelius, they spoke with tongues. When Paul laid hands on the Ephesians, they spoke with tongues. The pattern is consistent enough that the early church recognized it as the mark that the baptism had come.

There is good reason this sign should attend the baptism. The tongue is the hardest member of the body to bring under control. When the Spirit takes hold of a man so fully that even his tongue is yielded to God and used to speak words he did not form himself, it is a vivid proof that he has been immersed past the surface and into the depths. The part of us least willing to submit becomes an instrument of praise.

Acts 2:4 And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance.

Keep It in Its Place

Speaking in tongues is the evidence of the baptism, not the measure of a believer's worth. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, and tongues add nothing to it. A person is no less loved by God before this baptism and no better than his brother after it. We seek the fullness of the Spirit because the Lord offers it and because the work needs it, not so that we may have a badge to wear. The fruit of the Spirit, and love above all, is what marks a mature believer; the gifts are tools for service.

Section VII

The Fire That Drives Us to Do God's Will

John did not say the coming One would baptize with the Holy Ghost only. He said with the Holy Ghost and with fire. Fire in Scripture does two things. It burns away what is impure, and it sets things in motion with heat and energy. Both belong to this baptism. The fire of God purges the believer, burning out fear and self and the love of comfort, and it kindles a holy zeal that will not sit still while there is work to be done.

We see the change plainly in the disciples. Before Pentecost they hid behind locked doors, afraid of the authorities. After the fire fell, the same men stood in the open and preached Christ to the very crowds they had feared. The baptism did not give them a new message; it gave them the power and the drive to carry the message they already had.

Acts 4:31 And when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness.

This is what it means to say the fire drives us to do God's will. The flesh is naturally slow, timid, and easily turned aside. When a believer is immersed in the Holy Ghost and fire, a new energy takes hold of him. The things of God become a burning concern, prayer becomes fervent, witness becomes bold, and obedience flows not as a grudging duty but as the natural outpouring of a heart set ablaze. Jeremiah described it long before, when the word of God became a fire shut up in his bones that he could not hold in.

Jeremiah 20:9 ...But his word was in mine heart as a burning fire shut up in my bones, and I was weary with forbearing, and I could not stay.

The Heart of It

The fire of this baptism both purifies and propels. It burns away fear and kindles zeal, so that the believer is moved from the inside to seek God, to speak for God, and to do the will of God with willing strength.

Section VIII

The Baptism Opens the Gifts of the Spirit

One of the richest results of this immersion is that it opens the believer to the full operation of the gifts of the Spirit. When a person is fully yielded to and filled with the Holy Ghost, the same Spirit who has come in fullness now has free course to distribute His gifts through that life as He sees fit. The wisdom, the knowledge, the faith, the healings, the working of miracles, prophecy, discerning of spirits, tongues, and interpretation all flow from the one Spirit, and the baptism throws the door wide for them.

1 Corinthians 12:7-11 But the manifestation of the Spirit is given to every man to profit withal... But all these worketh that one and the selfsame Spirit, dividing to every man severally as he will.

The gifts are given for the profit of the whole body, not for private display. A believer immersed in the Spirit becomes a vessel through which God can supply what the assembly needs, a word of knowledge for one who is struggling, a gift of healing for one who is sick, a prophecy that comforts and builds up. The baptism does not make the believer the source of these things. It makes him available to the Source. The Spirit remains the One who decides what gift to give and when, dividing to every man as He wills.

The Heart of It

To be baptized in the Holy Ghost is to be opened to the Spirit's gifts. The fullness within makes the believer a ready channel, and the Spirit distributes through him as He chooses for the building up of the body of Christ.

Section IX

How a Believer Receives

If this baptism is for those who already believe, then the question naturally follows: how does a believer receive it? The Scriptures answer simply. It is received the way all the good gifts of God are received, by asking in faith. Jesus pointed His hearers to the willingness of the Father to give His Spirit to those who ask.

Luke 11:13 If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts unto your children: how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him?

Three things help a believer come to this place. First, thirst. The Lord invites the thirsty to come and drink, so a hunger and a real desire for more of God is the starting point. Second, faith. We receive the things of God by believing, not by striving or by working ourselves into a frenzy; we simply trust the Father to keep His promise. Third, yielding. Since the tongue is involved, the believer must be willing to open his mouth and let the Spirit give the utterance, not waiting for God to force the words but cooperating with the prompting that rises within.

John 7:37 ...If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.

A Gentle Encouragement

No believer should fear this gift or feel they must qualify for it by their own goodness. The Father gives the Spirit freely to His children who ask. Come thirsty, come trusting, and come willing to yield even your tongue to Him. The same Lord who poured out His Spirit at Pentecost has not changed, and the promise is still to all that believe.

Closing

Plunged Into the Fullness of God

We have seen two works of the Spirit set side by side. At salvation the Holy Spirit baptizes the believer into the body of Christ, sealing him and making him forever a member of the Lord's own people. Then the Lord Jesus baptizes that believer with the Holy Ghost and fire, immersing him fully in God, opening the gifts of the Spirit, and driving him with a holy zeal to do the will of God.

The consistent evidence of this immersion, shown again and again in the book of Acts, is the believer speaking with tongues as the Spirit gives the utterance. Yet the evidence is never the point in itself. The point is a life so filled and so surrendered that even the most unruly member, the tongue, is given over to God. Come thirsty, ask in faith, and yield to Him, for the One who is mightier than all still baptizes His people with the Holy Ghost and with fire.

Teacher's Guide

Answer Key

✦

For the teacher. This page prints after the worksheet for your reference during class discussion.

  1. What does baptize mean, and how does it help us understand the baptism in the Holy Ghost?
    To baptize is to dip, plunge, or fully immerse, putting a thing all the way under. Applied to the Holy Ghost, it means the believer is not merely sprinkled or lightly touched by God but plunged into Him, saturated and surrounded by His presence and power. Jesus draws the contrast between John's water baptism and the Spirit baptism that He Himself gives (Matthew 3:11; Acts 1:5).
  2. First baptism: the Baptizer and where we are placed.
    The Holy Spirit is the Baptizer, and He places us into the body of Christ. By one Spirit we are all baptized into one body (1 Corinthians 12:13). This happens to every believer at salvation and joins us to the Lord and to His people.
  3. Second baptism: the Baptizer and the element.
    The Lord Jesus is the Baptizer, and He baptizes us with the Holy Ghost and with fire (Matthew 3:11; Luke 3:16). The roles are reversed from the first baptism. There the Spirit puts us into Christ; here Christ puts us into the Spirit.
  4. How do we know it can follow believing?
    Jesus told disciples who already followed Him that they would be baptized with the Holy Ghost not many days hence, so it was still future for believers (Acts 1:5). The Samaritans believed and were baptized in water, yet received the Spirit only later through Peter and John (Acts 8:14-17). Paul asked the Ephesians whether they had received the Holy Ghost since they believed, a question that assumes the two can be separate moments (Acts 19:2).
  5. The sign at Pentecost, Cornelius, and Ephesus.
    In each case the believers spoke with tongues. At Pentecost they spoke with other tongues as the Spirit gave utterance (Acts 2:4); at Cornelius's house they were heard to speak with tongues and magnify God (Acts 10:46); at Ephesus they spoke with tongues and prophesied (Acts 19:6).
  6. Why tongues is a fitting sign, and why not a measure of worth.
    The tongue is the hardest member to control, so when even the tongue is yielded and used by the Spirit, it shows the person has been immersed past the surface. Yet tongues must never be made a measure of worth, for salvation is by grace through faith alone and tongues add nothing to it. Without love, even the tongues of men and angels are only sounding brass (1 Corinthians 13:1). The fruit of the Spirit and love above all mark maturity; the gifts are tools for service.
  7. What fire does, and the change in the disciples.
    Fire purges what is impure and energizes with heat and zeal. In this baptism it burns away fear and self and kindles a holy drive to do God's will. The disciples who once hid behind locked doors afterward spoke the word of God with boldness (Acts 4:31), having received power for witness (Acts 1:8). Jeremiah felt the word as a fire shut up in his bones that he could not hold in (Jeremiah 20:9).
  8. Relation to the gifts of the Spirit.
    The baptism opens the believer to the full operation of the gifts. The Spirit who fills in fullness now has free course to distribute His gifts through that life, dividing to every man severally as He wills, for the profit of the whole body (1 Corinthians 12:7-11). The believer is not the source but a ready channel.
  9. The Father's willingness, and how we receive.
    The Father is more willing to give the Holy Spirit than an earthly father is to give good gifts to his children (Luke 11:13). We receive by coming thirsty, asking in faith, and yielding to the Spirit, even being willing to open our mouths and let Him give the utterance (John 7:37-39).
  10. Does this baptism save us?
    No. Salvation is by grace through faith alone, and the believer is already sealed with the Spirit at the moment of faith (Ephesians 1:13). The baptism with the Holy Ghost comes upon those who are already saved; its purpose is power for witness and service, not salvation (Acts 1:8). It equips and sends; it does not earn or secure eternal life.
