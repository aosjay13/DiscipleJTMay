A Pronomian Teaching

Relationships

Love in the Home, the Church, and Toward All Men

✦

Introduction

God made us for relationship. From the beginning He declared that it was not good for man to be alone, and He has set us in families, in households, in the fellowship of His church, and in a wider world full of neighbors and even enemies. How we treat one another is no small matter of the faith. The Lord said that all men would know we are His disciples by our love one for another, and He bound up the whole law in two commandments, to love God and to love our neighbor.

The world is full of broken and bitter relationships, but the people of God are called to something higher. In the home between husband and wife, between parents and children, in the fellowship of the church, and even toward those who oppose us, we are to let the love of Christ shape every bond. We will look at what Scripture teaches about each of these relationships, that our homes, our churches, and our dealings with all men might be governed by the love that flows from God Himself.

Section I

Love Is the Foundation of Every Relationship

Before we speak of any particular relationship, we must lay the one foundation on which all of them rest. The Lord gathered the whole of God's law concerning our dealings with others into a single command, to love our neighbor as ourselves. Love is not one duty among many, it is the root from which every right relationship grows and the fulfilling of the law itself.

Matthew 22:37–40 Jesus said unto him, Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy mind. This is the first and great commandment. And the second is like unto it, Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself. On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets.
John 13:34–35 A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another. By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.
Romans 13:10 Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the fulfilling of the law.

Notice that love does not set aside the law but fulfills it. The one who truly loves his neighbor will not steal from him, lie to him, or harm him, for love works no ill. All the commandments concerning our neighbor are summed up and carried out in love. This love, flowing first from our love for God, is the foundation of every relationship we will now consider, in the home, in the church, and toward all men.

Love Fulfills the Law

The whole law concerning others is gathered into one command, to love our neighbor as ourselves. Love is not opposed to the law but is its fulfilling, for love works no ill. This love, flowing from our love for God, is the foundation of every right relationship.

Section II

Husbands and Wives

God designed marriage as a picture of Christ and His church, and He gave each spouse a holy calling within it. The husband is called to love his wife as Christ loved the church, giving himself up for her, a love marked by sacrifice and tender care, never by harshness. The wife is called to honor and submit to her husband as the church does to Christ. Both callings are rooted in love and lived out in mutual honor.

Ephesians 5:25–28 Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it; That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of water by the word, That he might present it to himself a glorious church, not having spot, or wrinkle, or any such thing; but that it should be holy and without blemish. So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself.
Ephesians 5:22–23 Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body.
1 Peter 3:7 Likewise, ye husbands, dwell with them according to knowledge, giving honour unto the wife, as unto the weaker vessel, and as being heirs together of the grace of life; that your prayers be not hindered.

See the kind of headship the husband is given. It is the headship of Christ, who loved the church and gave Himself for it, a leadership of sacrifice and service, not of domination. He is to dwell with his wife according to knowledge, giving her honor, treating her as a fellow heir of the grace of life. The wife honors and submits to such a husband as the church honors Christ. This is no harsh rule but a fellowship of love, each laying down self for the other.

Teacher's Note

Teach this with the same care given to all spiritual authority. The husband's headship is patterned on Christ, who washes feet and lays down His life, never on the domineering rule the Lord forbade among His people. A husband who uses these verses to control, belittle, or harm his wife has abandoned the very pattern of Christ that the passage commands. Honor flows both ways, and the wife is a joint heir of grace, never a lesser soul. Present marriage as mutual love and honor under the lordship of Christ, and never as license for any harshness or mistreatment.

Section III

Parents and Children

God has set an order in the home between parents and children, with duties on both sides. Children are commanded to honor and obey their parents, the first commandment to carry a promise. And parents, especially fathers, are charged to raise their children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord, neither neglecting them nor provoking them to anger, but training them in the ways of God with patient love.

Ephesians 6:1–3 Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right. Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise; That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth.
Ephesians 6:4 And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.
Proverbs 22:6 Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.

The duties run in both directions. Children honor and obey, which is right and carries God's promise of blessing. And parents are to train their children in the Lord without provoking them to wrath, raising them with both instruction and tenderness. The home is the first place where the faith is passed on, where children learn to love and obey God by the example and teaching of parents who walk in His ways. This is a sacred trust given to every Christian household.

Honor and Nurture

Children are commanded to honor and obey their parents, the first commandment with a promise. Parents are charged to train their children in the Lord without provoking them to wrath. The home is where the faith is first passed on, in both instruction and tender love.

Section IV

Brothers and Sisters in the Church

Beyond the family of blood, God has placed us in the family of faith, the church, where we are brothers and sisters to one another. Scripture is full of commands for how we are to treat one another in this fellowship, to love, to bear with, to forgive, to serve, and to build up one another. The many one another commands of the New Testament paint a beautiful picture of life together in the body of Christ.

Galatians 6:2 Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.
Ephesians 4:2–3 With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love; Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.
Hebrews 10:24–25 And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching.

See how rich the life of the church is meant to be. We bear one another's burdens, forbear with one another in love, keep the unity of the Spirit, and stir one another up to love and good works. We are not to forsake the gathering of the saints but to meet together and encourage one another, all the more as the day draws near. In the church we find a family bound not by blood but by the Spirit, where the love of Christ is to be lived out day by day.

The Law of Christ

To bear one another's burdens is to fulfil the law of Christ. The church is a family of faith where we forbear, forgive, serve, and build up one another in love. We gather, encourage, and stir one another to good works, living out the love of Christ in the fellowship of the saints.

Section V

Loving Our Enemies

The hardest relationship of all is with those who oppose us, who wrong us, who count themselves our enemies. Yet here the Lord gave His most striking command, that we love our enemies, bless those who curse us, and pray for those who persecute us. This is the love that sets the children of God apart, for anyone can love those who love them. To love an enemy is the mark of those who are like their Father in heaven.

Matthew 5:44–45 But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you; That ye may be the children of your Father which is in heaven: for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust.
Romans 12:19–21 Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord. Therefore if thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink: for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire on his head. Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.

This love does not mean we approve of evil or pretend wrong is right. It means we refuse to repay evil with evil, we leave vengeance to God who judges justly, and we seek the good even of those who harm us. We feed the hungry enemy and overcome evil with good. This is a love beyond mere human strength, possible only by the Spirit of God working in us, and it shines as a powerful witness in a world that knows only hatred for hatred.

Overcome Evil With Good

The Lord commands us to love our enemies, bless those who curse us, and pray for those who persecute us. We do not repay evil with evil but leave vengeance to God and seek the good even of those who wrong us. This love marks us as children of our Father in heaven.

Section VI

Peace, Forgiveness, and the Witness of Love

Running through all our relationships are two great threads that hold them together, the pursuit of peace and the practice of forgiveness. We are called to live peaceably with all men as much as lies in us, and to forgive one another as God for Christ's sake has forgiven us. Where peace and forgiveness reign, relationships are healed and the love of Christ is put on display before a watching world.

Romans 12:18 If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.
Colossians 3:13 Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.
1 John 4:20–21 If a man say, I love God, and hateth his brother, he is a liar: for he that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen? And this commandment have we from him, That he who loveth God love his brother also.

John makes the matter plain and searching. We cannot claim to love God while hating our brother, for the love of God and the love of others cannot be separated. The one who loves God will love his brother also. So we pursue peace as far as it lies within us, we forgive as we have been forgiven, and we let the love of Christ govern every bond. In this way our relationships become a living witness, showing the world the love of the God we serve.

Love Made Visible

We cannot love God while hating our brother, for the two loves are bound together. Pursuing peace and practicing forgiveness, we let the love of Christ rule every relationship. So our homes, our churches, and our dealings with all men become a living witness of the God we serve.

Conclusion

Bound Together by the Love of Christ

God made us for relationship and gathered the whole law concerning others into one command, to love our neighbor as ourselves. From this foundation flows everything else. Husbands and wives are bound in sacrificial love and mutual honor after the pattern of Christ and His church. Parents and children are joined in honor and tender nurture. The saints are knit together as a family of faith, bearing one another's burdens. And even our enemies are to be met with love, blessing, and good.

Through all these relationships run the threads of peace and forgiveness, for the one who loves God will love his brother also. By this love, lived out in our homes, our church, and toward all men, the world will know that we are the disciples of Christ. Let us then walk in this love, fulfilling the law of God in every bond, that our relationships might honor Him and bear witness to the love that He first poured out on us.

By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another.
