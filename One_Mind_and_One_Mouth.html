One Mind and One Mouth

Growing from Unity in the Work to Unity in the Word

✦

Brother Justin May · Work the Word Church, Madison · Wednesday Evening Service

The Main Text

1 Corinthians 1:10Now I beseech you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment.
Romans 15:5–6Now the God of patience and consolation grant you to be likeminded one toward another according to Christ Jesus: That ye may with one mind and one mouth glorify God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.

The Big Idea

One Sentence to Carry Home

God has given us one mind to work. Now He is calling us to have one mouth to speak, and that one mouth comes from all of us bowing to the same Word.

Introduction

A Church With a Mind to Work

Can Two Walk Together?

Amos 3:3Can two walk together, except they be agreed?

We start tonight with thanksgiving. Something good is happening in this house. People are showing up, pitching in, and carrying the load together. That is not small, and God sees it (Hebrews 6:10).

When Nehemiah rebuilt the wall of Jerusalem, the work went fast for one reason.

The People Had a Mind to Work

Nehemiah 4:6So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work.

Notice the phrase: all the wall was joined together. That is what we have been seeing. Hands joined, shoulders under the same load.

But notice something else. The wall was joined unto the half thereof. Halfway built. Good progress, not finished work.

That is where we are as a church. We have learned to work together, and now God wants to finish the wall. Amos asks how two can walk together unless they are agreed (Amos 3:3). We are walking together in the work. The next step is being agreed in the Word.

Transition

Tonight is not a rebuke. It is a “keep going.” We have the hands. Now let's get the mouth.

I · Ephesians 4

Two Kinds of Unity

Paul gives us both kinds in the same chapter.

The Unity of the Spirit and the Unity of the Faith

Ephesians 4:1–6I therefore, the prisoner of the Lord, beseech you that ye walk worthy of the vocation wherewith ye are called, With all lowliness and meekness, with longsuffering, forbearing one another in love; Endeavouring to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. There is one body, and one Spirit, even as ye are called in one hope of your calling; One Lord, one faith, one baptism, One God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in you all.
Ephesians 4:11–16And he gave some, apostles; and some, prophets; and some, evangelists; and some, pastors and teachers; For the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ: Till we all come in the unity of the faith, and of the knowledge of the Son of God, unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ: That we henceforth be no more children, tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine, by the sleight of men, and cunning craftiness, whereby they lie in wait to deceive; But speaking the truth in love, may grow up into him in all things, which is the head, even Christ: From whom the whole body fitly joined together and compacted by that which every joint supplieth, according to the effectual working in the measure of every part, maketh increase of the body unto the edifying of itself in love.

A.The Unity of the Spirit: Something We Keep (Ephesians 4:3)

God gave it to us when we were joined to Christ. We did not build it. We are told to keep it, to guard it like a fire you don't let go out. It is kept with lowliness, meekness, longsuffering, and forbearing one another in love (Ephesians 4:2). This is the unity we have been growing in: serving side by side and bearing with each other.

B.The Unity of the Faith: Something We Come To (Ephesians 4:13)

Paul says till we all come in the unity of the faith. That is a destination. We are not all there yet, and no church is. This is unity in what we believe and say, and it is the reason God gave teachers and pastors in the first place (Ephesians 4:11–12). The opposite of this unity is being children, tossed to and fro, and carried about with every wind of doctrine (Ephesians 4:14).

C.Ephesians 4:16 Ties Them Together

Every joint supplieth. That's the work, everybody doing their part. Fitly joined together and compacted. That's the agreement, joints that line up (Ephesians 4:16).

A body with strong muscles but joints that don't line up still limps. We have been building muscle. Now we line up the joints.

D.A Warning from Babel: Unity Alone Is Not the Goal

The People Is One

Genesis 11:4–6And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a name, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth. And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which the children of men builded. And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.

God Himself said that a people who are one, with one language, can do whatever they set their minds to (Genesis 11:6). Unity is that powerful. But Babel was united around the wrong thing: let us make us a name (Genesis 11:4). So God scattered them (Genesis 11:8–9).

The Lesson of Babel

Working together is powerful, and that is exactly why it has to be pointed at the truth. A church can be busy and united and still be building the wrong tower.

Teacher's Note: Babel and Pentecost

Many teachers point out that Pentecost looks like Babel turned inside out. At Babel one language was confused into many and the people were scattered (Genesis 11:7–9). At Pentecost the disciples were all with one accord in one place (Acts 2:1), many languages were heard (Acts 2:6–11), and the people were gathered into one body (Acts 2:41–42). Scripture never directly says Pentecost was the “reversal” of Babel, so present this as a picture the text allows, not a doctrine it states. It is a fair and helpful illustration as long as it stays an illustration.

II · The Church at Corinth

A Busy Church That Wasn't Saying the Same Thing

A.Corinth Had Everything Except Agreement

Paul says they were enriched in all utterance and knowledge, and came behind in no gift (1 Corinthians 1:5–7). They were gifted, active, and full of people. And yet Paul's very first correction in the whole letter is about division (1 Corinthians 1:10–11).

Is Christ Divided?

1 Corinthians 1:10–13Now I beseech you, brethren, by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye all speak the same thing, and that there be no divisions among you; but that ye be perfectly joined together in the same mind and in the same judgment. For it hath been declared unto me of you, my brethren, by them which are of the house of Chloe, that there are contentions among you. Now this I say, that every one of you saith, I am of Paul; and I of Apollos; and I of Cephas; and I of Christ. Is Christ divided? was Paul crucified for you? or were ye baptized in the name of Paul?

B.Look at What Paul Asked For

Paul's RequestWhat It Means
speak the same thingOur mouths agree
no divisions among youNo tears in the fabric
the same mindOur thinking agrees
the same judgmentOur conclusions agree

He did not say “be nice to each other.” He said speak the same thing. He was after agreement in what is said and believed.

Note how he appeals: by the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. Agreement is not about whose opinion wins. It is about all of us being under one name.

✦ Bonus: Two Greek Words Worth Knowing

“Divisions” is the Greek word schisma. It is the same word Jesus used for the tear in an old garment: the rent is made worse (Matthew 9:16). A division is a rip in the cloth.

“Perfectly joined together” is the Greek word katartizo. It is the same word used when James and John were mending their nets (Matthew 4:21), and when Paul says to restore a brother overtaken in a fault (Galatians 6:1).

So Paul's picture is simple. A division is a hole in the net. Being perfectly joined together is mending the net. And what happens to a fishing net full of holes? The catch slips right through it. A church whose members say different things will lose the very souls it works so hard to reach.

C.Their Division Was Over Personalities

Are Ye Not Carnal?

1 Corinthians 3:3–4For ye are yet carnal: for whereas there is among you envying, and strife, and divisions, are ye not carnal, and walk as men? For while one saith, I am of Paul; and another, I am of Apollos; are ye not carnal?

Paul calls it carnal. Not deep, not spiritual. Fleshly. Even the “I am of Christ” crowd was part of the problem (1 Corinthians 1:12). They used Christ's name as a club against the others.

The modern version sounds like this: “My favorite preacher says...” “Well, the one I watch says...” When the voice we quote most is a man instead of the Word, we have started a Corinthian church in our living rooms.

Paul's Answer: Is Christ Divided?

There is only one Christ, one cross, and one body (Ephesians 4:4). If He is not divided, His people have no business being divided (1 Corinthians 1:13).

III · The Apostles' Doctrine

The Same Thing Paul Spoke

If we are going to “speak the same thing,” the question is: the same as what? Not the same as the loudest person in the room. Not the same as the most popular teacher. The same as what the apostles delivered (Jude 1:3).

Doctrine, Then Fellowship

Acts 2:42And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.

Notice the order. Doctrine first, then fellowship. The fellowship grew out of a shared doctrine.

A.Paul Himself Said the Same Thing Everywhere

As I Teach Every Where

1 Corinthians 4:17For this cause have I sent unto you Timotheus, who is my beloved son, and faithful in the Lord, who shall bring you into remembrance of my ways which be in Christ, as I teach every where in every church.

As I teach every where in every church. Paul did not have one message for Corinth and another for Ephesus. He modeled the very thing he asked for.

As I Delivered Them to You

1 Corinthians 11:1–2Be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ. Now I praise you, brethren, that ye remember me in all things, and keep the ordinances, as I delivered them to you.
2 Thessalonians 2:15Therefore, brethren, stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle.

B.Paul Held Even Himself to the Same Message

Any Other Gospel

Galatians 1:8–9But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed. As we said before, so say I now again, If any man preach any other gospel unto you than that ye have received, let him be accursed.

As we said before, so say I now again. Paul repeated himself on purpose (Galatians 1:9). The gospel does not get a new edition. Even an apostle or an angel does not get to change it.

C.So What Did Paul Actually Speak?

Keep this simple and let Paul speak for himself.

He Spoke Salvation by Grace That Produces Good Works

Ephesians 2:8–10For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them.

He Spoke That Faith Does Not Cancel God's Law

Romans 3:31Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law.

He Spoke That He Believed All of What Was Written

Acts 24:14But this I confess unto thee, that after the way which they call heresy, so worship I the God of my fathers, believing all things which are written in the law and in the prophets:

Paul also called the law holy, and just, and good (Romans 7:12). When we say the same thing as Paul, we say grace saves, and grace bears fruit in obedience. Both halves. A believer who takes Paul at his word does not have to choose between them.

D.Paul Measured His Words by the Words of Jesus

Wholesome Words

1 Timothy 6:3–5If any man teach otherwise, and consent not to wholesome words, even the words of our Lord Jesus Christ, and to the doctrine which is according to godliness; He is proud, knowing nothing, but doting about questions and strifes of words, whereof cometh envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings, Perverse disputings of men of corrupt minds, and destitute of the truth, supposing that gain is godliness: from such withdraw thyself.

Wholesome words, even the words of our Lord Jesus Christ. Paul did not set himself up against Jesus. He pointed back to Him.

Look at what comes from teaching otherwise: envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings (1 Timothy 6:4). Wrong doctrine and division travel together.

E.Be Careful How Paul Is Read

Things Hard to Be Understood

2 Peter 3:15–16And account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation; even as our beloved brother Paul also according to the wisdom given unto him hath written unto you; As also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction.

Peter warned that Paul is easy to twist (2 Peter 3:16). A lot of division in the church at large comes from pulling Paul away from Jesus, Moses, and the prophets.

Speaking the same thing as Paul means reading Paul in agreement with the rest of Scripture, the way Paul himself read it.

IV · Five Things That Divide

What Pulls Our Voices Apart

A.Following Personalities Instead of the Word

We have already seen it in Corinth: I am of Paul; and I of Apollos (1 Corinthians 1:12). Love your teachers, but don't quote them above the Book.

B.Outside Voices That Never Get Tested

Paul warned about every wind of doctrine (Ephesians 4:14). Today the wind blows through phones, podcasts, and video feeds.

A church can hear one message when it gathers and then soak in five different messages the rest of the week. Then we wonder why we don't say the same thing. The answer is not fear of learning. It is testing everything by the Word (1 Thessalonians 5:21), which we will come back to in Section V.

C.Arguing Over Questions That Only Make Strife

The Servant of the Lord Must Not Strive

2 Timothy 2:23–26But foolish and unlearned questions avoid, knowing that they do gender strifes. And the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient, In meekness instructing those that oppose themselves; if God peradventure will give them repentance to the acknowledging of the truth; And that they may recover themselves out of the snare of the devil, who are taken captive by him at his will.

Speaking the same thing does not mean fighting over everything. Some questions only produce heat. When we do correct, it is with gentleness, patience, and meekness (2 Timothy 2:24–25).

Things Which Make for Peace

Romans 14:19Let us therefore follow after the things which make for peace, and things wherewith one may edify another.

Teacher's Note: Unity Is Not Sameness on Every Opinion

Paul tells us to receive the one who is weak in the faith, but not to doubtful disputations (Romans 14:1). Not every matter is a matter of the faith. Speaking the same thing means agreeing on what God has plainly said. It does not mean every member must hold identical opinions on things Scripture leaves open, such as methods, preferences, or the details of how a thing is carried out. Keep the main thing the main thing, and give room where God gives room.

D.A Careless Tongue

Out of the Same Mouth

James 3:10Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.
Proverbs 6:16–19These six things doth the LORD hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him: A proud look, a lying tongue, and hands that shed innocent blood, An heart that deviseth wicked imaginations, feet that be swift in running to mischief, A false witness that speaketh lies, and he that soweth discord among brethren.

The seventh thing on God's list is the one who sows discord among brethren (Proverbs 6:19). God put that on the same list as hands that shed innocent blood (Proverbs 6:17).

Sowing discord usually doesn't sound like an attack. It sounds like “Did you hear what they said?” or “I'm just telling you what I think.” One mouth means we stop carrying complaints around and start carrying questions to the right place (Matthew 18:15).

E.How Paul Defines a Divider

Mark Them Which Cause Divisions

Romans 16:17–18Now I beseech you, brethren, mark them which cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned; and avoid them. For they that are such serve not our Lord Jesus Christ, but their own belly; and by good words and fair speeches deceive the hearts of the simple.

Look closely. A divider is not simply someone who disagrees. It is someone who causes division contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned.

The Plumb Line

The doctrine is the plumb line (Amos 7:7–8). Unity is measured by it, and so is division. That is why we can't get to one mouth by just trying to be agreeable. We get there by all lining up to the same line.

V · The Acts 15 Pattern

How a Church Learns to Speak the Same Thing

The early church faced a serious disagreement, and they gave us a model for getting to one voice.

  1. They had a real dispute. There was no small dissension and disputation (Acts 15:2). They did not pretend it away.
  2. They brought it to the leaders. They went up unto the apostles and elders about this question (Acts 15:2). They didn't settle it in the parking lot.
  3. They came together to consider it. The apostles and elders came together for to consider of this matter (Acts 15:6).
  4. They heard the testimony of what God was doing (Acts 15:7–12).
  5. They went to the Scriptures. And to this agree the words of the prophets; as it is written (Acts 15:15).
  6. They came to one accord. It seemed good unto us, being assembled with one accord (Acts 15:25).
  7. They sent out one message with many mouths (Acts 15:22–27).

The Same Things by Mouth

Acts 15:27We have sent therefore Judas and Silas, who shall also tell you the same things by mouth.

One letter. Two more witnesses. The same things by mouth. That is speaking the same thing in action. And look at the fruit.

Established in the Faith

Acts 16:4–5And as they went through the cities, they delivered them the decrees for to keep, that were ordained of the apostles and elders which were at Jerusalem. And so were the churches established in the faith, and increased in number daily.

When the churches said the same thing, they were established in the faith and they increased in number daily. Our working together will bear even more fruit when our words agree with our work.

Practical Steps for This House

1Go to the Same Source

They Searched the Scriptures Daily

Acts 17:11These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.

Don't take any teacher's word for it, including this one. Open the Book. When we all go to the same Book, we start coming out with the same answers.

2Learn It Together, Not Just Separately

Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together (Hebrews 10:25). Wednesday nights and Bible studies are where the joints get lined up.

3Bring Questions In. Don't Carry Complaints Around.

If you hear something you don't agree with, go to the source and to the Scriptures (Matthew 18:15). A question asked in the right place builds. A complaint spread in the wrong place tears.

4Honor Those God Set to Watch Over You

They Watch for Your Souls

Hebrews 13:17Obey them that have the rule over you, and submit yourselves: for they watch for your souls, as they that must give account, that they may do it with joy, and not with grief: for that is unprofitable for you.

5Speak the Truth in Love

Speaking the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15). Truth without love tears the net. Love without truth leaves holes in it. We need both to mend it.

6Watch Your Words This Week

Before something leaves your mouth about doctrine or about a brother or sister, ask three questions. Is this what the Word says? Will this build up (Ephesians 4:29)? Would I say it to their face?

VI · John 17

The Prayer Jesus Is Still Waiting to See Answered

That They All May Be One

John 17:17Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth.
John 17:20–21Neither pray I for these alone, but for them also which shall believe on me through their word; That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us: that the world may believe that thou hast sent me.

Jesus prayed for us: them also which shall believe on me through their word (John 17:20). That is every believer who came to faith through the apostles' message.

Notice the foundation. Unity is built on their word, and thy word is truth. Jesus didn't pray for a unity that ignores the truth. He prayed for a unity that grows out of it.

Notice the Purpose

That the world may believe (John 17:21). Our agreement is part of our witness. A divided message confuses the lost. One mouth preaches louder.

Closing

Where the LORD Commands the Blessing

How Good and How Pleasant

Psalm 133:1–3Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity! It is like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron's beard: that went down to the skirts of his garments; As the dew of Hermon, and as the dew that descended upon the mountains of Zion: for there the LORD commanded the blessing, even life for evermore.

The oil started at the head and ran down to the hem (Psalm 133:2). Unity flows down from the Head, which is Christ (Ephesians 4:15), through the whole body.

There the LORD commanded the blessing. God doesn't just allow blessing where there is unity. He commands it (Psalm 133:3).

We have the mind to work. The wall is joined together unto the half (Nehemiah 4:6). Let's finish the wall.

Summary to Restate

  • We keep the unity of the Spirit (Ephesians 4:3).
  • We come to the unity of the faith (Ephesians 4:13).
  • We speak the same thing by all bowing to what the apostles delivered (1 Corinthians 1:10).
  • We test everything by the Book, not by personalities (Acts 17:11).
  • We guard our tongues and bring our questions to the right place (James 3:10).
  • And God commands the blessing where brethren dwell together in unity (Psalm 133:3).

The Appeal

For the believer: Is there a voice you have trusted above the Word? Is there a complaint you have carried around that needs to be brought to the right place, or laid down? Come and lay it at the altar tonight.

For the church: Come and pray together that God would make us of one mind and one mouth, so that with one voice we glorify Him (Romans 15:6).

For anyone not yet saved: The unity we've talked about tonight starts with being joined to Christ Himself. Repent and believe the gospel (Mark 1:15), for by grace you are saved through faith (Ephesians 2:8–10), and come be made one with His body.

Closing Prayer Focus

  • Thank God for the unity in the work He has already given.
  • Ask Him to bring this house into the unity of the faith (Ephesians 4:13).
  • Ask Him to guard every tongue and heal every tear (Psalm 141:3).
  • Pray Jesus' own prayer over the church: that they all may be one (John 17:21).
