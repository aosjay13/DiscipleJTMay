A Core Pronomian Foundation

Living Under the Melchizedek Priesthood

What it means in daily practice for the believer to walk under the better order of Christ, a priesthood distinct from the order of Aaron

✦

The Order We Live Under

Every believer lives under a priesthood, whether he has thought about it or not. A priest is the one who stands between God and man, who brings man to God and offers what man cannot offer for himself. Under the old order, that priest was a son of Aaron, and no Israelite came to God except through him. The whole shape of worship, the access to God, the offering of sacrifice, all of it ran through the Aaronic priesthood. To know which priesthood you live under is to know how you come to God.

We have learned in another teaching that the Aaronic priesthood was fulfilled in Christ and gave way to a better order, the order of Melchizedek. That is settled. But settling the doctrine is not the same as living in it. Many believers know in their heads that Christ is a Priest after the order of Melchizedek, yet they still come to God in their hearts as though the old veil were still hanging, as though they needed a human priest to carry their prayers, as though access to God were distant and uncertain. They hold the right doctrine and live as if they did not.

This teaching is about the living, not only the knowing. It asks what changes in a believer’s daily walk because he lives under the order of Melchizedek and not the order of Aaron. It will show four things that are now true of him: he comes to God directly, with no human priest in between; he is himself made a priest with a priestly work to do; he offers spiritual sacrifices that please God; and he honors the priesthood of Christ in how he gives. The order has changed. The life that flows from it should change with it.

Part One

A Better Order, Older and Higher Than Aaron

Before we live in the order of Melchizedek we should see why it is the better order, because the better order produces the better life. Melchizedek appears only briefly in the story of Abraham, yet Scripture lifts him up as a picture of a priesthood greater than Aaron’s. He was both a king and a priest, which Aaron never was. He had no recorded beginning or end of his priestly line. And Abraham, the father of the whole nation from which Aaron would come, paid tithes to him and received his blessing. The greater blesses the lesser, and so this priesthood stood above the one that came later.

Hebrews 7:1–3 For this Melchisedec, king of Salem, priest of the most high God, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings, and blessed him; To whom also Abraham gave a tenth part of all; first being by interpretation King of righteousness, and after that also King of Salem, which is, King of peace; Without father, without mother, without descent, having neither beginning of days, nor end of life; but made like unto the Son of God; abideth a priest continually.

See how every detail points to Christ. King of righteousness and King of peace, a priest who abides continually, made like unto the Son of God. The Aaronic priest held two things apart, the throne and the altar, but Melchizedek joined them, and so does Christ, who is both our King and our Priest. This is why the order matters. We do not come to a priest who only offers and then steps aside. We come to a Priest who is also the King, who reigns and intercedes at once, and who never dies and never has to be replaced.

Hebrews 7:24–25 But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood. Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them.

Here is the practical heart of the better order. Because Christ lives forever, His priesthood never changes hands. The old priests died and were replaced, again and again, so that no worshipper could ever lean on one priest for a lifetime. But Christ remains, and therefore He saves to the uttermost, all the way, to the very end, everyone who comes to God through Him. And He ever lives to make intercession. At this very moment, our Priest is praying for us. This is not a doctrine to file away. It is the ground on which a believer stands every day.

Why the Order Matters

Melchizedek pictures a priesthood older and higher than Aaron’s, joining king and priest in one person, abiding forever. Christ is the true Priest of this order. Because He lives forever, His priesthood never passes to another, He saves to the uttermost, and He ever lives to intercede for us. The better order gives the believer a Priest he can lean on for life.

Part Two

You Come to God Directly, With No Veil Between

The first great change in daily life is the most precious. Under the order of Aaron, the way into the presence of God was closed to the ordinary worshipper. A thick veil hung in the temple, and behind it was the holiest place where God met with man. Only the high priest could pass through, and only once a year, and never without blood. The message of that veil was plain. The way to God is not open to you. Stay back. Come no further. But when Christ died, that veil was torn from top to bottom, and the message changed forever.

Hebrews 10:19–22 Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus, By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh; And having an high priest over the house of God; Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.

Read what is now true of you. You have boldness to enter the holiest place, the very presence of God, by the blood of Jesus. The way that was once closed is now a new and living way, opened through the torn flesh of Christ. So the command comes, let us draw near. Not stay back, but draw near. And not timidly, but with a true heart and in full assurance of faith. The believer under the order of Melchizedek does not stand outside a veil hoping to be admitted. He walks straight in, because his Priest has opened the way and bids him come.

Hebrews 4:14–16 Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession. For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need.

This is the everyday practice of the better order. When you sin, you do not need a human priest to hear a confession and grant you access. You go straight to the throne of grace yourself, boldly, in the name of your great High Priest, and you obtain mercy and find grace to help. Your Priest understands your weakness, for He was tempted as you are, yet never sinned. So you are not coming to a distant figure who cannot relate to you. You are coming to one who knows your frame and welcomes you. No earthly priest stands between. The way is open, and it stays open.

Teacher’s Note

Many believers carry a hidden Aaronic mindset even while confessing Christ. They feel they must clean themselves up before they can pray, or that some special holy person has access they do not, or that God is far off behind a veil. Gently expose this. The veil is torn. The teaching to press home is the command itself, draw near, come boldly. These are not suggestions for the spiritually advanced. They are the birthright of every believer under the order of Melchizedek.

A practical word for a class: the proof that you believe the veil is torn is that you actually come, and come boldly, especially when you have failed. Running from God after sin is the old order. Running to the throne of grace is the new.

Direct Access Is Yours

The torn veil means the way to God is open to every believer. You do not need a human priest to carry your prayers or grant you access. You come directly to the throne of grace, boldly and in full assurance, through the blood and in the name of your great High Priest, who understands your weakness and welcomes you to draw near.

Part Three

You Are Made a Priest With a Priestly Work

The second great change is that under the better order, the believer is not only granted access to a priest, he is himself made a priest. This was unthinkable under the order of Aaron, where priesthood belonged to one family alone and an ordinary man could be struck down for presuming to do priestly work. But Christ has made His people a priesthood. Not a special class among them, but all of them together. This is one of the great dignities of the New Covenant, and it carries real work to do.

1 Peter 2:5 Ye also, as lively stones, are built up a spiritual house, an holy priesthood, to offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God by Jesus Christ. 1 Peter 2:9 But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people; that ye should shew forth the praises of him who hath called you out of darkness into his marvellous light.

Notice the word royal. A royal priesthood. This is the very joining of king and priest that we saw in Melchizedek, now shared with the people of Christ. We are made kings and priests under our King and Priest. And the priesthood is given a purpose. We are to show forth the praises of the One who called us out of darkness. A priest represents God to men and men to God. So the believer-priest declares God to the world around him and carries the world to God in prayer. This is not a title only. It is a calling with daily duties.

Revelation 1:5–6 Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood, And hath made us kings and priests unto God and his Father; to him be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Amen.

What then is the priestly work of the believer? It is first the ministry of prayer and intercession, standing before God on behalf of others, lifting up family, church, and the lost. It is the offering of worship and praise, the lifting up of the name of God. It is the carrying of the knowledge of God to those in darkness, so that they too might come. The believer does not offer animals on an altar, for that work is finished in Christ. He offers the spiritual sacrifices of a priestly life, and to those sacrifices we now turn.

A Royal Priesthood

Under the better order, every believer is made a priest, even a royal priest, joined to the King and Priest who saved him. This is not a title for a special few but the calling of all God’s people. The work is real: to intercede in prayer, to offer worship and praise, and to declare the praises of God to a world in darkness.

Part Four

The Sacrifices You Now Offer

A priest offers sacrifices, so a believer-priest must ask what he now offers. The animal sacrifices are finished forever, fulfilled in the one offering of Christ, never to be repeated. But Scripture does not leave the altar of the believer empty. It names a whole set of spiritual sacrifices that please God, and these are the daily offerings of the priestly life. They cost us something, as every sacrifice does, but they are offered in gladness and received by God through Christ.

The sacrifice of your own life

Romans 12:1 I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable service.

The first and greatest sacrifice is yourself. Not a lamb laid dead on the altar, but your own body presented as a living sacrifice, given over to God to do His will. This is the foundation of all priestly offering. Before a believer offers praise or service, he offers himself. The whole life is laid on the altar, holy and pleasing to God, and this is called our reasonable service, the only sensible response to the mercies we have received.

The sacrifice of praise

Hebrews 13:15 By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name.

The second sacrifice is praise, the fruit of lips that give thanks to His name. It is called a sacrifice because true praise often costs us something. To praise God in the hard hour, when the heart is heavy and nothing in our circumstances invites a song, is a real offering laid on the altar. We offer it through Christ, our great High Priest, who carries it up to God, and we offer it continually, not only when we feel like it.

The sacrifice of doing good and giving

Hebrews 13:16 But to do good and to communicate forget not: for with such sacrifices God is well pleased. Philippians 4:18 But I have all, and abound: I am full, having received of Epaphroditus the things which were sent from you, an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, wellpleasing to God.

The third sacrifice is doing good to others and sharing what we have. When Paul received a gift from the believers at Philippi, he called it a sacrifice, a sweet-smelling offering, well pleasing to God. So our kindness to the needy and our giving to the work of God are not mere good manners. They are priestly sacrifices laid on the altar and received by God as worship. The believer-priest serves and gives, and heaven counts it an offering.

How the Tithe Fits the Better Order

Some suppose that because the Aaronic priesthood has passed, the tithe passed with it. But the tithe did not begin with Aaron. It began with Abraham, who gave a tenth to Melchizedek long before the law of Moses was ever given. The tithe belongs to the order of Melchizedek even more naturally than to the order of Aaron, for it was first paid to Melchizedek himself. So when the believer brings his tithe today, he is not clinging to a fading Levitical custom. He is doing what Abraham did, honoring the priesthood of Christ, the true Melchizedek, with the firstfruits of all he has.

This is the pronomian way to see giving. The tithe is not a tax owed to a temple system that has ended. It is an act of worship offered to our King and Priest, who lives forever and blesses His people as Melchizedek blessed Abraham. The order it belongs to has not passed away. It abides in Christ.

Under the Order of Aaron

A human priest stood between God and the worshipper. Access was limited and guarded by a veil. Priesthood belonged to one family. Animal sacrifices were offered again and again, and never finally took away sin.

Under the Order of Melchizedek

Christ is our living Priest forever, and we come to God directly through Him. Every believer is made a priest. We offer spiritual sacrifices, our lives, our praise, our giving, accepted by God through the one finished offering of Christ.

The Whole Counsel

Walking in the Better Order Every Day

Gather the whole teaching together and the practical life of the better order stands clear. Christ is our Priest after the order of Melchizedek, older and higher than Aaron, joining King and Priest in one, living forever and never passing the office to another. Because He lives, He saves us to the uttermost and ever intercedes for us. And because we live under His order, four things are now true of us every day. We come to God directly, with no veil and no human priest between, drawing near boldly to the throne of grace. We are ourselves made priests, a royal priesthood, with the real work of intercession, worship, and witness. We offer the spiritual sacrifices of a priestly life, our own bodies, our praise, our doing good and giving. And we honor our King and Priest in our giving, as Abraham honored Melchizedek before the law was ever given.

The danger is to hold the doctrine in the head while living in the old order in the heart. To know the veil is torn yet still cower outside it. To be made a priest yet never do the priestly work. To have an altar for spiritual sacrifices yet leave it bare. The better order is not only something to believe. It is something to live. Draw near, because the way is open. Take up the priestly work, because it is yours. Offer the daily sacrifices, because they please God. Walk every day in the good of belonging to a Priest who lives forever and prays for you now.

Where It All Rests

We live under a Priest who never dies, who opened the way to God and bids us come boldly, who made us priests with Him, and who receives the offerings of our lives. The order of Aaron, with its veil and its repeated sacrifices, is fulfilled and past. The order of Melchizedek, with its open way and its living Priest, is ours in Christ.

Do not live as though the veil still hangs. Draw near, offer yourself, and walk as a priest of the better order, for your great High Priest lives forever and ever lives to intercede for you.

Hebrews 7:25 Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them. Hebrews 10:22 Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.
